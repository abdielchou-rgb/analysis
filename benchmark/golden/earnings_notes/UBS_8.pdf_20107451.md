# China Banks

# Q124 Preview: Widening divergence; Be selective

## Deeper revenue & PPOP decline; earnings to stay largely flat

China banks will kick off their Q124 result reporting season from late April (Figure 7). While we see another challenging season in banks' fundamentals - likely deeper revenue & PPOP decline with flat NPAT growth, we expect a further diverging trend across banks as pointed out in our Q423 wrap-up report. Despite limited turnaround in key drivers such as consumer finance, capital market, property sector, we turn more positive on major joint-stock banks (JSBs) as we believe market sentiment towards major JSBs have likely troughed given market values their increasing focus on shareholder return and profitability rather than pure asset expansion. As such, we expect Q124 revenue growth for major JSBs (excl. PAB) to outperform large banks (avg. -2.0% YoY vs -4.5% YoY) mainly on a much smaller YoY NIM contraction (avg. -17bps YoY vs -27bps YoY), though fee income could be a larger drag for JSBs. With mild cost-cutting and provisioning release, we expect NPAT growth largely flat for major JSBs (excl. PAB) and Big-6 SOE banks at +0.2%/-0.2% YoY. While most regional banks have not released 2023 annual reports yet, we expect large divergence to remain in Q124. (Figure 1)

## NIM still the key driver, with milder QoQ but significant YoY compression

We expect banks' NIM to continue trend down in Q124 given 1) loan repricing; 2) lower retail loan yield amid fierce competition; 3) continued time deposit migration; whereas some banks indicated a stabilizing trend in corporate loan yield. Going forward, more LPR cuts could be the biggest challenge, and deposit rate cuts could provide some cushion. While we forecast a smaller QoQ NIM decline (avg. -3bps/-5bps QoQ for major JSBs/large banks vs -6bps/-10bps QoQ in Q423), considering the base effect, most banks would still see significant YoY NIM decline, particularly large banks (-27bps vs -25bps YoY in Q423). Regional banks’ YoY NIM contraction could be milder vs national banks, while the trend of smaller QoQ decline is likely similar. Fee income drag was widely reflected in banks' Q423 results following bancassuance fee cut in Q323 and distribution fee cut for funds in mid-2023, which would still extend into Q124 (avg. - 10% vs -5% YoY for major JSBs/large banks). We expect banks to still report steady NPL ratio. While credit cost base for Q1/Q234 still had some room (avg. 107bps/124bps in Q1/Q223, Figure 3), we see more constraint for certain banks to cut provisioning from Q324 onward.

## Who would be the outperfomers or laggards?

We generally expect leading JSB & regional banks to outperform most SOE banks in the Q124 result season. Typical outperfomers could be CITIC bank, CCB and CSRCB, while laggards may include PAB, PSBC, BOCOM and ICBC. We expect CITIC to outperform with stabler NIM and likely positive revenue growth at 2.0% YoY. PAB, however, may still report double-digit revenue/ PPOP decline in Q124 (-13.4%/-15.8% YoY) from significant NIM & fee income drop on high base. Among large banks, CCB may catch up, while we turn more cautious on ICBC and BOC and forecast sharper top-line decline (-9.8%/-5.3% YoY) from drastic YoY NIM contraction (-38bps/-32bps YoY), though their NPAT growth may still be supported by cost-cutting and lower credit cost. We forecast PSBC and BOCOM to still deliver negative NPAT growth in Q124 as in A423. For regional banks, we expect CSRCB to lead in both topline (high-single digit) and bottom line (midto-high teens) YoY growth, whose Q124 NIM may post potential upside risk vs consensus, considering low base in Q423 and strong guidance for 2024. CQRCB, BOSH and BOBJ could record flat or mildly negative YoY growth in revenue and earnings in Q124.

## Focus on management guidance on performance and dividend policy

We expect the earnings season to be an overall negative catalyst in terms of fundamentals. Going forward, however, we suggest investors focusing on 1) whether NIM compression and fee decline to moderate sequentially; 2) whether the deterioration trend of retail book NPL to slow down; 3) timing to change the dividend payout frequency to at least twice a year, which would be a positive to banks' price stabilization; 4) capital level after the implementation of the new capital regulation, which is considered overall positive to SOE banks.

## Valuation Method and Risk Statement

We derive our price targets for H-share China banks based on a three-stage dividend discount model (DDM). We derive our price targets for A-share China banks based on P/B to ROE valuation methodology. We think major risks to China banks include: 1) deterioration in asset quality, underpinned by a weaker macro environment and domestic property market activity; 2) regulatory risks related to capital, liquidity and off-balance-sheet activities of banks; 3) deterioration in funding structure and balance-sheet liquidity positions, driven by a potential rollover of loans and a lengthening of asset durations; and 4) liberalisation of interest rates in the medium term and the consequent pressure on bank profitability.
