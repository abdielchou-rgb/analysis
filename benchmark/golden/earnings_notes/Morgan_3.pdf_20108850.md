## Executive summary

 Sentiment on the Chinese economy turned cautiously optimistic. 1Q GDP growth came in stronger than expected at 5.3%oya (7.4%q/q saar), though the growth momentum softened in 2Q (JPM forecast: 3.5%q/q saar).

 Uneven development: Outperformance in industrial production, export volume and manufacturing investment, under-performance in real estate investment and housing-related consumption, infrastructure investment / consumption / service in-between

 Withdrawal of policy support in April but a quick adjustment. April credit growth hit a new record low at 8.3%oya, and TSF flow registered the first decline since 2005, along with net decline in government bond issuance. The politburo meeting on April 30 emphasized the importance of policy continuity, calling for accelerated pace of government bond issuance and use of rate cuts and RRR cuts by the PBOC.

 Housing market corrections continued, despite the latest round of housing stabilization measures in May. The PBOC has announced three easing measures lately, including lowering of down-payment requirements for mortgage borrowing. In addition, a new policy initiative for the government to buy unsold homes has been launched, which is a desirable policy move to reduce housing inventory, increase public housing investment and indirectly encourage consumption from beneficiary households. Progress appears to be slow by far.

 Lingering deflation pressure. 1Q24 was the fourth consecutive quarter of negative GDP deflator, which likely will persist in 2Q and 3Q. It reflects demand-supply imbalance and lack of pricing power for the corporate sector.

 Our full-year growth forecast is 5.2%, assuming the economy will return to trend growth in 2H at 4.5%q/q saar. We do not expect additional fiscal stimulus, and expect one 10bp rate cut/additional 25bp RRR cut from the PBOC with lower TSF growth to 8.5% (vs. 9.5% in 2023).

 Two meetings in July: late July politburo meeting on economic policy guideline in 2H24, and 3rd Plenum on structural reforms in next five years. Do not expect major policy changes from the politburo meeting, do not expect a major surprise from the 3rd Plenum. The 3rd Plenum likely will stick to the guidelines of the 19th Party Congress with an updated reform agenda. Policy actions matter more than party documents to restore confidence. The most important reform areas include: fiscal reform; rural land reform; equal support for manufacturing and service upgrade in promoting innovations and industry upgrade.

## Understanding China’s policy framework

##  Two different narratives (government vs. market)

 Attitude towards 5.2% growth in 2023: “meeting the goal” vs. “weaker than expected”

 Industry policy: success in new economy sectors (solar energy, lithium battery, NEV) vs. excess supply and possible global trade tension

 Structural transformation: necessary transition cost vs. amplified cost due to implementation problem

 Inflation pressure: temporary vs. structural

 Policy response: targeted and gradual vs. large stimulus package

 Policy effectiveness: cyclical vs. structural measures

##  2024 policy outlook

 Macro policy is growth-friendly, but not stimulus-type

 Fiscal policy: augmented fiscal deficit is 11.6% of GDP, marginal higher than 11.5% in 2023. Fiscal impulse improved from -1.4% of GDP in 2023 to +0.1% in 2024, led by budgetary items and centra government

 Monetary policy: continuity in interest rate policy and credit policy, expanded use in structural monetary policy, liquidity operation to support fiscal policy

 Driving factors for policy rate cuts: inflation; banks’ NIM pressure; CNY and capital flow dynamics

 Exchange rate policy: smaller CNY volatility under the new regime

 Industry policy: prioritize manufacturing upgrade

 Changes in policy direction (demand support, equal treatment of manufacturing and service upgrade and of SOE and non-SOE, supply vs. demand-side housing policy easing) are more important than the size of macro policy support

## What to expect from the 3rd Plenum in July?

 Theme: Deepening reforms; Chinese-style modernization

 Low expectations for major surprises at the 3rd Plenum. The blueprint was laid out at the 19th Party Congress and reinforced at the 20th Party Congress. Also, policy actions/implementation matter more for investors.

##  Key reforms

 Fiscal reform: (1) fiscal structure between central and local governments; (2) increase new sources of fiscal revenue; (3) cut fiscal expenditure

 Rural land reform: farmer homeland will become tradeable and transferable

 New-quality productive forces: Equal treatment between manufacturing and service upgrades; equal treatment between SOEs and non-SOEs

 Other reforms: green economy; SOE reform; digital economy; unified national market; rural area reform; financial reform; high-level opening up
