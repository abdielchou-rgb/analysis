# Asia Pacific Equity Derivatives Highlights

## HK/China 4Q23 earnings: principal trading themes, market expectations and opportunities in stock options

The 4Q23 earnings season for Hong Kong/China is set to begin in late February. This report aims to outline the principal trading themes leading into the earnings season, analyze market expectations as evidenced by dispersion and implied earnings movements and identify opportunities within single stock options.

China SOEs in focus: As the earnings season unfolds, attention increasingly turns to China SOE reforms. Shares of Hong Kong-listed China SOEs have seen their 1M turnover higher by 20% over their 3M average. This comes against ongoing negative earnings revision breadth since 2022. Investors are drawn to SOEs for their value and defensive attributes. This interest is supported by SASAC suggesting “market value management<sup>”</sup> could become part of SOE management KPIs. However, investor engagement remains half of what it was at previous highs, showing measured optimism. News and market actions hint at more developments to come. Meanwhile, a moderate increase in implied volatility shows restrained excitement. Clearer details on SOE reforms, expected in the March earnings season, may boost investor interest and affect market movements.

Dispersion trading opportunities in HK: As of now, the entry point for entering dispersion trading is close to the lowest observed in the past two years. We observed that the realized dispersion for January 2024 has decreased by approximately 2 volatility points compared to the levels recorded in December 2023. Stocks with a track record of heightened dispersion compared to the current entry point include HSBC (5 HK), Trip.com (9961 HK), BYD (1211 HK), Geely Automobile (175 HK), CNOOC (883 HK) and Li Auto (2015 HK).

Earnings implied move: About 32% of Hang Seng and 17% of H-share names (by index weight) are expected to announce their 4Q23 results in February 2024. For companies due to report, the options market on average expects higher levels of volatility this quarter compared to the levels observed historically. Due to the hype surrounding SOE reforms, implied earnings day move seems expensive for most of the SOEs with reasonable options liquidity.

Options trades into the earnings: Considering fundamental catalysts and volatility pricing, we recommend the following options strategy: 1) buying call ratios on PetroChina (857 HK) to leverage potential gains from SOE reforms, 2) buying call spreads on NetEase (9999 HK) and Tencent (700 HK) to benefit from an improving outlook in the gaming sector; and 3) buying put spread collars on Xpeng (9868 HK) as a hedge against potential stock downside.

Our analysis, which segments Hong Konglisted stocks based on thematic relevance, uncovers a marked increase in the activity of shares associated with Chinese SOEs (Figure 1). Specifically, these shares have seen their one-month turnover exceed their three-month average by 20%, indicating a robust uptick in investor interest.

This heightened attention towards Chinese SOE shares occurs amidst a challenging backdrop characterized by persistently negative earnings revisions breadth—a metric that compares net revisions against total revisions (Figure 2). This trend underscores a continuous downtrend in earnings expectations that has beleaguered the market since 2021, with no immediate signs of reversal. Within this context, Chinese SOEs emerge as increasingly attractive for their value and defensive investment characteristics. This allure is further amplified by recent announcements from the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission (SASAC), which suggests the incorporation of “market value management<sup>”</sup> into the key performance indicators for SOE management, reigniting optimism for a revival in SOE reforms.

Given that investor interest in SOE reforms is not a new phenomenon, we review historical trends in investor sentiment towards SOE reforms. We seek to determine how much of the current market expectations are already priced into valuations and assess the room for further market reactions to this evolving investment narrative. We find long China SOEs has become a strategy increasingly accepted among investors. Yet, it's important to highlight that the current level of investor engagement with this theme is only half of what we've seen during peak periods of enthusiasm for SOE reforms.

Our analysis draws on several key observations:

The volume of news coverage on Chinese SOE reforms in Chinese media has risen to 1.3 times the standard deviation above the average since 2020 (Figure 4The volumfnws crag CiSOE  d t1.3   b 20). This concept refers to a tailored valuation approach designed to enhance the valuations of China SOEs. News reporting could significantly influence investor decision-making. The contribution of Chinese SOEs in the Hong Kong equity market's turnover is currently moderate, with these entities accounting for 27% of the market's turnover. While this figure is noteworthy, there's room for growth, particularly when we recall that turnover peaked at around 32% during the peak of interest in the ZhongTeGu initiative (Figure 5). This gap between the current turnover and the historical peak underscores the untapped potential for increased investor engagement with China SOEs. Should future reforms or favorable market conditions emerge, we could see a significant uptick in trading activity, possibly pushing turnover rates to match or exceed the highs observed during the peak of ZhongTeGu-driven enthusiasm.

For Chinese SOE stocks with liquid options, the 3-month ATM implied volatility has risen by \~4.4 points from January 2024 lows, indicating a growing interest in the SOE reform theme (Figure 6). This modest increase suggests that while there is an anticipation of volatility and an acknowledgment of potential reform impacts on SOE valuations, investor engagement with the theme remains moderate. Notably, during the peak of ZhongTeGu-driven enthusiasm, implied volatility in these stocks saw a more pronounced increase of 7 volatility points from the lows in April 2023 to the highs in May 2023. This contrast in volatility movements underscores a more reserved investor stance towards SOE reforms currently, compared to the more dynamic engagement

observed during earlier periods of reform hype.

## Dispersion pattern in HK stocks

During earnings season, stock prices often experience increased dispersion due to the influx of new information. This variance in earnings outcomes relative to market expectations leads to divergent stock performances, thereby increasing the dispersion in stock prices. To identify earnings sensitive stocks, we calculate the stock versus index realized volatility over a one-month window encompassing two weeks before and after earnings are released. We then evaluate the median excess realized volatility against the current stock versus index implied volatility. This comparison helps determine the market's pricing of expected dispersion compared to historical data. The hit ratio, defined as the likelihood of past earnings' excess realized volatility surpassing the current implied volatility spread, is calculated. This ratio ranks stocks based on the probability of profitable dispersion trading during earnings at the present entry level.

To illustrate the dispersion pattern beyond the earnings period, we present an analysis of the stock versus index realized volatility spanning 12 calendar months. Although most stocks exhibit increased excess volatility during earnings seasons, selecting stocks with a historical tendency to offer greater dispersion beyond earnings remains a key interest for investors. This consideration is particularly relevant given that typical dispersion trades are executed with tenors of six months or longer, whereas the earnings period constitutes only a fraction of this timeframe. Therefore, identifying stocks with higher dispersion probabilities can be crucial for optimizing long-term dispersion trading strategies.

By evaluating the volatility term structure of a particular stock, we can estimate the size of earnings day price move that is implied by the options market. The implied move can then be compared to average earnings-day moves delivered by the stock on past earnings days, in order to identify cheap or rich volatility. Options only provide information about the size of a price move; they do not imply the direction (for instance, if the implied size of a move is 5%, the stock may move up or down by 5% following the announcement).

Figure 11Curent impldvs hoca gz -y plots the implied earnings day move vs. historical earnings day moves for Hong Kong listed stocks. Our universe includes names with 3M average daily turnover greater than \$5M and 3M average daily notional option interest greater than \$50M. Names above the diagonal have a rich implied move compared to what the stock has delivered on past earnings days, and names below the diagonal are cheap relative to history.

With SOEs currently capturing investors' attention due to the hype surrounding the theme, we have observed that most of the SOEs with reasonable options liquidity show relatively expensive earnings day move. While Sinopec (386 HK) is the only one exhibiting cheap earnings day move, market implied move for stocks such as ABC, PetroChina, and China Life are on the side of expensive earnings day move compared to 3Y median of realized move (Figure 11Curent impldvs hoca gz -y). We think with the hype built around the theme, there is still room for volatility to be traded higher. The sectors of the SOEs under coverage encompass Energy, Financials, Real Estate, and Materials.

## Appendix: Methodology for Earnings-Day Volatility Analysis

In order to ascertain the expected size of an earnings-related move, one must back out the contribution to implied volatility that comes from the earnings announcement. This is determined by measuring the difference between implied volatility over the time period that includes the earnings announcement date and the time period that does not. The earnings announcement date can fall either before the maturity of the first month contract or between the maturities of the first and next-month contracts. The contribution to volatility that comes from the earnings announcement is calculated as follows:

If the earnings announcement falls before the maturity of the 1st month contract, earnings-related volatility will cause both the first and the next-month implied volatilities to become elevated by $\sigma _ { I }$ and $\sigma _ { 2 } .$ . However, the difference in volatility between the expiration of the 1st and 2nd month contracts (forward volatility) $\sigma _ { I 2 }$ is not affected by the earnings report.

## Risks of Common Option Strategies

Risks to Strategies: Not all option strategies are suitable for investors; certain strategies may expose investors to significant potential losses. We have summarized the risks of selected derivative strategies. For additional risk information, please call your sales representative for a copy of “Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options.<sup>”</sup> We advise investors to consult their tax advisors and legal counsel about the tax implications of these strategies. Please also refer to option risk disclosure documents.

Put Sale: Investors who sell put options will own the underlying asset if the asset<sup>’</sup>s price falls below the strike price of the put option. Investors, therefore, will be exposed to any decline in the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price below the strike potentially to zero, and they will not participate in any price appreciation in the underlying asset if the option expires unexercised.

Call Sale: Investors who sell uncovered call options have exposure on the upside that is theoretically unlimited.

Call Overwrite or Buywrite: Investors who sell call options against a long position in the underlying asset give up any appreciation in the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price above the strike price of the call option, and they remain exposed to the downside of the underlying asset in the return for the receipt of the option premium.

Booster : In a sell-off, the maximum realized downside potential of a double-up booster is the net premium paid. In a rally, option losses are potentially unlimited as the investor is net short a call. When overlaid onto a long position in the underlying asset, upside losses are capped (as for a covered call), but downside losses are not.

Collar: Locks in the amount that can be realized at maturity to a range defined by the put and call strike. If the collar is not costless, investors risk losing 100% of the premium paid. Since investors are selling a call option, they give up any price appreciation in the underlying asset above the strike price of the call option.

Call Purchase: Options are a decaying asset, and investors risk losing 100% of the premium paid if the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price is below the strike price of the call option.

Put Purchase: Options are a decaying asset, and investors risk losing 100% of the premium paid if the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price is above the strike price of the put option.

Straddle or Strangle: The seller of a straddle or strangle is exposed to increases in the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price above the call strike and declines in the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price below the put strike. Since exposure on the upside is theoretically unlimited, investors who also own the underlying asset would have limited losses should the underlying asset rally. Covered writers are exposed to declines in the underlying asset position as well as any additional exposure should the underlying asset decline below the strike price of the put option. Having sold a covered call option, the investor gives up all appreciation in the underlying asset above the strike price of the call option.

Put Spread: The buyer of a put spread risks losing 100% of the premium paid. The buyer of higher-ratio put spread has unlimited downside below the lower strike (down to zero), dependent on the number of lower-struck puts sold. The maximum gain is limited to the spread between the two put strikes, when the underlying is at the lower strike. Investors who own the underlying asset will have downside protection between the higher-strike put and the lower-strike put. However, should the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price fall below the strike price of the lower-strike put, investors regain exposure to the underlying asset, and this exposure is multiplied by the number of puts sold.

Call Spread: The buyer risks losing 100% of the premium paid. The gain is limited to the spread between the two strike prices. The seller of a call spread risks losing an amount equal to the spread between the two call strikes less the net premium received. By selling a covered call spread, the investor remains exposed to the downside of the underlying asset and gives up the spread between the two call strikes should the underlying asset rally.

Butterfly Spread: A butterfly spread consists of two spreads established simultaneously – one a bull spread and the other a bear spread. The resulting position is neutral, that is, the investor will profit if the underlying is stable. Butterfly spreads are established at a net debit. The maximum profit will occur at the middle strike price; the maximum loss is the net debit.
