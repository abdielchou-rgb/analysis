# Survey suggests labour market to improve in Q1; hiring intentions strongest in services

## UBS Evidence Lab data suggest job market to improve in Q1, led by services

China's job market slowed in Q423, based on UBS Evidence Lab's quarterly survey of 204 firms in 77 cities, but breaking down the data by sector reveals a divergence, with a clear rebound in construction. Services looks set to lead the recovery in Q124. Overall, a net 40% of the firms expect to increase hiring on a q/q basis in Q1, up 8ppts on the prior quarter; in services the jump is 20 ppts. Some seasonality might be at play; expectation of further consumption normalization may also be a factor. Macro level data suggest a largely stable labour market with lingering pressures.

## Construction jobs, wages rebound in Q423

Even as the overall share of firms increasing hiring rose in Q4, the share cutting hiring q/q grew by a higher margin. However, the net share of construction firms hiring more on a q/q basis jumped from 24% in Q3 to 41% in Q4, driven by a pick-up in current business orders. By contrast, the net share of manufacturing firms increasing hiring q/q continued to slide (to 10% in Q4 from 21% in Q3). In services, the net share of firms hiring more q/q slowed to 26% from 37% in Q3.

## Services to drive employment gains in Q124

Looking ahead, the hiring outlook is significantly stronger in the service sector with a net 55%, up from 35%, planning to hire more q/q. While hiring demand is marginally better in construction, it is slightly worse in manufacturing. Some 52% of all the firms expect their labour costs to grow q/q this quarter, up from 41% in the prior quarter and 38% in Q422. UBS Greater China consumer team is looking to a recovery in services (which employ 47% of the labour force) to support consumption this year (read more here).

## Who's hiring and who's not?

We spoke to senior managers at two global recruitment firms. Recent demand in South China has been in: 1) industries with technical barriers/in niches (e.g. new materials, optical modules); 2) domestic personal care firms going global; 3) new consumer brands (e.g. tea, coffee). Meanwhile, demand has slowed in hard tech, EV charging & solar sectors. One cross-sector theme appears to be a focus on higher quality talent. Firms are raising the bar for candidates & becoming more targeted in their search; many are also seeking individuals that can help drive industrial upgrading & digitalization projects.

## Macro level data show stable labour market with lingering pressures

Survey results seem to be more optimistic about future hiring despite current weakness. On a macro level, while overall unemployment held largely stable in Q4, elevated unemployment rate for young people and soft employment index within PMIs suggest that that the labour market remains under pressure. The more optimistic corporate outlook might be related to expectation of further consumption normalization, especially in travel related service sectors around CNY holiday, and some seasonality factors. We expect further labour market improvement to support modest consumption recovery in 2024, albeit likely at a slower pace.

## Construction jobs, wages rebound in Q4

China's job market slowed further in Q4, based on UBS Evidence Lab's quarterly survey of 204 firms across 77 cities: even as the share of firms increasing hiring climbed 3ppts, to 43% in Q4 from 40% in Q3, the share cutting hiring q/q grew by 7ppts from 10% in Q3 to 17%, the highest level since this data series began in Q320 (Figure 1). However, breaking down the data by sector reveals a clear divergence across sectors. The net share of construction firms hiring more on a q/q basis recovered from 24% in Q3 to 41% in Q4, led by an improvement in current business orders; by contrast, the net share of manufacturing firms increasing hiring q/q continued to slide (to 10% in Q4 from 21% in Q3); while in services, the net share of firms hiring more q/q fell to 26% from 37% in Q3 (Figure 2). Overall, wage growth improved in Q4 with 46% of firms reporting a q/q lift in average monthly wages, vs. 35% in Q3 (Figure 4). The pick-up was strongest in construction (Figure 5).

## Who's hiring and who's not?

We spoke to senior managers at two global recruitment firms - one in Beijing, the other in Shenzhen. The Shenzhen recruiter focuses on Southern China. In her experience, recent hiring demand has come from: 1) industries with relatively high technical barriers/ in niches including new materials and optical modules; 2) domestic personal care firms looking at going global e.g., via Amazon or other platforms into SE Asia and the Middle East; and 3) emerging consumer brands, e.g., new tea and coffee companies (one of the recruiters mentioned working with a new milk tea brand which is offering products at a lower price than Heytea; she also mentioned that a more premium coffee brand her firm works with is closing some stores amid price competition). In 2022 and early 2023, hard tech, EV charging and solar sectors were hiring, but the trend has since cooled, she said. As far as she is aware some internet companies also continue to lay off staff amid business line rationalisation.

The Beijing consultant specialises in senior exec hires at foreign firms. Last year, these were concentrated in industrial printing, pharmaceutical packaging, high-end new energy vehicles, and internet/cloud. She commented that, since 2022, her firm has received no new projects from some of the biggest foreign consulting companies; in fact, based on her knowledge, some foreign consultants were still laying off staff and cutting pay in Q3/Q423. As for her own industry, she thinks that the general trend has been one of downsizing since mid-2023; the other recruiter shared that while her firm has not cut wages she has heard of rival recruiters doing so.

One common cross-sector theme appears to be a focus on higher quality talent. One recruiter explained that clients are raising the bar for job applicants and becoming more targeted in their search, a sentiment that was echoed by a leading branding/marketing/ communications firms we met with in Shanghai recently. Companies are seeking individuals that can help drive industrial upgrading and digitalization projects aimed at improving internal management processes, said the recruiter in Shenzhen.

## Macro implications

Deterioration in Q4 hiring sentiment reflects sustained growth pressures, while the construction sector may have benefited from infrastructure FAI. Sustained property downturn, slow consumption recovery and declines in exports (albeit narrower than Q3) may have continued to depress corporate hiring momentum. That said, respondents from the construction sector reported stronger hiring intention than Q3, increased difficulty to hire, and rising monthly salary, all pointing to stronger construction activities. They may have benefited from better-than-expected infrastructure FAI, which accelerated modestly from 6.2%y/y in Q3 to 7.3% in Q4, partly due to deployment of special LG bond and special CGB issuance. Real construction VA growth also edged up by 0.1ppt to 6.7%y/y in Q4, reflecting improvement in construction activities.

Macro level data suggest largely stable labour market but pressures remain. Overall unemployment rate was largely stable at 5-5.1% in Q4, similar to September (5%). The “new” youth unemployment rate (age 16-24) that excludes students in school/university recorded 14.9% in December. While such reading is lower than the old series (including students) of 21.3% in June, the current reading is still elevated and not comparable to the old data series. Unemployment rate for age 25-29 recorded 6.1% in December. Unemployment for rural migrants edged down to 4.3% in December (vs 4.7% in September, 4.6% in Q3). The number of migrant worker that goes out saw stable growth of 2.7% y/y in Q4 (vs 2.8%y/y in Q3). Meanwhile, employment index in both NBS and Caixin manufacturing PMI as well as NBS non-manufacturing PMI remained below 50 throughout Q4. Overall, macro level data suggest a stable labor market with lingering weakness. See more about Q4 macro data here.

Survey results seem to be more optimistic about future hiring despite current weakness. Some seasonality might be at play, as labour shortage in the most labourintensive service sector could occur around the CNY holiday. Expectation of further consumption normalization might have also been a factor driving increased hiring intention, especially for the service sector, in particular for sectors related with tourism (e.g. catering, accommodation, travelling, etc). Given the 2024 CNY holiday will be the first fully normalized CNY holiday, some firms in the service sector might be planning to hoard labour to meet that demand. In addition, recovering real trade activities since Q4 may underpin hiring of some related service sectors.

Household saving rate rebounded while income growth softened on 2-year average basis. Even though the overall labour market remains stable and surveyed respondents expect hiring to improve in Q124, household sentiment softened in Q4. The Q4 household survey showed real disposable income growth improved to 6.7% in Q4 from 6.1% previously. That said, a low base was a key driver, while the 2-year average growth from 2021 decelerated. As such, households became more cautious in Q4 and accumulated excess saving increased further by RMB 500bn again to RMB 5.6trn (4.4% of GDP), after a short-lived small decline in Q3. Considering lingering labour market pressures, soft income growth and rising excess saving, we expect real household income growing by about 5% in 2024 (vs 6.1% in full year 2023, 4.5% of 2021-2023 average growth).

Consumption to recover modestly at a slower pace in 2024 with labour market improvement, while uncertainties mainly come from property activities. Under the assumption of a stabilized property market, we expect the household income and labour market normalization to continue in 2024, led by further recovery in the services sector. We expect household confidence to remain weak but not to worsen significantly further, which means some excess savings will be released to help boost consumption. As such, consumption recovery should continue. However, as base effects fades, we forecast services consumption growth to slow in 2024 and total real consumption growth to moderate to 5.5%. If property activities fails to stabilize in H1 and decline deeper, consumption recovery would be hindered by weaker labour market and income, negative wealth effect, and continued cautious consumer behaviour.
