# 4Q23 Earnings Final Cut – Biggest Miss Since 2018

Chinese equities' earnings in 4Q23 mark further deterioration and indicate a longer period of estimate cuts. 1Q24 results remain critical for any signs of stabilization.

As of April 30, 2024, 639 companies out of 765 (83%) in MSCI China had reported 4Q23 results, while 594 out of 761 (78%) MSCI China A Onshore had reported. Key takeaways:

MSCI China – worst quarter since 2018: 30% of constituent companies have missed consensus forecasts by number of companies, but the results were a marginal net beat if measured by weighted surprise (+7%) ( Exhibit 1 , Exhibit 2 ). The scale of the net miss in 4Q23 earnings, if measured by number of companies, is the largest since we started tracking earnings surprises systematically in 2018, reflecting a gradually expanding scope of earnings results deterioration.

A-share market – a similar disappointing scene: Reported earnings have fallen short of consensus notably by both number of companies (-38%) and weighted surprise (-16%). The scope of miss (by number of companies), as with MSCI China, is the worst in our documenting history; the magnitude (by weighted surprise) is close to historical trough. This quarter also marks sizable deterioration vs. 3Q23: the magnitude of the net miss by number of companies and weighted surprise worsened \~5ppt and \~8ppt, respectively. The miss also widened vs. the 4Q23 interim review, where the two measures stood at -35% and -11%, respectively.

In revenue terms – miss by number of companies: Both A-shares and MSCI China missed consensus revenue estimates by number of companies, but MSCI China posted a net beat and A-shares posted a net miss measured by weighted surprise. The magnitude of the miss by number of companies has also widened vs. 3Q23.

Sector observations – broad-based disappointment: All A-shares sectors showed net misses if measured by numbers of companies. Compared to A-shares' broadbased misses, MSCI China showed divergence: five sectors posted a net beat by one of the two measures (Consumer Discretionary, Energy, Financials, Industrials, and IT). Other sectors' earnings largely disappointed on a broad basis. Communication Services and Real Estate posted in-line results by weighted surprise.

Lack of signs of bottoming in earnings expectations suggests more patience through longer reduction process: We have seen re-acceleration of reductions in consensus earnings estimates since mid-March, synchronized with disappointing 4Q23 earnings. We expect further continuation of the downtrend given the final results we present in this report. 1Q24 results would be critical to monitor – to see if there are any signs of earnings expectations bottoming out, particularly given astrong quarter for real GDP but amid rising deflationary pressure ( Exhibit 15 ).

# MSCI China Earnings Surprises – 4Q23 Final Cut: Continued Miss vs. 3Q23 by Number of Companies

Note: As of April 30, 2024, 639 companies out of 765 in MSCI China had reported 4Q23 results. Our analysis of MSCI China earnings surprises is based on constituent companies that have reasonable quality data for consensus estimates, comprising 507 companies in MSCI China, representing 66% of the index by number of companies and 80% by index weight.

## Key observations

1) At the aggregate index level, reported earnings have fallen short of consensus forecasts by number of companies (-30%) but slightly beat consensus if measured by weighted surprise (+7%): See Exhibit 10 . 4Q23 showed a broad-based earnings miss by number of companies vs. 3Q23 (-29%). The scale of the net miss in 4Q23, as measured by number of companies, is the largest since we started tracking earnings surprises in 2018.

2) Sectors with earnings beats: Five sectors posted a net beat by one of the measures: namely, Consumer Discretionary, Energy, Financials, Industrials, and IT.

• Consumer Discretionary posted mixed performance. It beat consensus earnings if measured by weighted surprise, led by Consumer Services and Consumer Discretionary Distribution & Retail. The sector posted in-line results as measured by number of companies. Better-than-expected earnings from e-commerce companies drove the divergence given their heavier weight in the index. Key contributors are Meituan and PDD.

• Energy beat consensus earnings as measured by weighted surprise, with \~70% of index weight reported so far. The results are in line if measured by number of companies. China Oilfield Services and China Coal Energy are the main contributors.

• Financials posted mixed performance. The results beat consensus earnings by weighted surprise but were a net miss if measured by number of companies. The biggest contributors are China Life Insurance and China Minsheng Banking.

• Industrials posted mixed results: a net beat if measured by weighted surprise but missing consensus earnings by number of companies. CATL is the main contributor to the beat.

• IT posted mixed results: a net beat if measured by weighted surprise but a net miss by number of companies. The biggest contributor to the beat is Xiaomi (despite Huawei's strong shipment growth, Xiaomi sustained domestic market share in 4Q).

3) Sectors with earnings misses: Four sectors missed consensus earnings by number of companies and weighted surprise: namely, Consumer Staples, Healthcare, Materials, and Utilities. Only Communication Services missed consensus by just one of the measures.

• Based on 99% of Communications Services sector index weight reported, the sector missed consensus earnings estimates by number of companies. The results are in line if measured by weighted surprise. The biggest constituent, in the sector,

NetEase, posted a miss. Mango Excellent Media, Baidu and Tencent posted in-line results.

• Consumer Staples missed consensus earnings both by number of companies and weighted surprise. Key drags are Wens Foodstuff Group and Muyuan Foods Co Ltd.

• Healthcare missed consensus earnings both by weighted surprise and number of companies. Key drags are Shanghai Pharmaceutical and Fosun Pharmaceutical (2023 revenue was in line, while recurring net profit missed as a result of higher opex for sales team expansion, R&D investment, and COVID-related impairment).

• Materials posted a double miss. Key drags are Tianqi Lithium – likely owing to larger losses from the lithium producing business, due to the pricing mechanism in spodumene concentrate (SC) procurement applicable in 2023 and thus elevated offtake prices – and Ganfeng Lithium. Ganfeng's miss came mainly from weakerthan-expected margins in lithium conversion and battery manufacturing.

• Utilities posted a double miss. The key drag is Guangdong Investment. GDI's net profit fell 34.5% yoy in 2023 owing to pressure from the property business. It lowered 2023 DPS to make future DPS more sustainable.

Note: Real Estate is the only sector for which less than 30% of sector-level index weight has reported. Thus, we are not counting it.

# A-share Earnings Surprises – 4Q23 Final Cut: A Broad Net Miss Across All Sectors

Note: We use constituents of MSCI China A Onshore Index as a proxy for the A-share stock universe. Our analysis of A-share earnings surprises is based on constituent companies that have reasonable quality data for consensus estimates, comprising 504 companies in MSCI China A Onshore, representing 66% of the index by number of companies and 81% by index weight.

## Key observations

1) At the aggregate index level, reported revenue fell notably short of consensus by both number of companies (-38%) and weighted surprise (-16%): See Exhibit 10 . This is also a more disappointing outcome vs. 3Q23, as the magnitude of the net miss by number of companies and weighted surprise worsened \~5ppt and \~8ppt, respectively. The miss also widened vs. the 4Q23 interim review, where both measures stood at -35% and -11%.

2) Sectors with earnings beats: No sectors posted net beats by both measures. Only Consumer Staples posted in-line earnings if measured by weighted surprise.

3) Sectors with earnings misses: All 11 sectors posted net misses by both number of companies. All sectors except for Consumer Staples posted net misses by weighted surprise.

• Communication Services posted a large net miss by both number of companies and weighted surprise. Key drags are Wanda Film holding Co and Oriental Pearl Media.

• Consumer Discretionary missed consensus earnings estimates by weighted surprise and number of companies. Key drags are SAIC Motor Corp and Guangzhou Auto Group.

• Consumer Staples showed a net miss by number of companies. Main drag to the miss are Beijing Dabeinong Tech and Wens Foodstuff Group.

• Financials posted missed consensus earnings by both measures. Most insurers finished 2023 with lower earnings under the new accounting rules, down 12-59% across peers. A weak equity market and lower rate environment posed challenges for Chinese insurers; top misses were Industrial Bank and Ping An Insurance.

• Healthcare missed by both number of companies and weighted surprise. Key drags are Dian Diagnostics Group (profit shortfall mainly due to asset write-offs) and Beijing Wantai Enterprise Co.

• Industrials missed by number of companies and weighted surprise. Top misses were Juneyao Airlines and Shanghai Electric Corp.

• IT missed by both number of companies and weighted surprise. Longi Green Energy and Wingtech Technology led the top misses.

• Materials missed consensus by both measures. Jiangsu Eastern Shenghong and Ganfeng Lithium Group were the top drags.

• Real Estate largely missed consensus earnings by both number of companies and weighted surprise. Major contributors to the miss are China Vanke (worsened profitability and liquidity), and Shenzhen Overseas Chinese Town Co.

• Utilities posted a double miss. Key drags are Huadian Power International and Inner Mongolia Mengdian.
