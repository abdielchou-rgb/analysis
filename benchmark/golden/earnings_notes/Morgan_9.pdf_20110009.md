# Navigating NVIDIA 2Q24 – Tactical Bull/Bear Cases

Technology has become a one-stock story and NVDA remains the mainstay whether stocks advance or move lower. Demand accelerates but there is a product delay risk; the question is can the raised expectations match guidance? We see bull/bear tactical opportunities for the supply chain.

At what point do AI earnings level off? It is difficult to know where they might top, however, on the back of hyperscalers talking up longer-term spending expectations for AI, those longer-term views remain positive. But there is a debate about a Blackwell delay - most people know that this delay is possible but there is always someone that doesn't. We can't rule out such a delay, especially for the downstream system assembly supply chain but for now, our checks continue to point to strong steady growth, even as we transition to Blackwell.

The next move in tech hangs on NVDA – what are the bull and bear scenarios? A substantial run-up into this earnings season for AI stocks and a tactically robust quarter could lead stocks to consolidate gains, in our view. With an average price rally of 27% YTD for this AI group, NVIDIA's earnings release could be a volatile event but with a positive skew to our base case. Our bull case indicates 3-15% price upside if NVIDIA's revenue is above consensus – Innolight and FII are our key tactical picks ( Exhibit 2 ). Our bear case implies 5-10% downside for the group with order of preference reversed.

Channel checks support a robust quarter but the key is durability: The market for AI chips remains robust if we triangulate supply chain checks with strong purchasing and higher cloud capex. For earnings, the recovery is playing out for AI stocks in Asia – and if validated by NVIDIA's outlook, these stocks remain cheap and will continue to outperform, in our view. AI stocks dominate our global key picks, including NVIDIA going into earnings with durable strong sequential growth in the coming quarters.

## Where are we in this mega cycle relative to stock performance?

Is a US\$32bn October revenue guide going to be enough? The bar has been lifted for NVDA and its supply chain once again. We don't think the 3Q revenue guide will matter much this time around, whether it's the Street's US\$31.5bn expectations or the US\$33bn+ that some buy-side analysts are expecting that might move the stock. What may matter more is if the company can calm investor concerns about the possibility of the Blackwell delay (due to a chip-design respin). While we view the concerns as valid, we see no impact for upstream (foundry, CoWoS, HBM) companies' full year outlooks. NVDA remains the most sought-after silicon, with every major hyperscaler having increased their capex (although peaking on a YoY basis in 3Q24) to accommodate the lift in prices. The money that NVDA makes is the money the hyperscalers are spending, and all of this capital spend is going straight to NVDA's supply chain and that’s where the earnings are concentrated. Investors remain concerned about the sustainability risk and ROI that this poses as the numbers have risen so high so quickly but backed by sufficient capex intensity maintained at healthy levels for US hyperscalers.

How to position for the future? The importance of NVIDIA's earnings affects the entire AI universe, whether it is TSMC, SK hynix or DISCO as AI has become fully recognized by the market now. In our view, even if NVIDIA were to give a disappointing report for 3Q24, the stock would likely be given the benefit of the doubt by investors and buyers could resurface – so we wouldn't be moving away from AI stocks just yet. If we look at NVDA, consensus expects revenue to grow by 98% this year, while our analysts see yet another beat and raise as likely this quarter. But investors are paying 38x 12M forward P/E for the stock, which is slightly above/in-line with the long-term 10Y average based on consensus numbers. The same is true for AI pick-and-shovel names, which are trading on average at 30x 12M forward P/E. Thus it is actually difficult to say that we are in overvaluation territory for some of these AI names. We have to marry earnings with valuation, and for most names in our AI supply chain there is still earnings to support the multiples.

What is the market's concern? No one wants to spoil a good story but it will get tougher to get better comparisons from here as we move into late cycle conditions (Global Technology: Cycle Playbook – Preparing for a Peak (August 20, 2024)). On the one hand, there is this strong secular growth in AI trend that is real and multiyear, and on the other hand, you have expectations that have been raised over the last two years of what AI can do. And so now the market is trying to figure out how sustainable are these levels of spending that the hyperscalers are doing. The market is likely to be incredibly reactive from here and impatient. Level versus trend is a critical distinction to make in tech now, and indeed markets will eventually trade the second derivative.

Universe of opportunity expands when looking at what AI could bring. The importance of NVIDIA's earnings is in the entire AI halo. If NVIDIA's results were to disappoint, we still believe they could get the benefit of the doubt and buyers would likely resurface – so we don't recommend moving away from AI stocks. We continue to believe that leading AI companies will accrue the best returns – they see numbers going up and multiples going down as AI benefits flow through to earnings. We know that short-term earnings do not drive stocks, multiples do … but we are past that stage, and earnings matter increasingly from this point.

## It is going to be difficult to time that dip

Our long-term fundamental thesis on AI stocks is unchanged, even though the stocks have re-rated. We believe we are still far from bubble metrics and are wary that nothing goes straight up, so a little giveback in the price discovery phase is normal. Consensus forward earnings estimates continue to move higher for AI stocks, showing more resilience than the US economy. Even if we have an economic slowdown, corporations still need to invest in AI technology, or they will fear being left behind and becoming obsolete, in our view.

As such, one can be caught offside very quickly. This report is not about the tech cycle timing (or market timing), but time in the market. Not being invested in the best few days of the AI rally could cause investors to miss all of the big gains over an entire cycle, going all the way back to the last two decades, in our view. Using a weak period in the market to build positions and using time in the market to take advantage of the AI theme is our view.

If NVIDIA during its earnings call can meet consensus and reaffirm limited delay for Blackwell chip sales in 2H24, the Asian supply chain stocks may rebound, in our view.
