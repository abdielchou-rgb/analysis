## China equity strategy 2H2024 outlook

Our framework for China equities includes five domestic cycles (business cycle, earnings cycle, property cycle, credit/NPL cycle, and domestic rebalancing cycle) and three global cycles (geopolitical cycle, inflation cycle and Fed rate cycle).

For 2H24, we anticipate continued improvement regarding four out of the five domestic cycles with the NPL cycle being the exception. We also see higher uncertainty regarding the three global cycles during a record election year around the globe including the all-important US presidential election.

Business & earnings cycles: Based on JPM’s proprietary quant indicator, China’s economy is moving from being in modest Recovery in 2023 to modest Expansion in 2024. We maintain our call since 1H23 that the next peak in Expansion may arrive by 1H25, albeit a modest one given that property has gone ex-growth. Growth should take a breather in EM ex-China after a strong 1H24 (see Luis Oganes et al’s Emerging Markets Outlook and Strategy for 2H24: A more tactical approach to EM warranted amid a mixed macro picture and US election risks, 6 June 2024). For more discussions on EPS expectations, please refer to page 3 and page 8.

Domestic rebalancing cycle: The State Council’s Capital Market Guideline 3.0 shows a domestic rebalancing act tilting in favor of minority investors to help invite them back to A-shares (see Wendy Liu et al’s China Equity Strategy: China's stock market 3.0: Context & Investment ideas, 14 Apr 2024). Amidst PBoC's verbal pushback on low CGB yields, onshore investors we spoke with see limited room for CGB yields to dip lower. As such, this may trigger more onshore asset allocation in favor of equities. Longer term, retail participation in the onshore equity market should rise due to 1) household asset allocation diversifying away from physical property, and 2) the formation of national private pension plans for retirement investments. While investor expectations are low, the 3<sup>rd</sup> Plenum of the 20<sup>th</sup> Congress in July may introduce additional reforms for domestic rebalancing.

Property & credit/NPL cycles: Our analysis in 2022 and 2023 determined that China’s property risks are not as high as those of Japan and the US in terms of home valuation to GDP. The easing at the national level since April 30, 2024, has been unprecedented, and the wording turned even stronger in the State Council’s June 7 statement, inviting outside-the-box thinking to reduce housing inventory and to stabilize the housing market. JPM’s China Property analyst Karl Chan noted highfrequency property sales data and potentially upcoming policy easing are things to track. We reiterate our call that secondary home transactions are likely to rise, and prices are likely to stabilize by end 2024/early 2025, with further upside in 2H 2025 on favorable upgrader demographics from now to 2026.

The geopolitical cycle: JPM’s Global Equity Strategy team noted that while gridlock would be the most neutral outcome in the US presidential election, a Republican sweep in the White House, Senate and Congress, would manifest changes (e.g. raising tariffs, and more scrutiny on anti-trust). More importantly, the uncertainty itself could cause concerns for investors looking to manage risk exposure heading into the Nov 5<sup>th</sup> US presidential election (see Dubravko Lakos-Bujas et al’s Global Thematic Strategy: Key Upcoming Elections, Equity Implications, Policy-Linked Stock Ideas, 7 May 2024).

Inflation and central bank rate cycles: With the global expansion moving onto firmer ground in 1H24 as growth broadens across regions and sectors, the JPM Global Economics team has been skeptical of an immaculate disinflation and significant central bank easing (see Bruce Kasman et al’s Global Data Watch, 7 June 2024). JPM expects the global and US economies to end the year close to where they began – with core CPI rising at a roughly 3%ar. The FOMC left rates on hold on 12 June and the signal from the median dot for this year was a little more hawkish than expected, showing only one cut in 2024, vs the three signaled in March and the closer to two by the market. The median dot is projecting another four cuts next year (up from three in March) and four in 2026 (up from three in March) (see Michael Feroli et al’s FOMC guesses at fewer cuts in ’24, 12 June 2024). The JPM EM Equity Strategy team’s study shows that China outperforms when DXY has a weakening bias toward the CNY (see Pedro Martins Junior et al's Key Trades and Risks: EM Equities - EM Sprint Turned into a Marathon amid Global Ambiguities, 2 May 2024).

## 2024E: 5.2% GDP growth y-y vs Bloomberg consensus of 4.9%

2Q24 likely the weakest sequential quarter in 2024: JPM’s China economics team notes that China’s economic activity started strong in 1Q2024 but softened in 2Q2024, with volatility driven by LNY seasonality and changes in policy dynamics. JPM believes that policy adjustment since the April Politburo meeting can help cushion a similar weakening in activities seen in 2Q2023. JPM expects growth to fall below trend in 2Q2024 but stabilize around trend in 2H2024 (2Q/3Q/4Q real GDP at +3.5%/+4.5%/+4.5% q-q, or +5.9%/5.3%/5.0% y-y).

Fiscal policies are helping to cushion the physical property market: New government bond issuance rose to around Rmb1.2trn in May (vs a contraction of nearly Rmb100bn in April). In addition, a new round of housing policy relaxations was announced on May 17, which included additional demand-side easing (relaxation in mortgage downpayment and mortgage rates) and a new initiative to reduce housing inventory (Rmb300bn re-lending by the PBOC to support government purchase of unsold homes).

Monetary policy: number of rate cuts predicated on Fed’s action: JPM forecasts one 10bp rate cut, and a 25bp RRR cut in 3Q, together with 8.5% TSF growth, and 9% bank loan growth.

Growth uncertainties are likely to come from exports and consumption: For exports, this would be related to softening in external demand and trade tensions with the US and EU. For consumption, this would be related to weak income expectations, especially among upper-middle income households, and the state of the physical property market.

Pace of local government bond issuance and impact on infrastructure FAI China’s special local government bond’s budget stands at cRmb3.9trn for 2024, but only cRmb1.2trn (c32%) had been issued over 5M2024 (Figure 5), the second slowest pace since 2020. There remains Rmb2.7trn in the annual quota to be issued in the rest of 2024. Local governments have been keen to lower LGFV risks, such that they net paid down LGFVs over 5M2024 (Figure 6). Over 5M2024, infrastructure FAI grew by 5.7%, excluding power, heat, gas and water, which are mainly backed by central SOEs and grew at a much higher pace of 23.7%. Recently, STCN reported that the NDRC and the MoF completed the screening of special local government bond projects in 2024, with c38k projects qualified for raising Rmb5.9trn. A pickup in issuance in 2H2024 could lend support to infrastructure FAI.

## Business & consumer confidence and deflationary pressures

NBS’s Consumer Confidence Index eased to 88.2 in Apr 2024 from 89.4 in Mar 2024, with the main drags from income and employment, which declined by 1.9 and 1.6, respectively, while the willingness to spend edged up by 0.1. The growth gap between M1&M0 and M1&M2 are at cycle troughs, reflecting weak business confidence (Figure 8 & Figure 9). China’s April Politburo meeting statements confirmed that policy makers recognized weak consumer demand and weak business confidence as key challenges faced by the economy. The April meeting called for fiscal spending to stimulate domestic demand (Rmb1trn ultra-long CGB issuance starting in May, faster issuance of special local government bonds, and execution of the trade-in programs). JPM’s Haibin Zhu expects deflation will end but low inflation will stay in 2024. JPM’s 2024 forecasts for CPI and PPI inflation stand at 0.5% and -0.5%. Reflecting this forecasted trend, the GDP deflator will likely stay in negative territory through 2Q and 3Q2024.

## The rise of local institutional investors in Asia

Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) in India: The Indian SIP recorded 54% AuM increase from INR6.8trn at end Mar 2023 to INR10.7trn at end Mar 2024. Nearly one-fifth (19 percent) of the industry’s total AuM is backed by inflows through the SIP route, according to news reports, which is considered to be a convenient investment option, particularly for retail investors. The AuM of open-ended equity funds grew 55% from INR15.2trn in Mar 2023 to INR23.5trn in March 2024, as

reported by The Economic Times (accounting for c6% of India equities’ total market cap).

BOJ’s purchase of equity ETFs over a decade: According to EPFR, starting in December 2010, and proceeding at a higher pace from 2013, the BOJ expanded its holdings to the point it became the dominant player in Japan’s ETF market. Since the start of the program in 2010, the percentage of the market held by the BOJ has grown exponentially to nearly 70% of the entire market going into the final month of 2020. As of end April 2024, we estimate that the BOJ owned c7% of Japan equities’ total market capitalization.

Superannuation in Australia: Superannuation is the self-funded pillar for retirement savings in Australia. Employers make compulsory payments with mandatory minimum "guarantee" contribution of 11% of their ordinary time earnings (rising to 12% from 2025). Contributions to superannuation accounts are subject to a concessional income tax rate of 15%, lower than the overall personal income tax rate for most workers (up to 45%). Workers can contribute additional superannuation beyond the 11% minimum, subject to the maximum amount per year of A\$27,500. At end 2023, Australians have A\$3.7trn in superannuation, with roughly half in equities, or c20% in domestic equities, for those products where asset allocation statistics can be traced. Assuming US\$0.74trn in domestic equities, this would be equivalent to 28% of total Australian equity market cap.

## 1. Property, property, property

Our analysis in 2022 and 2023 determined that China’s property risks are not as high as those of Japan and the US in terms of home valuation to GDP, but it does have a large outstanding inventory to digest (Wendy Liu et al’s China Equity Strategy Report: Ramifications from a cooling property asset, 26 Aug 2022; China Equity Strategy: Client feedback on our managed property deflation report, 13 Oct 2022; China Equity Strategy Report: Lessons from Japan: Outperforming stocks post-Heisei boom and their likely parallels in China, 18 Jul 2023; China Equity Strategy: Lessons from Japan: Is MXCN the Topix in 96 or 98? 22 Aug 2023).

The easing on the central level since April 30 has been unprecedented, and policy wordings turned stronger in the June 7<sup>th</sup> State Council meeting statements, which urged further optimizing of real estate policies. The statement proposed that responsible authorities "should fully understand the new changes in the supply and demand relationship in the real estate market, meet the people's new expectations for high-quality housing, strive to promote the implementation of the policies and measures that have been issued, and continue to study and reserve new policies and measures to reduce inventory and stabilize the market."

JPM China Property analyst Karl Chan noted that in addition to the high-frequency property sales data, more substantial details on policy support for the property sector post the 3<sup>rd</sup> Plenum and the scale of government's housing inventory acquisition are the two potential catalysts to watch out for.

We previously estimated that there was a total of 3.4-3.9bn sqm inventory held by property developers, including c400mn sqm of unsold but completed commodity residential housing inventory and 50-60% (c3-3.5bn sqm) of the 5.9bn sqm residential GFA under construction (as of end 2023) unsold.

Breaking down the estimates to the provincial level this time, we estimate that the value of GFA under construction totaled cRmb62.7trn, at an ASP of Rmb10,625 (Figure 36). 50-60% of the Rmb62.7trn unsold plus cRmb4.2trn GFA completed but unsold would result in Rmb35.6-41.9trn inventory (with the mean at Rmb38.75trn). Assuming the “national team” buys 20% of the inventory at 50% of the ASP means financing amounting to Rmb3.6-4.2trn (scenario analysis in Figure 34).

## 2. All eyes on the ${ \mathfrak { z } } ^ { \mathfrak { r } \mathfrak { d } }$ plenum and possible reforms

The ${ \boldsymbol { 3 } } ^ { \mathbf { r d } }$ Plenum of the 20th Party Congress will be held in July and is expected to focus on deepening reforms. JPM’s China Economics team noted that at the high level, it is worth watching whether the CCP prioritizes economic tasks as its core tasks (Haibin Zhu et al’s EMOS – China: Back to trend growth in 2H, 6 Jun 2024). Specifically, important reform areas that could affect China’s growth outlook over the long term include:

(1) Fiscal reform, including new sources of fiscal revenues, stricter disciplines on fiscal expenditure and a new structure between central and local governments, which is important to resolve local government debt problems;

(2) Land reform, especially rural land reform to allow rural homes to be tradeable (the value increase can be used to finance public housing and social safety network in the new urbanization process); and

(3) New productive forces that include equal support for manufacturing and service upgrades as well as between SOEs and non-SOEs.

One area to track regarding the plenum is if there will be stronger message regarding the protection and promotion of the private sector: On 21 Mar 2024, the NDRC noted at a press conference that the government was making more efforts to promote private-sector investments, including:

(1) Drafting of the "Law on Promoting Private Economy" to provide legal protection for the private economy and to create a stable and predictable institutional environment for private investment.

(2) Expanding the investment scope for private-sector participation and encouraging more partnership between local governments and private-sector capital, especially in infrastructure projects.

(3) Providing supports to POEs on, for example, loans and land usage, and building the database on national key private-sector investment projects.

## Finally, a tail event but with precedent in Hong Kong in the 1970s

An amnesty in Hong Kong in November 1977 pardoned officials involved in petty corruptions prior to 1 January 1977, and helped to improve law and order in Hong Kong and restored economic vitality by removing an overhang. As Grenville Cross, a criminal justice analyst and ex-director of public prosecutions in Hong Kong, described, after the ICAC’s establishment in February 1974, “it sought to investigate all types of corruption. This triggered concerns, particularly because, in some areas, such as the enforcement of the anti-vice laws, corruption was an established way of life... as a means of supplementing quite meagre incomes. Within the police force, corruption was rife, and there was huge opposition to the ICAC’s investigations of its officers, past and present.”

## 3. Sector consolidations toward unified national market

The central government of China has been promoting the idea of “a unified national market” since early 2022, and it has reiterated a much-needed acceleration in the execution of this agenda. We think a "unified national market" would mean less duplicative capex, faster exits of less competitive businesses, and better coordination of capacity utilization and final output.

CSI300’s 1Q24 100bp NPM contraction likely partly reflected such deflationary pricing pressure due to overcapacity in the industrial space. JPM forecasts PPI to rise from -1.4% in May to -0.5% by year-end. NBS data for end March 2024 showed that among the 17 industries, six were at/above historical averages (chemical fibers, oil & gas, chemicals, textile, mining, and coal). Eleven were below historical averages: autos, nonmetallic minerals, electrical machinery, electronics, food production,

utilities, non-ferrous metal, special equipment healthcare, general equipment, ferrous metals.

## Risk considerations

## US presidential election risks

JPM’s Global Equity Macro team notes that while gridlock is the most neutral outcome, and a Republican president plus Republican dominated senate and house may manifest changes (e.g. raising tariffs, and more scrutiny on anti-trust), the uncertainty itself could cause concerns for investors looking to manage exposure to key US policy themes (Dubravko Lakos-Bujas et al’s Global Thematic Strategy: Key Upcoming Elections, Equity Implications, Policy-Linked Stock Ideas, 7 May 2024).

Furthermore, JPM’s EM Macro Research team notes that the risks around the November US election are likely skewed negatively. Given a lack of risk-premia in EM assets ahead of this, there is an element of complacency given how recent election results have produced non-consensus outcomes (Luis Oganes et al’s Emerging Markets Outlook and Strategy for 2H24: A more tactical approach to EM warranted amid a mixed macro picture and US election risks, 6 June 2024).

 Tariff war 2.0: regarding the 10% tariff on all US imports and 60% tariff on imports from China articulated by Trump (alongside a difficult review of USMCA), JPM estimates that a tariff war 2.0 (from 25% to 60% tariff)

could be 40% bigger in impact than tariff war 1.0, based on the extra tariff hikes. But actual outcomes are likely to be more complicated. The co-efficient of economic impact could be much smaller given that China’s share in US imports has declined by one third (21% in 2018 vs. below 14% in 2023), and share of China exports to the US in China’s total exports has declined by nearly one fourth (19%+ in 2018 to <15% in 2023). If tariff war 2.0 involves a spillover of tariff increases by other trading partners or US tariff increases on goods produced in a third country by Chinese companies, the impact could be more disruptive. In addition, policy reactions also matter.

## Central bank rate cycles: high for longer

With the global expansion moving onto firmer ground in 1H24, JPM Global Economics team has been skeptical of an immaculate disinflation and significant central bank easing (Bruce Kasma et al’s Global Data Watch, 7 June 2024). JPM expects the global and US economies to end the year close to where they began – with core CPI rising at a roughly 3%ar.

Among the global major central banks, the Riksbank of Sweden cut rate by 25bps on 8 May, and the ECB and Bank of Canada also eased by 25bps in the first week of June and guided that the path ahead will likely see rates continue to move lower. JPM believes that for Canada and Sweden, further easing looks likely as inflation has moved close to central bank targets. For the ECB, Fed, and BoE inflation data have shown varying degrees of improvements and with JPM expecting solid growth to be sustained through the rest of 2024, there is likely a patient shallow easing ahead. JPM US Economics team now forecasts one 25bps cut in 2024, likely in November, vs CME FedWatch consensus of just under two cuts of 25 bps each this year. The FOMC left rates on hold on 12 June and the signal from the median dot for this year shows only one cut in 2024, vs three signaled in March (Michael Feroli et al’s FOMC guesses at fewer cuts in ’24, 12 June 2024).

JPM’s EM Equity Strategy team noted that momentum of USD strength / weakness matters more than the level for average monthly return of EM equities. An historical analysis suggests that average EM equities returns are the weakest when the USD is below average and gaining value – the mix we saw in 1Q24 (Pedro Martins Junior et al's Key Trades and Risks: EM Equities - EM Sprint Turned into a Marathon amid Global Ambiguities, 2 May 2024). Figure 48 elaborates on the four combinations for selected EM markets relative performance vs USD level and momentum. We observed that EM Equities performs 1) +4.0% when the USD is below average and weakening; 2) +3.5% when the USD is above average and weakening; 3) -2.9% when the USD is above average and strengthening; 4) -3.0% when the USD is below average and strengthening. If the USD is above average and weakening, this has historically favored China equities.

## A rebalance between value/high yielders and growth

With the value/yield-biased sectors outperforming the MSCI-China benchmark substantially YTD (e.g. energy: c24% outperformance, utilities: c14%, telecom: c11%, and financials: c8%, by 19 Jun 2024), and with China macro recovery momentum looking set to pick up in 2H 2024, we see relative value emerging from growth stocks.

As such, we trim the sector weight on energy from OW to Neutral, and from Neutral to UW in insurance given the YTM of long-dated CGBs trending lower. We are Neutral in Utilities that could benefit from tariff hikes, UW in banks that may suffer from more NPL pressure despite NIM stabilization. The OW on diversified financials that benefit from buoyant global markets and recovering A-shares offsets our UW on banks and insurance. We raise Telecom from UW to OW on attractive dividend yield, expanding FCFs and potential upside from the reported dividend tax waiver for Southbound investors. Specifically on stocks, we prefer quality SOE names/sector leaders with strong fundamentals in the value/high-yield space, e.g. China Mobile/China Telecom in telecom, CNOOC/PetroChina in energy, CCB/ICBC/CMB/Ping An Insurance/China Life/Futu/CITIC Securities in financials, and CR Gas and Longyuan Power in utilities.

On the other hand, in anticipation of the upcoming strong earnings momentum, we upgrade IT to OW, and maintain the OW for Industrials, as both sectors show stronger incremental growth from 2023 to 2024E. Notably on stocks, we include Kingdee, Weichai Power, CRRC Times Electric, Hengli Hydraulic, and Wanhua Chemical as beneficiaries from cyclical recovery.

## Balancing exposures to domestic demand vs. exports

There has been rising crowdedness in investor positioning for strong exporters, as they have benefited from stronger-than-expected DM demand vs. weaker-thanexpected domestic recovery since re-opening. For 2H24, while JPM expects the DM strength to hold, geopolitical uncertainties may rise as the US election nears. Meanwhile, our quantitative analysis suggests the next peak in Expansion, albeit likely a moderate one, may arrive by 1H2025 (see page 8). With the latest macro prints indicating slightly better-than-expected domestic consumption after a long string of disappointments, we maintain OW on Consumer Durable & Apparels under Consumer Discretionary, and Neutral for Consumer Staples, anticipating the sustaining albeit moderate recovery among sector leaders.

For stocks, we balance geographic exposure by marginally favoring domestic leaders such as Anta, Xtep, Moutai, CR Beer and Mengniu, over big exporters such as Haier, Midea, CATL, and BYD. We note Haier and Midea’s products are less contentious, and both have offshore production plants. For the latter, offshore productions are being ramped up, and as of now may absorb higher tariffs internally given respective operating leverage.
