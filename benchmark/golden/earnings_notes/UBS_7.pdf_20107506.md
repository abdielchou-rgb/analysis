# Q2 A-share outlook: New momentum gathering

## A new round of market rally is building

2024 kicked off with the A-share market sharply undershooting expectations. To investors' relief, liquidity risk in the market was relieved as a number of government agencies vowed to safeguard the capital market. But the market's upward momentum recently slowed as investors reassess the growth recovery in the Q1 earnings season. In our view, A shares will likely gain new momentum from earnings upward revisions and policy tailwinds on high-quality capital market development in Q2.

## Q1 earnings to surprise the market to the upside

We expect EPS growth for the A-share CSI 300 to rise from 3% in 2023E to 8% in 2024E on a revenue growth rebound brought about by a nominal GDP growth recovery and improved margins for downstream sectors. Indeed, the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) data showed industrial profit rose 16.2%/10.2% YoY in Q423/2M24, indicating an earnings recovery may already be kicking off. We think Q1 earnings results, which will be disclosed by end-April, may surprise to the upside given the low market expectations.

## Economic concerns overpriced; policy tailwinds underpriced

The equity risk premium of the A-share market is close to a historical high, seemingly pricing in a further slowdown scenario. However, we see the overall economic reality as a mix of property weakness (-), lack of confidence (-), industrial production strength (+), consumption recovery (+), and 'investor-oriented' capital market policy (+). Thus we think the market is overpricing growth headwinds and underpricing policy tailwinds.

## Tactical style, sector allocation and investment themes

On style allocation, we think 'growth' may slightly outperform in Q2 along with the return of 'risk-on' sentiment, but high dividend yields should remain part of defensive positioning within 'value'. We expect large caps to outperform small to mid caps due to higher earnings visibility and potential inflows from long-term sources of capital. In terms of sector preferences, we are overweight electronics, food & beverage, oil & coal, and telecom. On investment themes, we like quality SOEs under the "valuation system with Chinese characteristics".

This year kicked off with the A-share market sharply undershooting our and investor expectations. The Wind all A-share Index retreated a rapid >17% at the lowest, smallcaps experienced larger declines, and the CSI 2000 fell >33%, seemingly pointing to liquidity risks.

The good news is that a number of government agencies have made comments since mid-January to show their resolve to safeguard the capital market. On top of a number of positive policy signals sent by the CSRC, we note: 1) RRR and interest rate cuts by the People's Bank of China (PBoC); 2) Central Huijin's announcement stating its full recognition of the allocation value of the A-share market currently (see report); 3) Vice Premier He Lifeng's proposal to increase support for high-quality listed companies; 4) the State-Owned Asset Supervision and Administration Commission's (SASAC) step in to incorporate market cap management into its assessment of heads of central stateowned enterprises (SOEs); and 5) a Government Work Report highlighting the need to strengthen the intrinsic stability of the capital market. ## Earnings may surprise the market to the upside

Now that major A-share indexes have retraced their January decline, onshore investors we spoke to have turned their focus back to the inflation and property market outlook, while also closely monitoring how the company earnings recovery will play out. In the near term, we think the A-share market's upward slope could turn somewhat less steep heading toward late April, when the Q1 earnings season starts. The market may not have a new wave of momentum until more clear signs of an earnings recovery emerge.

That said, we continue to believe that a rebound in nominal GDP growth could lift company revenues, sending EPS growth for the CSI 300 Index up to 8% in 2024E, from 3% in 2023E. Indeed, industrial profit rose 10.2% YoY in Jan-Feb 2024, indicating an earnings recovery may already be kicking off. We think Q1 earnings results may surprise to the upside, as the buy side is generally expecting low single-digit earnings growth for 2024.

## Economic concerns appear to be overpriced

The A-share market's equity risk premium (ERP) is now at 1 standard deviation (SD) above its historical average, indicating subdued risk appetite. The prolonged weakness in the property market and its drag on the economy has taken a toll on investor confidence. Admittedly, activities in the property market have yet to stabilise. UBS China Activity index, a proprietary high-frequency data tracker, shows that property sales weakened further to -47% YoY in March, versus -40% YoY in Jan-Feb and -10% YoY in Dec 2023.

Apart from the property sector, however, we find the general economy is faring relatively well. In fact, recent data point to a wide divergence between the health of the property sector and the rest of the industrial realm. March manufacturing PMI jumped to 50.8%, up 1.7ppts MoM. Industrial production (+7.0% YoY in 2M24) and industrial power consumption (+9.7% YoY in 2M24) keep recovering. The price of copper, an indicator of industrial demand, remains elevated.

China's export growth accelerated beyond expectations to 7.1% YoY in Jan- Feb, the strongest reading since March 2023. While shipments to the EU and Japan remained in a year-over-year contraction, exports to the US again registered expansion, recovering to 5.0% YoY in Jan-Feb from -6.9% YoY in December 2023. PMI numbers from overseas are promising as well. The March ISM non-manufacturing composite stayed well above 50 at 51.4.

Consumption is another bright spot. Although the growth of some retail sales items (eg, going-out related products and catering sales) has cooled off due to a fading base effect, sales of housing-related items like home appliances and furniture have improved from December 2023. In addition, auto sales growth for Jan-Feb picked up to 8.7% YoY from 4% YoY in December 2023 due to price discount promotions. Moreover, services PMI improved a further 1.4ppt to 52.4 in March, indicating that a consumption recovery is already under way.

We think the overall economic picture is mixed, with weakness in the property sector offset by strength in industrial/consumption. But despite this, market valuations remain close to trough levels. Therefore, we think the market is focusing too much on weakness in the property market, while also overlooking the positive signals from other parts of the economy.

We think continued strength in the industrial space will counter the property downturn's negative impact on FAI growth. As property sales and new starts stabilise and bottom out in Q2, we expect households to regain confidence and look for a gradual release of excess savings to support consumption.

## Policy tailwinds appear to be underpriced

As we discuss in our report "Potential tailwinds from CSRC's new policies; A-share market picking up", the CSRC has called for the A-share market to be 'investor-oriented and promote high-quality development of the capital market'. We believe the potential tailwinds from policy support is being underpriced, if not overlooked. In the four policies document issued by the CSRC on 15 March 2024, market value management is named as a key area of focus. We believe it refers to market value management initiatives including: 1) normalisation of the pace and intensity of IPOs to strike a balance between the primary and secondary markets; 2) share buybacks initiated by listed companies; and 3) higher dividend payouts to reward investors.

Although listed companies rewarded their investors through dividends and share buybacks for a total of Rmb6.8trn over 2015-2021, a whopping Rmb10.0trn was raised through IPOs, secondary offerings, rights offerings and preferred stocks, leading to an accumulated shortfall of Rmb3.3trn over this period. It is plausible that investors in the secondary market believe that financing activities have been 'draining secondary market liquidity'.

However, the situation has been improving since 2022 when financing activities as a whole were under pressure, while A-share companies have been ramping up dividend payouts each year. IPOs remained muted in the first quarter of 2024, raising Rmb221bn, down -59% YoY. Moreover, news outlets have reported on a large number of IPO candidates withdrawing their applications recently.

The total amount of dividends and share buybacks reached Rmb1.86trn last year, triple the amount in 2015. With the implementation of the details listed in the four documents, we believe listed companies will improve in terms of corporate governance and prioritising investor returns.

## What are the sources of potential inflows?

We note that ETFs have been increasingly popular since the second half of 2023. Equity ETFs saw a net increase of 474bn units for the full year of 2023. In the first quarter of 2024, the net increase reached 114.4bn units, up 263% YoY. ETFs have been particularly active during periods of market turmoil, when the trading volume of CSI 300 ETFs has spiked multiple times to record highs. In stark contrast is the weakness in mutual funds. Mutual fund issuance has been muted year-to-date due to weak market performance and a lacklustre macro environment. However, with market sentiment and company fundamentals improving, we think A-share performance could see a further rebound and mutual fund issuance, as a lagging indicator, may recover in our base case.

For foreign participation in the A-share market, we have seen strong northbound inflows post Chinese New Year. Offshore investor inflows through the northbound scheme have reached Rmb57.9bn since 19 February, reversing the net outflow trend since August 2023. Historically, relatively large northbound outflows generally coincide with market troughs. We believe stronger fiscal policies and a stabilisation of macro and corporate earnings data may well boost global investor confidence. An A-share rally would likely attract the return of global funds. Once the offshore 'smart money' turns to net buyers, we think onshore investors' risk appetite would likely be lifted, leading to a positive feedback loop.

## Tactical style/sector allocations and themes

Our research shows growth's outperformance correlates with market beta. In other words, when the market trends up, growth generally outperforms value. Value style and high-dividend names with defensiveness have outperformed the broader market YTD due to the market slump in January.

On style allocation, we think 'growth' may slightly outperform in Q2 along with the return of 'risk-on' sentiment, but high dividend yields should remain part of the defensive positioning within 'value'. For large caps versus small caps, we expect large caps to benefit from higher earnings visibility, an improving macro environment, and potential net inflows from ETFs and global investors.

On investment themes, we continue to like quality SOEs under the "valuation system with Chinese characteristics" (VCC) theme. For many years, SOEs have been assigned substantially lower valuations by investors in both the A-share and Hong Kong equity markets. In terms of PE multiple, non-financials SOEs have traded at an increasingly deep discount to their non-SOE peers. In fact, since 2021 non-SOEs' PE multiple has been more than double that of SOEs. However, we believe such a large valuation gap cannot be explained by differences in earnings growth.

To some extent, the earnings growth of SOEs is indeed slower. When we compare the earnings results of non-financials A-share SOEs and non-SOEs, we see that non-SOEs' annual profit growth clearly outstripped that of the SOEs' in seven of the past ten years. However, if we make an aggregate comparison, the lead is not as wide, with profit growing at an average rate of 13% for non-SOEs compared with 10% for SOEs over a span of 10 years (2013-22).

In recent years, non-SOEs have found it more difficult to sustain their lead in earnings growth. Although non-SOEs greatly outperformed the SOEs in 2020, the latter grew more than four times as fast as the former in the following year during a much more challenging business environment. However, if we take valuation into consideration, the picture starts to look muddled. SOEs, whose profits have grown much faster since 2021, are trading at a steep discount to non-SOEs, though the discount narrowed in 2023 as VCC gathered popularity.

## Valuation Method and Risk Statement

For various stocks across the industries we cover in the Hong Kong and mainland China stock markets, we use a variety of valuation approaches, including DCF models, Gordon growth model analysis and relative valuation analysis using various multiples such as PE, EV/EBITDA and P/BV.

We think the risks facing China's equities include a hard landing for the property market, a capital exodus associated with currency depreciation and slow structural reform progress. In our view, any government policies that do not adequately address these risks could result in a shock to the market. For example, an excess of stimulus policies could pose a risk to the transition from an investment-driven to a consumption-driven economy and increase the debt of government and state-owned enterprises.
