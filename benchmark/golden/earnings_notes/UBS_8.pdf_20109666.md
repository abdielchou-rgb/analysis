# Mutual Fund Positioning Quarterly - 3Q24

The Mutual Fund Positioning Quarterly reviews sector and factor positioning of long only managers based on size and style box. All data as of 6/30/24.

## Large Cap

Core: Portfolio positioning in Health Care and Industrials is well above normal levels. Technology remains extremely underweight. Managers are underweight quality, high P/E, and beta, with further reductions to all three in 2Q.

 Growth: PMs have elevated positioning in Industrials, Technology, and Financials. Portfolios are tilted toward margin growth, ROE, and price momentum, with low exposure to rising interest rates.

 Value: Managers exhibit a stronger than normal preference for Communications at the expense of Financials and Industrials. PMs increased already elevated positioning in size and quality, while further reducing price momentum.

## Mid Cap

Core: Tech and Staples positioning is more elevated than normal after further increases. Industrials and Financials ownership is more depressed. Managers show greater preference for higher P/E stocks, and against size and quality.

Growth: Managers own more Technology than usual, less Health Care and Financials. PMs showing elevated preference for price momentum and EPS growth, and lower than normal positioning on size.

 Value: Portfolios have greater than usual ownership in Staple & REITs at the expense of Technology, Industrials, and Financials. PMs have an elevated preference for growth at the expense of size, beta, and price momentum.

## Small Cap

 Core: Positioning in Technology, Industrials, and Financials is lower than normal, with Health Care more elevated. Managers have a stronger preference for higher rates and low P/E stocks at the expense of size and beta.

Growth: PMs maintained their elevated preference for Discretionary and Financials at the expense of Technology which they further reduced. Portfolios are more tilted than usual toward higher interest rates and away from size and beta.

 Value: Manager positioning in Industrials and Discretionary is lower than normal, with Utilities and Staples the beneficiaries. PMs exhibiting a stronger preference for ROE and higher interest rates at the expense of beta and price momentum.
