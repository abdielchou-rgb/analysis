# MNC Sentiment Index (4Q23): Soaring Sentiment

Our proprietary MNC Sentiment Index registered a sequential rise of 4% from a high base, continuing to gap higher vs. China. A high sentiment augurs well for India's capex cycle, balance of payments, corporate profits and share prices, in our view.

alphawiseα

Multinational Corporation (MNC) Sentiment index rebounds: The index rose by 17% sequentially in 4Q23 following a 15% decline in the previous quarter. It was up 4% YoY for the quarter ended Dec-23 vs. a 17% decline in the previous quarter (from an all-time high in Sep-22). India's rising position in world order, a multipolar world, the growing consumer market in India and government incentives for investments are driving MNC interest into India (India Bluepaper). Indeed, India has changed materially over the past decade and is now arguably a more attractive destination for foreign companies. India is our top pick in EM.

Relative to China, the MNC sentiment gapped higher for the 10th quarter as the MNC sentiment on China gained marginally on a sequential basis. From a geographical perspective, sentiment was up across all regions, primarily led by Japanese companies. From a sector perspective, Energy and Utilities outperformed, whereas Energy was the key laggard.

What is the MNC Sentiment Index? Our machine-powered MNC Sentiment Index gives real-time feedback on how MNCs judge business conditions in India. The India MNC Sentiment Index tracks quarterly results transcripts from companies in major global indices and scores the sentiment of management commentary, based on a proprietary machine-learning program. Our data go back to 2010, allowing us to put recent scores into a longer-term perspective.

MNC Sentiment Index = important proprietary indicator: Strong sentiment is likely to result in higher investments and therefore more corporate profits. We find our MNC Sentiment Index has a strong association with foreign direct investments (FDI), profits and the direction of share prices. Rich MNC sentiment could play an important role in driving India's coming capex boom. We remain overweight on Industrials, Financials, Consumer Discretionary and Technology in our sector model portfolio.

## What Is the Index Telling Us Right Now?

## MNC Sentiment Index rises, get closer to all-time highs

Based on our proprietary index, MNC sentiment towards India was up QoQ in 4Q23, approaching the peak levels made in Sep-22. India’s policy work around lifting corporate profits and boosting manufacturing and foreign direct investments (FDI) in the context of a multipolar world is likely keeping sentiment high.

Our MNC Sentiment Index has a strong association with FDI inflows – and hence capex. Thus, it boosts corporate profits and has an association with share prices. We are buyers of Industrials, Financials, Consumer Discretionary and Technology stocks with a 12- to 18-month view.

## Index Rises Sequentially

• The index was up sequentially after a decline in previous quarters, up 17% YoY for the quarter ended December 2023, and coming closer to peak levels made in 2022. Economic conditions appear to be strong and improving, India's evolving consumer pyramid, the multipolar world thesis and government policy support are all likely contributors to overall sentiment ( Exhibit 4 ).

• Relative to China, where we run a similar index, India has now outperformed for 10 quarters running after underperforming for six quarters. The MNC sentiment toward China rose marginally on a sequential basis ( Exhibit 2 ).

• From a geographical perspective, the sentiment index reported a sequential rise for companies across regions, led by Japan ( Exhibit 5 ). On an absolute basis, the sentiment score is the highest for EM and US companies and lowest for European companies.

• From a sector perspective, Utilities and Energy outperformed in the latest quarter, whereas Real Estate and Technology were the main laggards ( Exhibit 6 and Exhibit 7 ). Health Care, Communication Services and Materials have the highest absolute sentiment score, while Energy continued to report the lowest absolute score. (Please note that some sectors had fewer data points.)

## Why Has the Index Remained Strong?

The sector-specific incentives in the context of a multipolar world are likely reinforcing India's attractiveness as a manufacturing hub, given its favorable demographics, lessexpensive labour force and large domestic market, leading to improved investments and sentiment from MNCs.

• Multipolar world thesis: There is a push from changing world trade relationships, as exhibited by the ongoing US-China trade tensions. This, in turn, is leading to a rethinking of trade dependence on a particular country.

• There is a focus by the Indian government on promoting domestic production through targeted policy measures. The government has cleared production-linked incentive (PLI) schemes for 14 sectors, with total incentives worth Rs2.47 trillion (US\$32.9 billion). This is targeted at augmenting indigenous manufacturing capacity to make India globally competitive. Such incentives are supplementing other measures, including lower corporate tax rates, creating solutions for land acquisition and the repeal of the retroactive tax law.

• India's evolving income pyramid implies that it offers market opportunities to companies across the world and across product categories. Since we opine that India will account for 20% of incremental global GDP in the coming decade, it is not a marketplace that can be ignored.

## Why Is the Index Important? MNC Sentiment = Earnings and Share Prices

Our proprietary Sentiment Index has a strong association with FDI ( Exhibit 7 ). Higher FDI implies more investments. The most important ingredient in profits is the rate of investment. The declining investments of the past decade explain most of the weakness in profits. So, if the investment cycle starts to turn up as a consequence of improving MNC sentiment, it could lead to higher capex and thus higher profits. Our proprietary MNC Sentiment Index could be a good tool to judge medium-term profit potential ( Exhibit 8 ). In fact, we find that the change in the MNC Sentiment Index is also coincident with the change in the equity index ( Exhibit 1 ) – underpinning the point that there is a directional association between macro and corporate fundamentals and the MNC Sentiment Index.

Given the view that growth could be accelerating, led by investments, we think domestic cyclical sectors could do well relative to externally facing sectors or defensive ones. Accordingly, we are overweight on Industrials, Consumer Discretionary and Financials in our sector model portfolio to play the likely improvement in MNC sentiment for the medium term.

• Industrials: Strong government capex and a nascent pickup in private capex drive our overweight.

• To us, Consumer Discretionary growth is likely to be strong in view of rising incomes. We expect a recovery in rural demand to aid overall consumption demand. Benign raw material costs add to mix.

• Peaking short rates, higher credit growth and low credit costs imply outperformance for Financials, especially for the non-bank lenders.

## What is the Index about?

The Morgan Stanley Global MNC Sentiment Index for India is a robust machine-driven smart indicator to gauge global MNCs' forward-looking views on India. The India MNC Sentiment Index tracks quarterly results transcripts from companies in major global indices and scores the sentiment of management commentary, based on a proprietary machine-learning program. Our data go back to 2010, allowing us to put recent scores into a longer-term perspective.

Our China equity strategy team runs a similar product. While the China MNC tracker provides an independent perspective on business conditions in China, the India tracker also serves the purpose of gauging FDI and investment trends ( Exhibit 10 ).

## How will the sentiment indicator inform portfolio decisions?

Source: Refinitiv, AlphaWise, Morgan Stanley Research

The MNC Sentiment Index tells us about the views that multinational companies hold regarding India and its economy. The score tells us how good MNCs are feeling about India, how well their businesses are doing in India, and their likely investments in India. We find the index has a good association with FDI, earnings growth and the equity index. To that extent, the most important information contained in the score is the likely direction and pace of the investment rate, which, in turn, has a direct bearing on earnings and, therefore, stock prices.

## What is our methodology?

We have used our proprietary sentiment models to analyze corporate earnings scripts for global multinational companies contained in the leading stock market indices across the world going back just over a decade.

For example, for the quarter ended December 2023, we tracked corporate statements from around 2,700 companies. We found that 124 of these companies had a comment on India. Rather than isolating key words, we used a deep learning approach to analyze complete sentences that included the words "India" or "Indian," along with the preceding and following sentences. Such comments varied from a statement on market conditions or demand to capex plans. In all, 212 unique data points were unearthed by our technique for the said quarter. Exhibit 11 and Exhibit 12 give a historical perspective on the number of companies citing "India" in their statements and the number of unique data points embedded in the index.

The model generates a single score (on a scale from -100 to +100, based on machines interpretation of one or multiple data points) for each of the companies and their comments in the sample. We designed five buckets into which a score between -100 and +100 would fall. These buckets correspond to the level of optimism and pessimism on India that global MNCs conveyed to the market. The score saw a rise in December 2023, at 42.6 vs. 36.5 in the previous quarter, possibly reflecting strong economic data plus continuing policy reforms by the government. This is how the scores can be interpreted:

• Very Positive: 61 to 100

• Slightly Positive: 21 to 60

• Neutral/Limited Signal: -20 to 20

• Slightly Negative: -21 to -60

• Very Negative: -61 to -100

## Where we could be wrong

Our sentiment model combines deep learning with human expertise to quantify domainspecific sentiment. However, the presence of linguistic nuances, particularly across regions and languages, and ambiguity in transcripts could hinder the model's accuracy.

We also use inconsistent scores within a company commentary to identify scenarios where our model might be wrong or generate mixed signals.

Our sentiment model analyzes only sections of transcripts in which India is mentioned specifically. There could be additional information related to India that is not captured in our modeling process. We see potential for future research in this area with advancements in deep learning techniques.
