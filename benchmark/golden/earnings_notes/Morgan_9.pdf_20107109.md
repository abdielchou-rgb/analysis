# Asia Pacific Equity Derivatives Highlights

HK/China 4Q23 earnings update, implied move and options trades

4Q23 Earnings Update for HSCEI/HSI Constituents: We review 4Q23 earnings trends among Hong Kong-listed companies, providing an update of earnings surprises relative to consensus estimates, the subsequent stock price reactions, and a comparison of these earnings moves against historical patterns. Notably, an impressive 86%/60% of reported companies in HSCEI/ HSI have surpassed consensus expectations. The stock market's response to earnings generally mirrors the direction of the earnings surprise, with the magnitude of these moves being proportional to the degree of earnings surprises. Furthermore, we observe that on the day of earnings announcements, single-stock moves have significantly exceeded historical averages, with sectors such as Consumer Discretionary, Information Technology and Financials demonstrating particularly pronounced volatility. Considering the ongoing debates surrounding a potential end to the three-year EPS downward revision cycle in China, coupled with the heightened sensitivity of these sectors to China<sup>’</sup>s earnings outlook, we anticipate that a higher level of dispersion will persist in these sectors.

Earnings implied move: About 59% of HSI and 80% of HSCEI names (by index weight) are expected to announce their 4Q23 results during March 19 to March 29. For companies due to report, the options market on average expects higher levels of volatility this quarter compared to the levels observed historically. We note that the implied earnings moves are relatively expensive for names in Information Technology, Materials, Heath Care, Energy and Financials, and relatively cheap for Consumer Discretionary and Communication compared to the 3Y median of realized move.

Options Trades into the Earnings: Considering fundamental catalysts and volatility pricing, we recommend the following options strategies: 1) Reiterate buying Meituan gamma into earnings, 2) Position for PetroChina<sup>’</sup>s dividend upside with a long call ratio overlay and 3) Pessimism overdone? ## Earnings implied move

## HK/China 4Q23 earnings volatility analysis

About 59% of HSI and 80% of HSCEI (Figure 7Earnigs epot chdul fHSI CmFigure 8Breakdown fvgimpl hstc  by) names (by index weight) are expected to announce their 4Q23 results over March 18 to April 5, 2024, representing 55 companies in HSI and 40 companies in HSCI. The reporting companies include some mega-cap names such as AIA, Tencent and Meituan among others.

By evaluating the volatility term structure of a particular stock, we can estimate the size of earnings day price move that is implied by the options market. The implied move can then be compared to average earnings-day moves delivered by the stock on past earnings days, in order to identify cheap or rich volatility. Options only provide information about the size of a price move; they do not imply the direction (for instance, if the implied size of a move is 5%, the stock may move up or down by 5% following the announcement).

For companies due to report, the options market on average expects higher levels of volatility this quarter compared to the levels observed historically, as per our earnings move methodology. The average options implied earnings day move is 2.2% for stocks that have not reported 4Q23 earnings, compared to the average of 3y realized move median of 2.0%, and average of last quarter of 1.8% (Figure 8). We note that the implied earnings moves are expensive for names in Information Technology, Materials, Heath Care, Energy and Financials, and cheap for Consumer Discretionary and Communication Services compared to the 3Y median of realized move (Figure 8). As discussed in the previous section, we expect names in Consumer Discretionary and Information Technology to show higher dispersion and larger realized earnings day move this quarter (see 4Q23 Earnings Update for HSCEI/HSI Constituents).

## Appendix: Methodology for Earnings-Day Volatility Analysis

In order to ascertain the expected size of an earnings-related move, one must back out the contribution to implied volatility that comes from the earnings announcement. This is determined by measuring the difference between implied volatility over the time period that includes the earnings announcement date and the time period that does not. The earnings announcement date can fall either before the maturity of the first month contract or between the maturities of the first and next-month contracts. The contribution to volatility that comes from the earnings announcement is calculated as follows:

If the earnings announcement falls before the maturity of the 1st month contract, earnings-related volatility will cause both the first and the next-month implied volatilities to become elevated by $\sigma _ { I }$ and $\sigma _ { 2 } .$ . However, the difference in volatility between the expiration of the 1st and 2nd month contracts (forward volatility) $\sigma _ { I 2 }$ is not affected by the earnings report.

Mathematically, forward volatility can be expressed as:
![](images/57c15995a4ff799cb040dbb11ee72c1dd6c8e30e33a26936966b846569467b94.jpg)

$$
\sigma _ { 1 2 } = \sqrt { \frac { \sigma _ { 2 } ^ { 2 } T _ { 2 } - \sigma _ { 1 } ^ { 2 } T _ { 1 } } { T _ { 2 } - T _ { 1 } } }
$$

Here, $T _ { I }$ and $T _ { 2 }$ equal the time to maturity of the 1st and the 2nd month contracts, respectively. First-month volatility equals the sum<sup>1</sup> of the volatilities unaffected by the earnings (forward volatility) and the contribution to volatility resulting from the earnings announcement $\sigma _ { E a r n i n g s }$ . Therefore, the contribution to the volatility resulting from the earnings announcement $( \sigma _ { E a r n i n g s } )$ equals the difference between the 1st month volatility and the forward volatility:

$$
\sigma _ { E a r n i n g g s } = \sqrt { T _ { 1 } ( \sigma _ { 1 } ^ { 2 } - \sigma _ { 1 2 } ^ { 2 } ) } = \sqrt { ( \sigma _ { 1 } ^ { 2 } - \sigma _ { 2 } ^ { 2 } ) \frac { T _ { 2 } T _ { 1 } } { T _ { 2 } - T _ { 1 } } }
$$

The above contribution to the volatility resulting from the earnings announcement $( \sigma _ { E a r n i n g s } )$ is the excess amount on top of the volatility not affected by the earnings report, the forward volatility $\sigma _ { \scriptscriptstyle { I 2 } }$ To arrive at the total volatility implied by the options market $( \sigma _ { T o t a l } )$ , we use the following relationship:

$$
\sigma _ { \overline { { { \tau o t a l } } } } = \sqrt { \sigma _ { E a r n i n g s } \mathrm { \Omega } ^ { 2 } + \sigma _ { U n o f f e c t s \dot { \mathrm { \Omega } } } \mathrm { \Omega } ^ { 2 } } = \sqrt { \sigma _ { E a r n i n g s } \mathrm { \Omega } ^ { 2 } + \sigma _ { 1 2 } \mathrm { \Omega } ^ { 2 } }
$$

Alternatively, if the earnings announcement falls after the maturity of the 1st month contract, earnings-related volatility affects the next month implied volatility $( \sigma _ { 2 } )$ only.

![](images/ec92d537c71543115c074e8babd63c1739cb54088dfba3d21e7e1d6a23437f2c.jpg)

The next-month volatility is the sum of the volatility unaffected by earnings (first-month volatility) and the contribution resulting from the earnings announcement $( \sigma _ { E a r n i n g s } )$ . In this scenario, the contribution to volatility resulting from the earnings announcement $( \sigma _ { E a r n i n g s } )$ equals the difference between the 2nd month volatility and the 1st month volatility:

## Risks of Common Option Strategies

Risks to Strategies: Not all option strategies are suitable for investors; certain strategies may expose investors to significant potential losses. We have summarized the risks of selected derivative strategies. For additional risk information, please call your sales representative for a copy of “Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options.<sup>”</sup> We advise investors to consult their tax advisors and legal counsel about the tax implications of these strategies. Please also refer to option risk disclosure documents.

Put Sale: Investors who sell put options will own the underlying asset if the asset<sup>’</sup>s price falls below the strike price of the put option. Investors, therefore, will be exposed to any decline in the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price below the strike potentially to zero, and they will not participate in any price appreciation in the underlying asset if the option expires unexercised.

Call Sale: Investors who sell uncovered call options have exposure on the upside that is theoretically unlimited.

Call Overwrite or Buywrite: Investors who sell call options against a long position in the underlying asset give up any appreciation in the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price above the strike price of the call option, and they remain exposed to the downside of the underlying asset in the return for the receipt of the option premium.

Booster : In a sell-off, the maximum realized downside potential of a double-up booster is the net premium paid. In a rally, option losses are potentially unlimited as the investor is net short a call. When overlaid onto a long position in the underlying asset, upside losses are capped (as for a covered call), but downside losses are not.

Collar: Locks in the amount that can be realized at maturity to a range defined by the put and call strike. If the collar is not costless, investors risk losing 100% of the premium paid. Since investors are selling a call option, they give up any price appreciation in the underlying asset above the strike price of the call option.

Call Purchase: Options are a decaying asset, and investors risk losing 100% of the premium paid if the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price is below the strike price of the call option.

Put Purchase: Options are a decaying asset, and investors risk losing 100% of the premium paid if the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price is above the strike price of the put option.

Straddle or Strangle: The seller of a straddle or strangle is exposed to increases in the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price above the call strike and declines in the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price below the put strike. Since exposure on the upside is theoretically unlimited, investors who also own the underlying asset would have limited losses should the underlying asset rally. Covered writers are exposed to declines in the underlying asset position as well as any additional exposure should the underlying asset decline below the strike price of the put option. Having sold a covered call option, the investor gives up all appreciation in the underlying asset above the strike price of the call option.

Put Spread: The buyer of a put spread risks losing 100% of the premium paid. The buyer of higher-ratio put spread has unlimited downside below the lower strike (down to zero), dependent on the number of lower-struck puts sold. The maximum gain is limited to the spread between the two put strikes, when the underlying is at the lower strike. Investors who own the underlying asset will have downside protection between the higher-strike put and the lower-strike put. However, should the underlying asset<sup>’</sup>s price fall below the strike price of the lower-strike put, investors regain exposure to the underlying asset, and this exposure is multiplied by the number of puts sold.

Call Spread: The buyer risks losing 100% of the premium paid. The gain is limited to the spread between the two strike prices. The seller of a call spread risks losing an amount equal to the spread between the two call strikes less the net premium received. By selling a covered call spread, the investor remains exposed to the downside of the underlying asset and gives up the spread between the two call strikes should the underlying asset rally.

Butterfly Spread: A butterfly spread consists of two spreads established simultaneously – one a bull spread and the other a bear spread. The resulting position is neutral, that is, the investor will profit if the underlying is stable. Butterfly spreads are established at a net debit. The maximum profit will occur at the middle strike price; the maximum loss is the net debit.
