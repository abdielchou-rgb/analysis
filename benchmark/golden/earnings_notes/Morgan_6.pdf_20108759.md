# European Equity Derivatives Outlook

## H2 2024 European equity derivatives outlook and trade ideas

Investors’ positioning in European derivatives remains tilted to longs, despite the increased hedging activity which followed the announcement of snap elections in France. Uncertainty is likely to remain in the coming weeks and some excess volatility risk could persist in the medium term, but we find that French election risk premia elevated and we are inclined to fade it via low-risk option trades.

Dividend derivatives: Euro STOXX 50 2027 dividend call spread collars are attractive following their sharp correction and increase in dividend option volatility. We recently took profit on Banks 2025 dividend futures, but keep previously recommended DEDZ4 futures and DEDZ5 calls.

Banks index options: the increase in SX7E implied volatility makes directional trades with a net-short volatility bias attractive. We recommend zero-cost SX7E Sep-24 put spread collars to hedge a potential downside acceleration and SX7E Sep-24 call ratios, structured to receive a net credit to play a potential relief rally.

Playing a range-bound summer: We think that the Euro STOXX 50 could remain relatively range-bound during the summer month. We recommend playing the upside part of the expected range with SX5E Sep-24 KO calls or Sep-24 Call flies or adding hedges for a moderate correction via SX5E Sep-24 KO puts.

Fading the volatility spike: The VSTOXX move seem excessive, especially relative to the VIX. We recommend buying Jul-24 VSTOXX put ratios or buy Jul-24 VSTOXX puts funded by VIX puts, to fade the European vol spike in a protected way.

CAC outperformance: French snap elections have exacerbated the CAC’s relative underperformance. To play a fading of French election risk, we recommend CAC outperformance options and Jul-24 call ratios on the names which corrected the most.

Thematic return dispersion: return dispersion (Palladium structures) have delivered very well YTD and offer leveraged upside on a further divergence between stock returns. We reiterate AI-themed and SX5E palladium structures to take advantage of current improved entry levels.

US election dispersion basket: we provide an optimized Dec-25 vol swap dispersion basket with European names that have exposure to China trade policies and may find themselves in the eye of the storm in the coming months.

# French risk not enough to materially alter net-long positioning bias

We recently commented on the fact that investors positioning on Euro STOXX 50 futures had reverted to net-long levels last seen in 2017. ## Fundamental considerations

As we suggested in the section on French election risk, uncertainty remains high, given that the outcome of the election is not clear, nor is it clear beyond relatively broad-brush policy ‘concepts’ how specific policies may affect individual sectors. The sectors that have been showing most strain are banks, public service providers such as toll road operators, renewables and utilities.

Given that many of the policies proposed by either NR or NFP are likely to be fiscally expensive, it is plausible that the new government may consider the introduction of a windfall tax in financial services and/or energy. Both have seen precedents in other European countries, with financial services being targeted particularly often across various European countries including Spain, Sweden, Lithuania and Italy. Especially, the example of Italy appears instructive in that an initial, particularly punitive tax proposal on NII was watered down materially within two days in response to a strong, adverse market reaction.

Other areas that may be affected include public service providers such as toll roads and the profitability of utilities operators. Both NR and NFP have vowed to reduce household bills. Apart from scrapping VAT on energy bills – a policy that has been touted and would be neutral to companies – details are scant and it is not possible to provide any scenario analysis at this point. Many potential policies, such as capping the end-user prices in utilities for instance would be in breach of EU pricing frameworks. If France did opt to challenge EU on the existing pricing framework, it is conceivable that France would find allies in other countries. Beyond the question of how and which individual sectors could be affected by policy initiatives, there is the uncertainty of when policies would start to be effective and if FY24 (and thus 2025 dividends) would already be affected. We should point out that we consider chances of an outright dividend ban being imposed to be small. In this department note, individual sector analysts discuss how French stocks may be affected.

# Playing a partial reversion of CAC underperformance

French sovereign risk spreads continue to widen in relative terms as the market tries to digest the implications of French snap elections. Equities also reacted strongly to the news with the CAC 40 underperforming \~2% relative to Euro STOXX 50 since the announcement and by over 8% YTD. Within the CAC, we note a concentration of risk centering around predominantly banks,toll road operators, renewables and utilities, which all underperformed disproportionately. Much of these moves appears to be driven by a general risk-off mood, rather than specific, quantifiable downside scenarios. The market is principally concerned around three main questions:

Fiscal slippage as a result of costly, unfunded but popular policies (e.g. lowering retirement age, unwinding reforms on unemployment benefits, increasing public sector wages and pensions, scrapping VAT on energy bills)

Sector-specific government interventions such as the introduction of a windfall tax for Financial Services and/or Energy or specific price caps for Utilities and or public services such as roads)

• Challenging EU rules and frameworks (e.g. Maastrich treaty rules on fiscal prudence, energy price framework)

Current projections based on polls anticipate a hung parliament with a preponderance of seats taken by RN (195-245 seats) and the left-leaning alliance NFP (190-235 seats). This outcome may well be taken positively by markets, given that it would curtail either side’s ability to put through their respective agendas effectively and avoid some of the worst case scenarios. Middle ground between the two parties exists in aiming to reduce energy and utility bills for households and unwinding reforms on unemployment benefits. Both are fiscally draining policies but it remains to be seen whether the two sides can find a way to work together on these policies not just in principle but also in detail.

We believe a consensus is building whereby markets deem the worst-case outcome of the elections to be a majority win by the NFP. This said, coordination and execution across the multitude of parties of the alliance will not be easy. Marine Le Pen, on the other hand, struck a conciliatory tone in an interview over the weekend and vowed to respect institutions and work with Macron. Similarly, one of RN’s policy architects, Jean-Philippe Tanguy, stated that “RN will hold the line on deficits and present a credible plan. The markets will be severe on us, so we really have no choice but to do so.” The recognition of the constraints any adverse impacts from financial markets impose is reassuring. Both Liz Truss’s mini budget and Meloni’s windfall tax on banks must be serving as very valuable lessons. This is particularly true for the case of Liz Truss where her party has never recovered politically from the fallout in financial markets that ensued. The reassurances to businesses and financial markets that emerge from RN camp seems to suggest that Le Pen has her eyes on the presidency and does not intend to squander her first genuine grasp of power reminiscent of Liz Truss, and instead follow the example of Meloni in Italy. How this acknowledgement of the reality of financial markets can be squared with campaign promises remains to be seen. Clearly, much uncertainty remains, but much of the negativity is already in the price and we may be in a situation of ‘buy the rumour and sell the fact’, or to be more precise, the reverse of it.

For additional risk information, please call your sales representative for a copy of “Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options.” We advise investors to consult their tax advisors and legal counsel about the tax implications of these strategies. Put Sale: Investors who sell put options will own the underlying asset if the asset’s price falls below the strike price of the put option. Investors, therefore, will be exposed to any decline in the underlying asset’s price below the strike potentially to zero, and they will not participate in any price appreciation in the underlying asset if the option expires unexercised.

Call Sale: Investors who sell uncovered call options have exposure on the upside that is theoretically unlimited.

Call Overwrite or Buywrite: Investors who sell call options against a long position in the underlying asset give up any appreciation in the underlying asset’s price above the strike price of the call option, and they remain exposed to the downside of the underlying asset in the return for the receipt of the option premium.

Booster : In a sell-off, the maximum realized downside potential of a double-up booster is the net premium paid. In a rally, option losses are potentially unlimited as the investor is net short a call. When overlaid onto a long position in the underlying asset, upside losses are capped (as for a covered call), but downside losses are not.

Collar: Locks in the amount that can be realized at maturity to a range defined by the put and call strike. If the collar is not costless, investors risk losing 100% of the premium paid. Since investors are selling a call option, they give up any price appreciation in the underlying asset above the strike price of the call option.

Call Purchase: Options are a decaying asset, and investors risk losing 100% of the premium paid if the underlying asset’s price is below the strike price of the call option.

Put Purchase: Options are a decaying asset, and investors risk losing 100% of the premium paid if the underlying asset’s price is above the strike price of the put option.

Straddle or Strangle: The seller of a straddle or strangle is exposed to increases in the underlying asset’s price above the call strike and declines in the underlying asset’s price below the put strike. Since exposure on the upside is theoretically unlimited, investors who also own the underlying asset would have limited losses should the underlying asset rally. Covered writers are exposed to declines in the underlying asset position as well as any additional exposure should the underlying asset decline below the strike price of the put option. Having sold a covered call option, the investor gives up all appreciation in the underlying asset above the strike price of the call option.

Put Spread: The buyer of a put spread risks losing 100% of the premium paid. The buyer of higher-ratio put spread has unlimited downside below the lower strike (down to zero), dependent on the number of lower-struck puts sold. The maximum gain is limited to the spread between the two put strikes, when the underlying is at the lower strike. Investors who own the underlying asset will have downside protection between the higher-strike put and the lower-strike put. However, should the underlying asset’s price fall below the strike price of the lower-strike put, investors regain exposure to the underlying asset, and this exposure is multiplied by the number of puts sold.

Call Spread: The buyer risks losing 100% of the premium paid. The gain is limited to the spread between the two strike prices. The seller of a call spread risks losing an amount equal to the spread between the two call strikes less the net premium received. By selling a covered call spread, the investor remains exposed to the downside of the underlying asset and gives up the spread between the two call strikes should the underlying asset rally.

Butterfly Spread: A butterfly spread consists of two spreads established simultaneously – one a bull spread and the other a bear spread. The resulting position is neutral, that is, the investor will profit if the underlying is stable. Butterfly spreads are established at a net debit. The maximum profit will occur at the middle strike price; the maximum loss is the net debit.
