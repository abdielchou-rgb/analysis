## China regional banks

## FY23 & 1Q24 summary and FY24 outlook update: marginal improvement in differentiation

Our readings from FY23 and 1Q24 results of China Regional Banks (CRB): 1) The performance divergence among China regional banks became more pronounced in 1Q24, due to three main reasons: widening gaps in core business income (NII) growth, differences in investment income performance, and the impact of bad debt provision release. 2) Some banks saw a q/q NIM improvement in 1Q24, which surprised the market. However, we expect the overall NIM trend for CRBs to remain downward, while high-quality regional banks are likely to maintain stable NIM in 2024. 3) Fee income growth remains sluggish, due to households' cautious attitude towards risk investments and the policy headwinds. We expect CRB's fee income to continue being negatively impacted by cautious investment sentiment and mutual fund distribution fee rate reform. 4)The average NPL ratio of the CRBs we cover remained flat for two consecutive quarters from 2Q23 to 3Q23 and showed marginal improvement for two consecutive quarters since 4Q23. With less pressure from troubled loans, we believe CRBs generally supported 1Q24 profit growth by releasing provisions.

Recent marketing feedback: In recent interactions, we have felt a marginal improvement in investors' interest in Chinese regional banks, although it has not yet returned to pre-2022 levels. Optimistic investors believe that the profit growth contraction of high-quality regional banks has come to an end and their valuations are at relatively low levels. On the other hand, relatively cautious investors tend to adopt a defensive strategy by holding high-dividend regional banks, as they believe high-quality regional banks, such as BoNB are unlikely to return to the high profit growth rates at \~20% in the short term. Overall, the marginal rebound in NIM for some regional banks in 1Q24 has effectively boosted investors' confidence in the CRBs’ fundamentals and earnings outlook. Additionally, marginal improvement in asset quality indicators has alleviated investors' concerns about macro risks related to the real estate sector and LGFV debt.

Investment recommendation: Our current top picks are CSRCB (growth pick) and BoNJ (high dividend yield pick). We expect CSRCB's profit growth in FY24-26E to be 19%/18%/18%. The company’s NII is protected by its loan pricing ability in micro and small business sector through a differentiated customer positioning strategy. The solid investment income performance indicates significant progress in its business diversification strategy. Bank of Nanjing offers a dividend yield of 5.5% (as of 16 May close), and its fundamental performance is notably better than other high-dividend CRBs. Therefore, we recommend Bank of Nanjing as our yield pick. Finally, while we remain positive on Bank of Ningbo's long-term performance, investors should also notice several potential risk factors:1) A relatively conservative investment strategy limits room for FY24 trading income growth; 2) Fund distribution income is more negatively impacted by further policy changes; and, 3) Investors may be concerned about EPS dilution from further refinancing needs in 2025.

## Earnings Growth: Divergent among CRBs

In 1Q24, there was a divergence in revenue and profit growth among China's regional banks (CRBs). Among CRBs we cover, BoHZ and CSRCB had relatively fast profit growth, at 21.1%y/y and 19.8% y/y respectively. BoNB, BoNJ, BoBJ and BoSH achieved low-to-mid single digit profit growth while CRCB’s 1Q24 profits contracted by -11% y/y. From revenue perspective, CSRCB’s 1Q24 revenue growth of 12% y/y was the strongest, making it the only bank in our coverage to report double-digit growth. BoHZ’s revenue growth of 4% y/y was modest compared with its 21% y/y profit growth, as its strong profit growth was mainly supported by a reduction in ECL charges and provision release. BoSH and CRCB reported revenue contraction in 1Q24, at -1% y/y and - 3% y/y, respectively, and the weak NII performance was the biggest drag for both banks. Overall, although profit growth (7% y/y on average) slowed in 1Q24 vs 1Q23 (12% y/y average), the regional banks in our coverage were able to maintain a faster earnings growth pace than large banks (vs JSB +0.4% y/y and SOE banks -1.6% y/y), and we observed more substantial performance divergence compared to the past.

## Fee income: fee cuts and weak investment sentiment are the key drags

 Fee income was weak with regional banks among our coverage all reporting fee contraction in 2023, and the contraction narrowed in 1Q24. In FY23, CRBs (excl. CSRCB) on average reported -24% y/y fee contraction and the contraction narrowed to 9% y/y in 1Q24. We believe the disappointing performance was related to the weak investment sentiment amid bear cycle and fee rate cuts on bancassurance and mutual fund distribution business. BoNJ’s fee performance in 1Q24 stood out among our coverage, with fee income +29% y/y, which we believe was due to its increased bond underwriting activities and marginal improvement in asset management business. Looking forward, we expect fee income to remain weak in 1H24, as 1H23 was a high base in terms of insurance agency fee income (the insurance agency fee cut was introduced in Sep-23), and we expect it to take more time for households to rebuild the investment sentiment.

Fee income faces further policy risks in 2H24-FY25. In July 2023, the CSRC implemented the "mutual fund industry fee reform work plan", which aims to gradually reduce the comprehensive fee rate of the mutual fund industry in three phases, following the path of “Asset managers – Brokers- Fund distributors”. The CSRC has already reduced management and custody fees for actively managed funds (mainly affecting fund managers) and lowered stock transaction commission rates in mutual funds (mainly affecting brokers). CSRC indicated that the next step will focus on fund distribution, further regulating fees such as subscription and redemption fee rate, with related reform measures expected to be introduced by the end of 2024. Based on previous fee reform measures, we believe this new reform could negatively impact the fee income (mainly agency fee income) of regional banks in 2H24-2025. Among the regional banks we cover, Bank of Ningbo is expected to be the most affected, as it has the highest fee income from mutual fund distribution.

## Balance sheet analysis: Balance sheet expansion slowed down in FY23

In 1Q24, CRB’s loan and deposit growth were relatively balanced. In 1Q24, CRBs we cover achieved q/q loan growth of 6% on average. On the deposit side, the growth was relatively divergent (average 6% y/y), with BoNB and CSRCB taking the lead and reporting \~15% y/y deposit growth. CRBs in general slowed down balance sheet expansion in FY23, due likely to insufficient credit demand. BoSH reported both weak loan and deposit growth in 1Q24, both at 3% y/y, and we expect its NII to be continually under pressure in 1H24.

In FY23, CRB’s corporate loan growth (+15% y/y) outpaced retail loan growth (+10% y/y). This trend is in line with our expectation, as the retail loan demand remained weak in FY23 under economic uncertainties, and mortgage early repayment was an issue, especially in 1H23. BoNB was able to maintain the fastest retail loan growth in FY23 (+30% y/y), which demonstrated its leading position in the retail business among CRBs. CSRCB also recorded +14% y/y retail loan growth, benefiting from the strategy to target the underserved rural individuals.

 We believe regional banks may continue to reduce high-cost deposit liabilities under regulatory guidance. In 1Q24, we observed that some regional banks have already started to control deposit growth on the liability side (e.g., BoNJ and BoSH deposits grew by only 4% and 3% QoQ, respectively, in 1Q24). Some management teams have clearly stated that they will reduce the pursuit of scale expansion in the future and place greater emphasis on cost control. According to media reports (link), the regulator started prohibiting commercial banks from privately offering high interest rates to deposit customers in April. We expect the pressure on CRB's net interest margin to ease on the liability side.

## NIM: We expect stabilization in FY24 NIM

FY23 NIM contraction is in line with expectation. In FY23, the NIM of our covered CRBs contracted by 13bps on average. The decline was within our expectation, and we attribute the drag to: 1) downward loan yields followed by multiple rounds of LPR cuts in FY23, and 2) the rigidness in deposit cost – Although banks are guided to lower the deposit rates, the migration of demand deposit to the higher-yield time deposit amid weak capital market restrained the cost decline. In FY23, CRBs we cover faced an average decline of 26bps y/y in loan interest rates, at the same time, their deposit costs only decreased by -1bps y/y.

In 1Q24, we note a rebound in quarterly NIM for banks with related disclosure. Among our coverage, only CSRCB, BoNB and CRCB reported quarterly NIM, all of them saw improvement in 1Q24NIM as compared to 4Q23 level (+24/+5/+2 bps respectively). The reversal of downward trending NIM is better than our expectation, and we expect stabilization in NIM trend in 2024. Marginal improvement in NIM could be a positive signal: Although the Feb 24 5-year LPR cut (-25 bps) indicates further downward pressure on NIM in 1Q25, we believe high quality CRBs could stabilize their NIM in 2H24 through margin protection efforts on both the asset and liability sides.

 The average NPL ratio of CRBs has continued to fall since 2Q22. ## Capital adequacy: refinancing need raised for BoHZ

CET1 ratio on average weakened in 1Q24, with BoHZ seeing the most pressure from the absolute level. Among our covered CRBs, BoHZ had the lowest CET1 ratio (8.46% in 1Q24), although it marginally improved from 4Q23 level. Therefore, BoHZ faced the greatest pressure to supplement its capital. BoHZ has issued plans to replenish the CET1 capital via private placement, while it is still pending regulatory approval. The slow progress on the refinancing plans may restrict future business growth and limit dividend distribution of BoHZ.

Dividend payout: CRBs under our coverage in general raised their dividend payout ratio in FY23, with BoNJ, BoBJ, BoSH, CRCB’s payout ratio reached 30%, and all the above-mentioned banks have reached the dividend yield above 5.5%, on forward 12M basis. BoHZ raised the dividend payout ratio by 0.7pcts to 23%, while the absolute level is relatively low compared with the high-dividend CRBs, given the constraint from its capital adequacy. BoNB increased its dividend payout ratio in FY23 from 14.8% to 16%. The increase in the payout ratio yields provide 20% y/y cash dividend growth for shareholders (vs. EPS growth of 11% y/y). In FY23 report, CSRCB aims to balance the company's capital needs with shareholder returns by distributing a 10% stock dividend (with a cash dividend of RMB 0.25 per share). Given that CSRCB's EPS growth rate is expected to be significantly higher than 10% (JPMe 13%), we believe CSRCB's shareholder distribution plan is beneficial for shareholders. The capital market has also responded positively: after CSRCB disclosed its annual dividend plan (on 30 Apr), its stock price increased by 11% (vs. the SSE bank index's 2.5% as of 16 May close).
