We expect full-year return to be around 7.0-7.5% assuming a slight widening in credit spreads (12bp to SOT+205) as UST yields trend lower (-30bp to 3.9% for 7-yr UST). We are agnostic between IG and HY, and within IG have a preference for corporate over financials, and the <sup>l</sup>ong en<sup>d</sup> o<sup>f</sup> <sup>th</sup>e curve.

<sup></sup> Clipping coupon. Asia credit had a decent start for 2024 with the JACI clocking up 1.42% total return in 1Q24. While there have been stronger quarters for returns, we believe that such a performance is still commendable considering that the quarter saw a 33bp rise in 7-year UST yields, which fortunately was buffered by 42bp of tightening in credit spreads. Essentially, the first quarter’s return came solely from carry. This is hovering \~6%, which we think is decent for a higher triple-B index with a 4.5-year duration. Another important point to note is the gains were rather broad-based, as most major segments (except IG sovereign) managed to end the quarter in positive territory, and both Asia IG and HY have outperformed peers in DM and EM. We believe these factors underscore the point that the resiliency of Asia credit remains largely intact.

<sup></sup> Narratives for credit fundamentals have not changed much. China remains an area of concern as it is still contending with its property and local government problems. One mitigating factor is probably that recent economic data suggest that damage to the economy is thus far well contained. Such compartmentalization is also well reflected in the credit world as rating downgrades are highly concentrated in several well-demarcated trouble areas (e.g., property developers, local governments, and AMCs); these sectors accounted for the lion’s share of downgrades (86% of number of downgrades in 2023 and 1Q24). Asia IG ratings will remain resilient given that two-thirds of Asia issuers are government-related entities, and thus benefit from rating uplift that provides a cushion for any pressure on standalone fundamentals, which remain largely intact. Asia HY has started the year on a cleaner slate after three years of cleansing that had led to double-digit default rates (10-17%) in 2021-23; in 1Q24, we have only suffered four defaults totaling US\$2 billion (0.8% default rate). This is running well below our full-year forecast of US\$11 billion (4.5%). While we are keeping to our default forecasts for now given that there are several situations teetering at the edge, the 1Q24 run rate still provides some comfort that there are no major negative surprises.

<sup></sup> Technicals remain supportive. 1Q24 supply tallied up to US\$37 billion (-7%yoy), the lowest print since 2017. This is tracking close to our full-year forecast of US\$125 billion after adjusting for seasonal factor, and we don’t think supply will pick up materially from here given that local bank loans and bonds remain much cheaper sources of funding. With supply coming in below the US\$38 billion of maturities in 1Q24, the market had effectively returned US\$15 billion of capital (maturities + coupon payments - supply) to investors, and the full-year number could add up to US\$114 billion (\~12% of JACI market capitalization) after factoring in this year’s maturities totaling US\$187 billion that are backloaded. On the demand side, commercial banks (particularly Chinese banks) remain active participants in the primary market. While some of these demand flows are being channeled to the DM market, this is mainly due to lack of supply from Asia and not driven by valuations. Attractive yield levels have also supported demand from insurance companies and private banks clients. One positive swing factor this year is that demand from real money accounts has also turned more supportive. While Asia and EM funds are still reporting outflows in 1Q, the pace has moderated somewhat from last year’s level. Anecdotally, we have seen some spillover demand from US IG funds, which has kept overall demand from global accounts in positive territory.

## Summary of views

<sup></sup> Yields still matter more than spreads. While credit spreads (SOT+192bp) are near the post-GFC tight, we would argue that current valuations remain attractive for Asian investors that are still yield- rather than spread-focused. Current yields at 6.2% are near the upper end of the historical trading range. Also, spreads do not look too out-out-sync compared to levels last seen in the high interest rate environment before the GFC back in 2005-2007. Such a focus on yields would probably explain why the JACI has been trading largely within a range of 6.20-6.40% for the most part of this year, a rather narrow 20bp range, despite the swing in UST yields. Such observations also equally apply to Asia IG that should be more sensitive to UST yields; JACI IG has largely traded within a narrow range of 5.2-5.4%.

<sup></sup> Expect full-year return to be around 7.2%. Given our view that investors are more focused on yield, we expect spreads could widen by 12bp to close the year at SOT+205bp as UST yields start to trend lower in 2H24; our US Rates Strategy team expects the 7-year UST yields to end the year 30bp lower at 3.9%. All these moving parts would still translate into 5.65% of total return till year-end. Together with 1Q24 return, this would bring the full-year return to 7.0-7.5%, which looks rather decent. We recognize that our return forecast is sensitive to rate movements. In the worst-case scenario that rates stay higher for longer, we stil believe that the JACI could deliver close to 6.0-6.5% total return for the full year. We see rates remaining the key risk for the market. Another risk factor is geopolitics. This could create some short-term volatilities, but we don’t expect this would lead to lasting impact given strong technicals from Asia investor base.

<sup></sup> Key strategy and investment themes. We are keeping to our agnostic view between IG and HY. Within IG, we still prefer corporates over financials, particularly the long end of the credit curve. For financials, we see better value in the lower part of credit curve, including some AT1 instruments that are trading wider than HY corporates. We recognize that we could still see some near-term rates volatility but see any such weakness as an opportunity to add duration. Our key investment themes are: 1) structural growth stories (Macau gaming, Indian macro and Asian consumers); 2) defensive sectors (utilities and infrastructure, and government-owned financials); 3) the lower part of the capital structure (bank AT1/T2 and corporate hybrids); 4) some credit curve plays (belly of China SOE/TMT and long-end Indonesia quasi-sovereign); and 5) selected recovery names.
