# 2Q24 Pre-announcements: Better than 4Q23 Yet Still Negative

2Q24 A-share pre-announcements recorded net negative 4.6% by number of companies. Reporting seasonality suggests a further delay for earnings growth reacceleration amid softening macro prints since late 2Q. 20 stocks to own and eight to avoid based on our screening.

2Q24 A-share pre-announcements still negative but better than 4Q23 trough: Net negative of -4.6% by number of companies (vs. -14.2% in 4Q23 and -4.2% in 2Q23) and net positive of 2.8% by market cap (vs -4.7% in 4Q23 and 0.5% in 2Q23). Analysis is based on 1,650 pre-announcements, 32% by number of companies and 25% by market cap in A-shares ( Exhibit 1 ).

\- Larger companies have shown higher resilience, while small caps have shown the most negative surprises (-11.7%) ( Exhibit 4 ): We take this as a proof that shocks to corporate earnings under the weakening macro backdrop are uneven across difference sizes of companies, with smaller ones more vulnerable.

\- Broad-based net negative alerts by number of companies across industry groups: Real Estate Management & Development, Consumer Services, and Media & Entertainment saw the most net negative alerts. Household & Personal Products, Telecommunication Service, and Automobile & Components stood out with their largest net positive alerts ( Exhibit 5 ).

\- Potential data seasonality suggests that it is too early to call a bottoming out of earnings growth: For both A-share and MSCI China pre-announcements, there seems to be a pattern of concentrated negative alerts toward year-end (4Q) with much lighter reporting for other quarters. Such patter has become more dominant, especially since a disclosure rule change that became effective in 1Q22 and makes 1Q and 3Q pre-announcements voluntary for A-shares. Comparing like-for-like shows a deterioration of net negative alerts vs. 2Q23, both in terms of market aggregates ( Exhibit 1 ) and across different company sizes ( Exhibit 4 ).

For MSCI China, a net positive of 1.4% pre-announcements by number of companies has been recorded so far, although based on a rather small set of data points (141 alerts, representing 22% by number of companies and 7% by market cap ( Exhibit 9 )): It recovered from 4Q23's trough at -4.2% and is slightly better than 2Q23 at 0.5%. The softening macro prints in 2Q may bring pressure to 3Q results.

# Overview of China A Share – 2Q24 Pre-Announcement: Still Negative but Improved from 4Q23 Trough

As of July 23, 2024, \~1,650 A-shares had issued 2Q24 pre-announcements, representing 32% of the market by number of companies, and 25% by market cap. Key observations:

1. 2Q24 A-share pre-announcements still net negative at -4.6%, a similar level to 2Q23 and bounced back from 4Q23 record low ( Exhibit 3 ): In aggregate, Ashares have recorded a net negative of 4.6% by number of companies, similar level to 2Q23 (-4.2%), and marking meaningful improvement vs. 4Q23 (-14.2%). (Note: we compare with 2Q/4Q due to disclosure rule change.)

2. Earnings recovery among companies of different scale is skewed, with largecap companies much more resilient ( Exhibit 4 ): Larger companies have tended to be more resilient amid the uneven recovery. Small caps showed the most negative surprises (-11.7%), while mid caps registered net negative of -8.0%. We continue to advise sticking to industry and category leaders for higher earnings stability.

3. Measured by number of companies, 17 out of 25 total industry groups issued net negative alerts ( Exhibit 5 ): Real Estate Management & Development, Consumer Services, and Media & Entertainment saw the most net negative alerts (i.e., the number of negative alerts outweighed the number of positive alerts in percentage terms in those industry groups). Household & Personal Products, Telecommunication Service, and Automobile & Components stood out with their largest net positive alerts.

4. Measured by market cap, net positive of 2.8% posted positive alerts, improved from net negative of 4.7% in 4Q23 ( Exhibit 6 ): Of the 25 industry groups, 14 had collective market cap where positive alerts outweighed those with negative alerts the most in percentage terms, led by Technology Hardware & Equipment, Automobile & Components and Semiconductors. The other industry groups posted net negative alerts, dragged down by Real Estate Management & Development, Software & Services, and Financial Services.

# Earnings Estimate Revisions (May 31, 2024-July 23, 2024): Continue Negative Revision for Broad-based Group, but Large Caps Show More Positive Revision

As of July 23, 2024, 311 companies, representing 50% of MSCI China by number of companies, have had consensus earnings estimates raised or lowered >1% during the assessment period (May 31, 2024-July 23, 2024). We have analyzed analysts' earnings estimate revision trajectories by industry group during the period in terms of both number of companies and index weight. We run the analysis based on consensus data.

## Key observations based on consensus data:

1. If measured by index weight, a net 22% of MSCI China saw 2024 consensus earnings estimates revised up during May 31-July 23: 39% of MSCI China by index weight saw their 2024 consensus earnings estimates rise during the period vs. 18% with a decrease ( Exhibit 11 ).

2. If measured by number of companies, a net 14% of MSCI China constituents saw 2024 consensus earnings estimates revised down during May 31-July 23, down 32ppt from the 46% net negative in our 1Q24 analysis: Consensus 2024 EPS estimates of 112 MSCI China constituents were raised by more than 1% during the period, while those of 199 constituents were lowered ( Exhibit 12 ).

3. Upward 2024 consensus earnings estimate revisions during the period is therefore skewed toward large-caps.

4. Sectors with net positive revisions: By number of companies, only Consumer Durables & Apparel and Insurance showed net rises in 2024 consensus estimates. By index weight, eight industry groups showed net rises in 2024 consensus estimates, including: Consumer Discretionary, Transportation, Consumer Services, Consumers Durables & Apparel, Materials, Insurance, Technology Hardware, and Media & Entertainment.

5. Sectors with net negative revisions: By number of companies, the majority of industry groups (19 out of 25) showed net declines in 2024 consensus estimates (i.e., number of downward estimate revisions outweighs number of upward revisions in percentage terms), led by Software, Consumer Staples Distribution & Retail, Real Estate. Measured by index weight, 13 out of 25 industry groups had net negative revisions, led by Software, Real Estate, Auto and Financial Services.
