## Developed Markets

## United States:

It was a relatively quiet Friday with market participants awaiting a busy calendar in the week ahead. In Japan, the curve flattened amid higher market implied probability of a July BoJ rate hike. JPY also traded in a narrow range ahead of next week’s meeting. In the Euro area, market participants are awaiting further clarity on the inflation trend with CPI releases around the continent next week as well as for the Euro area which will be informative for the ECB policy rate path. In the UK, market pricing still implies a 50/50 chance for whether the BoE will cut rates at its August meeting next week.

The main event of the day was the June PCE release in the US. After the quarterly Core PCE data surprised to the upside, many market participants feared an unexpected increase in the June data. However, Core PCE data was largely in-line with expectations and prices rose 0.18% m/m (MSe: 0.18%; C: 0.2%; P: 0.1%) which kept the y/y rate at 2.6% (C: 2.5%; P: 2.6%). Overall, the reading eased the fears of acceleration and confirms the continued improvement in US inflation. This reinforces the belief that the Fed will cut rates at its September meeting, and our economists believe the Federal Reserve will open the door to a September rate cut at the July FOMC meeting.

USTs rallied following the PCE release led by the front-end and belly of the curve which further accelerated the steepening of the curve that has been seen this week. Absent other first-tier economic data or Fedspeak, USTs largely traded in a narrow range for the rest of the NY session. The USD also marginally weakened following the release, but was fairly range-bound and ended the day relatively unchanged.

Overall, due to a number of catalysts, but seemingly primarily driven by increased confidence in more imminent Fed rate cuts, the UST curve steepened this week. 2y yields have rallied \~13bp while the long-end finished the week largely unchanged.

Increased confidence for a September Fed cut supported US equities with all major sectors in the S&P 500 up on the day. Overall the index rose 1.1% on Friday and finished the week only down 0.8% even with the major losses mid-week. Gold also benefited from the soft inflation data reinforcing Fed rate cuts and gained 1.0%.

## Japan:

The JGB curve began to twist flatten in the Tokyo morning ahead of the 2y JGB auction. It seemed that unwinds of steepener trades accelerated as market participants gradually priced in a higher probability for a 15bp rate hike at the upcoming July BoJ meeting. Despite the rate hike possibility as well the Tokyo CPI release which exhibited resilient service inflation, the 2y JGB auction was strongerthan-expected with a cut-off price of 99.975 (C: 99.970). However, the long-end sold off into Tokyo close. The JGB curve bull-flattened; 2y JGB yield was unchanged at 0.390%, 10y JGB rallied 1.0bp to 1.055%, and 30y JGB rallied 1.5bp to 2.145% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve bull-flattened and outperformed JGBs across the curve, especially on the longer-end. XCCY basis widened across led by the long-end.

After the large swing overnight, USD/JPY traded in a narrow range of 153.40 to 154.14 during the Tokyo session. Following steady growth data in the US on Thursday, it appeared that the unwinding of JPY carry trades temporarily came to a halt. In the London morning, USD/JPY climbed up toward 154.50 level alongside strong equity performance. Risk-sensitive currencies such as AUD, SEK particularly outperformed, and AUD/JPY moved higher toward 101.33 in the London morning. Although USD/JPY climbed higher ahead of next week’s BoJ meeting, pressure to unwind carry trade may further accelerate again in the case of higher volatility of the pair and a lower expectation for carry.

## Europe:

European risk-sensitive currencies outperformed in G10 as market participants priced in more easing from the Fed after the June PCE data provided further evidence of a decelerating path in US inflation data. GBP and NOK both gained 0.2% against the USD. CHF underperformed with USD/CHF up 0.2% and EUR/CHF up 0.3%. EUR (+0.1%) remained supported ahead of next week’s CPI data , trading around 1.0860 in the NY afternoon.

Dollar Bloc:

The USD weakened marginally after June PCE data confirmed the deceleration trend in US inflation, but retraced early losses. Ultimately, the DXY USD Index was relatively unchanged. AUD led the G10 gains and strengthened 0.2% against the USD. NZD also gained, albeit in a smaller magnitude, and was up 0.1%. CAD continued its decline and USD/CAD rose 0.1%.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ traded mixed in a narrow range ahead of US PCE data. After the overnight retreat on Thursday, CNH continued to decline\~0.4% and erased all the gains from Thursday morning and fell to 7.2658; CNY declined modestly to 7.2535 per USD alongside a stronger USD/CNY fixing of 7.1270. IDR continued its depreciation trend and pushed USD/IDR higher to 16,290. KRW also declined for a second consecutive day; USD/KRW rose 0.3% to 1,387. After two days of markets being closed, PHP appreciated 0.2% to 58.35 per USD. Meanwhile, TWD resumed trading higher in the morning session, but gradually lost the upside momentum. Ultimately, USD/TWD ended the session largely unchanged at 32.84, despite the slump in local equities (TWSE: -3.3%) and US\$2.6bn of foreign equities outflows, the largest amount since April 2024. MYR continued to strengthen and rose 0.1% to 4.66 per USD. INR remained stable at 83.72 per USD. THB rose moderately to 36.08 alongside a rally in gold prices.

AxJ rates also had mixed moves. In China, the bond market rallied alongside a report which disclosed several major Chinese banks cut deposit rates by a range of 5 to 20bp; 10y CGB yields extended declines, down 2bp to 2.18%, the lowest level since 2002. Meanwhile, the CNY NDIRS held largely unchanged. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bear-flattened 2bp while the 3s10s KTB yield curve bull-flattened 2bp. In Thailand, the 2s10s ThaiGB yield curve twist flattened 2bp following the moves in USTs on Thursday. A similar pattern was seen in Hong Kong, where the swap curve twist flattened 4bp given its high correlation to US rates. In India, the 2s10s NDOIS curve shifted 4bp higher while 10y G-sec yields fell 4bp to 6.93% amid reports that global funds increased exposure to long-term debt in India. Indonesia bonds remained stable.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• ECB Executive Board Member Schnabel says the pace and extent of ECB rate cuts will depend on the data, noting that service inflation is proving to be particular sticky. She highlights that "persistent services inflation shows that the 'last mile' of the fight against inflation is particularly difficult"

## Emerging Markets

• MAS maintained the prevailing rate of appreciation slope of the S\$NEER policy band and held no change to its width and the center level. MAS anticipates growth momentum in the Singapore economy should improve in 2H24, and GDP growth is likely to come in closer to 2-3% for the full year 2024 given that the manufacturing and financial sectors will be bolstered by the broadening tech upturn and the expected decline in global interest rates. Regarding the inflation outlook, MAS lowered its headline CPI forecast for 2024 to 2.0-3.0% (P: 2.5%-3.5%), reflecting lower-than-anticipated private transport inflation in recent months. Meanwhile, Core CPI is expected to average 2.5%-3.5% for 2024 and fall further to \~2.0% in 2025

• RBI released draft guidelines to tighten standards related to the Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR). The proposals include: 1) banks to assign additional 5% run-off factor for retail deposits enabled with internet and mobile banking; 2) unsecured wholesale funding provided by non-financial small business customers shall be treated in accordance with the treatment of retail deposits; 3) level 1 High Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) in the form of government securities shall be valued at an amount not greater than their current market value; 4) in case a deposit, hitherto excluded from LCR computation (for instance, a non-callable fixed deposit), is contractually pledged as collateral to a bank to secure a credit facility or loan, such deposit shall be treated as callable for LCR purposes

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
