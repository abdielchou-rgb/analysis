# From the Grind to the Unwind

Recent political events led to price action, suggesting unwinds of popular carry trades in Mexico and France. While the domino effect may be contained for now, we caution against adding to popular trades like US yield curve steepeners, which could be at risk if economic activity data disappoint.

## Global Macro Strategy

We discuss worrisome signs involving politics and fiscal deficits and how these coincide with an increasingly downbeat US consumer, a smaller US cash flow deficit relative to last year, and no additional banking system liquidity – leaving us cautious on risk. We also discuss how the US election may affect US rates, and how the French and US elections skew the risks toward a lower EUR/USD exchange rate.

## Interest Rate Strategy

In the US, we exit long 3m10y receivers and receive July FOMC OIS swap at 5.31%. In the euro area, we maintain our structural receive EUR 1y1y and maintain our long ERZ4 97.125 call. In cash, we maintain our 2s10s BTPs steepeners vs. Bunds. In the UK, we maintain SFIU4/U5 flatteners, and keep our pay 5s10s30s SONIA fly and short 15y ASW positions. In Japan, we shift from long 10y JGB ASW outright to 5s10s ASW box flattener. We also shift from long 20y JGBs on 10s20s30s fly to 5s20s30s fly. We maintain pay 10y on 2s10s20s fly.

## Currency & Foreign Exchange

We continue to like EUR/GBP as an attractive positive carry hedge against election risks and policy divergence. We think that GBP will react more to the May inflation print, released the day before the BoE meeting, than the rate decision itself. We look to next week's Norges Bank meeting and maintain our bullish bias on NOK crosses. We discuss the recent resilience of USD/JPY despite weak US inflation data.

## Short-Duration Strategy

SOFR returned to RRP+1bp this week, but we identify key factors to watch for repo conditions as excess liquidity in the front end comes into better balance. MMF AUM has approached a record high of \$6.535 trillion ahead of the June corporate tax date, which likely is driving temporary outflows. MMF additions to bill holdings in May highlight the significance of the September FOMC meeting.

## A Focus on Fiscal in France and Elections Elsewhere

"Elections have consequences", so they say. It seems even the prospects of elections have consequences too. Events in France dominated headlines and market price action over the past week, even as the US CPI report and FOMC meeting surprised in different ways.

Our economists suggest that in most French parliamentary election scenarios, investors should expect the fiscal outlook to deteriorate – prompting a widening in French OATs vs. German bunds (see Implications of French Elections).

• In our view, the 10y OAT/bund spread should range-trade between 65 and 80bp, with an expected value on the 10y spread in the \~70bp area.

• For the euro, the main risk lies in the implications of the results for the future of the European Union. We maintain our suggestion to sell EUR/GBP as an attractive positive carry hedge against election risks and policy divergence.

In addition, prior to President Macron announcing snap parliamentary elections for the National Assembly, Mexico elected its first female president – prompting a sharp move higher in the USD/MXN exchange rate (see A Mexico Moment for Markets, and Exhibit 1 ).

## Noisy Rates Amid Noisy Election

For Treasuries, our election outcomes generally support a path to lower nominal yields across outcomes. A Republican sweep would be complicated, with the potential for a higher term premium and divergence between lower real yields and higher breakevens as investors try to parse through the uncertainty of combined policy impacts on the US economic outlook. We see marginally higher yields, at least initially, under a Democrat sweep.

To detail our thinking, there are five major policy prongs on which US rates, and broadly macro markets, will turn: 1) immigration, 2) tariffs and trade policy, 3) tax cuts and TCJA 2017, 4) composition and independence of the Fed, and 5) deregulation.

The last time we covered the US presidential election, a united vs. divided government framework was enough to think about the election impact on US rates, and more broadly, macro markets. A united government came with higher prospects of fiscal spending and higher rates, and vice versa (see Exhibit 29 and 30). Not anymore.

## Elections Have Consequences: Watch Out Below, EUR/USD

Over the next 2 to 3 weeks, markets are likely to focus in on the first US presidential election debate (on June 27) and the first and second rounds of the French legislative elections (held on June 30 and July 7). The impact that the election results we've seen so far in June have had on markets suggests that investors will focus in on these risk events, particularly given their potential implications for policy.

• Our core view remains that EUR/USD should move lower due to expected changes in yield differentials, as over the course of the cycle, we expect markets to price in a more dovish path for the ECB relative to the Fed.

We explain below how the US election debate and the French presidential elections interact with this core view, and we recommend maintaining short EUR/USD exposure over this period.

US election risks: We think the USD is likely to gain against risk-sensitive currencies if investors see a rising likelihood of a second Trump administration due to perceptions regarding trade confrontation and the fiscal outlook. These gains could be particularly sizable if Republicans are seen as likely to control both the House and Senate as such an outcome would likely raise expectations for further deficit spending.

As we discuss in USD | USD sensitivity to policy risks , Trump administration trade and immigration policy actions were associated with softer risk demand, a lower AUD/USD, and a lower USD/JPY. USD/JPY declines may be limited by JPY's shifting correlation to risk and by higher BoJ policy rate expectations than in 2018-2019 (implying local yields have more flexibility to drop if growth is impacted by US tariff policy). However, history suggests that short AUD/JPY positions may again serve as an effective hedge to rising risks of trade policy confrontation in a potential second Trump administration.

French election risks: OAT-bund spreads widened and the EUR weakened as President Macron announced that legislative elections would be held at the end of June. Our economists expect that a Rassemlement National majority in the French National Assembly would lead to a 1.8-2.5% of GDP increase in the government's fiscal deficit, while a majority for the union of left-wing parties could raise the deficit by 3.5-5.0% of GDP. Both outcomes could put additional pressure on OAT-bund spreads, but it is less apparent what the fiscal response would be.

Greater fiscal spending would be good for growth, but would be inflationary and lead to a more hawkish ECB, all else equal. This should be supportive of the EUR. However, a substantial widening of the deficit that leads to credit rating downgrades would at least partially offset that policy support for the EUR.

In our view, the biggest risk to the EUR from the French elections has little to do with fiscal policy but what an RN majority would mean for the French presidential elections in 2027 (or before, should it be called earlier) and France's position in the EU.

We can see that the EU risks are the bigger focus for markets by simply observing that the French CAC40 equity market dropped 6-7% this week, and the Euro Stoxx 50 index is down \~4%. If the focus were on the pro-growth implications of potentially looser fiscal policy associated with the election outcomes, then most likely equities would not have fallen so much.

As we note here, RN has stopped advocating the idea of "Frexit", which helps to mitigate a key risk for EUR. However, the EUR could still face some downside pressure in anticipation of RN adopting policies that are inconsistent with current EU rules, or that France begins to advocate for reforms to weaken European integration.

If so, this could lead to political tension and elevated risk premia on European assets given the risk that markets price a higher chance that progress on European integration comes to a halt.

June FOMC reaction: We turned neutral duration after the FOMC, given the Fed's conservative and patent approach. The dot plot wasn't fully updated after CPI and may already be stale for markets. We think the FOMC is looking at the establishment survey like markets and is not considering residual seasonality as a possibility behind the inflation trajectory.

Four reasons to stay tactically neutral on duration: 1) Retail sales could look healthy, 2) Yields are at the low end of their recent range, 3) Summer seasonality/ carry can lift yields higher, especially because the Fed is in wait-and-watch mode, and carry for being short has increased, and 4) With the first 2024 Presidential debate 2 weeks away, term premium expansion might be on the market's mind. We closed our long 3m10y receivers after the June FOMC meeting, and suggest receiving July FOMC OIS as an attractive risk-reward (3bp spent for 22bp payout).

## Euro area

Given the recent political developments in France, we reviewed the recent market moves and tried to project different ranges on the 10y OAT/Bund spread depending of the pricing of political risk-premium, which we roughly estimated to be currently worth between 40%-45%. Across scenarios we get an expected value on the 10y OAT/Bund spread in \~70bp area. Additionally, we review the composition of debt holders for OATs, noting that the high share of non-resident holdings could create selling pressure in the event of fiscally looser policies.

## United Kingdom

The June MPC meeting will be the main focus of the upcoming week and we expect the BoE to keep rates unchanged while laying the ground for an August cut. When it comes to price action, the May Inflation Report, ahead of the MPC, will matter the most in a time where data dependence is everything.

Service inflation is the key reading to watch and how close or far it will come in versus the BoE's last projections will determine the price action. Our economist expects service inflation to come in at \~5.4%, which should support front-end duration valuations and higher easing expectations. We see risks broadly balanced but still cheap valuations, and thus we maintain our maintain a SFIU4/U5 flattener ahead of the next key week.

On QT, we expect active sales to be further skewed towards more shorts and mediums, and less longs in the upcoming quarter; however, the market impact on longs' valuations should be muted as the delta versus the current pace is modest.

## Japan

We expect the combination of resilient USD/JPY and lower UST yield to drive further a flattening of the JPY OIS curve. Meanwhile, we expect JGBs to continue to trade with relatively high volatility amid uncertainty around the future QT schedule. While the BoJ announced it will release a detailed QT plan for the next 1- 2 years at the July policy meeting, uncertainty remains around the pace of QT, as Governor Ueda hinted that the reduction of JGB purchases will be "substantial."

Moreover, Ueda said that a rate hike at the same time as QT would be possible if underlying inflation develops in line with the BoJ's outlook. The market uncertainty around the QT size and rate hike may keep domestic investors on a wait-and-see mode regarding JGBs purchases.

We see the market continuing to price in a reasonable chance of a July hike as well as a relatively large reduction in JGB purchases on less than 10y tenor. We therefore shift our long 10y JGB ASW outright to a 5s10s ASW box flattener. We also shift our long 20y JGBs on 10s20s30s fly to a long 20y JGBs on 5s20s30s fly. We maintain our pay 10y on 2s10s20s fly.

Said another way, we do not think duration at current levels is cheap. We suggest being tactically neutral on duration. However, July FOMC pricing at 3bp is a cheap option given that economic data are slowing, and we suggest receiving the July FOMC OIS (at 5.30%) as an attractive risk-reward play.

## Overall, we see four reasons to be neutral currently:

1. Data dependence: Near-term economic data could come in healthy - especially retail sales, as projected by our economists

2. Optical levels: Yields have moved to the low end of their recent range

3. Summer seasonality and carry considerations can lift yields higher, especially because 1) the Fed is in a conservative wait-and-watch mode (see below), and 2) Carry available for being short has increased after recent rally in Treasuries

4. Election risks in the vicinity as the first 2024 Presidential debate is about 2 weeks away – possibly drawing focus on the fiscal picture

The key risk to receive July OIS is that the Fed does not deliver a cut in which the contract loses 3bp.

## BoE Preview: Balanced Risks Amid Cheap Valuations

After the beat in the April inflation print (the services reading came in at \~5.9%Y versus the BoE's forecast of \~5.5%Y), near-term easing expectations have been priced out substantially ( Exhibit 34 ), and have remained anchored to current levels since then. We think that a data-dependent approach and the lack of commitment to a specific rate path from the BoE are among the main reasons explaining current valuations.

In our view, the most recent price action also confirms these considerations, as the UK strip only outperformed the Euribor one marginally, with easing expectations just a touch higher after the last (fairly soft) labour market print. Meanwhile, a significant miss in US CPI has helped terminal rates expectations to reprice significantly lower as the correlation with the Fed's expectations stays close to multi-year highs ( Exhibit 35 ).

Looking ahead, focus should turn to the May Inflation Report, ahead of the June MPC, which remains the most price-driven data release, given the BoE's focus on the services reading. The market reaction will likely depend on how far or close the services reading comes from the last BoE forecast at \~5.25%Y, as this might support more or less commitment towards the start of the easing cycle. According to our economist, a services reading in the 5.4/5.6% range should support an August cut and, in such as scenario, easing expectations should be repriced significantly higher. Conversely, a print at 5.8%Y+ might lead to a hawkish change in the forward guidance with respect to risks of inflation persistence receding.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Definition of terms

Buy/Long: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be positive over the relevant time period.

Sell/Short: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be negative over the relevant time period.

Selling protection or Buying Risk: The analyst expects that the price of protection against the event occurring will decrease over the relevant time period.

Buying protection or Selling Risk: The analyst expects the price of protection against the event occurring will increase over the relevant time period.

Pay: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will increase.

Receive: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will decrease.

Unless otherwise specified, the time frame for recommendations included in the Morgan Stanley Fixed Income Research reports is 1 - 3 months and the price of financial instruments mentioned in the recommendation is as at the date and time of publication of the recommendation.
