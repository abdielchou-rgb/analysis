## January 24

JGB sell-off deepens, focus on BoJ normalization; Strong US PMIs start UST sell-off; Solid UK PMIs lower implied BoE cuts; USD/CNH ends little changed, despite PBoC RRR cut; BoC's Macklem says cuts are premature; CLP gains amid FinMin sales; DXY at 103.25 (-0.4%); US 10y at 4.176% (+4.8bp).

• JGB sell-off deepens as market participants continue to position for a sharper BoJ normalization cycle, while focus in the long-end turns to the looming 40y JGB auction.

• Surprisingly-strong preliminary January S&P Global US PMIs pushes US Treasuries into the red until a large (2.0bp) tail in the \$61bn 5y UST auction catalyzes a renewed sell-off in US duration.

• Solid preliminary January S&P Global UK PMIs bear-flattens the gilt curve as the market-implied BoE Bank Rate path inches higher; our economists no longer forecast a 1Q24 UK growth contraction.

• USD/CNH (-0.1%) struggles to sustain an initial move lower after the PBoC announces a surprise 50bp RRR cut, while local equities (Hang Seng: +3.56%) rally markedly.

• BoC Governor Macklem says the Bank needs “to give these higher rates time to do their work,” and reiterated it is “premature” to discuss rate cuts.

• CLP (+0.4%) advances as the Chilean FinMin sells another USD 150mn (USD 1.05bn since Thursday).

Elsewhere in Asia, the PBoC delivered a surprise reduction of 50bp to the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) with additional 25bp targeted cuts on relending and rediscount rates. Alongside this announcement, USD/CNH (-0.1%) retreated from session highs and moved lower for the session. A blend of lower services PMIs across the euro area and strong European corporate earnings provided for gains in both European government bonds and European equities (SX5E: +2.20%); the Euro Stoxx 50 closed at its highest level since 2001. Preliminary January services PMIs fell in France to 45.0 (C: 46.0; P: 45.7), Germany, to 47.6 (C: 49.3; P: 49.3), and for the euro area, to 48.4 (C: 49.0; P: 48.8). Even with a mild richening in EGBs, EUR (+0.3%) quickly reversed the prior session’s losses against USD and advanced to the 1.090 level.

Our economists now look for 1Q24 UK GDP growth of 0.1% q/q, from a mild contraction previously. A slight bear-flattening of the UK gilt curve transpired as market participants repriced the implied BoE Bank Rate path slightly higher. Our economists maintain their call for the first cut in May, whereas market pricing implies a higher probability (90%) for the first cut to be delivered at the June BoE meeting.

US Treasuries had been resilient to the sell-off in JGBs overnight and entered the NY session richer across the curve – with outperformance in the 5y sector. The bullish trend to UST price action ended abruptly alongside the 9:45am ET release of the preliminary January S&P Global US PMIs. Both the manufacturing and services PMI were better than expected, with manufacturing now back in expansion territory. Given this unexpectedly strong growth data, USTs pushed lower through Tuesday’s closing levels.

At the same time, the Bank of Canada released the rates decision from its January monetary policy meeting. As widely expected, the Bank left its policy rate at 5.00% (C: 5.00%) but lowered its growth forecasts for 2023, 2024, and 2025 in its Monetary Policy Report (MPR). Acknowledgement of slowing economic activity supported the front end of the Canadian bond curve, with additional hikes now seemingly off the table. As 2y US-Canada yields differentials moved in the US’s favor USD/CAD (+0.5%) gained, amid the broader CAD weakness.

The next leg of the UST sell-off transpired in the NY afternoon alongside a notably weak \$61bn 5y UST auction. The 5y auction stopped-out at 4.055%, a 2.0bp tail, the largest tail since September 2022. Auction internals confirmed the poor result as a large drop in the allotment to indirect bidders to 60.9% (P: 70.6%) more than offset the rise in the allotment to direct bidders to 18.7% (P: 15.4%), which brought the allotment to primary dealers to 20.4% (P: 14.0%) – its highest since June 2022.

Despite the parallel \~5bp rise in yields across the UST curve, the US dollar (DXY

USD Index: -0.4%) finished generally weaker against its G10 peers. Despite considerations about policies which may arise from another Trump presidency (following New Hampshire primary results), MXN (+0.5%) advanced against the US dollar.

Lifted by a few milestones in Tech equities (Nasdaq 100: +0.55%), the S&P 500 (+0.08%) notched its fifth consecutive gain, while more cyclical pockets of the market (Russell 2000: -0.73%) notably lagged.

## Japan:

JGBs initially sold off, led by the belly, as market participants continued to position for a sharper BoJ normalization cycle, after the hawkish press conference on Tuesday. Long-end JGBs were relatively well supported by dip-buying demand from real money participants. However, outcomes of the JGB purchase operations (1-3y, 3- 5y, 5-10y, and 10-25y) were particularly weak in the long end. Long-end weakness eventually caught up with the weakness in the belly, and 30y JGBs sold off 12bp d/d at one point in the Tokyo session. However, appetite to buy the dip appeared near the 3:00pm Tokyo close and 30y JGBs ended the Tokyo session +7.5bp d/d at 1.805% on a simple yield basis, with 10y JGBs +8bp d/d, to 0.715%. 40y JGBs lagged ahead of the 40y JGB auction and sold off by 9bp d/d on a simple yield basis. We believe the 40y auction will be met with reasonable demand from real money market participants but may not be enough to digest the JPY600bn issuance. TONA OIS bear-steepened alongside JGBs. XCCY basis tightened, led by the long end.

Despite the hawkish BoJ outcome, USD/JPY recovered above 148.50 on Tuesday alongside higher UST yields. During the Tokyo session on Wednesday, USD/JPY returned to 147.50, as TY futures rallied. It appeared the impact of the BoJ’s hawkishness was short-lived, and Fed pricing is seemingly the main driver of this pair. Other CCY/JPY pairs also traded lower; EUR/JPY broke below 160.50, before UK PMIs came in stronger than expected.

## Europe:

It was an eventful trading session in London hours for G10 currencies. The US dollar started on the back foot as 10y UST yields fell by a few basis points. The US dollar sell-off was then exacerbated, particularly against EUR and other European currencies as PMIs were released. Despite mixed headline European PMIs, with a slight miss relative to consensus expectations and an increase relative to December, improvements to manufacturing and stickiness in services prices paid translated to EUR strength.

The UK followed with even stronger PMIs data, which boosted GBP/USD with upside surprises in both manufacturing and services. Finally, an upside surprise in US PMIs led to a rise in 10y UST yields and USD strength, which pared what had until then been a weak session for the US dollar. The DXY USD Index still closed the London session 0.4% lower, with outperformance in CHF (+0.8%), JPY (+0.6%), SEK (+0.5%), and NOK (+0.4%). Their outperformance was seemingly driven by the US dollar, given a lack of local catalysts.

European duration rallied in the London morning, after soft PMIs in France and Germany; 10y Bund yields fell to \~2.29%. The euro area figure was close to

consensus expectations, due to a strong rebound in the rest of the euro area (which comprises \~42% of the EA composite PMI, according to our economists’ estimates). Relatively strong PMIs in the UK and US spurred a subsequent sell-off. Overall, 10y Bunds closed at 2.34% (-1bp d./d). The front end was little changed ahead of the ECB on Thursday as curves bull-flattened; in swaps (against 6m), 2s10s flattened by 0.5bp and 10s30s steepened by 1bp. Peripheral spreads initially tightened and widened afterwards (10y BTP-Bund spread: -0.5bp). Core ASW traded sideways and closed mixed, with Schatz ASW \~unchanged, while the rest of the complex cheapened \~0.5bp, which flattened the boxes.

## Dollar Bloc:

The London session ended with CAD the only G10 currency weaker against the US dollar, after the BoC kept its policy rate unchanged and revised its key forecasts (inflation and growth) slightly downward.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies depreciated against USD. IDR and KRW underperformed, both down 0.6%. MYR sustained losses, down 0.2%, to 4.74, after BNM left its policy rate unchanged. It received limited support from the China stimulus measures, unlike SGD and CNH. Both currencies gained 0.1-0.2% after the PBoC announced a 50bp RRR cut. AxJ rates were mixed; Korea, Indonesia, and Malaysia 10y yields rose \~2-3bp, while Singapore, Thailand, and China yields fell by \~2-3bp. In China, the NDIRS curve shifted lower by 3bp.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session stronger driven by risk-on sentiment. COP (+1.3%) outperformed peers and retraced most of its underperformance from Tuesday. MXN (+0.5%) and BRL (+0.4%) strengthened amid few local drivers, while CLP (+0.4%) was supported an additional USD 150mn in FinMin sales (USD 1.05bn sold since Thursday). LatAm rates generally rallied; DIs ended 2-3bp lower. CAM (1y: -5) and IBR (1y: -6bp) had gains concentrated in the front-end, in line with a move in front-end USTs for most of the session. After Mexico 1H January/2H December Core CPI came in at 0.25% (C: 0.25%; P: -0.01%), TIIEs ended the session with losses in the front-end (+3bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The Bank of Canada held its policy rate unchanged at 5.00% (C: 5.00%) at its January meeting. The Bank gave a nod to recent weakness in economic data but cautioned it is still concerned about "risks to the outlook for inflation, particularly the persistence in underlying inflation." The Bank noted developments in core inflation remain key. This sentiment was further echoed by BoC Governor Macklem, who stated economic demand is no longer outpacing supply, but inflation remains too high as inflationary pressures persist. Macklem said the Bank needs “to give these higher rates time to do their work,” and reiterated it is “premature” to discuss rate cuts. The January Monetary Policy Report (MPR) revealed growth was marginally lowered in 2023, 2024, and 2025. Inflation for 2024 was revised downward, but the Bank maintained its expectation for inflation to return to the 2.0% target in 2025. Despite acknowledgement to slowing economic activity, developments in core inflation remain key – especially in shelter inflation. Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. ## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
