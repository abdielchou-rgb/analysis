# Correction: Shifting preference with changing capital market dynamics

While it's too early to call the bottom for share prices in the broker industry, we highlight CITICS and East Money as likely be more resilient, and their franchise strengths should enable them to lead peers once ROE rebounds.

We factor in sharply lower IPO volume in 2024 but see normalization in 2025 together with a modest ADT (average daily turnover) rebound. As discussed in our China Brokers Insight report, we think austerity measures in capital markets since 2H23 will continue to weigh on multiple businesses for brokers in 2024, while progress made in rules setting could help accelerate normalization in the regulatory environment. As such, we cut our IPO forecasts and now expect Rmb110bn IPOs in 2024, -68% yoy, followed by Rmb220bn in 2025 and Rmb350bn in 2026. We see a 5% decline of ADT in 2024 and 6.8% growth in 2025 and 2026. We think investors should be positioned for potential beneficiaries given the over-arching shift from fund raising to improving shareholder returns, and focus on players that can maintain franchise strength and accelerate market share gains when the cycle turns.

We upgrade East Money (300059.SZ) from EW to OW, as we think the internetbased retail broker will be among the first to benefit from gradually recovering retail investor confidence and trading activity, which should lead the recovery of other capital market activities, such as funds raising. This will also allow it to accelerate share gains in brokerage, margin and mutual fund distribution by converting its large user base. Its notably narrowed valuation premium to traditional brokers offers a more reasonable entry point, in our view.

We upgrade CITICS-H (6030.HK) from EW to OW. We like CITICS' balanced business mix which has already helped it deliver much lower earnings volatility and a more stable cost income ratio in the past several years, which should ensure a consistent client-centric strategy. We think its franchise strength should continue to help it consolidate share in underwriting, M&A and other capital market services, and lead peers in ROE recovery to 8.1% in 2025 ­– with the potential to return to double digits ROE if market conditions improve more meaningfully.

We downgrade CICC-H (3908.HK) from OW to EW. Shifting IPO and regulatory dynamics could continue to weigh on CICC's investment banking, and equities & derivatives businesses. Its elevated cost income ratio implies more imminent pressure to rein in the cost base, but it could be challenging for the firm to maintain market-relative incentives to top talent at the same time. We think these factors combined will add to some uncertainty on the firm's ROE in the next couple of years and thus we stay on the sidelines for now.

## Cutting IPO and ADT forecasts while expecting a modest ADT rebound from 2025

We cut market ADT by 7% for 2024, now expecting Rmb829bn vs. Rmb858bn in 1H24. This implies trading velocity will moderate from 315% in 2023 to \~290% in 2024, as the high velocity program trading which was 30% of A share volume in 2023, will be met with higher scrutiny as well as higher trading costs. We expect a modest rebound in ADT, by mid single digits, in 2025 and 2026 driven by recovering retail investor sentiment.

With only Rmb410bn in the IPO pipeline as of July 2024, we expect IPO volume to fall 68% to around Rmb110bn in 2024 driven by increased focus on quality rather than quantity. This should drive up to 40% yoy decline in investment banking fees in 2024 for the major brokers we cover. While we forecast a rebound in IPO turnover to Rmb220bn in 2025 and Rmb350bn in 2026 (similar to 2023's level), it will depend on the corporate earnings cycle and to what extent policy makers will allow a more diverse IPO mix.

We also expect tightened regulation on derivatives to result in a notable slowdown in leading brokers' balance sheet-driven growth. On the other hand, while falling bond yields since 2H23 have supported bond trading income, we believe the shifting monetary policy from quantitative to a rates based system and PBOC's clear preference for higher long-term rates suggest further upside in bond trading gains, as in the past 12 months, might be limited. Therefore, we expect brokers to generate largely flat investment income for 2025e and 2026e. On the other hand, lower funding costs from slower balance sheet expansion will likely support net interest income.

## Updating forecasts and price targets for A share brokers

On average, we cut revenue forecasts for China brokers by 17% for 2024 and 21% for 2025, in view of short term tightening in multiple business lines. We now expect 9% revenue decline on average in 2024, but an 8% and 11.7% rebound in 2025e and 2026e, respectively. We expect CITICS and East Money to post more modest revenue declines, by low single digits, in 2024 while also leading peers in a rebound in the next couple of years.

We expect CITICS, HTSC and East Money to report more stable brokerage income helped by their stronger client franchises, which support stable or expanding market share. We expect Galaxy to post weaker brokerage income due to market share losses. We think GFS and CMS will likely see more pressure from lower mutual fund trading commissions due to the new rules capping fund commissions to a single broker to 15% – which will impact brokerage revenue.

We cut investment banking fees by 30-50% for major underwriters such as CITICS, CICC, HTSC, and Haitong, for 2024 and 2025 as a result of the expected sharp decline in IPO volumes. We think investment banking fees should start to rebound from a low base in 2025 as we expect IPO volumes to gradually recover in 2H25 with improving industrial firm margins and potentially a more diverse IPO mix.

## Upgrade CITICS-H from EW to OW: stable franchise to ride through cycles

CITICS' edge is not only in its size, being the largest broker in China with a >Rmb1.4tn balance sheet, but also its balanced mix in business lines across retail and institutional brokerage, investment banking, equities and derivatives, FICC, and asset management. Revenue contributions are balanced across business lines where CITICS holds top market share in each: It has #2 market share in brokerage measured by trading volume, #1 share in margin finance, and #1 share in domestic equities and bond underwriting, while its asset management arm (not including China AMC, the mutual fund subsidiary) has >Rmb1.3tn AUM in 2023, the largest among all brokers. The balanced business setup helped CITICS to deliver a resilient earnings trend, in particular much lower quarter-to-quarter volatility than peers, that has been tested during the down cycle since 2022.

More importantly, we believe CITICS' investment banking franchise has the winning ingredients needed in new capital market dynamics, which we discussed earlier: a comprehensive set of offerings leveraging off its diverse business mix, a large PE/VC investment arm, and large potential to collaborate with CITIC Bank or the CITIC Group of companies as well as an established HK franchise (CLSA). The combination of these strengths should allow CITICS to accelerate market share gains in investment banking and other businesses across both the retail and institutional side.

The firm also handled cost controls well with its cost income ratio managed within a tight range of 49%-54% in the past four years across up and down cycles, compared to wider swings of over 10 percentage points that were seen in many other peers. This shows CITICS' prudent operational management which could improve the consistency of strategies and avoid sharp turns that could hurt its competitiveness.

Despite similar challenges as faced by other brokers in 2024, we think CITICS' competitive strength in the above areas will support its head-of-peers ROE recovery to 8.1% in 2025 with potential to return to double digits ROE if market conditions improve more meaningfully. With a new price target of HK\$14.1, we upgrade CITICS-H to OW from EW.

## Upgrade East Money from EW to OW: much more attractive entry point given market share gain potential

East Money's core offering is a comprehensive digital information platform combined with low cost and convenient trading in stocks or mutual funds. The firm is a direct beneficiary of higher retail investor confidence and trading activity. We think East Money's capability to accelerate market share gains in brokerage, margin and mutual funds are intact despite a much slower pace during market downturns in the past two years. The capability stems from its large and trading-focused user base and a scalable business model by converting this traffic. Notably, its main East Money App has maintained an upward trend of MAU in the past two years, reaching 22.6mn in June 2024, up 17% compared to Dec-2021, after which market volatility rose notably. The MAU rose >180% when compared to Dec 2019, showing the high stickiness of East Money's user base once they start to use the company's offerings.

Given the fundamental shift of capital market priority from fund raising to balance shareholder returns, along with many policy efforts and rule-building to improve listed firm quality, protecting smaller shareholders and punishing fraud, we think it will translate into improving retail investor confidence over time and drive higher retail engagement. East Money should benefit directly from this trend as it could accelerate the conversion of users to brokerage clients, while existing clients could trade more actively and contribute to fee income upfront. Notably, we see more room for its market share in margin finance, at 2.8% in 2023, to catch up to its brokerage market share at \~4% in 2023. Better retail sentiment will help accelerate this pace, which will improve the ROE of its capital as it will be redeployed from fixed income investments to margin finance.

On the other hand, we think the concerns on mutual fund fee rate cuts, which started in 4Q23, are partly reflected in earnings and the expectations are well priced in. While current market debate is on potential further cuts to trail commission by end-2024 – which could impact East Money's mutual fund revenue in addition to previous management fee rate cuts – our analysis above suggests the trail commission rate, as a mechanism to balance wealth managers and fund managers, should be left to the market to decide. Therefore we are more positive that East Money can largely maintain the margin on its mutual fund business, given its strong bargaining power and a large AUM base.

While we are baking in a modest \~7% growth in market ADT in 2025 and 2026, we expect East Money's brokerage revenue to post double digit growth assuming 20-30bps market share gains each year. We think market share gains in margin finance and reduced interest expense to finance bond trading, should drive >30% growth in interest income for 2025 and 2026 which should be a main support to earnings. With a low cost base, we expect earnings to return to \~18% growth on average for 2025 and 2026, following modest decline of 2% in 2024. If market conditions improve more meaningfully, we think East Money, as a retail broker, will see its earnings also benefiting earlier than other traditional brokers, as trading volume and margin demand, which directly represent investor risk appetite, should lead the recovery of other activities such as IPOs.

Since May 2022, East Money's forward P/E premium to major A share brokers has narrowed from \~13x to only 3.7x, by July 2024, reflecting slower market share gains, bigger-than-expected cyclical pressure which resulted in weaker earnings trend. See our note here. We think East Money still deserves a premium to traditional brokers due to its scalable business model and market share gains potential. In particular, East Money's cost income ratio at \~30% is structurally lower than traditional brokers at well above 50%, due to its scalable business model. With notably narrowed premium to traditional brokers, we think the current valuation is a more attractive entry point. We upgrade the stock to OW from EW with its target price unchanged at Rmb14.0.

Our valuation methodology for East Money remains PEG-based with weighted average of base (60%) bull (20%) and bear (20%) case values, unchanged from before. We see 30.6%, 16.9% and 11.4% EPS CAGR for 2024, 2025 and 2026 under bull, base and bear scenarios, respectively, which corresponds to 1.8x, 1.2x, 1.0x PEG ratios, respectively, unchanged from before.

## Downgrade CICC from OW to EW: still uncertainty in its future ROE path

CICC felt notable impact from the capital market adjustment: Investment banking income dropped 47% in 2023 with a notable cool down and regulatory tightening in the IPO market. While CICC specializes in orchestrating mega deals, the fragile market environment has pushed back many large deals which weighed on CICC's revenue more than others. The rising scrutiny on equity trading, short sell, quant and derivatives has also impacted CICC's ability to expand business in these areas where it has a leading edge.

We think another challenge is how to achieve cost optimization while maintaining marketoriented incentives to its professionals. Notably, CICC's cost income ratio rose over 10 percentage points from 58.9% in 2020 to 69.2% in 2023, the second-highest within our coverage (Haitong is the highest at 80% but with a more serious revenue issue), with headcount rising 62% during the same period, the fastest within our coverage. This could lead to more pressure than peers to optimize the cost base and also more difficulties given the notably expanded team, while revenue uncertainty in areas such as investment banking and equity trading will likely linger for a while. It could be even more challenging to maintain market-relative incentives to top talent at the same time, which is crucial for

the firm's long term competitive strength.

While the firm's ROE has slipped to 6.4% in 2023 from 12% in 2021, we still see some uncertainties on both the revenue and cost front in the near term which might add to the challenges for CICC to restore ROE to an industry-leading level as before. As such, we downgrade CICC to EW from OW with a new target price of HK\$9.30. On the positive side, we expect a gradual recovery in HK's IPOs and trading volumes should lend more help to CICC than to peers.

# Cutting estimates and price target for NOAH while we see upside remaining with potentially increasing shareholder returns

In light of capital market pressure in the short term, we also cut earnings forecasts for Noah as one of its key drivers is the distribution of capital market-related products. Market volatility and weak sentiment in 2024 could continue to impact demand for high margin products such as private funds. Separately, we think insurance brokerage commission rate cut could impact Noah's insurance revenue, which has been a main support to revenue in 2023.

On the other hand, we think Noah's footprint restructure in Mainland China could also lead to near term volatility in operating trends. To focus resources in bigger cities, the firm has reduced its presence to 16 cities in Mainland China by mid-2024, while the number of relationship managers (RMs) was reduced to 1109 in 1Q23 from 1252 in 4Q23. Another reason for the restructure could be increasing compliance needs, in our view, given stricter regulatory oversight on the distribution of private funds, mutual funds and insurance products. We think the firm is trying to reorganize the RM team to make sure they serve the clients while being properly licensed. This should reduce long term regulatory risk but could lead to some turnover in the short term.

We expect the restructure and smaller RM team could lead to some cost savings potential, but it's likely to come with a lag. At the same time, the firm will likely continue to invest for its overseas franchise in HK and Singapore and continue to expand RM team there. Therefore we expect opex to be sticky in 2024 which will weigh on earnings.

Overall, we are baking in 1.5% decline in client AUA (assets under advisory) in 2024 as domestic restructure and exit in some cities could negatively impact client assets stability, while inflows to products such as private funds should be quite muted in the near term. We cut revenue by 21%, 27%, 28% for 2024e, 2025e and 2026e, respectively, mainly led by lower distribution related one-time commissions and AUA related recurring service fees. We reduce profit forecasts by around 40% from 2024e to 2026e and see Noah's earnings dropping 33% in 2024, while returning to mid-teens growth in 2025 and 2026.
