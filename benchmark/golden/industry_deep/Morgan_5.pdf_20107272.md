# Japan: Tokyo CPI report showed inflation stickiness

The March Tokyo CPI report showed the stickiness of Japan<sup>’</sup>s inflation. The global standard measure of core prices rose 0.2%m/m sa in March, leaving the oya inflation rate at 2.3%. The core (ex. fresh food) measure, the de-facto headline, also rose 0.2%m/m sa, to 2.4%oya, reflecting the rise in underlying electricity and gas prices. Although these were not very strong accelerations compared to the last year, broader momentum in underlying prices remains solid. Global core inflation was up 2.4%, 3m/3m saar, an acceleration over its average 2.0% pace in the first two months of the year.

Although inflation is showing stickiness, as we have long expected, we also see some moderation in the pace of cost pass-through in recent months. Reflecting weak domestic demand and limited additional cost pressures from abroad, prices for those items that had risen sharply over the past two years, such as core goods and dining out, have become flat since the beginning of the year. Services prices were firm, with a 0.1%m/m sa rise, leaving services price inflation up 2.0%oya, but this was largely attributable to strong momentum in culture and recreation fees. Indeed, supported by the rise in inbound tourists, culture and recreation fees have been on a rising trend since late 2022, and their oya inflation has been running at over 8% since November 2023.

We think the moderation of price momentum since the beginning of the year is just temporary. In addition to the rise in electricity prices in February, the government<sup>’</sup>s energy subsidies will be terminated by midyear. And the pass-through of higher wage costs to inflation is still only halfway through. We expect inflation to reaccelerate after April, when firms will be making price changes at the start of the fiscal year.
