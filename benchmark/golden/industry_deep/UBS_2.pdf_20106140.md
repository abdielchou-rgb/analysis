## Around Emerging Markets in 10 minutes

## UBS’ strongest views across the 20 biggest EM economies

In this note we synthesise UBS's macro, FX, equity, rates and credit strategy views on the 23 largest EM economies. We outline the core themes per economy, highlight our preferred trades, and identify key signposts and dates to monitor over the next 6m. See page 3 for our latest trade recommendations and pages 5-7 for 10 key charts.

## 3 key themes for EM investors in H1'24

1) Fed easing and EM: myth vs. reality. With insurance cuts well priced, EM risk premia highly compressed, and RRP drawdown likely nearing exhaustion, we see Fed easing offering EM limited help from here. 2) China weakness is priced into local equities, but little else. Peaking property completions, infrastructure investment and credit growth point to muted spillovers from expected GDP stabilisation this year. 3) Ongoing EM disinflation to support rates, stabilise equities, hinder FX.

## Weakening FX fundamentals, but consider tactical longs closer to March FOMC

We remain cautious on EM FX and are focussed on idiosyncratic opportunities. We see three main headwinds for the asset class. 1) Among key 'high-yielders', carry has fallen into the bottom quartile of its post-GFC range (from the 67th percentile a year ago). This carry erosion will likely make it harder for EM FX to look through persistent global trade weakness. 2) EM FX risk premia remains at very low levels, historically consistent with negative total returns over the next 3-6m. 3) Current account improvements in most of EM will now likely stall as domestic demand recovery intersects with flattening China imports. As such, while we would consider tactical longs in EM FX closer to the March FOMC meeting (only 12bp priced), we aren't confident this potential boost will sustain. We continue to see CLP, COP, CZK, HUF and CNH offering weakest risk/ reward.

## Equities: Strong EPS recovery in train; favour Korea/Taiwan on tech upcycle

We see a strong earnings recovery (13.2% CAGR for 2024-25E, spearheaded by the recovery in Korea and Taiwan tech) and easing consumer headwinds limiting downside in EM equities. We maintain our YE target of 1020 on MSCI EM. Broadening demand recovery, supply discipline and inventory digestion should support the tech upcycle. Themes driving our market selections include 1) Domestic strength (prefer markets with higher EPS sensitivity to resilient domestic GDP), 2) EM tech upcycle, 3) Falling real yields (prefer markets where returns have higher sensitivity to falling real yields), 4) ability to absorb potentially higher oil prices. We remain OW Korea and Taiwan (Tech upcycle), and inexpensive markets with bottom-up idiosyncratic stories (such as Brazil and Malaysia).

## FI: Ageing rockstar? The opportunity set may be narrowing after a strong run

Local debt has been EM's rockstar, outperforming the US both in a stabilising UST yield environment last year and in a rising UST yield environment so far YTD. This remains our favoured asset class for '24 amid our expectations for below-consensus US and EM inflation, underpinned by core goods normalisation, sub 3% y/y M1 growth in half of major EM countries, and limited China stimulus. However valuations are increasingly demanding after the strong run since Nov and risk/reward in a few markets has turned outright negative. We continue to target 5-7% GBI & 3-5% EMBI returns into YE. Front end Latam local debt remains our preferred exposure.

While China equity valuations look depressed, the same cannot be said for Chinese FX, where our valuation models point to \~6% overvaluation. 2 key drivers of this rich valuation are a) China's moderating share of global exports and b) a weakening basic balance, which could slip further this year on recovering tourism flows and ongoing reorientation of global supply chains. In the most bullish equity phases in China, foreign equity flows to China amounted to no more than 0.6%/GDP, which is roughly how much we see the current account slipping this year (0.4%/GDP). Fade rallies in CNY, long China internet as a right-tail hedge.

China's weak property market had limited spillovers to the rest of the world in 2023 as import volumes held up remarkably well. Commodities such as iron ore & coal saw abovetrend import volumes in '23. We see downside risks from here as credit growth, infrastructure investment and property completions likely moderate. This should present a higher bar for EM commodity currencies (e.g. ZAR, IDR, CLP) and credit spreads this year.

EM risk appetite remains at levels very close to the highs seen in early '23 around China reopening optimism. Key laggards have been China equities and still-high US rates volatility, but this has been balanced by robust performance in EM ex China equities, EM local debt, and subdued vol in EM FX and US equities. This relatively high level of risk appetite, which was only surpassed in phases of very strong global growth (2017 and late 2020), will likely limit the upside from Fed easing this year.

EM currencies held up strongly through '23 relative to their historical relationship with global trade growth. This likely reflected the strong rush for carry trades, which remain popular today, amid strong global volatility compression. These carry buffers are now thinning, however, and EM implied volatility has converged to very low levels. Continued weakness in global trade may drive a low intensity selloff in the next 3-6m, we think.

Despite expectations for strong Fed easing this year (150bp cuts priced at the time of writing), carry among EM's core high yielder currencies is back into the bottom quartile of its post-GFC range, a level last seen in mid-21. Carry is falling most rapidly over the last 3m in HUF, CLP and COP. Lower yielders e.g. THB, ILS have, by contrast, generally seen carry improve.

Amid improving inflation and limited labour market pain thus far, consumer confidence appears to be improving at the margin across most EM economies. This should help underpin an earnings-led recovery in EM equities through the year (we forecast MSCI EM at 1020). It may be much less helpful for EM FX if it is a) being driven by dovish EM central banks that erode EM-DM rate differentials and b) it interrupts the trend of improving current account balances that most of EM enjoyed in 2023.

EM local debt, FX neutral, has been our preferred asset class. We remain positive, seeing 5-7% GBI EM total returns into year end, but value appears to be becoming increasingly narrowly concentrated after the strong run in Q4. Our economists are now only materially below market pricing in Colombia, Brazil, Mexico and Korea in EM. We'd look to add exposure to this asset class at better levels but at the margin are now less bullishly positioned than we were 3m ago.
