# Global Macro Commentary | Global

# February 29

BoJ's Takata says 2% target is within sight; JPY exhibits broad strength against peers; UST rally commences amid continuing claims uptick; European rates end richer; CLP advances on strong retail sales; MYR gains on intervention comments; DXY at 104.13 (+0.2%); US 10y at 4.250% (-1.4bp).

• BoJ Board Member Takata believes the 2% price stability target is finally within sight and the BoJ needs to consider flexible and nimble steps for withdrawal of ultra easing measures.

• JPY exhibits broad strength against all G10 peers. USD/JPY slides as low as 149.20, before month-end-related demand supports USD with the pair closing 0.5% lower, at 149.98.

• US duration losses cease alongside in-line PCE inflation data and a rise in continuing claims to 1905k (C: 1875k), the highest since mid-November; month-end activity keeps the long end supported.

• Despite strength across several national European CPI readings, European duration turns richer, led by the long-end, alongside US Treasuries.

• CLP (+1.1%) leads LatAm currencies after Chilean retail sales are substantially above consensus expectations at 2.2% y/y in January (C: -1.5%; P: -1.6%).

• MYR (+0.6%) gains against USD amid comments from the Malaysian Second Finance Minister, who says the government is committed to defending and strengthening MYR.

## in 2023.

In the London morning session, most national European CPI data came in slightly above consensus but largely in line with our economists’ forecasts for resilient euro area core and services inflation. European duration sold off and curves bearflattened. 10y Bunds sold off over 5bp in the London morning, through the 2.50% level. Focus then turned to the US, where headline and core PCE inflation data for January came in line with consensus expectations but a downward revision to December spurred a rally in the US, which spilled over into Europe (10y Bunds: 2.41%, -4.8bp). Curves continued to flatten in the rally and EUR 2s10s and 10s30s closed 5bp and 1bp flatter, respectively. Nominals underperformed real yields amid the inflation data across Europe. In money markets, whites and reds were little changed while greens rallied by \~4bp. Core ASW cheapened, apart from Schatz ASW, which richened alongside repo into month-end.

## Dollar Bloc:

Most G10 currencies weakened against the US dollar; the DXY USD Index rose 0.2%. JPY was the only G10 currency that strengthened against the US dollar as USD/JPY (-0.5%) dipped below 150. JPY gains came in anticipation of policy normalization, after BoJ Board Member Takata said the BoJ’s price target was “coming into sight.” USD/CAD ended the NY session unchanged. CAD retraced intra-session gains after an upside surprise in 4Q23 Canadian GDP, although final domestic demand remained subdued.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies rallied broadly ahead of the January US PCE data release. CNH, INR, TWD, and PHP relatively underperformed and exhibited stability. China’s Politburo maintained an unchanged tone at its monthly meeting. IDR was the main underperformer, down 0.2%, to 15,719, amid the sell-off in core rates. MYR led the gains, up 0.6%, to 4.74 as the Malaysia Second Finance Minister said the government is committed to defending and strengthening MYR. THB and KRW gained 0.2% amid USD weakness, even though KOSPI (-0.4%) underperformed.

AxJ rates displayed mixed performance. Korea rates underperformed. The NDIRS curve shifted 3bp higher after the Deputy Finance Minister said Korean February inflation may exceed 3%. In India, the NDOIS curve shifted 1bp higher while the 10y G-sec yield fell 1bp ahead of the 4Q23 GDP release. In China, the 30y CGB yield continued its downtrend, 3bp lower, to 2.46%, even as the equity market outperformed (CSI 300: 1.9%). Yet, the 5y CNY NDIRS yield rose 1bp. Hong Kong and Singapore rates also outperformed following the overnight move in USTs, as 1y swap yields fell 2bp. In Thailand, the 1y NDIRS yield edged 1bp lower, while the 5y NDIRS yield rose 1bp amid reports the Thailand government plans to maintain the current fuel tariff to mitigate the impact of rising power costs to the public.

## Latin America:

CLP (+1.1%) led LatAm currencies after Chilean retail sales substantially beat expectations, at 2.2% y/y (C: -1.5%). The rest of the complex had a quieter session;

MXN (+0.2%) strengthened and BRL ended the session unchanged. Rates ended the session lower. TIIEs ended the session lower in the long end (10y: -5bp), while DIs and the IBR curve rallied \~7bp. The CAM curve underperformed following the stronger-than-expected data; the belly ended unchanged.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• BoJ Board Member Takata spoke at the regional leader meeting at the Shiga prefecture. Market participants regard him as one of the most hawkish members on the BoJ board. He said while there is still uncertainty in the Japanese economy, he believes the 2% price stability target is finally coming into sight. He noted the gradual pass-through from higher wage growth to services prices and provided an optimistic view for the upcoming spring wage negotiation after positive feedback from many corporates, with regards to another base-pay increase. He said the BoJ will need to consider nimble and flexible policy actions including the exit strategy for removal of the YCC framework, NIRP, and overshooting commitment

• A BoE statement announced Clare Lombardelli will succeed Ben Broadbent as the next Deputy Governor for Monetary Policy (DGMP) of the Bank of England

• Atlanta Fed President Bostic reiterated he believes it is appropriate to start easing monetary policy this summer. He said the January PCE inflation data shows bumps along the way, but even with the latest readings inflation is still coming down

• Chicago Fed President Goolsbee said there may be more disinflation from normalizing supply chains, and one must be careful extrapolating the January PCE inflation data forward

## Emerging Markets

• PBoC said it will continue to work on facilitating cross-border trade as well as investment and financing in Shanghai. It pledged to support Shanghai in expanding its high-level opening up financially and to push forward its digital yuan trial

• RBI proposed a four-year roadmap beginning in 2025-2026 for regulated entities to disclose climate-related financial risks under the standard framework. Beginning in 2025-2026, RBI, commercial banks, financial institutions, and top and higher layer NBFCs will be required to provide information on governance, strategy, and risk management. Starting in 2027- 2028, they will also be required to start submitting metrics and targets for disclosure

• BSP said Philippines’ inflation is likely to settle within the range of 2.8-3.6% y/y in February (January: 2.8%). The central bank noted upward price pressures would come from continued price increases for key food items, such as rice, meat, and fish, along with increased petroleum prices and

electricity rates. Lower prices of vegetables, fruits, and sugar could contribute to downward price pressures

• BI Deputy Governor Agung said the central bank will not cut policy rate yet due to still-high global uncertainty and IDR volatility. He noted the domestic economy needs a boost from policy rates, but as BI is not able to cut rates yet, it is encouraging growth through macroprudential policy instruments. He reiterated the central bank will keep maintaining IDR stability in line with market mechanism and forecasts the Fed to cut rates in 2H24

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
