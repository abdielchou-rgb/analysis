# Bull vs. Bear: Nominal GDP Well Below Consensus

Politburo and PBoC pledged "additional tools" to support growth. But policy remained reactive, and our 3Q nominal GDP is thus tracking at \~4%Y, well below consensus of 4.7%Y, although real GDP is tracking at 4.5%Y (Consensus: 4.7%Y).

To bring you a variety of perspectives, we have picked several charts that consider both bull and bear arguments for China's economy:

• Bull arguments: Pace of fiscal budget rollout has accelerated, and PBoC pledged "additional policy tools" to accommodate fiscal easing. A start of Fed rate cut cycle from September may help ease RMB depreciation pressures and reduce constraints on PBoC's monetary easing.

• Bear arguments: The continued reactive and supply-centric policy has not been able to stem private demand weakness, as reflected by the faded effect of mid-May housing-easing measures, softening wage growth (particularly for entry-level jobs), and the broad-based consumption slowdown. These suggest deflationary pressures remain elevated.

Bull Arguments

## Incrementally Faster Fiscal Rollout to Support Infrastructure Capex

Infrastructure capex YoY returned to double digits in Jun-Jul, with faster deployment of gov’t bond proceeds financed in May-Jun. We believe infrastructure capex growth could improve further in the coming couple of months, as the net gov’t bond financing amount has picked up to Rmb1.1trn in August MTD (vs. Rmb0.6trn in July). That said, while the supply-centric policy stimulus could help defend real GDP growth for now, it comes with a cost of continued excess capacity and thus lingering deflationary pressures.

## PBoC Governor Signals Further Reactive Monetary Easing

PBoC governor said on August 15 that the central bank is preparing “additional policy tools” to better accommodate fiscal easing. It also pledged to accelerate the implementation of announced measures, and enhance policy transparency through better market communication. That said, the overall policy framework has remained reactive and supply centric, as PBoC reiterates the importance of maintaining policy patience and refraining from any “drastic tightening or easing” and continues to emphasize support for high-tech sectors. Therefore, we maintain our forecast of modest 20bp policy rate cuts for Sep-Dec this year.

## Start of Fed Rate Cuts May Ease RMB Depreciation Pressures

USDCNY has appreciated by \~1% from 7.25 on Aug 1 to 7.17 currently, aided by increased market expectation of Fed rate cuts and expectation of yuan carry-trade unwinding, given the yen carry-trade’s unwinding. Different from the yen (where carry trades are dominated by financial investors), the bulk of yuan carry trades are by exporters parking trade proceeds in dollars. Since 2022, the FX conversion ratio of China’s goods trade surplus has fallen from 50% to 22% today, which means an accumulation of US\$450- 500bn dollar position. Our US Economics team expects three 25bps Fed rate cuts this Sep-Dec and a further four in 1H25, which could lower the US-China yield gap by 130-150bps, marginally easing the RMB depreciation pressures. But for the yuan carry trade to unwind, more policy stimulus is needed to revive the domestic demand.

Bear Arguments

## Sluggish Job Market, Especially for Fresh Graduates

As it is, nominal wage growth has decelerated to 4.4% in 2Q24, well below 2020-22 average of 6.2% and pre-Covid level of >8%. The wage slowdown has been more severe for new gradates. Evident of this, the Caixin new economy entry-level salary growth has dropped from \~10%Y in 1H22 to -5% in July 2024. Youth employment has also been a concern. The graduate placement profile of China’s top five universities shows that only 18% graduates found jobs in 2023 (vs. 27-30% 5-10 years ago), while the share of students pursuing further study domestically has increased to 56.6%, as compared to 39% a decade ago.

## Continued Housing Market Downturn

Secondary housing transactions in the ten large cities we track has fallen back to early-May levels, reflecting a rapid cooling of the mid-May policy easing impact. What is more concerning is the continued decline in property price amid sluggish market sentiment and elevated housing inventory, which has rendered a negative wealth effect on households, curbing consumption. This points to the need for more proactive policy support to digest housing inventory, ideally through central gov’t leveraging.

## Consumption Weakness Persists

Consumption demand remains sluggish, given slowing wage growth and negative wealth effect from continued housing price decline. While retail sales YoY picked up by 0.7ppt to 2.7% in July, its seasonally adjusted MoM was still weak at -0.8% (vs. -2% in June), with a sequential decline across all key categories. Meanwhile, new household loans have been significantly weaker than in previous years since 2Q, with monthly new loans averaging at –Rmb20bn, suggesting that households have been on the verge of deleveraging.
