# Rising momentum volatility, narrowing breadth

Asia/EM equities momentum reversed following lower US CPI data and Fed Chair Powell's testimony on July 9-10. We remain balanced on Value vs Growth in APxJ/EM but today turn neutral in Japan. Increasing volatility of momentum is a key portfolio risk given narrow equity breadth.

QUANTWISE[]

## Key Takeaways

■ In APxJ/EM, Momentum has underperformed by 2% July MTD, mainly due to HF degrossing and short covering.

In Japan, we turn neutral on Value vs Growth given potential USD/JPY inflection on Fed/BoJ convergence.

EM equities YTD returns have been highly concentrated, with 10 stocks driving >70% of gains versus their total index weight of 19%.

Macro factor drivers and preferences: The market is now fully priced for the September Fed rate cut that our economists have been forecasting ( Exhibit 13 ). With more disinflation evidence, US 10Y yields have trended below 4.2%, while implied bond volatility has also fallen MTD. The curve has steepened as the ratesensitive front end dropped more relative to the long end of the curve, and our rates strategists expect further steepening. Our Morgan Stanley Asia Risk Sentiment Indicator has improved to neutral levels. We remain balanced on Growth vs Value in APxJ/EM, preferring Quality and Earnings Revisions.

## Momentum factor volatility increasing amid narrow breadth: We have

experienced 10 one-day momentum drawdowns over 2% YTD in 2024 vs 2 such events in 2023 ( Exhibit 3 ). We expect momentum volatility to remain high given market concentration, as the 'effective number' of EM stocks has declined to just 62 (vs. 1,328 constituents). YTD EM return has been highly concentrated with TSMC and Tencent contributing 50% of index gains (vs. total 12% index weight) and the top 10 stocks driving > 70% of gains (vs. 19% weight) ( Exhibit 6 ). Only 37% of MSCI EM constituents have outperformed the index over the past 12 months, which is 8th percentile on 20-year history ( Exhibit 1 ).

Neutral on Japan Value: We close our bullish call on Japan Value and recommend a balanced stance vs. Growth. The factor is highly correlated with JPY and US 10Y yields, and we are approaching a key macro inflection point. Our economists forecast simultaneous BoJ hikes and Fed cuts from 3Q24, with our macro strategists seeing USD/JPY reversal into year-end alongside further declines in US 10Y yields.

## Market Dynamics and Style Implications

## Latest Style Performance Overview in Asia/EM

Momentum has underperformed MTD in July in APxJ/EM: Following a 3rd consecutive US CPI downside surprise and Fed Chair Powell's testimony on July 10, APxJ/EM securities underwent two days of Momentum reversal of roughly 4%. We attribute this to the hedge fund degrossing process as they cover their funding shorts in China. We expect the Momentum factor to remain volatile. Exhibit 3 indicates that there have been 10 Momentum drawdowns over 2% YTD in 2024 vs only 2 such events in 2023.

Breadth has narrowed in 2024: As we pointed out earlier, Emerging Markets return has been concentrated in a few stocks. The share of index returns generated by the top 10 contributors has been >70%, which is only slightly behind the record year of 2020 (on post-2013 history). However, this has come from a smaller share of index weight, at just 19% in total for those 10 stocks on average this year ( Exhibit 5 ). We also quantify EM market concentration and breadth via other measures. In Exhibit 4 , we calculate the effective number of constituents, via the inverse of the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (sum of squared % weight), which stands at just 62 vs. an official 1,328 constituents. Concentration has been increasing and is now close to levels last seen in 2H 2020. Meanwhile, market breadth is narrow, as measured by the 37% of stocks outperforming the index YTD ( Exhibit 1 ), the 43% of stocks with positive return in June ( Exhibit 7 ), the 52% of stocks above their 200-day moving average ( Exhibit 8 ), confirming this has been a concentrated index rally.

Not expecting US small cap rally to drive small caps in APxJ/EM: According to our previous Fed Pivot Playbook for Asia/EM equities, under the prior four pivots, on average small caps underperformed large caps on 1-6M horizons. MSCI APxJ small caps have outperformed their large cap peers by 28% since January 2021, unlike the 31% underperformance seen between the Russell 2000 vs. S&P 500 up until 10 July 2024. Finally, we note that APxJ/EM small cap outperformance has tended to happen during the early business recovery phase from a trough/recession. Thus, we have low conviction in small cap outperformance in APxJ/EM at this juncture.

## Factor Monitor – Monthly Update July 12, 2024

## Methodology

Factor return calculation: To evaluate historical factor performance, we rank stocks as at month-end within defined universes, and form quintile portfolios based on the underlying factor values. We go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile (i.e., long the top 20th percentile of stocks within the defined universe and short those in the bottom 20th percentile), rebalanced monthly. We then calculate the performance of the time series of this L/S market-neutral portfolio over various time horizons.

Composite formation: To calculate the composite factors, we first z-score the individual factor values at each monthly observation within the defined universe. Individual factor zscores are then averaged into composite scores for each stock on an equal-weighted basis.
