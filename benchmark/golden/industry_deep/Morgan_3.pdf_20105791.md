# Turning Neutral on EM FX

We turn neutral on EM FX thanks to downside surprises in US data, still decent EM FX carry and reasonable valuations. But we hold a USD-bullish bias given the recent USD weakness overshoot and aggressive rate cut expectation.

FX & EM Strategy: The GBI-EM index yielded 12.7% in 2023 despite all the challenges from a hawkish Fed, China slowdown, US regional banking crisis and increasing geopolitical tensions. The dispersion of the country return is the highest in history, allowing investors to generate decent alpha.

We turn neutral on EM FX from bearish as: 1) The market will likely bounce between the hawkish/dovish Fed and easing/tightening financial conditions; 2) US data surprising on the downside more versus EM; 3) EM FX still offers decent carry; and 4) EM FX valuations seem fair. But we hold a bullish bias on USD given the USD weakness overshoot seen late last year.

Sovereign Credit Strategy: November and December front-loaded half of the 14% 2024 full-year expected return from our outlook, leaving it mostly down to carry from here, given that we don't see reasons to adjust our spread forecast tighter. If anything, we see spreads wider in 1Q24 due to stretched valuations, increased supply, downside risks to oil prices and some pushback on near-term easing priced from global central banks. Given this view, we suggest reducing beta in portfolios, even after an already weak first week. In BBBs, we replace our Hungary like stance with Uruguay. In BBs, we replace our Colombia and South Africa like stances with Brazil and Morocco, moving Colombia directly to a dislike stance. LatAm Macro Strategy: We explore three patterns that tend to occur in January in LatAm markets, related to FX performance and inflation patterns. We keep our structural 5-year TIIE receiver, but add a tactical 1-year TIIE payer. We also add a 5- year COPxIBR receiver and long BRL/MXN.

Asia Macro Strategy: We revisit the three themes from our 2024 outlook introduced in November 2023: 1) China is still facing growth challenges without a clear catalyst ahead; 2) We still like long carry in Asia FX with some caveats; and 3) We change our duration view from bullish to slightly bearish. In FX, we close all our previous FX trades and add short TWD/INR, short CNH/INR and long USD/THB. In rates, we like receiving 1-year CNY, long CNY FX-hedged 1-year CGBs, paying 1-year INR and receiving 2-year KRW versus USD without beta adjustment.

• The US has a regional banking crisis;

• China growth disappoints the market;

• In addition to Russia-Ukraine, there is another regional conflict in the Middle East.

## Turning neutral on EM FX

As we discussed in Global EM Strategist: Nine Surprises for 2024, December 11, 2023, a stable and strong EM FX is required for the GBI-EM index to outperform the EMBI index. This is exactly what we have seen in the last two months of 2023, with EM FX generating 3.8% in November and December.

Our global FX team turned neutral on USD last week (see G10 FX Strategy: Moving to Neutral on the USD, January 4, 2024) as slowing US growth, falling UST yields, shrinking US real rates and broad risk-on had led to broad USD weakness. However, it is unclear that such a trend would continue because the Fed could push back against the market getting ahead by itself. Over 160bp of rate cuts priced in for 2023 is much more aggressive than our economists' forecast and the Fed's own dots.

On the back of our global FX team's view change, we would like to turn neutral on EM FX as well for the following reasons:

## The dynamic of the Fed and the financial conditions index

First, the market would continue to deal with the dynamic of the Fed and the financial conditions index. The broad USD and UST remain the most important factors for EM FX.

When USTs sold off and USD appreciated in October, the Fed saw the market doing the heavy lifting by tightening financial conditions, and hence turned less hawkish at its November meeting.

However, as the market became cheerful on the soft landing in the US and financial conditions eased significantly in the past two months, the Fed might feel uncomfortable about the aggressive rate cut in the price. Hence, there is a risk that the Fed sounds a bit more hawkish at the upcoming January FOMC meeting.

Such a dynamic doesn't necessary mean that EM FX would sell off significantly from here given the rally. Rather, it suggests that the market is likely to be range bound.

## Whose data surprise on the downside? US or EM?

Second, the relative strength of the currency is reflecting the relative performance of the economy. As shown in Exhibit 9 , EM FX has largely traded in line with the US-EM surprise index difference. The recent trend is that while EM data are softening a bit, particularly in

Asia (see Asia Economics: PMI Update: A Broad-based Decline, January 3, 2024), US data are worsening at a faster pace than EM data. The US surprise index has reached its lowest level since May 2023.

The question from here is whether such a trend would continue, and further data weakness could raise concerns about a hard landing in the US, which in the past has been supportive for USD. As far as our economists are concerned, they believe that a soft landing is more likely than a hard landing (see 2024 US Economics Outlook: Slowing Growth, Easing Policy, November 12, 2023). It would also be difficult to argue for a recession in a US election year.

Hence, should the current trend continue (without a US recession), this environment should be supportive for carry in EM FX.

## Carry has declined, but remains above historical averages

As shown below, on a vol-adjusted basis EM FX carry peaked in July 2023, when EM central banks started to deliver rate cuts. The drop has been quite sharp, particularly in LatAm and CEEMEA, though the starting points were also particularly elevated.

In the absence of a sharp pick-up in global volatility, we think that carry appetite should remain supported and EM FX should still offer some carry in 1H24, as we expect the ongoing easing to broadly occur at a cautious pace. That said, as overall carry is shrinking, we would expect to see more dispersion among EM currencies based on their rates profiles (and potentially on other idiosyncracies).

In 1H23, prior to the start of rate cuts, almost all of LatAm FX and some CEEs such as HUF and CZK were favoured carry proxies, as suggested by our analysis below. However, the onset of rate cuts in most of these countries did result in a fall from grace for several of them, particularly for CLP, HUF and CZK. BRL managed to avoid these pressures, likely thanks to the BCB's cautious pace and impeccable forward guidance.

MXN is a very particular case, as we explain in LatAm Macro Strategy: Four Things That Usually Happen in January . We think that a great percentage of MXN carry trades in 2023 were held against EUR and JPY shorts, and significant stops in these positions were likely triggered late last year, particularly by BoJ-related headlines. As MXN longs against USD remain crowded based on our positioning indicators and some of the previous funders have lost their appeal, there is limited room to add more MXN longs to capitalise on its carry.

As a reminder, we expect very constructive MXN sentiment to start to subside over the coming months, pressured by concerns relating to the impact of a deceleration in the US economy this year, and political risks.

## Valuation not rich or cheap

Fourth, given the divergent performance of EM FX in 2023, it would be difficult to gauge the overall EM FX valuation. Instead, we rely on our idiosyncratic risk premium model in EM FX, which looks at the idiosyncratic risk premium after removing the broad USD factor and EM FX carry factor. The result shows that most EM FX seems to be fairly priced ( Exhibit 12 ) and none of the currencies we track have a risk premium over 1.5 standard deviations – a criterion we call rich or cheap.

In addition, the dispersion of these currencies' risk premium is also close to a record low, indicating less extreme valuation across EM FX.

## Strategy implications

We believe that EM FX will trade sideways in the next few weeks, but as our global colleagues discussed in G10 FX Strategy: Moving to Neutral on the USD, USD might gain some footing in the short term thanks to the positioning, seasonality and overshoot in the USD sell-off.

Hence, we would like run a portfolio with relative trades in EM FX with some tactical USD bullishness.

In Asia, we close short CNH/TWD, long USD/CNH 6m straddle and short INR/IDR. We add short TWD/INR, short CNH/INR and long USD/THB.

In LatAm, January tends to be the second-best month of the year for LatAm FX (after December), as seasonality factors remain mostly supportive. Of course, average historical patterns are partly contingent on sample size and exceptions may occur. BRL tends to be most favoured by seasonality factors in January. Given our recent USD stance shift to neutral, we are hesitant to add short USD exposure in the region (especially at current levels) and prefer relative value plays instead. We add long BRL/MXN, as our analysis suggests that MXN is no longer a favoured carry proxy in the region and technicals look challenging.

On US Treasury yields, the forecast by our US Treasury strategists is 3.95% for the 10-year by end-2024 – in other words very close to current levels. This could be revised lower at some point given that they remain constructive on US rates in the medium term. On EM sovereign credit spreads, spreads may still appear cheap at the index level, but once stripping out the defaulted credits it ends up being much tighter. Similar to our US credit strategists, we see no reason to be adjusting our credit spread forecasts tighter at this point. Indeed, any further downwards pressure on oil prices would leave most oil exporter spreads looking rich.

Tighter spreads towards our bull case of 350bp would likely come from two scenarios. First, significant inflows to global fixed income, which would include EM sovereign bonds, could push spreads tighter across the board as the focus remains on yields as opposed to relatively tight spreads. Second, a significant easing in financial conditions that opens up market access for the weaker single Bs and CCCs as thus allows the only truly cheap part of the index to also rally. For now we don’t want to get ahead of either of these two factors, given the recent rally.

Too fast, too furious price action should see bonds consolidating from here, with spreads in general slightly wider: The rally has so far been in two stages, kicking off on November 1 with the trio of US Treasury refunding announcements, a big drop in the ISM manufacturing PMI and the November FOMC press conference where Chair Powell insinuated that further hikes were off the table ( Exhibit 19 ). ## South Africa: Time to lighten risk

Our decision to upgrade SOAF to a like stance was predicated on cheap valuations as the main driver. Since mid-December 2023, we have seen 10-year spreads outperform peers (barring Ivory Coast and Armenia) leaving SOAF versus the BB average at a one-year low. This, combined with SOAF now having the highest positioning relative to other comparable BB credits, leaves us cautious at these levels and so we now think that it's prudent to take some chips off the table and turn neutral from our like stance.

What’s more, we are now mindful that the political risk premium ahead of the national elections could start getting priced in, leaving the curve vulnerable to underperformance from here. Recent polling data show waning support for the ruling party, creating uncertainty as to who the ANC could have to form a coalition with.

## Also removing Colombia like stance, moving straight to a dislike stance

Similar to South Africa, our like on Colombia in the year ahead outlook was nearly entirely driven by valuations and existing negative market sentiment. In other words, the argument was not that the fundamentals were improving much. Today, valuations are still far from rich but they have compressed a lot, both outright and versus peers. If EM spreads overall come under more pressure in the coming weeks, we would expect Colombia to underperform.

More idiosyncratically, there are also still various elements worth monitoring. Fiscals are the main focus. Over the holiday period, the president announced that diesel prices will not be adjusted unlike earlier promises from the finance minister, and that gasoline prices will not be adjusted further either. This would increase the FEPC deficit by around 0.4% of GDP according to the latest CARF report, which over time will have a fiscal impact on the central government too. This now almost certainly means that the fiscal rule will not be achieved next year, with a deficit towards 5.5% possible, worse than prior assumptions.

Colombia is planning to issue US\$3 billion in 2024, although with every 1pp in fiscal deficit widening versus the target of 4.5% increasing issuance needs by around US\$700 million. Given that Colombia issued in November, it reduces the immediate need to come to markets. However, given spending plans and the recent rally, we don't exclude it trying to issue soon again.

Given the very flat 10s30s curve, we would reduce in the long end and in particular COLOM 2051. We also still suggest buying the CDS-bond basis via 5Y CDS and Colombia USD 2029.

## 1. January tends to be the second-best month of the year for LatAm FX

While the robustness of the so-called 'Santa rally' myth that investors usually look for every December cannot be statistically tested, our quick analysis of LatAm FX price action going back to 2010 does suggest that December tends to be the best month for gains. This is particularly true for exporting currencies, which tend to also benefit from positive

seasonality at the end of the year.

Historically, however, January has been the month to bring more uniform gains for FX longs across the region, as seasonality factors tend to remain more uniformly supportive, then disperse throughout the year. Of course, average historical patterns are partly contingent on sample size and exceptions may occur. Given our recent USD stance shift to neutral, we are hesitant to add short USD exposure in the region (especially at current levels) and prefer relative value plays instead.

Our stance on Asia local markets has been more constructive, as we argued for both carry trades in Asia FX and receiving trades in Asia rates (see 2024 Asia Macro Strategy Outlook: Three Themes into 2024, November 20, 2023).

The three themes we proposed in November 2023 have more or less played out in the past two months. We would like to provide the following updates:

## 1. A sub-par China recovery is not translating into immediate CNY weakness

In the outlook, we made the point that despite the government's pro-growth stance, it would be difficult for the market to be convinced about the recovery, which could drive improved sentiment.

The Central Economic Working Conference in mid-December did strike a pro-growth tone (see China Economics: CEWC: Modest Stimulus vs. High Target, December 12, 2023). However, the overall message isn't too different from July's Politburo meeting, which caused the initial rally but this faded quickly.

The next few events to watch would be the Third Plenum, the Two Sessions in March and April's Politburo meeting.

The Third Plenum is indeed something to watch closely, as it could serve as an important event to drive the market. However, based on the information available, it is unclear whether the government would use the Third Plenum to send a more forceful message to the market. We will also watch if security would be on the agenda.

The Two Sessions would be to announce the GDP growth target and fiscal deficit. But based on media reports, the fiscal expansion would be less than our economists' or market expectations (see China Musings: Reflation Progress and Next Signposts, December 14, 2023).

This leaves April's Politburo meeting as the last event to watch in the next three months. The growth momentum into 2024 isn't great. Based on the high-frequency number, our economists estimate that growth in 4Q is just 0.7%Q, which translates to sub-3%Y into 2024, short of the government's target of 5%Y.

Compared to the start of 2023, which had a favourable base effect and reopening impulse, 1Q24 seems quite challenging if current growth momentum stays. Hence, it could increase the chance that the government steps on the easing pedal more aggressively. But it would only happen should data continue to disappoint in the next three months.

From a market perspective, the trend is weak equity, lower rates but stable FX. We have taken a more neutral stance on CNY in China: Your Questions Answered (5), November 30, 2023. It is clear that the PBOC wants to cap any upside in USD/CNH beyond 7.30 and there is no short-term indicator which suggests otherwise.

Our view on CNY is that the balance of payments fundamentals support a weaker CNY, which could help to alleviate China’s 3D problems and buy some time for policymakers to deliver more meaningful reflationary policies (see China's 3D journey: The CNY outlook in a new equilibrium ). But in the short term we don’t see signs that the PBOC will let it go immediately. Hence, we are neutral on CNY and would use it as a funding currency.

We have been recommending short CNH/TWD. Given the rally in TWD, we would like to exit short CNH/TWD. Instead, we would like to add short CNH/INR, which could work well should USD trade a bit stronger in the next few weeks into the FOMC. We set the target at 11.2 and stop at 11.8. We close our long USD/CNH 6m straddle as volatility has picked up a bit.

In China rates, we believe that the PBOC would need to cut rates in the next few weeks in order to help the NIM for the banks who are tasked to do many services at the same time: buying government bonds, addressing the local government debt overhang, helping property developers, helping SMEs and reducing mortgage rates. We continue to hold receiving 1-year CNY NDIRS.

## 2. Long carry in Asia FX

One thing we didn't define properly in the outlook is the definition of carry. When USD is weak, it would benefit some of the high yielders such as IDR in Asia. We don’t see INR as an outperformer in a weak USD environment because of the RBI's stance. This is one of the reasons why we recommended short INR/IDR in the outlook.

But when USD is strong, actually USD could act as a high-carry currency to own.

As discussed in the Turning neutral on EM FX , we see EM FX trading sideways in the next few weeks. But in the short term, there is a risk of some USD rebound. This is because the Fed might want to push back against the easing financial conditions and market pricing of rate cuts in March, which could cause some repricing of front-end rates in the US.

We would like to sell into USD appreciation given our view of a US soft landing as discussed in FX & EM Strategy: Turning Neutral on EM FX , but we see some tactical opportunities.

With such a view in mind, we would like to close short INR/IDR. We re-add short TWD/ INR 3m NDF to position for some tactical adjustment in TWD (see TWD: Where to after the rally? ). We set the target at 2.60 and the stop at 2.80.

We also recommend long USD/THB as a carry trade. THB has been one of the bestperforming currencies in Asia and hasn't adjusted to the recent USD appreciation. This is due to the favourable tourism seasonality and elevated gold prices. But should US real rates rise and gold prices fall, THB could underperform. We set the target at 36.5 and the stop at 34.

## 3. Add bullish duration trades selectively

This is the theme we would like to make some moderate changes to. We have been recommending receiving front-end rates in Asia, namely China, Korea and India. Our view on China hasn't changed much and we still like receiving 1-year CNY NDIRS.

In addition, we would like to add onshore CNY FX-hedged 1-year CGBs as a good carry trade. Onshore 1-year CGBs still offer over 2% yield thanks to the PBOC liquidity tightening to support the currency. Meanwhile, 1-year FX forward points remain quite soft, and much lower than offshore CNH forward points.

This has created a good opportunity to earn carry by buying CGBs and hedging with onshore CNY points. The net result is that such a trade offers 5.66% yield or 82bp pick up over 1-year SOFR. Investors could earn good carry and add leverage via Total Return Swap. The Chinese bond market saw over US\$37 billion of inflows last November, probably driven by such flows. But since then, the yield pick-up has increased, which makes the trade more attractive.

The risk to the trade would be a sharp sell-off in China rates. We set the target at 1.7% and stop at 2.3%.

## China's 3D journey: The CNY outlook in a new equilibrium

For full details, see China's 3D Journey: The CNY Outlook in a New Equilibrium, December 17, 2023.

The impact of the 3D challenge on CNY: After being one of the strongest currencies globally in 2020 and 2021, CNY has lost 9.5% against USD in 2022 and 2.6% in 2023. A combination of the 3Ds (debt, deflation and demographics) and geopolitical tensions has caused a structural slowdown in China and raised the question of whether CNY could weaken further and whether CNY internationalisation would halt.

We believe that the 3D challenge could weaken CNY, and a weaker currency helps to alleviate China’s 3D problems and buys some time for policymakers to deliver more meaningful reflationary policies. To that extent, currency weakness, if managed well, could help China to exit a debt-deflation trap faster.

How does China compare to Japan? In the 1990s, the massive current account surplus allowed Japanese investors to recycle the surplus into global assets, cementing JPY's safehaven status. China's current account surplus is narrower and global asset holdings are smaller, offering CNY less support. China's balance of payments dynamics now are weaker than 1990s Japan. But China's FX reserves are much bigger and the government still restricts capital outflows. Hence, the PBOC has both the willingness and ability to contain large CNY depreciation. We believe that a larger FX adjustment is off the table as such an adjustment is against the government's priority of financial stability and security.

We expect a 1-2% annual depreciation in the CNY CFETS in 2024-25: The PBOC has been advocating that "CNY is the stabiliser of the balance of payments" and "CNY should be kept in its reasonable equilibrium". Past experience suggests that the PBOC would let CNY depreciate should China's balance of payments worsen, and CNY CFETS has been in the range of 92.0-102.0 since 2015 (versus the current 98.3). We thus believe that the PBOC would be comfortable with a cumulative 2-4% depreciation in the CNY CFETS over the next two years, to move it towards the lower end of the historical range. In our bear case of a major housing downturn amid an insufficient policy response, the PBOC may allow a 4-5% annual depreciation in the CNY CFETS index over 2024-25 to ease entrenched deflationary pressures.

CNY internationalisation has made great progress but the pace is likely to slow down in the next few years. China would rely more on trading with geopolitically friendly countries in CNY. But whether these countries would like to hold CNY would depend on the size of CNY assets and their return.

## TWD: Where to after the rally?

TWD was the best-performing AXJ currency over the last two weeks of December ( Exhibit 54 ), rallying by 2.1% to break below the 31.0 level. This was driven by a combination of broad USD weakness, large inflows to the local equity market and local lifers' hedging, in our view. Indeed, in the last two weeks of December, Taiwan equities saw US\$3.5 billion of foreign inflows, nearly half of the US\$6.9 billion equity inflows for the full year ( Exhibit 55 ).

Where do we go from here? After the sharp rally in late December, TWD has been retracing the move since the start of the new year, in line with the broader USD rebound. But TWD has been one of the underperformers in the region, likely due to positioning adjustment as well as weakness in equity markets.

In the near term, this could have room to continue. An interruption in the trend of lower USD and US yields could pause lifers' hedging flows. USD/TWD also reached oversold territory in late December. Given the sharp rally seen, we think that the risk/reward of being long TWD at these levels is not that attractive. This is especially if we make a comparison to KRW, which did not rally much in late December but has weakened more than TWD since the start of the year ( Exhibit 54 ).

Therefore, we close short CNH/TWD and pivot to a tactical short TWD/INR position. To be clear, we still see room for TWD to gain in the medium term on the back of the tech upcycle supporting exports and the equity market, as well as potential hedging flows from local lifers and exporters as the Fed moves towards rate cuts. We would be looking for opportunities to be long TWD again after a correction.

What about the historical appreciation around elections? Taiwan will be the first to kick off a year filled with elections, with its legislative and presidential elections this Saturday (January 13). Polls will open from 8am-4pm HKT, with votes counted immediately after, so preliminary results are usually known by that evening (unless it is too close to call). Our previous analysis shows that TWD has historically appreciated after election day, but it also suggests that other global factors could be more important drivers of price action.

## Definition of terms

Sell/Short: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be negative over the relevant time period.

Selling Protection or Buying Risk: The analyst expects that the price of protection against the event occurring will decrease over the relevant time period.

Pay: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will increase.

Receive: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will decrease.
