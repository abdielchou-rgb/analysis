# May TSF reflects continued rationalization of loan growth

TSF growth recovered slightly in May, to 8.4%, supported by government bond issuance. Loan growth remained weak with mix shift towards discounted bills. Growth in corporate deposits and M1 declined further, partly owing to efforts to reduce selfcirculating loans and deposit classification change.

TSF growth recovered sequentially in May to 8.4%, in line with our expectation, supported by government bond issuance – other credit remained weak: New TSF added Rb2.07tn in May, reversing the negative numbers in April. This was mainly supported by government bond issuance after much slower issuance yoy in January-April 2024 owing to more careful review of projects and efforts to prevent idle funding after government bond issuance.

Notably, government bond issuance added Rmb1.225tn in May, \~60% of new TSF from gradual approval of government bonds for projects being assessed since the beginning of the year.

Loan growth remained weak, adding Rmb816bn in May, down Rmb406bn yoy, likely reflecting more rational loan extension in response to reduced window guidance on the pace of loan growth and policy efforts to reduce self-circulating loans.

We believe that it is becoming more clear that policymakers now value quality more than quantity and are dedicated to squeezing out inflated credit for slower but more sustainable credit growth.

Loan growth slowed further – with mix shift towards discounted bills: Household loans weakened in May, adding Rmb76bn in May, down 79% yoy. Growth in both long-term and short-term household loans slowed further, likely because:

1. Mortgage repayment could stay at elevated levels;

2. Banks are cautious about consumption loan extension for high-quality credit; and

3. Growth in inclusive financing loans has been more rational given reduced window guidance after aggressive expansion in the past 2-3 years.

In addition, while corporate loans added Rmb740bn, the mix has shifted towards discounted bills, reflecting SOE banks' effort to support headline May TSF. Mediumto long-term corporate loan growth also moderated, partly because restrictions on LGFV loans are still tight. We expect some further slowdown in growth in coming months.
