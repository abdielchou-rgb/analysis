# Global Macro Commentary | North America

# June 21

Weak Euro area PMIs support global duration rally; USTs retrace gains after beat in US PMIs; 10y OAT/Bund widens to highest level since 2012; BoJ's Uchida open to rate hike; THB outperforms amid announcement of new MoF measures; USD strengthens; DXY at 105.83 (+0.2%); US 10y at 4.255% (-0.4bp).

• USD strengthens as the preliminary June US Composite PMI surprises to the upside and remains in expansionary territory while Euro area PMIs are on the soft side (DXY USD Index: +0.2%).

## Developed Markets

## United States:

The week ended in a surprisingly quiet session as Global PMIs headlined the economic calendar. Overnight weaker-than-expected Euro area PMIs largely supported a global duration rally and strengthened the USD. German, French, and European PMIs all surprised to the downside and bonds rallied accordingly. The UK PMIs were a mixed bag with strength in manufacturing partially offsetting the weakness in services. Further, UK price pressures accelerated and ultimately gilts sold-off in the aftermath of the PMI data and upside surprise in UK retail sales.

USTs opened the NY session \~4bp richer due to the soft Euro area PMIs. However, yields fully retraced the move and rose 4-5bp across the curve after preliminary US PMIs surprised to the upside. The service sector led the upturn and increased to the highest level in over two years. The expansion in services was also supported by manufacturing which continued to rise, despite losing some momentum. Meanwhile, selling price inflation cooled to a five-month low and input price inflation slowed to a level below the average of the last year. The survey signaled resilience in business activity coupled with softening price pressure. The slowing of inflation pressures capped the sell-off in UST yields. USTs traded in a narrow range in the NY afternoon amid low liquidity, and ended the day largely unchanged.

Despite being an eventful weak, the upward pressure in yields from a transition to risk-on sentiment from European market participants was offset by data that started to signal some economic slowdown alongside cooling inflation. This resulted in UST yields ending the week fairly unchanged.

On Friday, S&P 500 trading volume spiked with \~\$5.5tn in options estimated to have expired and alongside key ETF rebalancing. Despite the significant volume, the S&P 500 (-0.2%) closed just modestly lower with technology stocks leading the declines.

## Japan:

JGBs sold off across the curve throughout the Tokyo trading hours following the sell-off in USTs on Thursday. From the Tokyo morning, JGB yields began to rise especially in the long-end as higher-term premium priced in on the back of higher UST yields. Although the super long-end JGBs rallied on Thursday in response to reports that the MoF is considering issuing bonds with shorter maturities, on Friday the bull-flattening move partially retraced. In the Tokyo afternoon, the front-end accelerated the selloff as JPY continued to weaken. By the Tokyo 3pm close, 5y JGB sold off 2.5bp d/d to 0.540%, 10y JGB sold off 2.5bp d/d to 0.975%, and 30y JGB sold off 2.5bp d/d to 2.145% on a simple yield basis.

TONA-OIS curve bear-flattened and outperformed JGBs particularly in the super long-end. XCCY basis widened across the curve. In the Tokyo evening, the BoJ announced it will hold a bond market group meeting on July 9-10. Meanwhile, reports indicated that the MoF plans to tweak the size of liquidity tap auction across the maturity buckets. They plan to reduce the amount of the 15.5-39.0y sector by JPY100bn, but increase the amount of the 5-15.5y sector by JPY50bn. The MoF also released the minutes of the “debt management meeting”, which suggested that the MoF was considering reducing the long-end JGB issuance.

## Carry-focused trades continued to support USD/JPY and USD/CHF amid

Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari's hawkish comments on Thursday. After weakerthan-expected Japan May nationwide inflation data released early Tokyo morning, USD/JPY accelerated the pace of its rising and briefly touched 159.13 before trading in a range of 158.70 to 159.10 throughout the rest of the Tokyo trading hours. USD/ JPY traded lower to 158.70 level into the London morning as weaker-than-expected Germany and France June PMI data drove a broad risk-off move. EUR/JPY sold off below 169.50 instantaneously.

## Europe:

After a busy week in G10 FX, it was a quiet day for price action. Main events included German, French, European, UK, and US PMIs, the first three of which surprised to the downside, followed by a mixed result in the UK and a beat in the US. Subsequently, European fixed income broadly rallied which sent the EUR down and it broke below 1.07. Ultimately however, part of the sell-off was reversed but the EUR remained below 1.07 and closed down -0.1%. The funders performed poorly as carry traders sought out funding in the low yielders; JPY and CHF both fell -0.3% during the London session. SEK weakness was cut short and it fell only -0.1%, due to it’s sensitivity to US rates, whereas NOK weakened further, down -0.2%, as both equities and oil dropped. GBP showed little to no reaction to the UK PMIs and

ultimately closed down -0.1%.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar caught a bid against most of G10 as US PMI remained in expansionary territory and beat to the upside, which sent USTs higher, but without an equity rally. In the Antipodeans, AUD underperformed, as weaker commodities weighed on the currency and fell -0.2% while NZD closed flat. In Canada data was mixed, which suggested that the -0.1% sell-off in CAD was US dollar driven as opposed to locally driven. Ultimately, the DXY USD Index rose +0.2% during the London session.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ exhibited mixed moves on Friday, but traded in a narrow range. CNH gained slightly to 7.2880 per USD aided by the steady USD/CNY fixing rate (7.1196) and tightened offshore liquidity given that 1m CNH HIBOR rose 9bp to 2.71%. USD/CNY remained largely unchanged alongside underperformance in the equity market (CSI300: -0.2%) despite reports that Canada is preparing tariffs on Chinese-made electric vehicles. THB outperformed and rose 0.3% to 36.67 per USD alongside rallies in gold prices and the local equity market (SET: +0.8%), as PM Srettha announced the MoF will set measures to support the local stock market next week. INR rallied 0.2% to 83.51 per USD while media reports disclosed that the RBI intervened in the FX market at 83.62 per USD on Thursday, and would protect the rupee at 83.70 for the next few weeks. KRW and TWD depreciated 0.3% and 0.2%, respectively, due to their high beta to USD. IDR continued its downward trend, as USD/IDR rose 0.1% to 16,450, approaching the highest level since April 2020.

Similarly, AxJ rates also had mixed performance. In China, the 2s10s NDIRS curve shifted 1bp lower while 10y CGB yields rose 1bp to 2.25%. In Korea, 3y KTB yields fell 3bp to 3.17% alongside underperformance in local equities (KOSPI: -0.8%). Meanwhile, the short-term 2y KRW NDIRS fell 4bp to 3.24%. In Hong Kong, the 2s10s IRS curve twist flattened 2bp. In Thailand, the 2s10s NDOIS curve bullflattened 2bp while 10y ThaiGB rallied 3bp to 2.70%. India and Singapore rates remained largely unchanged.

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
