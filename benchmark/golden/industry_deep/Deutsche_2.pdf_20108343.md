Since our ‘Long China’ call on February 2, the CSI 300 is up 16% and the Hang Seng 24%. We argued that China’s stock market problems were more a sentiment issue than a structural problem. Now, that sentiment appears to have turned. In this piece, we summarise the key reasons why we remain bullish on Chinese markets in the short term and point out some benefits found in Chinese markets that investors are struggling to find in developed markets:

• Uncorrelated assets: Almost every major market is less correlated with Chinese stocks than 5 years ago.

Consumer sentiment: ‘Uncertainty’ indices have fallen and spending has jumped Excess savings: The doubling of bank savings since 2021 offers significant capacity for further spending growth in contrast with the US and Europe where excess savings are winding down.

• Margin of safety: Equities have a strong earnings yield over that of bonds Stimulus: Investors underestimate the constant drop-feed of small stimulus measures as they contrast with the large, more one-off packages used in the West

There are several obstacles to a full Chinese market recovery. In particular, retail investors – which are key to sentiment – still have a stronger preference to save rather than invest. In addition, stimulus efforts continue to underwhelm the market. Meanwhile, property market debt acts like the Sword of Damocles hanging over the economy, and geopolitical tensions are encouraging a gradual decoupling with Western companies.

The challenges of the Chinese economy, though, are well known and, we believe, well priced into markets in the short term. So, barring a significant unforeseen event we remain optimistic about the Chinese market recovery.
