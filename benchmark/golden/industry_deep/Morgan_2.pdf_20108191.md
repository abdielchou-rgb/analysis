## Better China trading could have some more to go; implications for regional/sector trades

After a terrible spell between Jan <sup>’</sup>23 and January of this year, where MSCI China lost almost 40%, it is now up 25% from the lows - top chart. While we do not believe that the longer term structural concerns of deflationary backdrop, excess capacity, real estate demand-supply imbalances, credit saturation and global decoupling are finished, our tactical view remains that the more positive China trading could last through summer, through July-August, on increasing market hopes that the worst of housing weakness is potentially behind us, and until the US elections heat up in earnest.

● Our EM strategy team notes there is still an EM investor underweight on China, with half of bearish positions closed, but another half to go, and the valuations probably have another 10-15% upside before closing the discount to historical, from current 10.0x towards cca 11.5x forward P/E.

A more bullish tactical China stance, post our long held bearish view, was one of the drivers of our upgrade of Eurozone equities in Q1. We continue to believe that Eurozone risk-reward has improved, and that the region will at least hold its own vs the US, whether the overall market goes up or down.

UK (OW) is also starting to trade better of late, erasing the almost 10% relative weakness seen earlier in the year - middle chart. We believe UK equities sectoral tilts will be more supportive, the market is still exceptionally cheap and it offers by far the highest dividend yield out of major indices.

• At sector level, we think commodities remain interesting as a way to position for more positive China trading, both Mining and Energy. Metal prices are likely to be supported by low inventories and better demand, and Brent is unlikely to move much lower than 80USD, while commodity equities offer a high FCF yield and attractive valuations. We are less positive on some of other traditional China plays, such as Autos (UW) and Luxury (N). Pricing is a significant risk for both, as well as a potential volume disappointment, leaving their elevated margins at risk.

More broadly, while we keep our style tilts of Growth vs Value, High vs Low Quality and Large vs Small for now, where all 3 are ahead by 3-8% ytd, at sector level we have been arguing that Defensives such as Utilities and Staples should start to trade better than certain Cyclicals, in particular the Consumer Cyclicals, and that call is irrespective of direction of bond yields from here. While some Cyclicals such as Banks keep trading very well, we note that last month Utilities and Staples are the best in the US, and Real Estate, Utilities, Staples and Healthcare are top 4 sectors in Europe.

Now, more positive tactical China call is clearly a big help for EM group, given the almost 30% index weight in EM. Having said that, we do not believe EM is a buy vs DM, and keep our Neutral EM vs DM stance, that we entered this year with. The headwinds for EM remain Fed higher for longer, and stronger USD / weaker EM FX. As the bottom chart shows, EM equities typically struggle to outperform DM when their currencies are under pressure.

## Equity Strategy Key Calls and Drivers

We are concerned about inflation staying too high if there is no slack created in the economy, adverse bonds demand-supply with negative term premia, consensus expectation of profit acceleration of almost 20% between Q1 and Q4 of this year, which doesn’t typically happen, especially if the economy softens in 2H, consumer tailwinds potentially turning, as well as concentration and leadership reversal hurting the market. At the core, the Goldilocks view that market embraces in Q1 of inflation/rates moving lower but at the same time of earnings acceleration and economy having no landing remains an inconsistent one. In fact, the Growth-Inflation trade-off could end up the opposite, as seen in recent ISM showing a spike in pricing and slowing orders. Together with seasonally poor time for markets coming up and still stretched positioning, we look for more of a consolidation in equity markets over the next months. Within this, we advocate for some of the rotations to come up/have legs. Growth is ahead of Value ytd, small caps are heavily behind again ytd. We fundamentally stay with Growth, Quality and large caps tilts, but the turn could be coming, where small caps tended to perform better as ECB starts to ease. Regionally, we have in Q1 taken profits on US vs Eurozone OW, as well as tactically exited our long-standing China bearish stance. The latest market move to a more Defensive trading should have legs, such as recent outperformance of Utilities and Staples, alongside commodity sectors.
