## India Equity Strategy

1QFY25 Earnings Preview

We expect 1QFY25 earnings to be driven by Financials, Autos and Healthcare, while dragged down by global cyclicals like Oil & Gas. Near term, our sector allocation remains aligned largely with the domestic cyclical plays amid positive earnings momentum, superior economic growth and policy continuity. We remain Overweight on Financials, Auto, Real Estate, Healthcare and Industrials. In this note, we collate J.P. Morgan estimates for the 1Q earnings season (ended June 2024) and see two key messages: (1) moderate top-line growth expectations (Nifty +7% YoY ); (2) margin contraction of 24 bps QoQ for Nifty 50 (while for JPM Universe we expect a margin contraction of 30 bps).

For the Nifty 50 companies, we expect aggregate Revenue/EBITDA/PBT/ PAT to register YoY change of +7%/+5%/+2%/+3% (using Bloomberg consensus estimates for the non-covered companies). Median growth is estimated at +7%/+10%/+10%/+10% YoY for Revenue/EBITDA/PBT/PAT. Sequentially, we expect weaker top-line (-3%) and earnings (-12%) in 1Q. Consensus estimates for MSCI India are +13%/-6% YoY for Revenue/PAT respectively.

For JPM covered companies, we approach earnings season expecting +8%/ +0%/-1%/-1% YoY growth in aggregate Revenue/EBITDA/PBT/PAT (estimated median Revenue/EBITDA/PBT/PAT of +9%/+12%/+13%/ +13% YoY). Sequentially, we expect weaker top-line (-2%) and earnings (- 11%) in 1Q. On an aggregate basis, we expect 30 bps QoQ margin expansion. See Table 4, Table 5 and Table 6 for company estimates. • By sector, we expect the following trends:

(i) Financials: We expect earnings prints across lending financials to be sequentially weaker in 1Q (our coverage’s cumulative net income: -4% q/ q; and +10% y/y) given headwinds from slower CASA growth and elevated LDRs. FY25 is likely to see a full year relative NIM decline vs. FY24 - RBI’s data on system spreads has continued to show a decline in 1Q. However, flex on ROA remains courtesy primarily of opex and that’s a lever we expect banks to pull to keep returns at target levels. Given a sanguine environment for credit, we expect growth/ EPS revision to be the primary driver for stock prices hereon over ROE. We stick to our two primary thematics of “Capex over Consumption” and “Saver to Investor”. Key stocks where we think the earnings delta can be better on higher growth are ICICIBC/ KMB / CIFC/ SBIN/ SHFL and HDFCAMC.

(ii) IT Services: We believe India Techs should report a minor improvement in momentum supported by large deals and seasonality with limited signs of discretionary spend recovery in line with our recent channel checks, JPM’s CIO Survey and ACN’s better than feared numbers.

While we expect some pick-up in growth momentum this quarter at PSYS/INFO/ LTIM from large deal ramps, we think short cycle discretionary deals could continue to underwhelm, providing limited room for optimism about a sustainable recovery just yet, and don’t expect any upgrades to FY guides. That said, the set up for the sector appears vulnerable to sharp moves on earnings/growth beats or misses this time. The recent run up in NSEIT (up 15% vs NIFTY 5%) over the last month means stocks are trading at the upper end of valuation bands that raises the bar on beats and can drive sharp negative reactions on misses. Among scale firms, we believe INFO/LTIM should lead, with HCLT/WPRO/TECHM laggards. For midcaps, we could see potential positive surprises at PSYS/KPITTECH with CYL/MPHL laggards. Into 1Q earnings, our top picks are INFO/TCS/LTIM/PSYS/KPITTECH, and key avoids are WPRO/TECHM/CYL/MPHL/COFORGE. Longer term, we prefer defensive names INFO/TCS and growth champions PSYS/COFORGE/LTTS - all OW.

(iii) Consumer Staples & QSR: In the staples space, we estimate Q1FY25 demand trends to be largely stable with some improvement for rural. We expect the FMCG pack to register low to mid single-digit vol growth, largely steady vs the March quarter. EBITDA margins should continue improving y/y, but with moderating gains as advertising spend remains firm and the base catches up. For our staples universe, we forecast \~7% revenue and \~10% EPS growth (avg). NIFTY FMCG Index has narrowed its relative underperformance post national election results amidst expectations of increased government spend on reviving the rural economy. Hence the budget narrative will be closely watched. Our preferred picks going into Q1 print are: a) GCPL b) HUVR c) NEST d) CLGT. In the QSR space, Q1FY25 should see mixed trends on comps trajectory with pizza players witnessing improving SSSG/LFL trends (vs Q4), while comps of chicken/burger players will likely be stable to slightly weaker. Low ticket discretionary spending continues to be challenged. Channel mix should incrementally move in favour of delivery, further helped by hot weather conditions. The focus on the value layer continues to increase with free delivery (JUBI) and new menu innovation (WESTLIFE, Dominos Rs99 lunch) in order to drive transactions. Restaurant operating margins should be lower y/y across the pack (although better q/q) on account of store level operating deleverage. Even as the pace of recovery continues to be uncertain, we believe a weaker base and own interventions should support comps momentum through FY25.

(iv) Metals & Mining: We forecast India Metals and Mining names to post a mixed bag of earnings in 1QFY25. We continue to see tailwinds for Aluminum producers (HNDL, VEDL), and expect both companies to post strong YoY and QoQ EBITDA growth, given the sharp rally in LME prices. Ferrous companies should post divergent results, with SAIL benefitting from improved long product pricing and lower coking coal costs, while JSTL/TATA’s profitability could be impacted due to a combination of flattish ASP (HRC), adverse operating leverage (planned maintenance) and potentially higher iron ore costs (for JSTL due to NMDC’s price hikes). We expect domestic steel prices to be range-bound and our J.P. Morgan colleagues expect China steel exports to remain at a high level in 2H24 due to weak domestic demand. However, we see an improved 2HFY25 for JSTL/TATA due to volume ramp-up from ongoing capacity commissioning and supportive raw material costs. We like Hindalco going into earnings and forecast 26% YoY consolidated EBITDA growth in 1Q, driven largely by strong performance in India Aluminium. Within our coverage, HNDL and JSTL remain our preferred picks.

C (v) Pharma & Hospitals: Pharma companies are expected to report a decent quarter with Rev/EBITDA/PAT growth of 10%/11%/18% YoY. We forecast aggregate EBITDA margins to remain flat YoY as improvement in gross margins is likely to get offset by higher R&D spends. We forecast domestic revenues to grow by 11% YoY for our covered universe driven by strong growth in the chronic segment and improved MR productivity. Q1 is a seasonally soft quarter due to the lower incidence of medical cases and elective procedures (due to holidays). Margins are also comparatively lower due to negative operating leverage and impact of annual salary increments. In the Hospitals space, we expect average occupancy to marginally improve by 50bps YoY as impact of new beds/acquired beds will likely weigh on Rainbow/Max. We expect overall sector margin to improve by 130bps YoY (64bps for hospitals business) driven by operating leverage and improved cost structure. Among our covered names, we expect Fortis to report a strong quarter and Rainbow to report a soft quarter. Our top picks are Max Healthcare, Rainbow Children’s Hospitals and Apollo Hospitals.

(vi) Autos & Energy: We expect Autos to post top-line and earnings growth of +10%/+23% YoY respectively, while sequentially we see earnings decrease by 37% after a robust last quarter. 1QFY25 will be weak for most OMCs due to weaker GRM and also qoq lower auto-fuel marketing margins. In Energy, top-line is estimated to have a moderate +7% growth while PAT should decline by 14% YoY. See Figure 1 for more updates at sector level.

Over the last one month, MSCI India’s consensus earnings expectations for 2024/25 were marginally revised down by 0.2% and 0.1% respectively. A higher number of companies in Communication Services and Banks had earnings revised upwards. For FY25, Motherson, M&M, Sundaram Finance, HNAL, BHEL, Indigo, Ashok Leyland, Cummins, BHE, NMDC, Vedanta, and Hindalco witnessed greater than 5% earnings revisions (Table 1, Table 2 and Table 3 . Overall, for 2024 consensus earnings estimates, Communication Services, Real Estate and Banks had >1% upward revisions while Insurance, Utilities and Industrials had downward revisions of <-1% (Figure 2). MSCI India consensus earnings growth for CY24/25 is 11%/16% respectively.

Macro backdrop: While core inflation remained benign and is at a multi-year low, delay of the monsoon has meant an extended heat wave in parts of the country and this has pushed up food prices which will keep headline CPI sticky at \~5% for the next few months. Apr and May saw GST collections of INR 2.10tn (+12.4% YoY) and 1.73tn (+10.0% YoY) respectively, while Mar quarter’s GST collections average INR 1.74tn. Industrial production came in at 5.0% YoY in April 2024, from an upwardly revised +5.4% growth in the previous month. Industrial production rose by 5.9% in FY24 (vs 5.3% for FY23). Manufacturing PMI averaged 58.2 vs 57.5 the previous quarter, and Composite PMI averaged 61.0 vs 61.2. INR was flattish QoQ and ended the June quarter at 83.39/\$ (vs. 83.40/\$ at March quarter-end). Brent crude price averaged \$84.9/bbl in 1QFY25, up 0.2% QoQ (Mar quarter average \$84.8/bbl), while 10Y yields averaged 7.08% in the June quarter (vs 7.11% in Mar quarter).
