# Key Agency Revisions - February 2024

We summarise February forecasts from the IEA, EIA and OPEC. The magnitude of supply downgrades exceeds that of demand downgrades this month, leaving balances slightly tighter MoM. Revisions to US supply growth estimates were a common theme, with changes mostly to the downside.

## Key Takeaways

一 IEA – Raised its non-OPEC supply growth forecast by 130 kb/d, left demand estimates largely unchanged. Still sees global demand at 103.0 mb/d in 2024.

OPEC – Kept estimates unchanged for demand in 2024 and lowered non-OPEC supply growth estimate by -150 kb/d. Still sees global demand at 104.4 mb/d in 2024.

■ EIA – Left global demand largely unchanged but dropped its US supply growth estimate by a significant 140 kb/d. Now sees global demand at 102.4 mb/d in 2024.

一 Summary – Downward revisions to supply but demand mostly unchanged leaves market balance estimates for 2024 a fraction tighter MoM.

This report summarises estimates from the IEA, OPEC and the EIA, and compares them with our latest estimates from The Oil Manual: Cautiously Optimistic (Part 1).

January saw global 2024 demand growth estimates remain largely unchanged across all three agencies. The IEA and OPEC tweaked their forecasts down by -20 kb/d and -10 kb/d respectively, while the EIA increased its projection by +30 kb/d. The IEA's and OPEC demand growth estimates for 2024 continue to differ by over 1 mb/d. The minor negative adjustments seen by the IEA and OPEC reflect a tougher macroeconomic outlook weighing on oil demand in 2024, particularly in Europe. The EIA's small demand growth upgrade for 2024 was also not particularly positive, as it derived from downgrades to both 2023 and 2024 estimates, with the 2023 negative demand revisions exceeding those to 2024 numbers (-80 kb/d in 2023 vs – 50 kb/d in 2024).

The EIA has become slightly more bullish on demand growth in 2025, raising its estimate by +80 kb/d to 1.29 mb/d of growth. This is still considerably less than OPEC's forecast of 1.85 mb/d of demand growth in 2025, which remained unchanged from last month's report. The upgrade was largely based on raising the 2025 demand growth estimate for the US by +100 kb/d, going from almost zero in January's report to +0.11 mb/d of expected growth in February's report.

OPEC-12 crude oil output decreased by 350 kb/d over January according to OPEC secondary sources, led by large production declines in Libya (-160 kb/d), Kuwait (-110 kb/ d) and Iraq (-90 kb/d). Direct reporting from OPEC members was also consistent with the secondary source data this month. The IEA data are also in close agreement, recording a 330 kb/d drop in OPEC-12 production MoM. The drops in production arose from protests in Libya shutting in the major Sharara oil field, and compliance with OPEC cuts in the case of Iraq and Kuwait. Whilst this was stronger compliance than had previously been seen in these countries, OPEC cuts were still lower than the implied target of \~550 kb/d of cuts in January.

February saw a mixed bag of revisions for 2024 non-OPEC supply growth. Both OPEC and the EIA made sizeable downward revisions to their supply growth estimates of -150 kb/d and -80 kb/d, respectively. The EIA's downgrade was a result of it cutting its US supply growth estimate by 140 kb/d for 2024, now seeing only 240 kb/ of US supply growth in 2024. The reason for the change to OPEC's 2024 non-OPEC supply growth estimate mainly lies in upward revisions to 2023 data, particularly for the US, then downgrading 2024 US supply estimates. The IEA, on the other hand, raised its non-OPEC supply growth estimate for the second month in a row, upping it by +130 kb/d, driven by higher expected growth in Norway and the US by +60 kb/d each in 2024. A wide range of estimates now exists for 2024 non-OPEC supply growth, with the EIA estimating only 0.76 mb/d of growth compared to the IEA, which forecasts twice that level of growth at an +1.45 mb/d. This compares to August last year, when the estimates of all 3 agencies fell within the 1.2-1.4 mb/d range. One of the major differences is the EIA's considerably lower expectation for US supply growth.

For 2025, OPEC has left its estimates unchanged, while the EIA has made a significant upwards revision of +280 kb/d. This is due to delaying a significant amount of US growth from 2024 into 2025.

The EIA (the only agency to forecast OPEC production) has raised its 2024 OPEC production forecast by a marginal 30 kb/d MoM. The IEA saw OPEC+ spare capacity rise +0.3 mb/d MoM to now stand at 5.7 mb/d, with Saudi Arabia holding 55% of this spare capacity. Saudi's share of the spare capacity has shrunk 5% MoM as Kuwait and Iraq have stepped up production cuts.

Overall, February estimates for the market in 2024 are fractionally tighter MoM. The direction of revisions, small though they may be, is mainly to the downside for both supply and demand. However, the magnitude of downward supply revisions is greater. Consequently, this leaves the balance of the market for this year slightly tighter. The minor tweaks to demand estimates have mainly been in the face of slightly tougher than expected macroeconomic conditions, as GDP growth forecasts also see downward revisions. US supply growth estimates have been a contentious point this month, with both OPEC and the EIA dropping their forecasts while the IEA raised its projection. Changes to US forecasts have partly been spurred by the large, unexpected production drop-off seen in January due to a severe weather spell in the US.
