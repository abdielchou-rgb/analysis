# Global Equity Strategy Quantitative Framework Update

Each month, we update key parts of our quantitative framework:

1) Scorecards - Our individual scorecards rank sectors based on valuations, sensitivity to our proposed macro scenario, earnings momentum, price momentum (where extreme), positioning, and quality fundamentals. The composite scorecard combines all these factors into one overall score.

2) Earnings versus Trend - We look at the EPS versus trend to evaluate one element of earnings risk as well as the fit with trend.

Regions: Our most preferred regions are Cont. Europe and parts of GEM. GEM moved up 1 place to be number 2 on the scorecard. Europe ranks a close third. Japan Unhedged remains number 1 on our scorecard and US is bottom for the second month in a row.

Sectors: In general, we are overweight 'growth' defensives, with software (focusing on the hyperscalers) followed by healthcare equipment being our most preferred sectors (and smaller areas, such as European defence, industrial gases and T&D-related utilities). Globally, healthcare equipment is at the top of the scorecard. On the cyclical side, we like copper, UK retailing, European/Japanese banks and tyres.

Healthcare equipment and banks are the top two sectors on our global scorecard.

Some of our least favoured sectors are luxury, global autos, capital goods, telecoms and energy. Luxury is abnormally oversold so a tactical bounce could happen but that is all in our opinion.

Notable changes are semis which has fallen 9 places to be third bottom and energy falling 7 places. Notable climbers are beverages.

Style: Semis have become exceptionally overbought (4.4 std), luxury and chemicals very oversold.

Market Direction: We do not cover market direction in this scorecard. Our Global MSCI AC World year-end target is 830 (with the risk on the upside), see here.

## Summary:

## Regional Scorecards:

Notable changes this month were GEM, up 1 place to 2nd (based on monetary conditions and sharp improvement in relative economic momentum) and Europe ex UK, down 1 place (owing to a deterioration in relative economic momentum) to 3rd. Cont. Europe moved up 1 place to score top of the valuation scorecard followed by the UK. The US has now been bottom ranked for the past two months. Japan ranks top (fuelled by monetary conditions and earnings momentum) - the danger is that an October rate hike will cause monetary conditions to deteriorate.

## Global Sector Scorecards:

 Overall Scorecard: Healthcare Equipment ranks top followed by Banks. Beverages rose the most over the past month moving up 8 ranks to 7th. Semiconductors was the biggest faller over the last month, falling 9 ranks to 25th and Food Retail ranked bottom (unchanged).

 Valuation: Food Producers (2.4std cheap) and Beverages are the cheapest two sectors. Semiconductors and Food Retail are at the bottom (2.8std and 1.2 std expensive). The cheapest sector on P/E relatives is now beverages (2.4std cheap) and most expensive is semis (2.2std above its norm).

 Macro: Autos is the biggest beneficiary of our macro scenario (rising markets and PMIs, while inflation, bond yields and currency are neutral), followed by Capital Goods. Food Producers and Pharmaceuticals ranked at the bottom.

Earnings Momentum: Pulp & Paper moves up 1 place to be top followed by Retailing up 2 places. Autos saw the biggest decline from the last month, falling 14 ranks to 26th and ranked second bottom. Metals & Mining rose the most, moving up 12 places (to number 4).

 Price Momentum: Transportation is the most oversold at 4 standard deviations, while the most overbought is Semiconductors at 4.3 std dev.

 Net buy recommendations by analysts: Pharmaceuticals and Software are the biggest consensus shorts.

 Quality Fundamentals: Semiconductors and Healthcare Equipment rank best. Chemicals fell 2 places to be second bottom, Pulp & Paper was bottom.

## European Sector Scorecards:

Overall scorecard: Banks ranks top followed by Autos. Insurance rose the most over the past month moving up 9 ranks to 5th and Metals & Mining rose 8 places. Pharma was the biggest faller over the past month (falling 9 ranks) and Diversified Financials ranked bottom.

 Valuation: Tobacco (unchanged), Food Retailing (up 2 places) and Autos are the cheapest three sectors. Capital Goods (1.6 std expensive) and Pharmaceuticals (down 8 places and 0.9 std expensive) are the most expensive two sectors. Banks at 2 std cheap on P/E relatives is still cheaper than any other sector on P/E relatives.

 Macro: Capital Goods is the biggest beneficiary of our macro scenario (rising Markets and PMIs; and neutral on inflation, bond yields and Currency); followed by Autos. Pharmaceuticals & Food Producers ranked at the bottom sequentially.

Earnings Momentum: Pulp & Paper leads with the highest 12M fwd EPS change and earnings breadth. This is followed by construction materials (down from top) and then food Retailing (up 17 places). Semiconductors saw the biggest decline from the last month, falling 11 ranks to 20th. Tobacco and Autos (down 7 places) rank bottom.

 Price Momentum: Luxury is the most oversold sector (1.8 std). The most overbought is Retailing (1.3 std dev).

 Net buy recommendations by analysts: Technology Hardware and Food Producers are the biggest consensus shorts, while Beverages and Chemicals are

the consensus longs.

 Quality Fundamentals: Pharmaceuticals and Semiconductors rank the best while Tobacco fell to the bottom.

## Regional Risk Appetite

Back to contents

Our Risk Appetite scorecard ranks regions based on equity risk appetite (reward for higher volatility sectors). We look at the rolling cross-sectional 6m linear regression coefficient of average return on standard deviation for each region. We work out the same for global sectors and subtract it from the absolute risk appetite of each region. Finally, we standardise relative risk appetites against their five-year history. Regions with lower risk appetite relative to global equity markets relative to history are ranked at the top.
