## April 29

Acute JPY strength prompts MoF intervention talk; Tsys bullflatten through borrowing estimates; OATs and BTPs outperform, Bonos lag; SEK lags on growth contraction; ECB's Wunsch cautions on cut signals; CNH gains with local equities; DXY at 105.64 (-0.3%); US 10y at 4.614% (-4.9bp).

• Acute JPY strength prompts discussion on intervention by the Japanese Ministry of Finance; media reports later suggest intervention occurred; USD/JPY (-1.3%) retreats from session highs near 160.

• Treasuries bull-flatten as a rally prevails through larger privatelyheld net marketable borrowing estimates, with focus on unchanged coupons at May refunding and downside risks from a QT taper.

• OATs outperform Bonos against Bunds, with Spanish underperformance likely a result of political uncertainty; 10y BTPs tighten 2.4bp against 10y Bunds.

• SEK lags despite the resilient risk backdrop as growth in the Swedish economy contracts by 0.3% m/m in both March and February, after a 0.4ppt downward revision.

• ECB Governing Council Member Wunsch expresses caution about sending a signal to market participants for a second consecutive rate cut in July.

• CNH (+0.3%) gains against the US dollar alongside a rally in local equities (CSI 300: +1.11%) with support from large northbound inflows (RMB13.6bn).

At 1:00pm JST, a precipitous drop in USD/JPY and broader JPY strength invoked discussion of longawaited FX intervention by the Japanese Ministry of Finance. MoF officials wouldn't confirm whether actual yen-buying intervention occurred but media reports later suggested intervention had occurred. Official disclosure from the MoF will only come at the end of May.

Macro markets had a lengthy wait until Treasury’s 3:00pm ET quarterly financing estimates for the next risk event. 1Q24 privately-held net marketable borrowing needs realized at \$748bn, below the estimate of \$760bn made in January. This was driven by higher cash receipts and lower outlays, despite a \$25bn higher ending TGA cash balance.

2Q24 privately-held net marketable borrowing estimates were revised up to \$243bn (P: \$202bn), given projections of lower cash receipts as the financing need increased by \$66bn. The TGA target for end-June was maintained at \$750bn and there was no assumption for a reduction in the pace of SOMA redemptions (Fed QT).

The larger financing need could have been to account for a presumed start of Treasury buybacks in the May quarter, which will be a maximum of \$60bn across Liquidity Support and Cash Management buybacks. The term “lower cash receipts” heavily depends on the perspective upon which the cash receipts projection was made. Cash receipts may not have been as high as projected relative to April 2022, or they may not have been as high as projected to also account for the additional cash outlays to fund Treasury buybacks.

The 3Q24 privately-held net marketable borrowing estimate was \$847bn. Treasury also did not assume a reduction in the pace of SOMA redemptions (Fed QT). Treasury also set an end-September target for the TGA at \$850bn (end-June: \$750bn).

At the May refunding announcement, at 8:30am ET on Wednesday, we still expect no change in Treasury coupon sizes given guidance from February that nominal coupons and FRNs would be unchanged “for at least the next several quarters.” We believe unexpected variations in borrowing needs will be addressed through changes to bill supply.

That includes less bill supply from lower borrowing needs should the Fed announce the decision to lower the monthly run-off cap on Treasury securities to \$30bn from \$60bn at the May FOMC meeting, as in our base case (for more, read here).

Treasuries ultimately bull-flattened as a rally prevailed through larger privately-held net marketable borrowing estimates and focused turned to May refunding, as well as an improving fiscal picture. The trailing 12m projected financing needs through September 2024 are \$1.367tn, this is 21% below the trailing 12m financing needs in the period ended September 2023. The July-September 2024 financing need of \$486bn is 7% below the prior-year period.

An initial drop in the S&P (+0.32%) on the higher-than-expected borrowing estimates was quickly reversed as the index closed the session above its 3:00pm ET level. JPY maintained its broad strength and was the clear outperformer in the G10, with USD/JPY (-1.3%) sharply lower.

## Japan:

Despite no official confirmation from the Japanese Ministry of Finance, price action in CCY/JPY pairs suggests it may have intervened in FX markets to strengthen JPY on the basis that recent FX moves have been more excessive than fundamentals. USD/ JPY sold off aggressively three times during London hours, with each sell-off slightly smaller than the last, and each time with a small reversal as some participants attempted to buy the dip. Ultimately, USD/JPY closed the London session near the 155.85 level, down 1.6%.

## Europe:

GBP (+0.6%) strengthened, although there was significant divergence in the Scandinavian currencies; NOK was (+0.5%) and SEK (-0.1%) after weak Swedish GDP data, which indicated a potential fourth consecutive quarter of GDP contraction. EUR (+0.3%) gained despite soft nationwide CPI data. CHF (+0.5%) gained amid US dollar weakness, with the DXY USD Index down 0.4%.

European duration traded relatively strong on Monday as regional inflation was softer. 10y Bunds rallied \~4.3bp and closed at 2.53% as the curve bull-flattened, while the front-end remained anchored. Total pricing of cuts to December 2024 returned to -72bp (Friday: -67bp). During the rally, the reds pack in Euribors experienced some relief and outperformed on the strip. France outperformed against both Germany and Spain, helped by the unchanged rating from both Moody’s and Fitch on Friday, while Spain was likely weighed down by the uncertain political developments, which led to relative underperformance against peers. Tighter semi-core spreads helped performance of other country spreads against core; 10y BTPs tightened by 2.44bp against 10y Bunds to 132bp. In ASW, cash underperformed slightly against swaps as the German complex tightened in short and intermediate maturities.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar was broadly weaker, driven by lower US yields. The Antipodean currencies fared particularly well – NZD (+0.7%) and AUD (+0.6%) – while CAD (+0.2%) lagged its risk-sensitive peers.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies exhibited mixed moves amid the JPY rebound and broad gains in regional equities. CNH outperformed peers and more than reversed losses from Friday. USD/CNH weakened \~0.3%, back to the 7.25 handle as local equities (CSI 300: +1.1%) advanced alongside large northbound inflows (RMB13.6bn). USD/CNY remained steady. SGD also outperformed; USD/SGD fell 0.2% to 1.36 following weakness in USD/JPY and USD/CNH. USD/KRW did not benefit from its usual correlation with USD/JPY or equity inflows and finished the AxJ session 0.2% higher at \~1,378. High yielders underperformed; IDR retraced all its gains from last week, 0.3% weaker to 16,255, and INR was 0.2% weaker to USD/INR 83.48.

AxJ rates were mixed. China was the key underperformer in the region, as 5y NDIRS yields spiked higher by 7bp, the most since July 2023, to 2.18%, the highest level

since late January. The 1s5s curve bear-steepened by 1bp. This came amid a rally in Chinese equities and media reports that the PBoC recently window-guided some regional banks to reduce duration and leverage on bond holdings. The 2s10s CGB curve bear-flattened by 3bp. Indonesia rates also underperformed; 10y INDOGB sold off 4bp to 7.20%, a level last reached in late October. Hong Kong and Korea front-end swap yields rose 2-3bp as curves bear-flattened. Singapore front-end swap yields fell by 1-2bp, and the curve bull-flattened. In India, the NDOIS curve fell 1-2bp, while the bond market remained stable.

## Latin America:

Most LatAm currencies were stronger as the US dollar weakened. CLP (+0.9%) led peers as copper prices rose \~2%, while COP (+0.9%) followed close behind despite lower oil prices. MXN (+0.8%) was stronger, although it failed to break below the 17.00 level, and closed at 17.03, while BRL (+0.1%) underperformed peers. LatAm rates rallied; the IBR curve outperformed, \~9bp lower across the curve. TIIEs traded lower than USTs in the front-end (1y: -3bp), and DIs marginally bull-steepened (January 2034s/2026s: +4bp) despite few local developments.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• ECB Governing Council Member Wunsch said the ECB should be cautious about the signal a second consecutive rate cut in July would send to market participants. He added pursuing back-to-back cuts would signal to the market that the ECB would cut at every meeting, which could lead to a repricing that may go too far

• ECB Governing Council Member Muller reiterated the ECB would likely cut rates in June

• ECB Governing Council Member Knot remarked the US inflation experience calls for vigilance and the 1Q24 wage data release will help in determine policy direction. Knot said rate path beyond June is too early to discuss

## Emerging Markets

• PBoC pledged to promote legislation in key and emerging areas of the financial industry, step up enforcement, and bring all financial activities under supervision. The central bank also pledged to further improve the social credit system and steadily promote the prevention of financial risks in key areas

• According to media reports, PBoC advised some regional banks to reduce duration and leverage on their bond holdings and avoid significant exposure to ultra-long bond investments in order to mitigate risks

• BoK Governor Rhee noted that how the better-than-expected Korea 1Q GDP growth will be reflected in the May economic outlook remains to be determined, when asked if it is difficult to exclude a rate hike

• BoT Governor Sethaput said the current policy rate is supportive of the economic recovery and consistent with trying to achieve an orderly deleveraging. He acknowledged it has been a tough balancing act trying to manage a weak economic recovery and monetary policy, but pointed out it is not the rate-sensitive components driving the sluggish growth. He noted the BoT expects inflation to gradually pick up back into the target range by the end of the year

• CBC disclosed overseas funds repatriated by Taiwanese firms to a special tax-break account stand at US\$4.2bn, or more than TWD138bn. These funds will be available for withdrawal in three years after the lock-up period expires in August. Based on this, approximately up to TWD40bn of funds per year can be freely invested in various assets, including the property market

• RBI kept the investment limit for foreign market participants in government bonds unchanged at 6% of outstanding stock for FY24/25. It kept the limit for state government bonds at 2%, and for corporate bonds at 15% of outstanding stock. The aggregate limit of notional amount of CDS sold by foreigners will be 5% of the outstanding stock of corporate bonds

• BCCh Governor Costa emphasized the committee's monetary policy corridor allows for different outcomes and flexibility in policy rate decisions depending on how situations evolve. While she did note the progress on inflation so far and that the policy rate will continue to fall, she also highlighted the pace needs to be cautious as macro environments are more uncertain. While the tone may be interpreted as hawkish, we think it is unlikely this would cause a large rally in CAM, as markets have priced in a terminal rate \~110bp higher than the central bank's neutral rate (4%)

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
