# EM Technical Watch: Cash, Meet Primary

Supply continues: EM sovereign supply has continued its very strong momentum, continuing to be at record YTD sovereign issuance at US\$65.6bn YTD, 38.8% of our total issuance expectation for this year and US\$12.5bn above issuance at this point last year. Issuance has continued to be met with extremely strong demand, with new issue concessions averaging 2bp for IG issuers and -7bp for HY issuers. New issues have also performed very well post-issuance, which we see as contributing to strong fund alpha in the past two months, of 20bp and 55bp, respectively. HY issuance has come back with five HY issuers having come to market in February. Kenya's issuance with a coupon of 9.75% was a positive sign for market access for lower-rated credits going forward. We also highlight Panama's three-tranche issuance that was met with very strong demand despite a weak fundamental path and caused the existing curve to rally in the secondary market. Potential upcoming issuers include Croatia, Abu Dhabi, the Philippines, and Qatar in the IG space, and Oman, the Dominican Republic, Serbia, Uzbekistan, and Colombia in the HY space.

Drawing down cash balances: Funds started this month having already drawn down on cash balances considerably after January's high levels of issuance. Funds reduced cash balances by 1.2pp and 2.5pp on an unweighted and weighted basis, respectively, in January, ending the month below 4y averages for the first time in over six months. Funds increased exposure to IG credits substantially, by 1.3pp to 16.44% UW, likely a product of issuance. Exposure to HY remained at 5.40% OW, below 4y average of 5.6% OW. This likely aided in demand for HY issuances this month. Exposure to Saudi Arabia (+0.65pp to 2.35% UW), Brazil (+0.51pp to 0.80% OW), and Mexico (+0.26pp to 0.33% UW), all of which were January issuers, increased the most. While the largest country reductions came from Hungary (-0.27pp to 0.09% OW), Egypt (-0.22pp to 0.35% OW), and the Dominican Republic (-0.20pp to 0.29% OW).

Local sees small inflows, hard currency still waiting for its allocation: Over the last four weeks, EM saw outflows rise to US\$2.0bn as core yields continued to drift higher once again. This was mainly driven by hard currency funds and blended hard/ local currency funds, whilst local currency funds continued to see inflows. These outflows picked up over the past week, with US\$1.1bn leaving hard currency funds. Within hard currency, the key outflow driver were sovereign funds, whilst corporate and blended sovereign/corporate fund flows held up with better performance. The most-asked question in our client meetings nowadays is when EM flows will return. To answer this, we return to our flows nowcasting model, which does not currently forecast flows, with mediocre total returns year-to-date – despite the spread rally – and middling risk demand over the past weeks constraining inflow predictions. Further clarity around the pace and depth of the Fed cutting cycle and an associated decrease in core yields would likely be key to improving these individual factors.

In 2024, the average expected weekly flow has been nearly flat at just US\$90m in inflows. Why are inflows not predicted for now? The auto-correlative factor, clearly, is one reason. However, lacklustre total returns given the core yield retracement (even with the spread rally) are another, and whilst financial conditions are in aggregate easing, Morgan Stanley's Risk Demand Index is only just now recovering to the recent highs recorded in January, June, and September 2023. Further clarity around the pace and depth of the Fed cutting cycle and an associated decrease in core yields would likely be key to improving these individual factors further. At that point, however, flows that come into EM may come too late to capture the full extent of the recovery in total returns.

## Hard currency technicals

Record supply: EM sovereign credit supply stands at US\$65.6bn YTD, 38.8% of our total issuance expectations for this year ( Exhibit 19 ). This compares to US\$53.1bn issued at this point last year. EM gross supply, i.e., including both sovereigns and corporates, stands at US\$143.8bn (see EM Hard Currency Credit Supply ).

• Sovereign issuers this month included Paraguay (US\$500m 2036, US\$500m 2031), Hong Kong (HK\$2.0bn 2026, CNH1.5bn 2026, US\$200m 2026, €80m 2026), Lithuania (€1.5bn 2034), Benin (US\$750m 2038), Bahrain (US\$1.0bn 2031, US\$1.0bn 2036), Turkey (US\$2.75bn 2034), Kenya (US\$1.5bn 2031s), Romania (€2.0bn 2031s, €2.0bn 2036 green), Panama (US\$1.10bn 03/2031, US\$1.25bn 03/2038, US\$0.75bn 03/2057).

• Most sovereign issuances this month were denominated in USD (58%) and EUR (32%), with the remainder denominated in BRL (7%), HKD, and CNH.

• The bulk of sovereign issuances came out of CEEMEA (77%), followed by LatAm (19%), and then Asia (4%).

• Three quasi-sovereign issuers came to market this month, out of Poland (BGOSK), Oman (MAZOON), and Saudi Arabia (PIFKSA).

• New issue concessions averaged 2bp for IG sovereign issuances and -7bp for HY sovereign issuances.

## Drawing Down on Cash

• Fund cash balances as of end-January calculated on a simple average basis were 5.1%, down 1.2pp compared to end-December, now below the four-year average of 5.7%. Cash balances on an AUM-weighted average basis decreased by 2.5pp in January to 5.6%, also below its four-year average of 6.4%.

• By rating, the aggregate HY overweight stayed flat in January at 5.40% OW, still below the four-year average of 5.58% OW. Aggregate IG positioning increased by 1.31pp to 16.44% UW, now above the four-year average of 16.85% UW.

• Within IG credits, exposure to A credits increased by 1.13pp to 13.13% UW, and exposure to BBB credits increased by 0.18pp to 3.30% UW. Within HY, exposure to BB credits increased by 0.40pp to 4.49% OW, that to B credits declined by 0.20pp to 1.58% UW, and that to CCC and lower-rated credits declined by 0.23pp to 2.49% OW (see EM Credit Fund Positioning ). Note that the remaining balance – once cash, IG, and HY allocations are accounted for – currently 5.92%, is made up of "other" holdings by funds. While this can skew outright numbers, the allocation trends are more important.

• By country, exposure to Saudi Arabia (+0.65pp to 2.35% UW), Brazil (+0.51pp to 0.80% OW), and Mexico (+0.26pp to 0.33% UW) increased the most. The largest country reductions came from Hungary (-0.27pp to 0.09% OW), Egypt (-0.22pp to 0.35% OW), and the Dominican Republic (-0.20pp to 0.29% OW).

• South Africa is still the largest overweight country (-0.11pp to 1.13% OW), followed by the Ivory Coast (+0.09pp OW to 1.11% OW) and Brazil (+0.51pp to 0.80% OW). China (+0.23pp to 3.77% UW) continues to be the largest underweight country, followed by the United Arab Emirates (+0.06pp to 2.87% UW) and Saudi Arabia (+0.65pp to 2.35% UW).

## HY Supply Back

Fund positioning reflects strong demand for new issues in January. As was seen last month, funds entered the year with very high cash balances, which was likely funds preparing to absorb the expected new issuance in the first months of this year. January's fund positioning data reflects this, showing significant declines in cash balances, which now sit below the 4y average for the first time since mid-2023 ( Exhibit 3 ). Fund positions in sovereigns that issued in January, including Saudi Arabia, Brazil, and Mexico, increased the most, further showing the market appetite for these issuances. This is consistent with the strong demand and performance of new issues we saw last month.

Despite funds entering February with relatively low cash balances and seeing ongoing outflows, this new supply has been well-absorbed by the market. This month, supply has continued to come at the highest pace in recent history ( Exhibit 4 ). We have seen nearly no new issuance concessions and strong performance post-issuance, especially on HY supply. Still, anecdotal evidence suggests that funds have resisted selling bonds despite this backdrop, which could suggest that they have continued to draw down on cash throughout this month instead to fund orders in the primary market. We see funds adding new issues in excess of their index weights as being a primary driver of fund alpha in the past couple of months, which was high at 20bp and 55bp, respectively (see EM Credit Fund Performance ).

We also note that HY issuers have returned to the market and have been met with very strong demand. This month saw five HY issuers come to market: Paraguay, Benin,

Bahrain, Turkey, and Kenya. On average, HY new issues have come 7bp tight to our assessment of fair value, implying a new issue concession of -7bp. This is the lowest average monthly new issue concession for HY names in recent history ( Exhibit 5 ). Despite often pricing tight to where the secondary curve opened on the day of issuance, new bonds have performed very strongly in the secondary market ( Exhibit 6 ). Funds still had low exposure to HY names compared with history at the start of this month (5.4% OW versus a 4y average of 5.58% OW, which likely aided with demand for new issues.

As outlined in our latest Global EM Strategist, we see Kenya's ability to price, albeit at 10%+ yield with a coupon of 9.75%, as a good sign for EM as it suggests that the door is opening for lower-rated issuers that had lost market access to come to the market. In this bucket of single Bs yielding over 10%, we see Angola, Nigeria, El Salvador, and Egypt as potential contenders for issuance this year.

Another notable issuer this month was Panama, which is a credit with substantial risks, including a high likelihood of a downgrade to HY that few thought would be able to successfully come to market in a large size this year. It was able to come with a threetranche US\$3.1bn issuance that was over 6x oversubscribed and has caused the curve to outperform BBBs by over 20bp in the secondary market in the week post-issuance. Adjusting our initial fair value assessment for outperformance of the curve ahead of issuance, these came with an average new issue concessions of 10bp, low relative to the past year.
