# BoJ Policy Analysis (Trend Inflation): Our Thoughts on the Policymaker’s View

## Growing importance of trend inflation in assessing underlying inflation

In our recently published BoJ policy analysis report, we showed that the trend inflation rate has risen to roughly 1.5% and cited the growing importance of this rate in assessing underlying inflation ( Exhibit 1 , BoJ Watch: April BoJ Outlook Report: Our Thoughts on the Policymaker’s View about its Policy Analysis (1 May 2024)). We received many inquiries from investors on this point and hence additionally discuss our impression on policymaker's views for other BoJ trend inflation models too.

Our previous report addressed the importance of "model 1 estimates" in identifying structural change in corporate price- and wage-setting behaviors. Practically speaking, policymakers, however, need to consider the possibility of potential arbitrarity on analysis due to the model specification of structural models such as model 1. Thus it would makes sense in policy discussion to also discuss based on reduced-form models.

In this point, estimated trend inflation of "Model 3", a quasi-structural model that factors in reduced-form perspective, would be useful. The Bank’s Monetary Affairs Dept. originally developed the base of model 3, and by going back even further, the model premises itself on a model formulated in a joint project by various central banks under former Fed Chair Ben Bernanke and former IMF Chief Economist Olivier Blanchard.　Model 3 is based on a structural VAR model with the inflation rate, short-term and long-term inflation expectations, and the wage growth to estimate the trend inflation rate ( Exhibit 2 ).

## Model 3’s trend inflation estimate can be linked to spring wage negotiation results and may rise further once Shunto outcome is reflected in wage data

It would be important to note that the model 3 can link other important factors such as Shunto while reducing a potential arbitrarity arising from specific structural model setting. For example, the model 3 utilizes scheduled cash earnings in the monthly labor survey, which exhibits high correlation to base-pay increase from spring wage negotiations, as wage data. Thus, estimated trend inflation may rise further once strong wage hike results increasingly show up in actual data on scheduled cash earnings ( Exhibit 2 , Exhibit 3 ).

Since we forecast a rise in scheduled cash earnings growth to roughly 3% from 2Q toward this summer, the BoJ might already be expecting further increase in the trend inflation　going ahead. In fact, we think this is an important point in confirming the certainty of the structure change. As we discussed in our previous report, a rise in trend inflation by the model 1 is driven by decline in the probability of a 0% level and rise in the probability of a 1-3% level in the trend inflation rate　( Exhibit 4 ).

Furthermore, since the Bank’s Outlook Report has referred to services inflation and scheduled cash earnings as comparison indicators, it would be important to note that the Bank can potentially connect with a discussion of increase in trend inflation with a rise in service inflation too.　 ( Exhibit 5 )

## Allowing the BoJ to flexibly discuss with the Bank's key officials who have various preferences, relative decline in the importance of existing underlying inflation measures, such as the weighted mean value

We think the model 3 can reduce arbitrariness of trend inflation estimates that comes from model specification, given model 3’s property of balancing characteristics as a structural model and a reduced-form model. This is a valuable point in discussions among key officials who have various policy discussion preferences at the Bank. For example, even in policy discussion with BoJ officials who have concerns about the arbitrariness of estimate results in structural models and those who question the weakness of the theoretical foundation of reduced-form models, the Bank’s current set of estimation frameworks allows the Bank to flexibly discuss with those whose preferences are different, in our view.

We estimated main indicators in the BoJ's Measures of Underlying Inflation (MUI) (TBA: Tues, 28 May) ahead of the Bank, reflecting the April CPI print. In our estimate, the weighted median slowed further to +1.0% vs. +1.3% in March, continuing the downward shift from a peak of +1.9% in February. Our estimate shows that the share of items with price increases likely exceeded 80% again, but the trimmed-mean CPI likely fell below 2% for the first time in 20 months. However, BoJ Governor Ueda explained in a recent speech that the Bank has adopted a stance of putting more emphasis on the “three approaches to capturing underlying inflation" including an estimate based on its economic models, than existing MUI that it has disclosed monthly up to now (see our report: Japan Economics: Nationwide CPI (April 2024): Still No Sign of Reacceleration in Services Prices (24 May 2024)). Thus we think that model-based trend inflation would get more importance for assessing underlying inflation in monetary policy discussions.
