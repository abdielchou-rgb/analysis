## US Economic Perspectives April CPI recap: Progress resumes

## Core CPI monthly change below a year ago for 16th time in 18 months

 We believe the three key pandemic years (2020-2022) have made seasonal adjustment of the monthly inflation data problematic, so we revert to a simple metric used by equity analysts: results compared to the same period a year ago.

 Using this metric, progress toward lower inflation resumed in April after stalling in March. April marked the 16th time in past 18 months where the not-seasonallyadjusted core CPI one-month change (+28bp) was below the change 12 months prior (+47bp).

 Despite the progress, core CPI inflation is still running well above the level consistent with the FOMC's PCE inflation target. 12-month core CPI inflation has fallen from a peak of 6.6% in September 2022 to 3.7% in today's release — a considerable 2.9pp decline. However, another 1.4pp decline would be needed for core CPI inflation to be consistent with the FOMC's 2.0% PCE inflation target.

## Headline CPI +0.31%: 12-month inflation slips to 3.4%

The headline CPI increased 31bp in April with 12-month CPI inflation edging down to 3.4% as both the monthly and the 12-month change for the CPI continue to run elevated compared to pre-pandemic norms. Despite the continued strength the headline CPI increase in April was below both our expectations and those of consensus (UBS Econ projection 0.39%, UBS Nowcast 0.37%, Bloomberg consensus average 0.37%).

 In April energy prices rose 1.1% as rising gasoline prices more than offset falling prices for electricity and natural gas. Energy prices have ceased being a notable factor pushing down headline inflation as their contribution to overall inflation swung from -1½ pp in June of last year to a +¼ pp contribution currently.

Food prices were roughly unchanged in April with prices for food at home (i.e. groceries) declining and prices for food away from home (i.e. restaurants) picking up. Inflation for both food at home and food away from home have slowed considerably from their peaks with food at home inflation currently running below its pre-pandemic pace. Nonetheless, the pickup in April for food away from home prices (to a 35bp increase) was a bit of a concern after two moderate months that suggested food away from home prices could be returning to the pre-pandemic pace. Food away from home prices tend to be one of the better measures of the current inflation trend.

## Core CPI +0.29%: Almost no surprises

Core CPI prices rose 29bp seasonally-adjusted in April with the 12-month core CPI change slipping to 3.6% (3.61%) in April from 3.8% (3.80%) in March. April's seasonally-adjusted core CPI increase was just a smidge below both our expectations and those of consensus (UBS Econ projection 0.31%, UBS Nowcast 0.33%, consensus average 0.30%).

Among the components, owners' equivalent rent (OER) continues to increase solidly, with a 42bp increase in April following 44bp increases in the past two months. The disinflation in OER has stalled since August despite new lease rents running at or below their pre-pandemic pace for the past year and a half. We expected almost no slowing in OER this month as a result of seasonal adjustment factors that we think have been overly sensitive to the pandemic years. These seasonal factors pushed up the April seasonally-adjusted OER increase roughly 4bp more than would be the case using pre-pandemic seasonal factors. ## Preliminary April core PCE prices +0.24%

 Based on today’s CPI, we project headline PCE prices rose 25bp, and core PCE prices rose 24bp, in April (compared to our expectations for 26bp earlier this week). The increase in PCE prices is lower than the CPI due to a lower weight on OER in PCE prices, as well as differences in medical services, motor vehicle insurance, and airfares, among other components.

 Today’s estimate of PCE prices would put the 12-month change for headline PCE prices through April at 2.6% (2.65%). For core PCE, today’s estimates would put the 12-month change at 2.7% (2.74%) — down from 2.8% in March and down 2.9pp from a peak of 5.6% in February 2022 and the lowest since April 2021.

 However, tomorrow's import price release, along with adjustments for some categories not published in today’s CPI (legal services, leased cars, repair of household furnishings), could alter these PCE price estimates.

## Smaller increases expected in May

 Retail gasoline prices have been falling since the start of May — and falling crude prices suggest the decline has further to run. As a result we project a modest increase of around 10bp for the headline CPI (seasonally adjusted) in the May report released next month. Currently we also project a slightly smaller core CPI increase in May (27bp) than in today's report (29bp) as vehicle insurance is expected to slow further.

12-month core CPI inflation is projected to edge down a handful of basis points in May, but remain at 3.6% to one-decmial place. Headline CPI inflation is also expected to remain at its current level (3.4%) in next month's report. In general, we expected core CPI inflation to trend down through the year amid increasing goods supply (particularly for motor vehicles), the pass-through of the slowdown in new lease rents into CPI rents, and an easing in labor pressures.

 We will review these forecasts as we go through the details of today's release and additional relevant data comes in.
