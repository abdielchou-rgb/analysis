# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## August 15

UST yields rise after strong retail sales and low jobless claims; Fed members are "open" to cutting rates soon; EGBs sell off amid improved outlook on US growth; local data support AUD and GBP; BSP cuts rates; strong US consumer supports equities; DXY at 103.05 (+0.5%); US 10y at 3.913% (+7.8bp).

• Broad-based strength in US retail sales and slowing jobless claims calm fears of a US recession and send USTs higher; USTs bearsteepen with a double-digit sell-off in front-end yields (2y UST: +14bp).

• Certain Fed members seemingly now support more imminent rate cuts in a tone shift, with St. Louis Fed President Musalem now saying it may soon be appropriate to adjust rates, and Atlanta Fed President Bostic saying he is “open” to a September cut.

• European duration sells off after strong data in the US ease growth concerns; thin liquidity amid local holidays exacerbates the move, and 2y Bunds close over 10bp higher.

• Improving risk sentiment and strong local data including a beat in July Australia job growth and solid UK 2Q24 GDP support GBP (+0.2%) and AUD (+0.2%) as they became the only two G10 currencies to strengthen against the US dollar for the day.

• With economists and market pricing split on their expectations, the BSP delivers a 25bp rate cut as the central bank expects inflation to trend downward within its target range despite the uptick in July data; USD/PHP rises 0.1%.

• Improving expectations around the strength of the US consumer following well above-consensus retail sales data support consumer US equities, with the S&P 500 up 1.6%, and strengthen the USD (DXY USD Index: +0.5%).

It was a busy day in terms of economic releases in the US, but in general the data demonstrated a strong picture of the US economy, which eased growth concerns. It also continued to support a gradual monetary policy easing.

Retail sales beat estimates and our economists' forecast with a robust release that showed broad-based strength with solid gains from electronics, motor vehicle sales, groceries, and home furnishing. The data showed that consumption remains solid, which helped curb fears that consumers are pulling back significantly.

In addition to the strength of the consumer, labor market data also pushed back against recession fears. US initial and continuing jobless claims ticked down, which continued to support the belief that the labor market may not be as weak as the July payrolls report suggested. Various other data releases also eased growth fears; the Empire State Manufacturing survey rose more than expected, and while the Philadelphia Fed Business Outlook index contracted, our economists note that on an ISM-adjusted basis, it remains in expansionary territory.

Unsurprisingly, USTs bear-steepened following the data releases with a double-digit sell-off in 2y yields. The 2s10s curve flattened by 6bp. Market pricing for Fed rate cuts repriced higher, with the implied probability of a 50bp cut in September falling to \~25% from \~36% on Wednesday, and over 10bp of cumulative easing in 2024 was priced out. The USD strengthened alongside the rise in yields, and equities rallied given the seeming strength of the US consumer.

Fedspeak on Wednesday showed a shift in tone following the continued disinflation trend. Atlanta Fed President Bostic, who earlier this week stated that he expected to cut rates "by the end of the year” given that he is looking for more data, now said that he is “open” to a September rate cut because the Fed cannot “afford to be late” in easing monetary policy. St. Louis Fed President Musalem said that the time may be nearing when it is appropriate to adjust rates, as the risk to both sides of the Fed’s mandate seem more balanced. This is in contrast to his remarks in June where he stated that it could take “quarters” for conditions to be in place for rate cuts.

The beat in retail sales data, which exhibited strength in the US consumer and eased fears of a weakening economy, was supportive of risk assets and US equities. The S&P 500 gained 1.7% as consumer discretionary, technology, and industrial stocks led the gains. Oil prices also rose on the back of the strong picture of the US economy, with WTI crude oil futures up 1.5%.

## Europe:

Strong retail sales data in the US strengthened the USD and drove European FX price action. Low-yielders underperformed and USD/CHF (+0.8%) traded back at levels before the downside surprise in non-farm payrolls on August 2. Robust UK 2Q24 GDP and improved risk sentiment supported GBP, which gained 0.2% against the USD.

European duration sold off as growth worries from the US subsided given the downtick in jobless claims and stronger-than-expected retail sales. The latest US data releases came out while most European markets were closed for a public holiday, so thin liquidity conditions likely exacerbated the moves. The 10y Bund sold off 8bp to 2.26% but outperformed on a cross-market basis. The US prints triggered a bear-flattening on the curve, with the front end leading the sell-off. Reds led the move higher during the day, and 1y1y rates slid as much as 12bp and completely reversed the gains posted so far in August.

On the curve, the 5y point underperformed on 2s5s10s while volatility continued to decline and 5s10s30s continued to richen. The data releases were supportive of a soft-landing scenario and a continuation of tighter EGBs spreads, with both OATs and BTPs tightening by \~2bp against Bunds.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar rallied and followed US yields higher, after a significant upside surprise in US retail sales. The DXY USD Index (+0.5%) rose back above 103.00. Improved risk sentiment in Australia following an upside surprise in employment growth resulted in a 0.2% gain in AUD against the USD.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies weakened slightly ahead of the US July retail sales release, which seemed like a sign of carry trades resuming after no major surprises from US CPI on Wednesday. USD/CNH gained 0.2% to 7.16 amid mixed China July activity data. MYR and IDR also underperformed; USD/MYR gained 0.4% given its high beta to USD/CNH, while USD/IDR gained 0.2% amid smaller-than-expected Indonesia July trade surplus. USD/PHP rose 0.1% after the BSP delivered a 25bp rate cut, which was not expected by some market participants. Ultimately, the pair finished the session largely unchanged, which was lower than where it was heading into the BSP decision. THB was the outperformer in the region, up 0.5% as the Pheu Thai-led coalition reportedly agreed to nominate Paetongtarn Shinawatra as candidate for prime minister on Friday.

AxJ rates saw mixed moves. China rates sold off; yields rose modestly by 1bp on 10y CGBs and 10y CNY NDIRS amid the mixed July data. 1y CNY NDIRS yields also rose slightly after the PBoC delayed its 1y MLF operation to August 26 as part of its effort to de-link MLF from loan prime rates. The PBoC opted to use the 7-day reverse repo to offset the impact of maturing MLF funds instead. Singapore rates underperformed; yields rose 3bp across the SGS curve, while the 2s10s IRS curve bear-flattened 1bp. In Indonesia, IndoGB yields fell across the curve, with 10y yield down 3bp, supporting the notion of carry trades resuming. In Thailand, 10y ThaiGB also rallied 2bp while 5y THB NDOIS yield rose 2bp amid reduced political uncertainty.

In CEEMEA FX, EUR/CZK was higher by 0.3%, EUR/HUF was up +0.1%, and EUR/ PLN was flat given the holiday. Risk sentiment in South Africa continued to improve following the US data, with USD/ZAR down -0.3%.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The Norges Bank maintained its policy rate at 4.50% at its August meeting (C: 4.50%). In line with expectations, the Board's focus was divided between currency weakness and softer-than-expected inflation data. These countering forces meant that Norges Bank stayed clear of committing to a first rate cut timing. Our economists expect a 25bp rate cut in December (for more, read here)

• St. Louis Fed President Musalem said the time is coming when it may be appropriate to adjust rates. He went on to state that the Fed is now focused on both sides of its mandate, as risks to both seem more balanced

## Emerging Markets

• The BSP cut its policy rate by 25bp to 6.25% in its August monetary policy meeting. Market pricing and economists were split on their expectations for the meeting. The central bank expects inflation to trend downward within the 2-4% target range despite the uptick in July. The risk-adjusted inflation forecasts were revised up to 3.3% for 2024 (P: 3.1%) and down to 2.9% for 2025 (P: 3.1%), with the balance of risks remaining on the downside for both years, but tilting modestly to the upside for 2026

• BSP Governor Remolona explained during the press conference that the policy rate was lowered as the central bank was “somewhat more confident in the inflation numbers coming down than in the GDP numbers going up.” He noted another 25bp rate cut is possible in October or December, but something “pretty bad” has to happen for it to lower rates by another 50bp this year. He revealed that the BSP assumes a 50bp rate cut from the Fed in September, with cumulative 100bp of cuts this year and another 100bp next year. He added that banks' reserve requirement ratio should be reduced quite substantially, though there was no decision on the timing yet

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.

## Analyst Stock Ratings

Overweight (O). The stock's total return is expected to exceed the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis, over the next 12-18 months

Equal-weight (E). The stock's total return is expected to be in line with the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis, over the next 12-18 months.

Not-Rated (NR). Currently the analyst does not have adequate conviction about the stock's total return relative to the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis, over the next 12-18 months.

Underweight (U). The stock's total return is expected to be below the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis, over the next 12-18 months.

Unless otherwise specified, the time frame for price targets included in Morgan Stanley Research is 12 to 18 months.
