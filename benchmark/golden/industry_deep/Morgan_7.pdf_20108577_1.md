# FOMC Preview: June Meeting

Slow inflation progress this year keeps the Fed on hold for longer. Chair Powell should emphasize patience. We still see a first cut in September. Our strategists maintain long 3m10y receivers.

## Key expectations

• The FOMC remains on hold at 5.375%. The FOMC statement describes progress on inflation as slowed rather than stalled. The Summary of Economic Projections will likely show a shift to two cuts this year instead of three, marking to market for stronger inflation data in 1Q.

• In the press conference, we expect Chair Powell to emphasize patience as the Fed awaits more convincing data and to maintain a cutting bias. We continue to look for three cuts this year starting in September.

• Our rates strategists do not expect markets to get much excitement from the dot-plot, but see an asymmetric risk toward lower yields emanating from the FOMC press conference, as well as the CPI print that precedes it. They continue to suggest holding 3m10y receivers.

• Given recent shifts in market pricing, our economists’ expectation that the FOMC would only change its forecasts slightly would probably not surprise investors and would drive little USD reaction.

• On the agency MBS side, our strategists remain long mortgage basis.

## June meeting

We expect the Federal Reserve to maintain its patient stance in both the statement and Q&A. We see risks to how progress on inflation is described in the statement, given there has been one good data print in April following the 1Q re-acceleration. We expect to see upward revisions to core PCE inflation in 2024 in the SEP and for the median dot to shift from three to two cuts this year.

## FOMC policy statement

Current conditions – we look for an important change to the characterization of inflation that is an acknowledgement of improvement in inflation data through April, though still not enough improvement to be convincing.

"Recent indicators suggest that economic activity has continued to expand at a solid pace. Job gains have remained strong, and the unemployment rate has remained low. Inflation has eased over the past year but remains elevated. In recent months, there has been a lack of further progress toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective has slowed."

GDP continues to grow at a "solid pace." We published our mid-year outlook on May 19 where we forecast GDP growth at 2.1% 4Q/4Q this year and next. The economy expanded by 1.3% Q/Q annualized in the first quarter, and we are tracking 2Q GDP growth at a solid 2.5% annualized pace (as of May 31) versus the Atlanta Fed GDPNow at 2.7% and NY Fed Nowcast at 1.8%.

Job gains "have remained strong." As of April, the three-month and six-month average of monthly net new jobs was 242k ( Exhibit 1 ). We are forecasting 220k total nonfarm payrolls in May (released June 7). Since the start of the year, the unemployment rate has moved up two-tenths to 3.9%, and we think it is likely to remain there in May (though in our forecast it edges up on an un-rounded basis).

Core PCE inflation has eased significantly over the past year ( Exhibit 2 ), but progress flattened out in 1Q24. We think progress resumed with the recently released April

data. The lack of progress has kept the Fed on hold for longer, and we expect the first 25bp cut in September, followed by cuts at every meeting through mid-2025. Our conviction that inflation will head toward target remains high. We expect faster deceleration ahead, with monthly readings moving below 0.20% – a 2.5% annual rate which we believe to be a comfortable pace for the Fed to begin cutting as inflation continues to fall.

## Risk to the statement

Since the last FOMC meeting, there has been a single improved inflation print in April. The risk is that FOMC officials have not yet gained enough conviction, and that they pair unchanged inflation language with a more concentrated move in the dot-plot to fewer cuts this year.

We think there is no need to alter any of the language around the stance on rates. For example, we expect that the Committee will remain "highly attentive" to inflation risks, and that it will not adjust the policy rate until it "has gained greater confidence" that inflation is moving toward target.

## Press conference

We think Chair Powell will stress that monetary policy is sufficiently tight and it is appropriate to give restrictive policy further time to work. When asked about the latest round of inflation data, we think he is likely to caution against taking a signal from the weaker print, say that continued patience is warranted, and again shift focus to sequential monthly prints and away from year-over-year comparisons.

## Summary of Economic Projections

At this meeting, Fed participants will update their projections for the economy and monetary policy. Our preliminary expectation is that incoming data suggest no change to GDP growth forecasts (2.1% 4Q/4Q in 2024 and 2.0% in 2025) and 0.1pp upward revisions to this year’s unemployment (4.1%) and core PCE inflation (2.7%) projections ( Exhibit 3 ).

Though these are minor changes, we think they could tilt the balance toward a median view of two cuts this year from three prior, and put upward pressure on the rate path in 2025.

Finally, the longer-run median rate of interest edged up in the March SEP. St. Louis President Musalem will first contribute his forecasts and dots at this meeting. We think he could contribute a longer-run dot around 3% (with no particular special insight into his views), which would leave the median rounding at 2.6%.

On the morning of the second day of the meeting, the BLS will be releasing the May CPI print. Participants are able to adjust their projections following the data release, if needed. Our expectation is for core CPI to increase by 0.28%M in May, consistent with a 0.21% increase in core PCE inflation. A few basis points higher on core inflation in May could lift core PCE inflation on a 4Q/4Q basis to 2.8% this year versus 2.7% as projected. We still think in this scenario that the median would shift to two dots, but we could see more of a "sea change" in dots moving from three to two cuts, instead of just a couple of participants. On the other side, if core PCE inflation is 0.1% or lower in May, that would imply 2.6% for 4Q/4Q core PCE inflation this year. In this scenario, it is still likely the median dot would move to two cuts given how volatile the data have been over the past quarter.

## The pathways for rate cuts

Market context into the meeting: The June FOMC meeting comes against a backdrop of generally weaker data in recent weeks, offset by a lingering concern that progress on inflation has slowed. This meant the market has been range-bound – with 10-year yields in a 4.30-4.70% range since mid-April, when March CPI delivered a third consecutive upside surprise to start 2024.

Nevertheless, 10-year yields are \~60-80bp higher than where they began the year, reflecting the effect of the three upside surprises in CPI earlier this year ( Exhibit 5 ). Interestingly, it's the CPI prints that have moved yields, not the FOMC meetings ( Exhibit 6 ).

With about \~2 cuts priced into the year so far and technically four FOMC meetings left (assuming cuts can, in theory, start in July), markets are around the middle of the distribution. What are the risks that could cause the Fed to deliver three cuts, or only one?

Given that there is a CPI print in the vicinity of the FOMC meeting, our economists see a stickiness to the base case that the Fed shows a median of two cuts in 2024 – with a substantial PCE (translated from CPI) surprise needed to move to one or three cuts in 2024. We think it is likely that such surprises could come from the press conference rather than the dots themselves.

One possible pathway investors can consider is if the Fed can contextualize the recent uptick in inflation as being driven by residual seasonality ( Exhibit 9 ) or being too narrow – essentially a view on the inflation path, rather than simply following the realized trends. In collaboration with our economics team, we noted in a recent report that residual seasonality played a key role in distorting the optics of core PCE, and that there could be deceleration in PCE ahead. If the FOMC or Powell discuss such insights, it could prompt the market to reverse much of its inflation-driven upside in yields.

Another possible pathway is if the FOMC or Powell continue to highlight the risks of an unexpected tightening of the labor market. There are some datapoints which suggest that the labor market is currently somewhere between a state of balance or some deterioration from its ideal state – for example, the ISM and S&P services employment numbers ( Exhibit 10 ), or the NY Fed survey showing consumers concerned about the job market. If Powell shares more concern about the labor market, it could also open the door for markets to price more cuts.

Overall, we go into the FOMC biased long duration and continue to suggest holding 3m10y receivers: We continue to abstain from curve steepeners, and we think that duration longs carry better "bang for the buck" than steepeners, especially in an "insurance cut" style cycle priced by markets (see The Problem with Steepeners, April 29, 2024).

## USD outlook

Since the FOMC last updated the SEP in March, price pressures have been somewhat more resilient than the committee anticipated in its forecasts. And the FOMC’s tone has shifted somewhat to acknowledge these data. For example, Chair Powell said on May 14 that restrictive rates may take “longer to work.”

Market pricing has adjusted to reflect these developments. On March 20 when the SEP was last updated, inflation swaps implied that headline CPI would be around 2.7% y/y at the end of 2024 – swaps now imply that headline CPI will be around 2.9%. Fed funds pricing for the end of the year has also risen from around 3.7% to 4.0%.

USD-positive scenario: We expect USD strength if Chair Powell signals that the FOMC is inclined to keep rates unchanged “for an extended period of time” (i.e., echoing recent comments from Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari) or if he were to signal that the FOMC’s patience with the pace of price pressure deceleration is wearing thin. Similarly, a 50bp rise in the implied fed funds path for either end-2024 or end-2025 (or a rise in the long-run fed funds projection) would likely catalyze a higher USD. In this scenario, USD gains would likely be most pronounced against NZD; our positioning tracker suggests that investors are currently most long NZD of all G10 currencies.

USD-neutral scenario: Given recent shifts in market pricing mentioned above, our economists’ expectation that the FOMC would only change its forecasts slightly (nudging 2024 core PCE higher by 0.1pp and the 2024 fed funds dot higher by 25bp) would probably not surprise investors and would drive little USD reaction.

USD-negative scenario: We expect USD to decline if May CPI (released on the second day of the June FOMC meeting) surprises to the downside, leading the committee to leave its March projections for core PCE and the fed funds rate unchanged in the June SEP. Similarly, USD is likely to soften if Chair Powell spends significant time in the press conference emphasizing that monetary policy is restrictive and the committee’s projections continue to imply a lower policy rate by the end of 2024. In this scenario, we would expect risk sentiment to be supported so USD declines may be most pronounced against GBP, where positioning does not appear stretched.

Our economists expect the Fed to stay the course at this FOMC meeting, holding frontend rates constant at 5.375%, as inflation data have failed to convince the market materially of its slowdown. However, while inflation flat-lined in 1Q24, our economists do see the latest April data, which marked an important deceleration from March, as the first in a series of weaker prints. This leads our economists to forecast the Fed to cut three times this year, at its September, November, and December FOMC meetings, and to continue the cuts at every meeting through mid-2025.

This leads us to an interesting junction, where our economists are expecting twice as many Fed cuts as the market. The market is generally expecting more of a higher-for-longer scenario, with only around 1.5 Fed cuts priced in for this year. This divergent path has disparate impacts on supply-demand dynamics for MBS and thereby mortgage spreads.

Indeed, in Exhibit 11 , we can see that changes in current coupon mortgage spreads have been highly correlated with changes in Fed rate expectations over the next two years, which we measure as the spread between the spot and 8th SOFR contract. We can see that as fewer Fed cuts are priced in, and the difference between the spot and 8th SOFR contract declines, mortgage spreads have tended to widen. We think that this relationship makes sense given the positive anticipated impact of Fed cuts on mortgage demand from various types of investors:

1. An upward-sloping yield curve along with Basel III endgame regulatory clarity (which could come as soon as August) would encourage US banks to invest in longer-duration securities like MBS. Given potential restrictions on HTM assets, we would expect their demand to primarily manifest in Ginnies from their AFS books. As a reminder, in 1Q24, bank portfolios continued to net add Ginnies while reducing conventionals.

2. Lower FX hedging costs (200bp lower on our forecasts) would encourage overseas investors to return to the MBS market, particularly Japanese investors ( Exhibit 12 ).

3. We expect Chinese commercial banks to continue to be active, particularly in current coupon Ginnies.

4. While REITs only own \~2% of the MBS market nowadays, we would expect their demand to increase on the back of higher NIM as well.

5. If retail investors migrate out of money market funds into index funds, MBS would benefit as 28% of the index. For reference, there is currently \$6 trillion in money market funds (for comparison, in 2012-17, money market funds averaged \$2.6 trillion AUM).

On the other hand, the one investor type whose demand would be negatively impacted from Fed cuts would be the Fed itself. A rate rally from Fed cuts would naturally result in increased prepays from refinance activity in mortgages. This would naturally lead to increased paydowns from the existing MBS that the Fed holds on its balance sheet. As the Fed net reduces its MBS balance sheet further, other types of investors will need to net add more MBS on the other side.

We expect the Fed to begin to taper the pace of QT at this upcoming June FOMC meeting, meaning that we expect the Fed to slow down the pace at which it is shrinking its balance sheet. However, we expect the Fed to only reduce the pace of Treasury run-off (by half to \$25 billion/month) and to leave the pace of MBS run-off to continue in full (assuming that MBS run-off doesn't exceed the stated \$35 billion/month cap). We expect the Fed to completely end QT and begin to keep its balance sheet size constant in March 2025. However, even then, we expect the Fed to continue MBS run-off, simply reinvesting the proceeds back into Treasuries instead of MBS. This way, the Fed would keep the total size of its balance sheet constant, but gradually shift its composition more toward Treasuries and away from MBS, which has been one of its stated objectives ( Exhibit 13 ).

This view seems to be well aligned with members of the Fed, and Fed Governor Michelle Bowman stated in a recent speech that (emphasis ours): "As noted in the FOMC's January 2022 Principles for Reducing the Size of the Federal Reserve's Balance Sheet, the FOMC intends to hold primarily Treasury securities in the longer run to minimize the effects of the Federal Reserve's holdings on the allocation of credit across the economy. [She] strongly support[s] this principle. Consistent with this statement, the FOMC will reinvest any principal payments from agency MBS holdings above the current runoff cap into Treasury securities. And once balance sheet runoff concludes, my expectation is that proceeds from agency MBS holdings would continue to be reinvested in Treasury securities in order to facilitate a transition of the Federal Reserve's balance sheet holdings to consist of primarily Treasury securities."

All in all, our view on MBS is quite levered to our view on the Fed cutting cycle. For investors who agree with our economists' view for earlier and more cuts, we think that mortgages are in a position to outperform from improved technicals that Fed cuts bring with them. We maintain our mortgage basis overweight recommendation, and see a 30- 50bp MBS index spread range in the scenario outlined above. On the other hand, for those who see a higher-for longer scenario, the MBS market may be more challenged in the near term, and we've recently witnessed how increased reliance on money manager demand can increase spread-widening risks when either outflows or overweight unwinds occur.

## Disclosure Section

Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)

Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any premium paid
