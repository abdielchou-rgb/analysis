# Assessing the Impact of the New Monetary Framework

We believe the new monetary policy framework effectively ends the China-style QE and will pave the way for more marketoriented rate pricing. We believe it will drive more risk-oriented loan pricing and better for banks with better risk management.

Notable shift in monetary frame on loan volume guidance as well as loan and deposit rates: Since the PBOC governor Pan Gongsheng laid out the path of monetary framework transition, we have seen a major shift in overall monetary policy framework. First, window guidance on loan growth, including window guidance on type of loans such as SME and industrial loan growth, has indeed been de-emphasized. Second, PBoC has established seven-day repo rate as the key policy rate, improved quality of seven-day repo rates by narrowing the band and deemphasized other rates, such as MLF, as policy rates. This has reduced direct policy influence to largely the short-term interbank rates from many factors such as pace of loan growth, type of loan growth, many types market interbank rates and several types of loan and deposit rates, which will make monetary policy transmission mechanism more transparent and market oriented.

However, the ultimate monetary transmission mechanism is still a work in progress: While LPR and deposit rates were cut 5-20bp after the seven-day repo rate was cut 10bp, we do not believe deposit and LPR will be 100% correlated with seven-day repo rates in the future. The LPR cut was largely due to a catch up cut by some banks, whose LPR was higher than the true rates to their best borrowers, an issue that Governor Pan mentioned during the Lujiazui forum. The deposit rate cut was also a catch up cut following a number of LPR cuts last year. PBOC had suggested the banks to wait for the clean up of the manual deposit rate supplement before cutting deposit rates again. Hence, some of the cuts are part of the steps needed for the framework transition. However, by arranging these cuts together, we believe PBOC wants to guide the market to establish some link between seven-day repo rates and loan and deposit rates, although not 100% correlated.

What the new frame might ultimately look like and what will be implications for bank earnings? Given the removal of window guidance on loan growth, banks have lowered 2024 loan growth plan and have started to be more rational on loan pricing, particularly on higher-risk loans. Over time, we expect some loan yield rebound on higher-risk loans such as SME and industrial loans, while loan yield on mortgages could go down more. Overall, however, we believe loan pricing will be more stable as banks become more rational on loan supply, which has been the key issue for irrational loan pricing. This will also be better for long-term loan allocation and should support ROE and NIM for banks with better loan pricing and risk management capabilities. In addition, we believe the squeeze to mid-size shareholding banks could also be reduced.

# New Monetary Policy Framework Will Notably Reduce Direct Policy Influence on Volume and Many Rates

Since the PBOC governor Pan Gongsheng laid out the path of monetary framework transition, we have seen a major shift on the overall monetary policy framework with reduced direct policy influence on loan volume and many rates, which will pave the way for some true market-oriented rate reform after some setbacks in the past few years. In addition, we believe the de-emphasizing of window guidance on loan and TSF growth has effectively ended the China-style QE, which is contrary to market expectations that China will start implement QE. In our view, this will be very favorable to more market-oriented and risk-based loan pricing in the future and further reduce risks on irrational capacity expansion in the industrial sectors.

## A quicker-than-expected major shift in China monetary policy framework

## Window guidance on loan growth has indeed been de-emphasized

A number of efforts were made before PBOC Governor Pan suggested the shift of monetary policy framework in Lujiazui Forum on July 19:

• The way to calculate financial GDP was modified in early 2024 to include bank NIM in addition to loan and deposit size. This serves to reduce local government incentives to push banks to expand balance sheets at the expense of margins in order to support local GDP.

• In PBOC's Monetary Policy Report of both 4Q23 and 1Q24, the Central Bank continued to guide market expectations to reduce focus on new credit growth, saying it has less relevance to economic growth than before due to many structural changes. PBOC also indicated the current level of long-term T-bond yields might not be a fair representation of future economic growth.

• State Council had meetings in April specifically to curb manual interest payments by some banks. This should deflate total credit by cleaning out low-quality selfcirculating loans and deposits.

• PBOC has published various articles after decelerating TSF data in recent months in an effort to manage market expectations.

## Established seven-day repo rate as the key policy rate and deemphasized policy nature of other rates

In the past two to three weeks, PBoC has established seven-day repo rate as the key policy rate, improved quality of seven-day repo rates by narrowing the band and deemphasizing other rates, such as MLF. In addition, PBOC has worked with banks to reduce LPR and deposit rates to establish some link between loan and deposit rates with policy rates, which will be the key channel of transmission mechanism.

## The new framework has reduced direct policy interference on many factors, paving the way for more market-oriented loan and deposit pricing

Under the previous quantitative-based monetary framework, PBOC tends to manage a wide variety of variables that could directly or indirectly affect credit growth, loan and deposit rates as well as market rates. This includes:

• Direct window guidance on bank credit growth targets;

• Direct window guidance on the growth of many specific types of credits, such as SME loans and industrial loans;

• Direct window guidance on certain loan rate level;

• Various structural monetary policies such as relendings to encourage credit allocation to certain areas, such as SMEs or manufacturers;

• MLF rate which will guide LPR;

• The volume of MLF, SLF and OMO as well as RRR levels, which will affect the liquidity of interbank market and influence rates in this market;

In the quantitative-based system, we believe window guidance has played the biggest role in affecting credit growth as well as various interest rates. On the other hand, PBOC's rate tools and liquidity management played a smaller role than window guidance, which is also affected by a rate corridor that was too wide. In addition, the most important issue with quantitative-based monetary framework and direct influence on too many factors is the distortion it creates on loan pricing and allocation, which will cause more longterm risks.

Under the new rate-based monetary framework, we think the variables to be managed by PBOC will be reduced, especially with moderating window guidance. On the other hand:

• PBOC will use seven-day reverse repo rate (OMO rate) as the main policy rate with a narrower interest rate corridor, which increases its effectiveness. It will be used to guide other rates set by PBOC such as MLF or SLF as well market rates such as DR007.

• PBOC will continue to use OMO and MLF to manage the liquidity of interbank market and banking system.

• PBOC will likely maintain the structural monetary tools, although we think they will merely serve as an incentive rather than hard requirements for banks to lend

## Ultimate Monetary Transmission Mechanism Is Still a Work in Progress

While PBOC is yet to finalize the monetary transmission mechanism, we think there will be some link between seven-day repo rates and loan/deposit rates although not 100% correlated. Rather, LPR and deposit rates could be based more on the participating banks' own lending considerations, such as risk preference, capital usage and competition, in our view.

## Link established between seven-day repo rates (the key policy rates) and loan/deposit rates

PBOC has de-emphasized MLF with a potential breakdown of the relationship between MLF and LPR - MLF cut came after the LPR cut this time with different magnitudes. Meanwhile, we think PBOC is guiding the market to link loan and deposit rates to sevenday repo rate (the key policy rate); LPR and deposit rate cuts all followed the seven-day repo rate cut.

However, we believe this round of LPR and deposit rate cuts does not purely result from the reduction in seven-day repo rate, but more like catch-up actions by banks as:

1) There is a need to reduce the gap between LPR and true rates to some banks' best borrowers. In fact, new corporate loan yield has been lower than the five-year LPR since 2Q20. This indicates deviation of true loan yield from LPR; and

2) Deposit rate cuts were suggested by PBOC to be implemented after the existing manual deposit rate supplement is cleaned up, which started in April 2024.

## Market factors could play a bigger role in loan and deposit rates under the new framework, in our view

With the elimination of window guidance on loan growth and the setup of key policy rates, we believe loan and deposit rates will be driven more by market factors under the new framework rather than administrative measures as before. The determination of LPR could be based on both OMO rates and market factors such as:

• Loan supply and demand, which should direct credit to areas with high demand;

• Credit risk level, where higher risk loans such as SME and industrial loans should see higher loan yield and vice versa;

• Market competition, especially more rational competition from SOE banks with less window guidance;

• Capital consumption, where capital allocation efficiency should be another key consideration when extending new loans;

• Various costs at banks, to keep profitability; and

• Bargaining powers at individual banks, which will ultimately depend on banks capability in serving customers, etc.

This should allow more rational loan pricing and is good for banks in the long run without hurting too much banks' NIM and profitability, as emphasized by PBOC several times. On the other hand, deposit rates should also follow policy rates more closely, and will also depend on the demand from the loan side as well as market competition.

## Impact on Banks from the New Framework

With the shift away from quantitative-focused framework, we expect more rational loan pricing at banks, which should help support banks' NIM and ROE. Specifically, we expect:

1) More rational loan growth with loan quantitative window guidance gradually abating. This should improve the efficiency of bank capital usage, while reducing NIM pressure and potential credit risks caused by misallocation.

2) Restoration of proper risk premium, which allows banks more discretion to price loans differently. We could expect divergent trend in different types of loans; SME and industrial loan yields will have room to rebound due to higher risks. Notably, SME loan yield has dropped notably since 2020, reaching 4.42% in 1Q24, which in our view is difficult to cover funding cost, credit cost and opex for banks; On the other hand, mortgage loan yield could continue to decline to levels more in line with its low credit risk and low capital consumption nature.
