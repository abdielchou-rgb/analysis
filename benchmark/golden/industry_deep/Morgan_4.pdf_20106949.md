# Global Credit Strategy

## Survey Says…

We present our key findings and takeaways across regions from the inaugural corporate credit investor survey.

## Key Takeaways

■ Most investors globally (\~65%) expect to maintain the same level of credit risk, with the skew more positive in Asia, followed by Europe and the US.

Sticky inflation/less policy easing ranked as the biggest risk, followed by recession concerns. Most investors don't see supply as a big risk.

■ Despite the strong tone in markets, most investors expect to see wider spreads through year-end.

– In US IG, the majority of investors (61%) expect Financials to outperform. The preference across maturities was for the 5-7Y compared to our bias for the frontend.

– In US leveraged credit, contrary to our view, investors remain cautious on CCCs. Real Estate, Communications and Cons. Discret. were the least preferred sectors.

European investors prefer fins>corps and dislike BBB-, long-duration and consumer discretionary. Respondents' preference for seniors>subs and B>CCC is in contrast to our view.

■ Asia investors expect China credit net supply to remain negative this year. They will increasingly focus on Japan and Australia as new avenues to add risk.

Overview

We recently concluded our global corporate credit investor survey. We thank clients for their strong participation giving us a robust data-set of results. We received over 150 responses, including a healthy mix across regions, investor types, and asset class (IG vs. leveraged credit). We summarize our key findings in this note.

Demographics-wise, roughly 60% of respondents were US based, while about 20% were split evenly between Asia and Europe. Across investor types, asset managers made up the largest chunk of respondents, at 57%, followed by insurance companies (13%), hedge funds (13%), and banks (12%). Looking at asset class focus, 101 respondents were IG focused, compared to 98 for leveraged credit. Below, we provide some overall takeaways for the complete data set. The later sections have more details across the different regions.

We start off with how investors view their credit risk positioning globally. A majority of respondents (65%) said they would maintain their current level of credit risk ( Exhibit 1 ). The remaining respondents were equally divided between adding and reducing credit risk for the global sample set. Notably though, there was a better skew here among Asia investors, where 26% looked to add risk vs. 6% looking to reduce. The add/reduce skew was worst for the US, with only 12% looking to increase, while 22% were looking to decrease. Europe was in the middle. While we did not have a specific question on regional preference, this data set shows that positioning is likely to skew more positive on Asia credit, followed by Europe and then by the US.

The biggest risk for investors was by far "sticky inflation/less easing than expected". Roughly 47% of participant investors ranked this as the biggest risk factor out of the 5 we laid out in the survey, and another 23% ranked it as the second. This was followed by a sizable camp that flagged 'weaker growth / recession' as the biggest risk. Geopolitical risks and sector / idiosyncratic risks came in and third and fourth ( Exhibit 2 ). Notably, very few participants saw corporate supply being a major risk, with only 4% ranking it as the top factor.

## US Investment Grade

• 68% of respondents expect spreads to trade north of 100bp, with another cohort of 30% stating that the index will stay range-bound between 80bp and 99bp.

• 36% of investors preferred to be positioned at the 5-7Y maturity, while 31% focused further out on the 7-10Y and 10Y+ tenors.

• Most investors, about 31%, ranked high BBBs as their preference, with low BBBs and AAA/AAs as least preferred.

• A clear majority (61%) think that Financials will outperform Non-Fins this year.

• Investors saw Real Estate as their least preferred sector (23%) followed by Consumer Discretionary (15%), Financials (13%), and Technology (11%).

At the broad index level, despite the strong tone in markets, our survey shows that most investors expect spreads to be wider through year-end. Out of 81 total US IG respondents, 68% expect the Bloomberg Corporate IG index to end the year above 100bp in spread, with the majority of this subset concentrated in the 100-124bp bucket (48%). On the other hand, only 3% of investors see spreads breaching the 2021 tights and trading through 80bp.

Across investor types, spread expectations were more two-sided among asset managers. While 38% saw IG spreads at 80-100bp (compared to 30% for the full sample set), about 8% saw spreads north of 150bp (also higher than the average). On the other hand, the remaining investor types were more concentrated in the 100-124bp range for YE spreads.

## Preference by maturity buckets

Across the curve, the 5-7Y bucket in IG had the highest preference, with 35% of respondents picking it as their preferred allocation. Roughly the same number of respondents favored the 1-3Y, 3-5Y, and 7-10Y bucket, at 18% each. Only 10% of respondents saw the very long end (10+Y) as the preferred maturity bucket. The tiering was notable across investor types as Asset Manager preference was centered on the 5-7Y sector, while banks preferred the front end. Insurance companies were skewed to the 7- 10Y bucket.

## US Leveraged Finance

• Like IG, most investors see wider spreads through YE. 75% of HY investors forecast spreads widening to 350bp+ (current level 315bp), and 70% of loan investors forecast spreads widening to 450bp+ (current level 439bp).

• 61% of HY and 73% of Loan investors view CCCs as unattractive. A majority (51% of HY and 67% of Loan investors) believe downgrades will intensify.

• Default forecasts reflect high dispersion. In HY, 46% believe defaults will rise above 4%, while 39% believe defaults will trend lower than 3.4%. Loan investors are split, with 42% believing defaults will rise above 4.5%, and 41% believing defaults will decline to below 4%.

• 67% of HY investors think recovery rates will improve to 30pt+, while 42% of loan investors think they will stay similar to current levels at 50-59pt.

• Real Estate is one of the least favored sector across HY and loan investors. In addition, HY investors find Communications and Consumer Discretionary unattractive, while Loan investors are avoiding Technology.

• 79% of both HY and Loan investors think that private credit will at least somewhat affect supply in 2024.

## US High Yield

The High Yield Index spread, now at 315bp, is approaching 2021 tight levels, and spreads of the higher-quality BB and single B cohorts are close to GFC tights. However, out of our sample of investors that focus on US HY, a clear majority expect spreads to end the year wider. 75% forecast the HY index spread will be north of 350bp by year end, concentrated in the 350-450bp bucket. Only 6% expect spreads to trade at a 2-handle, aligning with the ranges seen in 2021.

## Defaults and Recoveries

Similar to investors' dispersion in views around spread forecasts, Exhibit 14 shows a broad range of views around HY default rates. Of the respondents, \~14% believe defaults will come down to below 3% and \~14% believe defaults will increase to above 5%, suggesting some investors believe the tail scenarios may realize. With current T12m default rates at 3.72% by Moody's measure, the average respondent who believes YE defaults will track \~3.9% suggests investors tend to expect defaults to end the year slightly higher than they are tracking now.

Current T12m recovery rates track \~28.5pt for US senior unsecured bonds (by Moody's metric), and recoveries have been steadily increasing from post-COVID lows in July 2023 (\~26pt). As the post-GFC recovery rate average is \~40pt, many investors believe recovery rates will continue to trend upwards to 30-39pt by year end ( Exhibit 15 ) – normalizing to levels around the 40pt historical average. The next most common recovery rate prediction is 20-29pt, suggesting some investors believe recoveries will stay range-bound.

## Downgrades, Private Credit, and Sectors

Given the rapid growth of the private credit market in the last couple years, we asked survey participants about their outlook on private credit affecting HY supply. 79% said they expect private credit to somewhat or materially inhibit supply ( Exhibit 17 ). ## US Leveraged Loans

Unlike its HY counterpart that has been emboldened by the soft landing narrative and disinflation, Loans have experienced a little less conviction in the recent rally given some of the perceptions of risk around them. For instance, the floating-rate nature makes loan issuers more affected by changes in interest rates than companies that issue fixed rate bonds. In this section, we summarize investor attitudes regarding Loans.

## Asia credit investors expect China credit net supply to remain negative...

We were a little surprised at how unanimously Asia credit investors believe that China credit net supply will remain negative for 2024, even though this is our view going into 2024. • We see weaker nominal China GDP growth in 2024 that is skewed towards the downside, driving fewer issuance needs for Chinese corporates. Following this week's National Party Congress (NPC), our China economics team sees downside risk to its already below-consensus nominal GDP growth forecast (NPC: Fiscal Package Insufficient, March 5, 2024).

So far this year, the above two drivers have been playing out, and year to date China credit net supply is at -USD 12 billion.

Given the negative net supply in China credit, we see Asia credit investors decisively looking outside of the traditional Asia credit universe to meet their investment needs.
