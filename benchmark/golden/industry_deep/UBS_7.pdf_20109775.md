## 2) Falling TIPS yields should help tech

Ordinarily, into falling bond yields (or more accurately real bond yields i.e. 10 year TIPS), long duration non-cyclical sectors re-rate. This is partly because as bond yields fall investors become more sceptical of growth (as has occurred in the past week) and because the lower discount rate re-rates long duration assets (i.e. companies on high multiples).

European semis have historically been the most negatively correlated sector to the US TIPS yield (i.e falling TIPS yield is good for semis) and that is also close to being the case in the US (as we see in the Appendix). This certainly has not been the case in the past 2 weeks - even non-cyclical tech (i.e software) has underperformed.

## What is still worrying about semis?

## 2) They do not look cheap yet to us

The price to sales of semis relative to the market is still 2.6 std above its norm (although admittedly this does adjust for survivorship bias).

They look slightly cheaper on P/E relatives but these are still c1 std above their norm, and even here the P/E relatives tended to only be higher when semis EPS was highly depressed in recessionary periods or after the so called ‘tech wreck’ periods post 2001.

We examine what is a potential P/E multiple on potential addressable market.

Semis revenue peaked at 0.8% of GDP in the past cycle. If we assume that semis revenue can rise to 1% of global GDP by say 2030, then total semis revenues would be \$1.4trn (semis and SCE). If we apply the peak net margin of 27%, then semis would be then trading on a P/E of 16X. This is still quite high for what is quite optimistic assumptions and quite a long way out (i.e 2030).

## 3) Semis are still discounting a sharp rise in ISM

Semis have been one of the most cyclical sectors and hence their relative performance used to trade in line with ISM. Right now they are discounting an ISM of nearly 60, even if we exclude AMD and Nvidia (where there is clearly a structural trend in AI that distorts a historical fit).

We can see that semis profit margins and ISM used to have a very close fit and if this fit was unchanged, the current profit margin implied on 12 month forward earnings would require a very high ISM. (Obviously there is a question as to whether structural trends in AI alter this fit - but to some extent the profit margin of the analog names should continue to have had a closer fit with ISM).

## 5) China - a source of downside risk

China is 20% of revenue (much more for some companies) and accounts for roughly 30% of profits.

China nominal GDP growth is very poor (at just 4% in Q2 yoy and even worse quarterly annualised) but more worryingly China continues to have the early phase effects of a bursting of the triple bubble (credit, real estate and investment) at a time when property prices are falling sharply and producer prices/ export prices are also falling (owing to excess capacity). This is classic deflation and so far the policy response has appeared lacklustre compared to what has been required in other developed or emerging economies where credit and real estate bubbles burst.

Figure 16: China PMIs in manufacturing have been weakened again
![](images/3e350186ddd8ee13b9a8bd09ee703c8ac62ae814998598b2919b015ed3ba2933.jpg)
Source: Refinitiv Datastream, UBS

There are also 4 other concerns related to China: i) the degree to which demand may have been brought forward ahead of the US elections to front run any change in US policy on China; ii) our China economics team estimates that a 60% tariff in the event of a Trump presidency would initially hit GDP growth by 2.5%; iii) if sales that went to China are diverted elsewhere, would prices weaken?; iv) in some areas China has moved up the value added curve (e.g. in analogue).

# What is good about semis - the longterm fundamentals

i) Semis rank top of our EPS growth scorecard (highest trend growth of any sector at 12%); ii) they score top of our Global quality scorecard (this measures ROE relative to RoE volatility and balance sheet amongst other factors, see here) as shown in Figure 20; iii) their balance sheets are in good shape; iv) there does not appear to be overinvestment; and v) semis revenues as a % of GDP are still below the previous peak of 0.8%. So ignoring valuation and the cycle, this sector would normally be an overweight.

Finally, we would highlight that there has been very diverse regional performance for semis.
