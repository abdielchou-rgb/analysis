# US Treasury and Gilt Futures Rolls

We present our views on US Treasury and gilt futures contract rolls for the March to June cycle. We look at the fundamental drivers of the roll, investor positioning, delivery optionality, relative value, and repo. Finally, we suggest optimal rolling strategies.

TU Contract: Bearish: We are bearish on the TU roll given positioning and the trend of recent rolls with similar positioning.

FV Contract: Mildly bearish: We are mildly bearish on FV, as positioning and recent cycles are mildly bearish signals.

TY Contract: Mildly bearish: We are mildly bearish on TY, as cheapness to fair value slightly offsets the bearish positioning setup.

UXY Contract: Bearish: We are bearish on the UXY roll due to significant asset manager longs, recent rolls, and CTD relative value. We recommend asset managers roll early.

US Contract: Neutral: We are neutral given a mildly bearish positioning setup that is offset by recent rolls and cheapness of the front contract.

WN Contract: Mildly bullish: We are mildly bullish on the WN roll, as positioning and recent rolls are somewhat bullish signals.

UST Repo Outlook: Neutral: Repo has remained well behaved this year, which should continue over the coming weeks given a still-large RRP. Further ahead, we expect tighter funding conditions.

Gilt Contract: Mildly bullish: We see the roll trading rich. However, the short positioning in the front contract could lead to a further richening in the roll.

Relative value – Mildly bullish: The rich/cheap spread between the front and back CTD appears slightly lower than its 6-month mean ( Exhibit 5 ). As such, we are mildly bullish from a CTD relative value perspective but emphasize the potential from the CTD spread to continue to move downward.

Macro curve view – Neutral: The correlation between the 1s2s curve and the spread of future-implied forward yields (back contract – front contract) has been weak, with an Rsquared of 0.06. Given this – and the fact that we have a neutral curve view – we are neutral on the curve impact on the roll.

Repo risk and hedging: Bond futures prices are affected by repo rates through carry, which depends on the term repo rate to the delivery of the bond. Movements in the overall level of term repo rates can cause the roll to richen or cheapen, as repo carries a heavier weight in the price of the back contract, and changes in the spread between term repo for the front contract and term repo for the back contract can also impact the roll.

To hedge against repo risk, we use the nearest 1-month and 3-month SOFR futures contracts for the front and back Treasury futures: rate expectations implied by SOFR futures can be used as a proxy for 1-month and 3-month term repo. In the current roll, these contracts are SERH4 for the front contract and SFRH4 for the back contract.

To demonstrate the impact of repo on the roll, we examine past DV01-hedged rolls with and without hedges for repo and the performance of the repo hedges ( Exhibit 6 and Exhibit 7 ).

## UST Repo Outlook: Neutral

Moderate UST supply and higher rates have kept demand for repo financing stable so far this year, leading to well behaved funding markets. Looking ahead, we see tighter funding and gradually higher SOFR prints, particularly around the next two month-ends, driven by more UST supply. However, with the RRP balance still large, this is likely to materialize closer to quarter-end and into April, keeping SOFR largely anchored to the RRP rate over the coming weeks.

Funding markets have remained largely stable so far in 2024. This past month-end saw SOFR practically unchanged, coming in at 532bp (RRP +2bp). This compares with RRP +10bp around year-end and RRP +9bp around November month-end. This has led repo rates to remain largely anchored to the RRP rate over the past weeks ( Exhibit 39 ).

This has been mainly driven by a moderation in UST settlements in December and January, led by bills. As shown in Exhibit 40 , the timing of total UST net settlements has shaped swings in repo rates (measured via the UST general collateral or GC repo rate) relative to the RRP rate. As expected, large net settlements align with a wider UST GC - RRP spread and vice versa.
