Across various recent notes, we have been highlighting a range of ideas across regions, sectors, themes and single stocks. ## Macro first: Long European over US equities

We may be entering a period of 'goldilocks' in Europe if the ECB is cutting rates as economic growth improves. This contrasts with the US, where the Fed appears more likely to keep rates at a higher level for longer, with some risk that inflation will only retrace if growth materially weakens. Our innovative 'Market Cookbook' analysis shows the scenario relative performance potential and the sector divergences that are possible.

## GARP 1: Long UK small & mid cap (SMID)

UK small and midcaps derated materially after the 2016 Brexit referendum, and have not recovered. For a while, earnings were weak, too, but not any more. With recent tax cuts, upcoming rate cuts and the potential for further fiscal spending and EU alignment, we see a strong case that UK SMIDs represent growth at a reasonable price.

## GARP 2: Long European utilities

Utilities had negative earnings growth for a decade. We now see high single digit growth into the forecast horizon as investment spending leads to revenue growth and a regulated margin. Renewables, too, should benefit from lower yields and improved growth. The sector appears too cheap for the growth on offer.

European defence stocks have outperformed US AI 'winners' since the Ukraine/Russia war began. Renewed willingness to meet NATO spending commitments and a global trend of diversifying military equipment sourcing remain tailwinds. ## Macro first: Long EUropean over US equities

## The rationale in a nutshell

European equities rarely outperform US equities, and when they do, not for long... The US equity market is full of high quality, innovate and fast growing global leaders. European demographics, debts and political structures haven't been supportive. Europe is a more open economy and market that has historically been buffeted by a range of global headwinds in a way that the more closed US economy and stock market has not. However, right now, momentum is with Europe and may be turning against the US - the recipe for a period of relative outperformance that investors are not positioned for.

Goldilocks in Europe? - The ECB is widely expected to begin cutting interest rates at its upcoming meeting. This is because disinflation has progressed quickly and more convincingly in Europe and growth is current below trend albeit rising. This combination of lower yields but rising growth should be a nice combination for equity investors that have been pricing European equities with a high risk premia since Covid and the Russia/Ukraine war and energy crisis impacted the economy.

Higher for longer or stagflation risk in the US? - GDP growth has been slowing in the US. Despite this, core inflation pressures appear persistent and the Nowcast for the next CPI is currently at 0.38% - up from 0.29% last month. Our economists are less worried with a forecast of 0.25% but the risk remains that the Fed will be on hold at high interest rates for longer than the ECB or markets expect. Large cap tech have been immune to this tightening of financial conditions and posted very strong earnings growth but Jonathan Golub points out that this is slowing. The rest of the market may benefit from resilient growth but if the Fed are on hold for longer, the pressure on many businesses will be sustained. If inflation doesn't fall before GDP growth slows further, the market may need to reconsider a shift in the narrative to a period of stagflation (stubborn inflation but weakening growth).

## A 'Market Cookbook' scenario

Over short horizons, earnings matter less than valuations. Valuations in equities are driven by changes in the discount rate and growth expectations. We have developed a model that helps understand historic index and sector behaviour as those valuation drivers move. We consider changes in real yields and inflation (nominal yields) as well as credit spreads and a growth proxy (risk premia and growth).

In a scenario where Europe benefits from lower real yields but rising growth expectations (with stable inflation and credit spreads), we might assume the market would typically rise 2-3% over a month with cyclicals outperforming defensives.

In a scenario where the US struggles with stubbornly rising inflation expectations but weakness in growth expectations, we might expect that both real yields would decline and that credit spreads would widen somewhat. Historically, this has led to a market that is 2-3% lower over a month with cyclicals underperforming defensives. Energy and gold would outperform most.

## Catalysts

In the coming weeks, US growth and inflation data will be important in driving the US narrative and ECB rate cuts and consumer momentum will be important in Europe.

## GARP 1: Long UK small & mid cap (SMID)

## Rationale: moderate economic growth, recovery in real incomes, rate cuts

After a technical recession in H2 2023, GDP growth rebounded in Q1 2024. UBS Economists expect more moderate growth for the remainder of the year (UBSe 0.2% for 2024). The recovery in real incomes amid declining inflation should support the pick-up in growth in the latter part of 2024. Though tighter financial conditions and higher mortgage payments continue to drag on growth, expected rate cuts should provide an ease and further stimulate economic growth (link). BoE rates have peaked at 5.25%, and given the dovish MPC shift (the split of the vote and change in the forward guidance), our Economists expect the first rate cut in June (link). Economic improvement in the UK will bode well for companies, especially those more domestically exposed.

## Earnings Growth + Value = Growth at Reasonable Price (GARP)

Growth: We use the FTSE 250, which captures the 101th to 350th companies after the FTSE 100, as a proxy for SMIDs and more domestically-aligned UK companies. Our REMIX model forecasts +38% earnings growth for the FTSE 250 ex trusts on +17% revenue growth and improving net margins (7.5% for 2024e vs. 7% in 2023) (link). This view is supported by our REVS framework which shows that smaller companies have stronger regime and earnings scores relative to larger companies. On the other hand, whilst consensus also expect significant earnings growth this year (+25%), revenue growth expectations are relatively lower (+6.5%), resulting in high margin projections (11.2%).

 High domestic exposure: The FTSE 250 has the highest exposure to domestic revenues among the leading European indices, with c.50% of revenues from the UK and c.20% from Continental Europe. This positions most companies in the index to benefit more from both UK and European policy and government dynamics.

Consumer strength: The UK government announced a £10 billion (US\$13 billion) tax cut for 27 million workers effective last month, which reduced the rate of NI contributions by 2pp. The change in NI, which is worth £450 a year to employees on an average salary of £35,000, should provide some relief to consumers to spend on more discretionary items and services. Additionally, the new energy price cap (effective July 1) means that the typical annual bill paid for gas and electricity will fall by an average 7%, providing further support for consumer spending. Lower rates should also help ease interest expense on credit, loans and mortgages.

UK General Election: While the manifestos haven't been published, the key focus when it comes to the Labour election manifesto is likely to be on the costs and financing of the various proposed measures, particularly related to spending on green initiatives (link). The latest opinion polls put Labour well ahead of the Conservatives. Labour has previously said its election manifesto will include “big build” which implies possible support for homebuilders and infrastructure (transportation). The construction sector accounts for c.6% of gross value added in the UK. Though UK homebuilders are currently contending with multiple headwinds (challenging planning policy, fire safety tax/provisions, stretched affordability and ongoing CMA investigation), the group potentially stands to benefit from a Labour victory. Additionally, demand trends are improving from a low base driven by more stable mortgage rates, which indicates a reasonable level of pent up demand (link).

Reasonable Price : The UK equity market is frequently highlighted as a 'value' play, and our analysis highlights that this is especially true for small and mid cap (SMID) companies (link). The FTSE 250 was historically known for outperformance in Europe but cheapened 20-30% after the 2016 Brexit vote and has never really recovered. We think the current macro backdrop presents an opportune time for the FTSE250 to regain some valuation discount. We forecast a price return of 8% this year for the FTSE 250 ex trusts, compared to only 2% for the FTSE 100 (link).

Valuations for the index are attractive, even when adjusted for quality. On a quality adjusted basis, the FTSE 250 ex trusts is trading at a record high discounts relative to the MSCI ACWI and the Stoxx 600 (-31% and -15% respectively), further supporting our argument that high quality growth can be found at a reasonable price in UK SMIDs.

## GARP 2: Long European utilities

## The rationale in a nutshell

After a torrid few years of rising bond yields, inflation pressures, project management issues and a gas price spike and then collapse, European Utilities are settling down and look to be a good example of GARP - Growth at A Reasonable Price. That's not a phrase that has been easy to use for at least a decade. Most of the largest stocks are Buy rated by our analysts, with

Growth: After the GFC, European Utilities were stuck in a sluggish inflation low growth environment and as a result, saw earnings weakness of around 7% per annum for nearly a decade. Since 2019 however, growth has been +14% per annum. Investment and growth in renewable generation/profits was important early in this period. The rise in energy costs in Europe helped more recently. The outlook from here is that for the regulated providers, cost growth and investments mean revenues and earnings will also rise in the high single digits. Meanwhile, the renewable sector is one of the only mega-themes that has not regained momentum in the last year. Project management issues, cost inflation and debt pressures have been problems but as cost inflation abates and yields peak, these stocks too may regain some earnings momentum and justify moving off their trough valuations. With high payout ratios, dividends are appealing and earnings growth will lead to dividend growth too - as they are already.

Reasonable price (cheap!): A sector that has low or negative growth naturally deserves a low P/E multiple. The European Utility sector currently trades on 12x forward earnings estimates when other defensive sectors with similar growth in recent years and into the forecast period, like pharma, trade as high as 17x forward earnings.

Catalysts: The sector remains under pressure from high yields but these may soon abate if the ECB begins a cutting cycle. Gas prices have been rising recently which has been supportive. Continued earnings momentum and dividend growth will see utilities continue to rise and if yields decline, they could outperform other sectors as they revalue too. Investors highlight risks that governments have extracted super-profits taxes from utility companies in recent years. There are risks around excess gas supplies and Donald Trump's indications he could repeal parts of the IRA that have been supportive too.

## Does REVS support it?

Yes. The sector overall score is positive although held back by a few stocks still struggling to recover some earnings momentum (RWE, Enel, Veolia). For the larger Buy-rated names, the overall equal-weight score is 0.22 (+/-1 range). IBE, E.On, Engie and National Grid score strongly. The combined expected return is 27% with 5.5% being the dividend yield. The forward P/E is 12x although we note room for positive surprises on earnings as revenues grow. The sector crowding score is very low. This is not a trade that investors have been willing to participate in yet.

## Thematic: Long European defenCe stocks

## The rationale in a nutshell

This one might not need much explanation. Conflicts have arisen in Europe and the Middle East with other parts of the world experiencing tensions. European governments have begun to increase defense spending toward the NATO commitment threshold of 2% of GDP. A world reliant on US defence equipment is seeking alternatives as the US imposes some restrictions and for greater self-reliance. These factors have led to strong growth in European defence companies.

Few have realised that since the Russia/Ukraine war started, the European defence spending basket of stocks has significantly outperformed the US AI winners basket of stocks. Performance has slowed but continues to be market leading with a further 45% performance so far in 2024. Investors globally have not been particularly involved given the smaller number of European companies with exposure to the theme. Domestic investors have been selling into the rally. This means that despite the strong performance, most of the large companies remain Buy-rated with upside price targets and the REVS framework continues to favour them.

The regime is favourable, earnings momentum continues to be strong and investors have not excessively crowded into these stocks. Valuations do look more stretched currently but not excessively so for stocks that continue to see estimated revised higher and exceeding expectations in earnings season.

The top scoring stocks include Leonardo. Despite not being Buy rated, Rheinmetall and Thales also score well. The larger companies including BAE and Rolls Royce are still scoring positively, and these are Buy rated as well.

# Relative momentum: Long European media over Food & Staples Retailing

## The rationale in a nutshell

The European media & entertainment sector has outperformed F&SR in Europe by over 15% in the last year as it recovers previous underperformance through Covid as well as the disruption from tech sector dominance of the last decade. UMG and PUB have risen over 35% each since mid last year and are expected to continue their positive momentum because advertising and streaming growth rates remain strong. On these two stocks, our analysts point to a few key drivers of the ongoing performance:

"We reiterate our Buy rating on Publicis where we continue to see earnings momentum driven by strength in media including potential upside from account wins, and a recovery in Sapient growth from H2'24."

On UMG, "Post Q1'24 we reiterate our Buy rating. We believe a combination of momentum in paid streaming (penetration and price rises), cost savings and limited further spend on catalogue acquisitions (especially outside of Chord) means UMG can deliver strong FCF growth to >€2bn by 2025E. On our estimates the group trades on an attractive 4% FY25 FCF yield for a company delivering double digit EBITDA growth."

Vivendi is a smaller market cap company with exciting potential upside as an event driven stock looking to split out some of its subsidiaries.

For the European Food & Staples Retailing industry group, it's not that our expectations are materially negative. It is more that there continue to be unexciting returns expected from this defensive sector. The environment for outperformance of defensives is when risk premia are rising and the economy is slowing. For Europe, those are not in our forecast horizon and as rate cuts deliver some economic relief, we expect a growth reacceleration in Europe into 2025.

The analyst expected returns for the larger companies show a 12% spread favouring media companies over the next year.

On most REVS metrics, the media sector is preferred to F&SR. It is clear that with leading indicators rising, that European cyclicals are outpeforming defensives which comes through in the relative scoring on the 'regime' models. ## Stagflation risk: Long Gold Miners

## The rationale in a nutshell

While not quite a 'Europe' trade, some new feedback and analysis support this idea we thought worth including too.

In a recent report, Andrew Garthwaite pointed out a range of reasons to be bullish gold miners. These included:

 Central bank and investor diversification into gold

 Potential for a peak in real yields and the USD

 Catch-up to real gold price peak from previous years

 Gold miners are cheap and have been lagging the gold spot price move

In recent investor meetings, feedback suggests that beyond central banks, there is also retail buying of gold in China. Gold investment opportunities feature prominently in local advertising and investors tell us of colleagues and friends in China who have been buying gold recently. With restrictions on digital currency, gold retains its status as the dominant 'hard' store of value it has been in the past.

Finally, in our 'stagflation' scenario for US markets, gold miners came out on top. This was a scenario where we modelled the historic consequences of stubbornly rising inflation expectations as growth indicators weakened, real yields declined and credit risk premia rose.
