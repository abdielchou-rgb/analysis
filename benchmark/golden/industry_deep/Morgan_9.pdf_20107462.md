Powering GenAI

# Wide Differential Between GenAI Power Demand Growth and Power Grid Growth

Our recent collaborative “Powering GenAI” note, The Tortoise and the Hare (24 Mar 2024), has attracted a lot of attention from investors. An aspect that caught many investors attention was the large delta between our Sustainability Research strategy team’s projections for the growth of GenAI power demand and the growth of the power grid. Our team notes that their 2024 projected base-case US data center power demand growth, at 5.5 gigawatts (GW), is almost exactly equal to the sum of JLL’s assessment of new data center projects in major US markets (\~5.6 GW), and that a 2H23 Cushman & Wakefield (C&W) report on US data center markets projects \~4.0 GW of data centers under construction as of 2H23, a bit lower than Morgan Stanley’s and JLL’s projections.

Block, Inc.

## Downgrade to UW - Market Is Overestimating Cash App and Square Growth Potential

Our US Payments analysts recently downgraded Block (SQ, \$77) to Underweight. They believe the market overestimates Cash App’s long-term profitability and Square Seller’s volume growth. Their demographic analysis of the US Gen Z and Millennial population suggests consensus long-term Cash App growth assumptions are at risk, based on Cash App's already high market penetration — Cash App's account share of the young population is already as high as JP Morgan's approximate share in the age 26-76 cohort. They also expect limited incremental contribution from a potential new Cash App credit card and additional banking services. Meanwhile, our team estimates that SQ’s valuation likely already reflects a best-case scenario of Block becoming “Capital One for Gen Y/Z” — they note that Cash App is extracting meaningfully lower revenue per active account than Capital One did prior to and after their respective IPOs.

See Market Overestimating Cash App and Square Growth Potential Amid High Market Penetration; Downgrade to UW (4 Apr 2024)

## US Equity Strategy

## We Continue to View a 10-year Treasury Yield of \~4.35% as an Important Technical Level to Watch

Three weeks ago, our US Equity Strategists wrote that a 10-year US Treasury yield of \~4.35% could be an important technical level to watch for signs that rate sensitivity may increase for US equities. Last week, the benchmark yield pushed above that level for the first time this year, and stocks sold off — the S&P 500 index made its first break below the lower bound of a well-defined uptrend in the index since the rally began in October. Although they would view a move back above the lower bound as an unequivocal positive, they will be watching the yields-equities dynamic closely into the release of CPI data. In their view, if the 10-year sustains above 4.35-4.40%, they would still expect large cap, quality equities to outperform on a relative basis.

## Japan Equity Strategy

## Flows Data Point to Individual Investors' Growing Appetite for Domestic Equities

Our Japan Equity Strategists have been closely tracking flows since NISAs — tax-favored individual investment accounts — launched in January. The latest data show that the purchase volume of the most-acquired stocks under the new NISA program has continued to grow steadily, almost fully explaining the continuing rise Y/Y in transactions on the TSE Prime Market. Our Strategists view this as a sign of change in the nation’s longstanding household preference for deposits. They believe that if Japan’s households become active investors in domestic equities, it could drive a virtuous cycle in corporate growth and value. Japan remains our Global Cross-Asset Strategy team’s preferred equity market.

See NISA Tracker: NISA Purchasing Triples YoY in Jan-Feb, Outpaces Our Bullish Scenario (3 Apr 2024)

## Fed Rate Cuts Should Drive Biotech Outperformance

In light of our US Economists’ call for the Fed to start cutting in June, our Healthcare analysts collaborated with our US Equity Strategist and US Financials analysts to analyze Biotech industry dynamics during historical rate loosening / cutting cycles with regard to (1) stock performance, (2) the financing environment, and (3) the M&A outlook. They find that, historically, a rate-lowering cycle and lower-rate regime has had a favorable impact on all three elements. Importantly, they also emphasize that, ultimately, fundamentals — e.g., data, regulatory action, and launches — will be important drivers of individual stock performance.
