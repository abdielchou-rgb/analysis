# APAC Economic Perspectives Hong Kong: New budget for fiscal sustainability and growth recovery

## Fiscal deficit is set to narrow

The Financial Secretary unveiled the budget for 2024/25 on 28th Feb, including a set of measures to support the economic recovery while managing both fiscal revenue and expenditure against the continued deficit in the past quarters. The overall fiscal deficit is set to narrow to 1.5% of GDP for FY 2024/25(fiscal year, between April 2024 and March 2025), from the estimated deficit of 3.4% of GDP for FY2023/24.

## A set of measures to support growth recovery

The budget removes all the additional property market cooling measures, such as the special stamp duty and buyer’s stamp duty. The budget also increases the loan-to-value ratio for mortgage. The property market has undergone a period of adjustment against the backdrop of higher interest rates. The Chief Executive’s policy address in October 2023 scaled down some of the additional property cooling measures, but housing price had continued to moderate. With the full removal announced in the budget, UBS Hong Kong property team expects housing price to moderate around 5% in 2024 vs. previous expectation of a 5-10% decline. The budget also includes other measures to support recovery, e.g. the government will conduct monthly fireworks show to attract more tourism.

## Income tax adjustments

The budget increases the income tax rate for high income earners. For annual income exceeding HKD 5mn, the tax rate will be increased to 16% from currently 15%. The budget estimates this hike may impact 12k taxpayers, bringing extra HKD 910mn tax revenue. The budget also reduces the income eligible for tax exemption to HKD 3k, the estimated total revenue reduction is HKD 5.1bn (vs. HKD 6k and an estimated HKD 8.5bn revenue reduction for fiscal year 2023/24). Through these two changes, the incremental revenue increases would be HKD 4.3bn, approximately 0.1% of GDP.

## Enhance fiscal sustainability

The budget estimates fiscal reserve to stand at 24.5% of GDP by end-FY 2023/24 and 21.7% by end-FY 2024/25. These suggest that the overall fiscal condition is resilient. However, fiscal revenue is estimated to contract by 10.9% y/y in FY2023/24. Land sales premium dropped by more than 70%y/y. Fiscal expenditure has trended up and jumped since 2019 to stabilize the economy against the multiple shocks. Thus, fiscal deficit persisted in 2023 despite growth recovery. Therefore, the budget is paying attention to promote fiscal sustainability. The fiscal revenue growth is set to outpace expenditure in the coming years, bringing fiscal balance to positive by FY2025/26. The budget suggests that an amount of 95bn-135bn bond financing per year will be used for the Northern Metropolis and other infrastructure projects for coming 5 years. The detail design of bond financing shall be an important watch point ahead.

## Maintain growth projection at the low-end amidst external uncertainties

The budget projects GDP growth to be in the range between 2.5% and 3.5% for 2024. We expect the growth support measures from the budget to help the overall economy. That said, the external growth uncertainties remain substantial for Hong Kong. UBS China economics team expects mainland China’s growth to moderate further in 2024. Recently, UBS US economics team pushed back the first Fed interest rate cut expectation to May, from previous expectation of March. Thus, interest rates in Hong Kong may stay higher for longer than we originally expected. On balance, we maintain our 2024 GDP growth forecast at 2.5%, at the low end of government’s projection.
