## Japan Equity Strategy

## Japanese equities following FOMC: Ongoing announcement of initiatives to boost RoE with results reports

The BoJ took no action to counter yen weakness at its April meeting, the May FOMC meeting was dovish, and April US employment data was weak, so expectations for FRB rate cuts before the year-end have grown again. The heavy downward pressure on the equity market due to the yen<sup>’</sup>s rapid depreciation has eased for now given also that some market participants seem to be expecting a second round of forex intervention by the Japanese government. Even so, the yen remains weak at the ¥153– 155/\$ level, and is at a level friendly for equity prices given likely growth in both real wages and consumption, along with corporate earnings. The results season has now started in full, and there have been a number of companies announcing measures to accelerate improvement in capital efficiency, such as increases to RoE targets and to shareholder returns, including Hitachi, Komatsu, West Japan Railway, and Mitsui & Co.

Summary

Easing in downward pressure on yen and reduction in forex risk for equity market after FOMC, employment statistics

Ongoing announcements of measures to boost RoE including increasing shareholder returns and targets, with nearly 30% of companies having reported results

One-year on from TSE-led reform demands: Rise in weighting of companies with PBRs above 1.0x from 50% to 60%; improvement in RoE of low-RoE names

Downward pressure on weak yen for equity market eased for now:The BoJ took no action to counter yen weakness at its April meeting, the May FOMC meeting was dovish, and April US employment data was weak, so expectations for FRB rate cuts before the year-end have grown again. The heavy downward pressure on the equity market due to the yen<sup>’</sup>s rapid depreciation has eased for now given also that some market participants seem to be expecting a second round of forex intervention by the Japanese government. Even so, the yen remains weak at the ¥153–155/\$ level relative to the FY2023 average of ¥145/\$ and the FY2024 corporate earnings assumption of ¥141.4/\$. The equity market is likely to see this as providing support to corporate earnings in the near term. US economic indicators remain critical in the near term, but if FRB Chair Jerome Powell<sup>’</sup>s view that “the next move is down<sup>”</sup> is accurate, the outlook will be for a reversal in the depreciation of the yen toward year-end. Our forecast for the USDJPY at end-2024 is ¥153/\$, compared with the forward rate of ¥149/\$ and the Bloomberg consensus outlook for ¥147/\$, which is a slightly stronger yen level than the current level (Figure 1). We see this as a marketfriendly level for Japanese equities, as in this range there should be growth in real wages and personal consumption, and corporate earnings should benefit.

Ongoing announcements of increases in corporate targets for RoE, shareholder returns: The results season has started in full (just under 30% of companies had reported by May 2), and with many companies reporting full-year results, they have included increases to RoE targets and further rises in shareholder returns as part of efforts to boost capital efficiency in their new medium-term plans. Of companies reporting already, the digital solutions platform business is driving improvement in the structure of the business portfolio and earnings at Hitachi, and the announcement of a share buyback of up to ¥200 billion exceeded market expectations. Fujitsu also reported strong earnings, driven by the domestic sales of the service solutions segment, and announced a share buyback of up to ¥180 billion. Earnings were strong at Komatsu, which announced a share buyback of up to ¥100 billion for the first time in 10 years. Mitsui & Co. announced a share buyback of up to ¥200 billion and a hike in the dividend to ¥100 per share, underlining the efforts of the trading sector to boost shareholder returns following Mitsubishi Corporation<sup>’</sup>s announcement of a ¥500 billion buyback in February. Daiichi Sankyo announced a big increase in R&D investment, in addition to a share buyback of up to ¥200 billion. There were a number of positive catalysts in the new medium-term plan of West Japan Railway, including an increase in its operating profit target, a dividend hike in excess of its 35% policy for the dividend payout ratio, and an increase in its RoE target. At Denso, guidance was solid, and the ongoing sales of shares in Toyota group suppliers has made the market aware of the possibility of its largest ever share buyback (Figures 3-5).

Improvement in RoE, PBRs one year on from TSE demands: One year has now passed since the TSE requested that corporate management focus more on adding value with an emphasis on the cost of capital and share prices, and corporate reforms of the type noted above continue without pause. Among the 1,638 TSE Prime companies, there was an increase of 184 or around 10%, from 50% to 60%, in those with PBRs in excess of 1.0x (Figure 7). Of these, RoE improved at 117 companies. The PBR of TSE Prime companies overall (median) improved from 1.0x at the end of March 2023 to 1.2x at the end of March 2024 (Figure 8). Looking at RoE (last 12 months actual) at the end of March 2024, there was an improvement in low-ROE names (ROE of 5% or less; Figure 9). Share buybacks are at an all-time high of ¥10.1 trillion (announcement date basis, through the end of March 2024), pushing up RoE by 0.2ppt, according to our calculation. Dividends are also expected to be at the highest level ever.
