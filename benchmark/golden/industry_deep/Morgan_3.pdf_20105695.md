## 1. New Year’s Day holiday tourism

China had the three-day New Year<sup>’</sup>s holiday from December 30, 2023 - January 1, 2024.

Domestic passenger traffic: The Ministry of Transport estimated that daily average domestic passenger flow during the holiday reached 42.7mn person-time, rising 78.4%oya on a low base. In growth terms, passenger flow via railway and waterway outperformed, rising 177.5% and 72.9% compared to the 2023 New Year<sup>’</sup>s Day holiday period.

Cross-border passenger traffic: The National Immigration Administration reported that 5.2 million entry and exit trips were made over the three-day holiday, 4.7 times that of the 2023 New Year<sup>’</sup>s Day holiday, largely back to the 2019 level.

Tourism spending: The number of tourists reached 135mn person-time (vs. 52.7mn for the 2023 New Year<sup>’</sup>s Day holiday, or 826mn for the 2023 National Day holiday), 109.4% of the 2019 level. Tourism revenue recorded 79.7bn yuan (vs. 26.5bn yuan for the 2023 New Year<sup>’</sup>s Day holiday or 753.4bn yuan for the 2023 National Day holiday), 105.6% of the 2019 level. Tourism expenditure per capita ticked lower to 97% of the 2019 level on a comparable basis (vs. 98% for the 2023 National Day holiday).

• According Maoyan, China's box office revenue generated during the three-day holiday reached 1.5 billion yuan, higher than recent years.

Home sales: Beijing led the new home sales recovery after the latest policy efforts to lower mortgage rates and downpayment ratios, but other cities remained generally soft. According to CREIS, 40 major cities<sup>’</sup> new home sales fell 26% during the three-day New Year<sup>’</sup>s Day holiday, with a 3% modest fall in Tier 1 cities supported by the 26% growth in Beijing, a 17% drop in Tier 2 cities and a 50% decline in Tier 3/4 cities.

## 3. Government bond issuance and PBOC liquidity operations

Muted local government bond issuance into year-end. Special LGB issuance was 92bn yuan for December, part of which was small- and medium-sized bank special LGBs. There was no special refinancing LGB issuance in the second half of December, leaving full-month issuance at 23bn yuan (vs. 1013bn yuan in October and 353bn yuan in November).

• CGB issuance reached around 850bn yuan in December, roughly fulfilling the fullyear issuance target of 4160bn yuan.

Instead of an RRR cut as anticipated by the market, the PBOC expanded MLF operations in December, with net liquidity injection at 800bn yuan. The PBOC also scaled up open market operation daily pace, leaving a net liquidity injection of 158bn yuan. Looking ahead, we bring forward the forecast for the PBOC<sup>’</sup>s next policy rate cut (10bp) to 1Q (likely in March), and add another 10bp rate cut in 3Q (likely in September) in our baseline forecast. We also look for two 25bp RRR cuts, likely in 2Q and 4Q.

## 5. Domestic and international flights

Domestic and international flights picked up on holiday tourism. Flight execution for domestic airlines picked up into year-end, ending January 1 at 96.6% of the 2019 average (3dma, vs. 87.3% on December 13). Flight execution for international flights increased further, finally exceeding the 2019 average after the COVID outbreak, ending January 1 at 103.0% of the 2019 average (7dma, vs. 91.2% on December 13).

Outbound flights to major destinations are scheduled to pick up into the end of January when the Lunar New Year holiday approaches, led by ASEAN and ANZ. China<sup>’</sup>s scheduled flights to several destinations are supposed to increase in the coming four weeks, including to Japan (61.9%-->64.5%), Korea (76.5%-->79.0%), Singapore (94.0%-- >104.2%), Vietnam (67.8%-->76.9%), Thailand (56.0%-->71.2%), Malaysia (60.2%-- >80.3%), Hong Kong SAR (76.1%-->81.5%), Macau SAR (75.6%-->82.9%), Australia (101.8%-->109.3%) and New Zealand (121.8%-->134.5%). Thailand<sup>’</sup>s Prime Minister announced a permanent bilateral waiver of visa requirements with China for each other’s citizens from March.

## 6. Logistics and port freight

Growth of departing/arriving container and bulk ships<sup>’</sup> deadweight tonnage all moderated in December after the solid rebound in November.

Container ships usually carry consumer goods as well as some machinery equipment and electronics. For December as a whole, departing container ships<sup>’</sup> deadweight tonnage grew 9.2%oya (vs. 11.4% in November), softer than the 12.8% tracking growth as of December 13. Deadweight tonnage of arriving container ships rose 7.1% (vs.10.2% in November), also softer than the 11.9% tracking growth as of December 13.

Bulk carriers transport unpackaged bulk cargo for grain, coal, iron ore, steel etc in their cargo holds. For December as a whole, departing bulk ships<sup>’</sup> deadweight tonnage eased to 5.0%oya (vs. 13.6% in November), marginally stronger than the 4.4% tracking growth as of December 13. Deadweight tonnage of arriving bulk ships also rose 5.0% (vs. 10.4% in November), slightly softer than the 5.7% tracking growth as of December 13.

## 7. Consumption and production

Continued divergence in primary and secondary home sales. 30 major cities<sup>’</sup> new home transactions stayed soft, falling 11.5%oya in December (vs. -15.2% in November). Meanwhile, 8 major cities<sup>’</sup> secondary home sales continued to outpace the same period in 2022. Top 100 developers<sup>’</sup> aggregate contracted sales were weaker than expected on a 32%oya fall in December, with a 38% drop in SOE developers (a sharp widening from November<sup>’</sup>s -8%), and a 31% and 45% fall in non-distressed private developers and distressed developers, respectively.

Solid weekly pace in auto sales, anticipating a month- & year-end surge for the last week of December. As of December 24, CPCA estimates that auto retail sales rose 7%oya or 20%m/m nsa, and auto wholesales rose 17%oya or 5%m/m nsa. Meanwhile, NEV retail sales rose 22%oya or 10%m/m nsa and NEV wholesales rose 34%oya or 7%m/m nsa. CPCA forecasts total auto retail sales to grow 3%y/y in 2024.

• Box office daily revenue surged into the month-end, ending December 31 at 229.2mn yuan (7dma, vs. 93.6mn yuan on December 15).

Operating rates turned down into the New Year<sup>’</sup>s holiday, including steel rebar, coke oven petroleum asphalt plants, as well as semi- and all-steel tire plants.
