# Global Macro Commentary | Global

## February 7

Solid 10y auction snaps long-end UST underperformance; Riksbank's Jansson contextualizes March cut; European duration trades rangebound; SNB FX reserves increase, CHF weakens; CLP lags amid copper losses; USD/JPY is muted before BoJ's Uchida; DXY at 104.06 (-0.2%); US 10y at 4.121% (+2.1bp).

• US Treasuries retreat from session highs ahead of the \$42bn 10y UST auction, the largest on record, but supply concerns abate after the auction comes 1.2bp through.

• Riksbank Deputy Governor Jansson believes "a first policy-rate cut as early as in March is something that I currently do not consider impossible but nor do I think it is particularly probable."

• Peripheral spreads widen marginally in a rangebound session for European duration.

• CHF exhibits broad weakness after SNB FX reserves increase, which market participants interpret as a signal the SNB targeted CHF weakness and bought FX in January.

• CLP (-0.5%) lags LatAm peers against USD alongside a downturn in copper futures prices.

• USD/JPY (+0.2%) trades in a choppy manner before a speech by BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida.

price action was once again dictated by further declines in a regional US bank stock. As downside momentum abated, US Treasury yields then began to modestly rise, led by the long end, ahead of the largest 10y UST auction on record.

An overall uniform message from Fed policymakers that included Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari, Boston Fed President Collins, Richmond Fed President Barkin, and the first public remarks by Governor Kugler also likely biased yields higher. The overarching message was one that acknowledged it would likely be appropriate to cut rates “later” or “at some point” this year. Yet, all participants sought “greater confidence” before making policy less restrictive and emphasized the now two-sided risks around their dual mandate.

Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari called for two-three rate cuts this year, as appropriate. The big question for him is where the neutral rate of interest is. He referenced fundamental changes in the behavior of businesses and consumers have been longer lasting than expected. He also said the Fed is watching CRE “very carefully.”

At 1:00pm ET, the new \$42bn 10y UST auction stopped-out at 4.093%, 1.2bp through. Auction internals in line with the average of the last six new issue auctions was a notable feat, given the size was \~17% larger than the average of the last six new issue auctions. Relative to the November auction, the allotment to indirect bidders rose to 71.0% (P: 69.7%), the allotment to direct bidders rose to 16.1% (P: 15.2%), which left primary dealers with 13.0% (P: 15.1%). The bid-to-cover ratio rose to 2.56x (November: 2.45x). Despite the solid auction, US rates finished \~2bp cheaper across the curve.

In the NY afternoon, the Congressional Budget Office (CBO) released its 2024-2034 Budget and Economic Outlook. Compared to the May 2023 projection, the deficit for fiscal year 2024 is projected to be \$0.1tn smaller and the cumulative deficit over the 2024-2033 period is \$1.4tn (or 7%) smaller. According to CBO, the biggest factor contributing to smaller projected deficits is a reduction in discretionary spending because of newly enacted legislation (i.e., the Fiscal Responsibility Act). As a percentage of GDP, the FY24 deficit amounts to 5.6%, increases to 6.1% in FY25, and then shrinks to 5.2% in FY27 and FY28.

CBO projects FY24 net interest costs will be greater in relation to GDP than at any point since at least 1940, at 3.1%, and expects the interest burden to gradually climb over their forecast horizon. However, they expect 10y Treasury yields to average 4.6% over calendar year 2024 and 2025 – around 100bp above current marketimplied forwards of \~3.6%.

## Japan:

JGBs initially rallied on Wednesday, led by the belly of the curve, after the rally in USTs overnight on Tuesday. Long-end JGBs lagged ahead of the 30y JGB auction at noon in Tokyo. The 30y auction was in line with market pricing, with a 1.8% stop; a level most Japanese lifers have mentioned as their target for purchases. Although the auction result was not that strong, long-end JGBs gradually outperformed into the 3:00pm Tokyo close; 30y JGBs rallied by 1.3bp d/d, to 1.78%, on a simple yield basis. However, front-end JGBs gradually sold off into the 3:00pm Tokyo close and

## the curve bull-flattened.

TONA OIS twist flattened alongside JGBs, with the front end better paid. Market participants seemingly anticipated a hawkish message from BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida at his speech on Thursday. XCCY basis retraced its recent move and twiststeepened, with the front end better received.

USD/JPY fell from 148.50 to below 148.00 overnight on Tuesday after renewed concern about US CRE investments. In the Tokyo session on Wednesday, USD/JPY essentially traded rangebound \~148, without specific catalysts. Market participants will closely monitor BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida’s speech on Thursday for hints about the chance of NIRP removal ahead of the March monetary policy meeting. Outside of this, focus will be on the upcoming US CPI annual revisions and how they will affect the direction of USD/JPY now that the Fed has emphasized it will focus more on inflation developments rather than growth.

## Europe:

Risk-sensitive currencies, save for AUD, gained marginally in the London session, buoyed by a rally in equities. SEK outperformance relative to risk-sensitive peers stemmed from the Riksbank minutes, which, despite being balanced, tilted more hawkish than the press release. The biggest mover in the G10 was CHF, which depreciated by 0.5% against both USD and EUR. CHF weakness was driven by the increase in SNB FX reserves, which market participants interpreted as a signal the SNB targeted CHF weakness and bought FX in January.

European duration remained rangebound on Wednesday, in a \~4bp range. 10y Bund yields eventually closed 2.5bp higher, at 2.31%, after a dip below 2.27% alongside a sell-off in US regional banks. Similar to the past week's deterioration of risk sentiment, core ASW richened but was short-lived, and the complex closed mixed (Schatz ASW: -0.3bp; Bund ASW: +0.5bp). Peripheral spreads widened concomitantly (10y BTP-Bund spread: +1bp). In swaps (against 6m), 10s30s hovered near -20bp and closed unchanged. The 2s5s10 butterfly cheapened by over 1.5bp, while the 5s10s30s butterfly richened by 0.5bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

G10 FX price action was muted in London hours. The DXY USD Index ended the London session 0.1% lower, amid limited catalysts and a lack of conviction in either direction.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Minutes from the January Riksbank meeting included Deputy Governor Jansson’s opinion about a rate cut in March. He said it is “something that I currently do not consider impossible but nor do I think it is particularly probable (…) A first rate cut at the meeting either in May or in June currently feels significantly more realistic.” A further point of focus for various members was the risk to inflation posed by a weakening krona: “if the Krona were to end up in a depreciating trend again, there is a risk that inflation might not fall back towards the target in line with our forecast”

• Swiss National Bank reported its FX Reserves at the end of January to be CHF662.4bn (December: CHF654.2bn), the second consecutive monthly increase

• ECB Executive Board Member Schnabel warned “against adjusting the policy stance soon” amid sticky services inflation, a resilient labor market, and recent events in the Red Sea. She added, “we must be patient and cautious because we know also from historical experience that inflation can flare up again”

• Fed Governor Kugler said it may be appropriate to cut rates “at some point”, and she is optimistic inflation progress will continue, although she is watching the data closely to verify the continued progress in inflation

• Boston Fed President Collins said it would likely be appropriate to ease rates “later this year,” as the risk of persistent over-2% inflation has receded

• Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari said “2-3 cuts this year as appropriate now,” but noted the Fed can take time to assess the data before cutting

• The Summary of Deliberations from the January BoC meeting revealed the Governing Council agreed the current stance of monetary policy was likely “sufficiently restrictive” to achieve the 2% inflation target. Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. ## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
