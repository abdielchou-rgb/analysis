# US Big Data: Stable growth & elevated inflation

## Growth momentum remains stable and inflation elevated

The April US Big Data Growth Nowcast signal points to stability (-0.2) in m/m growth momentum vs. +/-0.75 acceleration/slowdown thresholds (Figures 1, 6). Relative to March reported data, the Nowcasts expect slower but robust growth in Nonfarm Payrolls (205k vs. 303k) and Retail Sales ex. Autos & Gas (+0.2% vs. +1%), a slowing decline in Auto Sales (-0.6% vs. -2%), and ISM Manufacturing (50.8 vs. 50.3) moving a bit higher in the expansionary territory. Please see page 2 for detail. The growth momentum metric anticipates combined m/m growth changes across the monthly indicators, accounting for recent data volatility. Historically, stability favours moderate market risk (Figures 7-10). Though not an input to the growth signal, the Nowcasts also expect CPI inflation to remain elevated: Headline (+0.37% vs. +0.4%), Core (+0.33% vs. +0.36%). Our economists' CPI forecasts are also elevated: +0.38% headline and +0.29% core.

## Risk assets do well in stability but 'higher-for-longer' risks remain

Historically, US growth momentum stability benefits risk assets (Figures 7-10). DM equities beat long-term averages, high yield credit delivered solid returns and the yield curve steepened amid weak USD performance. That said, the potential for a prolonged inflation upside and the Fed delaying rate cuts amid resilient growth, evident in the Nowcasts, poses "higher-for-longer" risks that tactically favour value vs. growth/quality. In the coming months, our US economists expect both inflation and growth to slow gradually, avoiding a recession, as labour markets cool. This is a more benign scenario, where risk premia remain subdued. A sustained slowdown amid sticky inflation is a more dangerous outcome that the market is not priced for, posing a risk to the recent cyclical performance and higher volatility. So far, consumer confidence has offset weakness in the rest of the economy amid higher interest rates but lower fiscal transfers and credit availability. However, hours worked and employment metrics, other than Nonfarm Payrolls have slowed, realigning with pre-Covid trends.

## Larger moves continue to be priced on the key Payrolls, CPI and FOMC days

The April Nowcasts are below consensus for Payrolls (205k vs. 246k cons.), above for ISM Manufacturing (50.8 vs. 50.1 cons.), and elevated but close to consensus on Headline (0.37% vs. 0.38% cons.) and Core (0.33% vs. 0.36%) CPI, Figure 3. These outcomes should support risk sentiment on balance, unless any surprise to the upside. Stock index option implied moves are above underlying volatility on these data release days (Figure 2), as inflation and policy remain the key drivers of market sentiment.
