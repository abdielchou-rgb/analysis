# Japan Equity Strategy: Monthly Wrap

What’s changed since the sell-off: Growth in long-only positions, shift from overseas demand-related to domestic demand-related and thematic stocks

Japanese equities experienced their largest drop ever on August 5 (down 25% from their July 11 high). We believe this was because, in response to the BoJ’s hawkish stance/FOMC’s dovish stance at their respective July meetings and weak US employment data, (1) expectations for Japanese and US monetary policies changed, (2) the risk of recession in the US increased, (3) yen carry trade unwound, and (4) increased market volatility led to dumping. Since then, share prices have recovered 70-80% of their declines. We wrote in the early hours of August 7 that Japanese equities had likely bottomed, and we slightly lowered our Japanese equities forecast on the morning of August 9 to reflect the outlook for yen appreciation, while pointing out that there was still upside. We expect market fluctuations in September due to Japan/US monetary policy meetings, the LDP presidential election, and the upcoming US presidential election, but if the yen appreciates to around ¥140/US\$ by the end of March 2025, the impact should be manageable (J.P. Morgan house view is ¥144/US \$). Following the sell-off, we look for market volatility to remain high for the time being, and our equity investment strategy-related focus points are domestic demand, defensiveness, resilience to yen appreciation, and corporate reforms. Among sectors, we recommend overweighting IT services, heavy industry, domestic consumption relateds, and financials. During our visits to Asian investors (August 19-30), we confirmed a constructive attitude toward Japanese equities, mainly among long-only investors, which we believe is another positive factor.

Stock price performance: TOPIX and the Nikkei 225 fell 2.9% and 1.2%, respectively, in August, underperforming the S&P 500’s and STOXX Europe 600’s rises of 2.3% and 1.3%, respectively (Figure 1 – local currency basis). In US dollar terms, TOPIX was down 0.4% due to yen appreciation, so the underperformance was relatively small on that basis. Japanese equities experienced their biggest decline ever on August 5 (down 25% from their July 11 high), and we believe the main reasons for this were (1) changes in the BoJ/ FOMC outlook (responsible for 15% of the drop), (2) unwinding of the yen carry trade (25%), (3) growing fears of recession (25%), and (4) dumping (35%). As of August 30, share prices had recouped 70-80% of their maximum declines at the time of the sell-off, and while all the aforementioned factors settled down, the impact of dumping receded significantly (report). Following the sell-off, we lowered our year-end Japanese equity forecast by 6% to factor in yen appreciation and growing concerns about a recession (August 9 report). On the other hand, we believe the resilience of the Japanese stock market to future shocks has increased since the sell-off, as long-only positions on Japanese equities have increased and yen short positions have decreased.

In August, domestic demand-related sectors such as retail, transportation & logistics, pharmaceuticals, electricity & gas, and IT & services, as well as overseas demand-related sectors such as electrical equipment & precision instruments, which are expected to grow even amid yen appreciation, performed well. Conversely, sectors that are hit hard by yen appreciation, such as autos & transportation equipment, machinery, basic materials & chemicals, and trading companies, performed weakly. The financial sector underperformed due to pressure from CTAs and others to eliminate positions, and the belief that the hurdle for the BoJ’s next interest rate hike has risen (Figure 2). The Nikkei 225/TOPIX (N/T) ratio was 14.3x as of end-August, up slightly from the previous month. Looking at domestic investors’ position indicators at start-August (before the sell-off: July 30–August 1) based on the QUICK monthly survey, we note that domestic institutional investors were overweight in financials, basic materials, communications, steel, machinery, construction, and real estate, and underweight in autos, consumer related, pharmaceuticals, food, utilities, and electrical equipment & precision instruments.

EPS estimates and valuations: Although consensus estimates were revised to factor in a stronger yen post sell-off, fundamentals (consensus EPS estimates) are strong. As of end-August, the TOPIX I/B/E/S consensus EPS estimate called for growth of 8.2% YoY (3M revision +1.7%) for FY2024 and 11.9% YoY (+2.2%) for FY2025 (Figure 28). In the US, as of end-August, the FY2024 EPS consensus estimate was +15.4% YoY (+20bps MoM), and in Europe, the FY2024 EPS consensus estimate was +4.6% (flat MoM) on an MSCI index basis (I/B/E/S). As for valuations, TOPIX’s 12-month forward P/E was 14.0x as of end-August (down slightly from 14.1x at end-July), while its 12-month forward P/B was roughly flat MoM at 1.30x.

Factors: In August, in the absence of any interest rate events specific to Japan, quality, growth, reversal and short-term momentum were strong, just like in the US market, while value, high dividend, and long-term momentum were weak (Figure 36, non-sector neutralized). Factors specific to Japan will likely be reflected from October onward, once the contrasting monetary policies of Japan and the US become a theme.

Fund flows: Following the sell-off, buyers of Japanese equities in August (week of August 2 to week of August 23) were overseas long-only investors, pension funds, retail investors via mutual funds, and business corporations. Pension funds (via trust banks) were one of the largest buyers, with net purchases of ¥0.6 trillion of repurchase positions. Business corporations were net buyers by ¥1.1 trillion due to buybacks of their own shares, while retail investors (including via investment trusts) were net buyers by ¥0.1 trillion (spot +¥0.0 trillion, futures -¥0.3 trillion, investment trusts +¥0.4 trillion). Meanwhile, overseas investors were net sellers of futures by ¥1.5 trillion.
