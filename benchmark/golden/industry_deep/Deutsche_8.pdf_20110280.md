# India – Signs of growth slowdown; Receive 5Y

We recommend receiving INR 5Y swaps in addition to our existing long IGB 7Y exposure in the Asia portfolio.

n Our highest-frequency daily growth indicator suggests the growth miss that first showed up with the March-June quarter may have now widened.

n Inflation outcomes should also be incrementally conducive to this position with good monsoons and a food-price supportive sowing season.

We remain more circumspect on FX, with RBI REER close to the stronger end of its decade-long range.

The technical rationale for choosing the tenor above for our trade over others is presented in the Annex.

Our readers will recall that GDP growth missed – vs. RBI’s Nowcast & forecast – for the March-June 2024 quarter. Our highest-frequency daily growth indicator suggests that the growth slowdown may have widened further since, possibly substantially (Figure 1).

Relying on any daily data necessitates the statutory warning that these are still early readings, and that the guidance might shift. However, we would like to highlight our cause for concern.

1 Manufacturing. The steepest drop is in daily electricity generation, which correlates almost perfectly with both the monthly generation numbers, released as a part of the Eight Infrastructure Industries release (August data due on September 30), as well as the electricity demand data (August data already out, Figure 2).

n Consumer durables. There is a substantial dip also apparent in car sales (August -9.3%, Figure 3) and two-wheeler sales (down to 3%, after averaging double-digits earlier in the year).

n Sales. Sales and earnings for listed large corporates – tracked by Bloomberg – are both showing signs of slowdown, again over the August-September period (Figure 4).

The weakness in data since August is increasingly palpable. The only highfrequency indicator still holding up somewhat is credit card transactions – although even that is showing some early signs of weakness in September mtd (-0.7% mom sa vs. +1.3% in Aug and Jul).

RBI's stance continues to be in favor of withdrawal of accommodation. Governor Das asserted, though, in a recent speech that “balance between inflation and growth is well-poised”. Were this high frequency data to get confirmed in more monthly and quarterly prints in coming days, this could shift the narrative further still towards more downside in growth.

The technical rationale for choosing the tenor above for our trade over others is presented in the Technical Annex below.

Inflation outcomes should also be incrementally conducive to both positions. RBI's biggest cause for concern has been on food inflation. There is good news in this context:

n Progress of this year’s monsoon has been better than historical averages with both (1) the geographical distribution, and (2) the temporal spread over the crucial sowing season in July-August doing well.

n Accordingly, the sowing season itself has gone well, up 2.3% yoy in acreage.

n Moreover, the shift in sowing towards more food crops (crucially pulses and cereals), and away from cash crops (cotton and jute) should also help reduce RBI’s worries on future food inflation.

We remain more circumspect on FX. RBI REER is still close to the top end of its last ten-years’ range (Figure 5). If the economy is indeed slowing, for which there may be emergent signs, then the REER should be weaker anyway. And, while lower Indian inflation will do some of the leg work – likely not more than a few pts worth – much of the heavy lifting will need to be done by NEER to get relative valuation in check.

## Technical Annex

Author's note: This section is intended to be technical and showcases some of the tools we use to pick the best rates expression for a macro view. These are useful over and above standard metrics such as carry+roll, carry/vol, percentile, Z-Scores, etc. If it is of limited interest, please feel free to skip this bit in its entirety as it has no implications for the macro view laid out above.

Here we are introducing three of the tools we use for rates analysis.

## PCA-based 5y equivalents

Our first port of call is to see what shape has the curve taken in the past few days. In India's case where a majority of the move is due to the level shift (or PC1) vs. little impact from changes in curve shape (PC2) or curvature (PC3), choosing pretty much any point to pay/recv for a macro view should work equally well. We can show this by looking at the 5y equivalent moves for other points on the curve as shown in Figure 6.

The way to read the chart is that a 1bp move in the 5y point has come at the same time as a 0.92bp move in the 1y over the past 21-days, and 0.96bp move over the past 60-days. Such a levels-driven shift is not always the case across Asia, but is often so in India. In contrast, Taiwan's curve is usually far steeper, where we can then make the choice on the best point vs. capital allocated, of course combined with the other tools below and our macro take.

## Cross-market terminal rate out/underperformance

The second tool we would deploy is the terminal rate pricing deviations on a cross-market basis to see if our recommendation has been an underperformer (i.e., yields have not fallen as much as others on our panel) or an outperformer. We look at various look-back periods to pick-out common moves across different markets, and by netting these out from the actual moves leaving us with only the idiosyncratic bit.

We want to reiterate our macro views will still overrule this tool, but this can increase or reduce our confidence in our call depending on the market conditions. The tool is meant to help us understand the initial setup for the trade.

Say, we want to receive rates, and the market is an underperformer – as is the case with India currently (Figure 7 and Figure 8) – we would ask is this because of technical reasons such as models paying (not good as these flows can last and we may be standing against momentum), or because the market's views differ from ours (good as this is hopefully where we have an edge to express). We believe in India's case it is the latter.

Also, it is useful in Asia's case to look at both 5y5y and 3y2y points to look at terminal pricing as not all our curves are liquid at the 10y point (to back out the 5y5y rate).

## PCA-implied curve deviation

The final tool we are introducing is PCA-implied intra-curve deviation. This is helpful in identifying "kinks" in the curve – usually introduced by flows. The analysis looks at the shape of the curve in its own history and comes up with an average vol-adjusted curve and sees how is the current curve shape different from that. We look at four look-back periods presented in the tables below. We also look at both the standard curve and standard fly positions (Figure 9 and Figure 10), and the forwards curve (Figure 11 and Figure 12) too.

Our 5y trade of INR-NDOIS looks ok here, certainly when compared with other points and their liquidity. While we will always have macro underpinnings in our work, tactical investors can pursue stand-alone trades on these screeners, particularly in flys, for their mean-reversion properties alone.
