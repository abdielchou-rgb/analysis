# September 6

August payrolls miss alongside fall in unemployment; USTs bullsteepen after struggling to digest NFP; Fed's Waller would support front-loading cuts; CAD weakens amid weak Canada labor data; risk assets fall; PHP and TWD outperform in region; DXY at 101.19 (0.1%); US 10y at 3.708% (-1.9bp).

• US August employment report contains a miss in the headline payrolls figure with notable downward revisions to the prior two months, but the unemployment rate ticks down to 4.2% (P: 4.3%).

• USTs struggle to find footing as market participants digest nonfarm payrolls data and fedspeak, which are unable to provide clear evidence on the magnitude of Fed cuts to come; USTs ultimately bull-steepen with the 2s10s curve disinverting as 2y yields reach their lowest level since 2022.

• Fedspeak leaves a 50bp cut on the table with Fed Governor Waller stating he would be “an advocate of front-loading rate cuts,” if appropriate; intraday STIR pricing implies as much as a 50% probability of a 50bp cut in September, only to unwind and price in \~30% chance by the end of the session.

• CAD weakens -0.5% against the USD as the currency is weighed down by Canada labor data with a higher-than-expected rise in the unemployment rate alongside a drop in full time employment.

• Risk assets interpret the US labor data as a bad sign for the economy with the S&P 500 (-1.0%) having its worst week since March 2023, high yield credit spreads widening, and brent crude oil futures tumbling lower (-2.2%).

• PHP and TWD outperform AxJ peers ahead of US labor data, strengthening 0.6% and 0.5% against the USD alongside gains in their local equity markets.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

With both the economy and the Fed seemingly beginning to shift gears, the US August employment report was closely watched for further clarity on the state of the labor market.

The highly anticipated release was a bit of a mixed bag. The headline payrolls figure was notably soft at 142k (C: 165k) and came alongside sizeable downward revisions to the past two months, with the already weak July figure being revised down to 89k (P: 114k). However, as expected, the unemployment rate fell to 4.2% (P: 4.3%), but was unable to fully reverse last month’s rise. The report undoubtedly had soft elements, confirming cooling in the labor market, but it was not necessarily weak enough to solidify market participants’ expectation for a 50bp cut from the Fed.

Given that the data was difficult to reconcile with a clear monetary policy path, the price action was volatile. UST yields fell sharply immediately following the release, alongside weakening in the USD, but both gradually pulled back the moves. STIR pricing was also volatile with market implied probability of a 50bp cut in September reaching 50% intraday, only to end the day with the probability below the odds priced in before the release. Market pricing implied \~30% chance of a 50bp cut.

With the Fed entering the blackout period before the September meeting this weekend, fedspeak was closely watched for guidance on the magnitude of rate cuts. New York Fed President Williams confirmed that easing is likely to begin as he stated “it is now appropriate to dial down the degree of restrictiveness in the stance of policy by reducing the target range for the federal funds rate.” However, he said he does not have a personal view on 25bp or 50bp.

Fed Governor Waller also stated that he believes it is important to start cutting rates in September. In terms of the size of cuts, he added that he will be “an advocate of front-loading rate cuts if that is appropriate.” Waller’s remarks reignited the rally in USTs and bets for a larger rate cut in September. However, some interpreted his remarks as signaling that it is better to start at a slower pace and that after the first cut, the magnitude and pace of easing can accelerate. This interpretation was largely driven by his statement where he noted that “if subsequent data show a significant deterioration in the labor market, the FOMC can act quickly and forcefully to adjust monetary policy.” Heading into the Fed blackout period, fedspeak seemingly confirmed imminent rate cuts but did not solidify a 50bp cut in September.

Ultimately, the UST curve bull-steepened. 2y yields fell 10bp and reached their lowest level since 2022, while 30y yields ended relatively flat. The 2s10s curve closed positive for the first time since July 2022. The USD also traded choppily but ended the day relatively flat.

Risk assets had a more consistently bearish reaction to the employment data. S&P 500 fell -1.7% which resulted in the index having its worst week since the banking crisis in March 2023. Oil prices continued to fall further amid demand concerns with brent crude oil futures down -2.2%.

## Japan:

In the Tokyo morning, while activity in short-end was relatively muted, 30y JGB yields traded lower following the overnight rally in UST yields. However, the sector retraced some of its strength and sold off alongside 20y JGBs into the Tokyo afternoon. The BoJ conducted JGB purchase operations in the 5-10y, 10-25y, and greater than 25y sectors, and the operation was digested smoothly across the maturity baskets. After the BoJ’s purchase operations, JGBs broadly rallied partly supported by the demand to square positions ahead of US employment data. Superlong JGBs rallied, and the 30y sector outperformed the most on the curve. The JGB curve ultimately bull-flattened; 5y JGB rallied 0.5bp to 0.495%, 10y JGB rallied 2.5bp to 0.845%, and 30y JGB rallied 3.0bp to 2.01% on a simple yield basis.

TONA-OIS curve also bull-flattened, but underperformed JGBs in the long-end. XCCY basis widened on broad sectors particularly in the short-end. Next week, there will be a 5y JGB auction on Tuesday, and the BoJ’s JGB purchase operation in 1-3y, 3- 5y, 5-10y, and 25y+ sectors on Wednesday.

While USD/JPY edged down marginally in the Tokyo morning, the pair accelerated its downward momentum in the afternoon alongside the rally in UST yields ahead of the US NFP release for August following weak US ADP/Jolts employment data. As of Tokyo 3pm, USD/JPY declined to 142.63 from 143.45 in the early Tokyo morning. Other CCY/JPY generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY, with risk sensitive currencies such as AUD, NZD particularly underperforming.

Europe:

Choppy price action in European FX as market participants digested the US nonfarm payrolls data. During the London session, risk-sensitive currencies weakened and NOK fell -0.9%, weighed on by a selloff in oil as well. SEK fared better than its Scandinavian neighbor and fell -0.5%, possibly propped up slightly by its sensitivity to front-end US rates (September and November Fed meeting pricing saw the probability of a 50bp cut pared during the London session). Sterling dropped -0.5%. EUR followed the risk currencies, and was down -0.4% during the London session, despite its recent sensitivity to US rates. CHF ultimately ended the London session flat relative to the US dollar, but thus saw gains of +0.3% over EUR.

## Dollar Bloc:

A tumultuous day in G10 FX with price swings following the August US labor market data release. After a couple of reversals, markets ultimately settled on riskoff with US yields lower and equities deeply in the red. As a result, risk currencies suffered, of which AUD and NZD felt the most pain, down -1.1% and -1% respectively. CAD was weighed on as well by its own August labor market data which did not meet expectations and ended the London session -0.5% weaker against the USD. The DXY USD Index gained +0.2% during the London session.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies strengthened broadly, but moves were mostly modest as market participants awaited the US August NFP data release. USD/CNH weakened slightly by 0.1% to \~7.0830 amid limited fresh local news. INR, SGD, MYR, and IDR also weakened \~0.1-0.2%. PHP and TWD led regional gains. USD/PHP fell 0.6% to 55.92 alongside a 0.4% gain in its local equity market. USD/TWD also weakened 0.5% as the local equity market gained 1.2% and foreign equity outflows stabilized at - \$22mn. THB gained 0.3% as its local equity market continued to outperform in Asia (SET: +1.6%), likely buoyed by Thursday’s news that the state fund would invest in Thai stock starting from October 1. KRW rallied 0.4% to 1,328 despite the 1.2% weakness in KOSPI as it was supported by the weak USD.

AxJ rates also rallied broadly following the move in global rates, but similar to FX, moves were mostly modest. China, India, and Thailand rates were all broadly stable. In China, 2y CGB yields fell 1bp while 10y and 30y CGBs remained stable amid media reports that suggested some 10y special bonds that the PBoC bought last week were being sold on Thursday. Singapore, Hong Kong, and Korea rates outperformed given their high correlation with US rates; 10y swap yields fell 7bp, 5bp, and 4bp, respectively. While Singapore and Korea’s 2s10s swaps curves bullflattened 2bp and 1bp, respectively, Hong Kong’s 2s10s curve was stable. Indonesia rates continued to rally for the third session; 10y IndoGB yields fell 2bp and returned to the August lows of \~6.6%.

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
