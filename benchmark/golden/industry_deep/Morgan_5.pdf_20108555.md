# Venture Vision: Meet the Dragons

Unlike "Unicorns" valued >\$1bn, "Dragons" have tried and failed to become a commonplace term. For the uninitiated, Dragons are companies that have raised >\$1bn in a single transaction. However, whilst they're less discussed, their importance seems to be gathering momentum under the radar.

## For more on AI venture funding: Do We Need 3000 AI Start-Ups?

Admittedly, it wasn't until last week that we heard of the phrase "Dragon" for the first time. The earliest reference we can find to Dragons comes from Livemint - an Indian media outlet focussed on venture/growth - in 2015. Since then it seems not to have caught the imagination of the investing world in the way "Unicorn" has.

We thought the term was due a second chance. The number of Dragon deals as a portion of all Unicorn deals (as well as a portion of all VC deals) has made all time highs in 2024. The amount of capital raised by Dragons is running at c25% of all capital raised by Unicorns in 2024 YTD, a record as far back as our data go. Certainly, some of this skew can be attributed to a market-wide slump in funding meaning that mega-deals appear disproportionately meaningful. However, with the amount of Dragon funding in 2024 set to (pro forma) comfortably exceed any year other than 2021, this fund-raising category is showing little effect from the higher-for-longer interest rate environment impacting other long-duration speculative assets.

If we take a longer term horizon than just 2024, this skew of Dragon raises in AI/ Autonomous becomes clearer. Looking back over the past 5 years, as much as 43% of all capital raised have been by Dragon companies in the Autonomous vehicle vertical. For context, the capital intensive vertical of Cleantech (focussing on battery breakthroughs predominantly) comes in at half this level.

After all, if the limiting factor in Autonomous driving is scale data, access to compute and machine vision model training for driverless cars, there are few companies able to reach scale worth backing. And those that do reach scale will need disproportionately more capital to compete against the lead that Tesla and other OEMs have established. See the views (here and here) from our US Autos team on the amount of data captured by GM each second:

One perhaps surprising element of this analysis - and is shown in part by the table above - is the unexpectedly high proportion of these large fund raises occurring in Europe. By comparison, Asian Dragon deals had been leading as a portion of total market capital deployed over the last decade. This has since faded as European Dragon companies have begun to take share in this exclusive category. With low expectations of Europe's ability to compete in some of these emerging technology verticals, trends like this present more of a reason to be optimistic on home-grown talent and technology.

## FINTECH RADAR – ADAM WOOD & MARK HYATT

The Fintech Radar (2 June 2024)

• #1 Banking platform Relay raised \$32.2m in Series B financing: This week (29th May), US-based small business banking platform Relay has announced that it has secured \$32.2m in Series B financing, led by Bain Capital Ventures (supported by BTV, Garage, Tapestry, and Industry Ventures). This brings Relay's total funding to \$51.6m to date. The new financing aims to accelerate Relay's development in product areas such as spend management, smart credit products and a financial API marketplace. The company notes that this funding round is the "next step toward Relay's overarching goal of delivering AI-powered predictive cash flow analytics to SMBs". According to the company, group revenue increased 3x in 2022 and close to 6x in 2023.

• #2 Swiipr raises £6m in Series A funding to drive growth in airline payments: This week (29th May) UK-based travel payments company Swiipr has announced that it has raised c. £6m in Series A funding, led by Octopus Ventures (supported by TX Ventures and Solano Partners). The company aims to use the funds to develop its strategy and product/technology, including investments in AI and data analytics to help drive international airline market growth. Swiipr is an airline specialised payments platform that enables airlines to digitise disruption payments, which the company states can greatly improve the passenger experience and drive cost efficiency by up to 60%. Following its launch in 2020, Swiipr is used by 26 airlines in 70 countries, including one major flag carrier.

• #3 Félix Pago secures \$15.5m in funding for cross-border transfers: According to press reports, this week (28th May) Latam cross-border payments fintech Félix Pago has raised \$15.5m in Series A funding to help fuel its growth in Latam and the US. According to Mastercard, remittance flows from the US to Latam and Caribbean countries represent a c. \$155bn market. Through its WhatsApp chatbot interface, Félix Pago is aiming to make the process of sending and receiving crossborder payments easier, and hopes to be able to capture some of that large market. The company is reportedly growing 30% month on month, and has signed multiple high profile partnerships, including Nubank or Latam e-commerce player Mercado Libre. The company plans to use the funding to expand into El Salvador and Nicaragua, followed by the Dominican Republic and Colombia, and then the rest of Latin America.

Unless otherwise indicated, the news reports referenced above have not been confirmed.

## Real-time Valuation Monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
