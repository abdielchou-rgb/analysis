# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## August 8

Low US jobless claims boost risk sentiment; hawkish rhetoric from RBA's Bullock; JGBs bull-flatten after strong 30y auction; UST bear-flatten; Banxico cuts rates by 25bp; RBI on hold; DXY at 103.23 (+0.0%); US 10y at 3.988% (+4.5bp).

• Smaller-than-expected US initial jobless claims ease fears of a deteriorating labor market and boost risk sentiment; equities rally (S&P 500: +2.3%), safe-haven currencies give up prior gains (USD/ CHF: +0.6%), and duration sells off (2y UST: +7.6bp).

• RBA Governor Bullock says she “does not see interest rates coming down quickly” and “will not hesitate” to raise them, if warranted; AUD gains 1.1%, supported by the hawkish RBA rhetoric and pickup in global risk sentiment.

• After Wednesday’s disappointing 10y JGB auction, an unexpectedly strong 30y JGB auction supports a bull-flattening in the JGB curve, with 30y yields rallying 10bp on the day.

• With economists and market pricing split on their expectations, Banxico delivers a 25bp rate cut in a 3:2 vote; Mexico 1y Swap Rates fall over 10bp following the decision.

## Japan:

The JGB curve bear-steepened in the Tokyo morning following the overnight move in US rates. The BoJ’s Summary of Opinion for the July meeting was released in the Tokyo morning. As expected, the minutes were hawkish and the Bank’s estimation that the neutral rate is “at least around 1%” may have temporarily weighed on the market especially in short-end sectors. Super long sectors also sold off, primarily driven by concession efforts ahead of the 30y JGB auction. The 30y JGB auction came out strong, supported by domestic real money market participants, with a cutoff price of 99.30 (C: 99.15). The super long sector rallied substantially following the auction, and JGB curve began to bull-flatten. Ultimately, the 30y sector rallied 10bp to 2.085% toward Tokyo close, even though it had traded at 2.23% before the auction. 2y JGB yields were unchanged at 0.265%, while 10yr JGB rallied 4.5bp to 0.83% on a simple yield basis.

TONA-OIS curve twist-flattened; super long sectors outperformed JGB, while the 2y sector underperformed JGB. The potential hawkish policy rate path described by the BoJ’s hawkish path in the Summary of Opinion supported better paying in short-end OIS. XCCY basis widened, led by the long end.

USD/JPY initially declined in the Tokyo morning to \~145.62 from 146.76, along with a decline in the stock market. By Tokyo afternoon, USD/JPY slightly reverted but largely traded in a range of 145.89-146.72 into the London morning without specific catalysts. Other CCY/JPY generally traded in tandem with USD/JPY.

## Europe:

In European FX, smaller-than-expected initial jobless claims in the US boosted risk sentiment which dominated price action. Sterling was bolstered by the risk-on sentiment post-claims, and rose +0.4% in defiance of rate differentials. The Scandinavian pairs, on the other hand, saw USD strength from higher US yields, which outpaced the positive risk sentiment that would otherwise have supported them. USD/NOK and USD/SEK both rose +0.4%. Safe havens unsurprisingly gave back some of their recent gains and, as a result, USD/CHF rose +0.5%.

## Dollar Bloc:

AUD gained 1.1% as it benefited from hawkish comments from RBA Governor Bullock, who said policymakers would not hesitate to raise rates if needed. The currency was also supported by the positive risk sentiment following lower-thanexpected US initial jobless claims. The reaction out of New Zealand was much more limited, and NZD gained just +0.1% during the London session, as falling Kiwi rates counterbalanced the otherwise positive global sentiment. Ultimately, the DXY USD Index was flat on the day.

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies rallied as the equity market weakness and global bond rally resumed. CNH, INR, and KRW were the underperformers in the region, and stayed largely stable. USD/CNH remained at \~7.171 despite a continued move higher in the USD/CNY fixing to 7.146, the highest since November 2023. USD/INR remained below the 84 level, at 83.96. USD/KRW finished the Tokyo session largely unchanged at 1,376 after it had gained as much as 0.3% intraday amid the equity market weakness – a possible sign that the positioning adjustment in KRW is largely complete. IDR and TWD outperformed in the region after lagging in the Asia FX rally during the carry trade unwinds in the past few weeks. USD/IDR weakened 0.9% to break below the 16,000 level, and reached 15,893.5, and USD/TWD weakened 0.8% to 32.42 even as TAIEX underperformed and fell -2%.

AxJ rates also rallied broadly. China was an exception; 7y CGBs sold off by 4bp to 1.99% amid media reports of selling by China’s state banks on Thursday morning. Other tenors on the curve also sold off \~2-3bp amid media reports of regulators slowing the approval process for new bond funds, and the Jiangsu province asking some banks to suspend government bond trading. 5y CNY NDIRS yield also rose 4bp, and the 1s5s curve bear-steepened 1bp. In India, 1y INR NDOIS yield rose 3bp as the RBI kept rates on hold with an unchanged stance and unchanged FY2025 forecasts. 5y NDOIS and 10y G-sec yields fell 1bp. Singapore rates outperformed, and yields fell \~7bp across the IRS curve. In Thailand, 5y NDOIS yield fell 5bp, and reversed half of the move higher on Wednesday post the upside surprise in inflation.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The BoJ’s Summary of Opinion for the July meeting was released and was in line with the statement and Governor Ueda’s press conference. However, some market participants viewed the Bank’s estimation of “at least around 1%” of neutral rate as more hawkish, though it should likely be discounted due to estimation error. • RBA Governor Bullock reiterated her hawkish rhetoric at a speech in New South Wales. In her speech, Bullock pointed to sticky services prices and strong domestic demand, and claimed the board had “explicitly” considered hiking in its meeting on Tuesday. She added that policymakers will not hesitate to raise interest rates again if needed

• Richmond Fed President Barkin said that he is optimistic about future inflation readings and that the recent broadening in disinflation will continue. He added that the Fed has time to assess the economy and determine the policy response

## Emerging Markets

• RBI kept its policy rate unchanged at 6.5% at the August meeting, with an unchanged stance as widely expected. There continued to be two dissenters who voted for a 25bp rate cut. The central bank maintained its FY2025 GDP projection at 7.2% and FY2025 CPI projection at 4.5%, with risks evenly balanced. With the monetary policy statement reiterating the vigilance on inflation trend and food inflation risks, which can potentially adversely impact inflation expectations, our economists continue to see RBI keeping rates on hold through the end of 2025. The view to hold rates is also supported by a robust growth trend warranting higher neutral real rates and headline inflation yet to align to the 4% target (for more, read here)

• RBI Governor Das said the central bank has to “remain vigilant to prevent spillovers or second round effects from persistent food inflation and preserve the gains made so far in monetary policy credibility.” He added the RBI cannot be complacent just because core inflation has slowed considerably, as food inflation pressures cannot be ignored. He noted the resilience of India’s financial system amid volatility in global markets, highlighting that lower INR volatility shows stability in India’s financial system. Das also called for banks to do more to attract household savings to prevent liquidity issues

• RBI Deputy Governor Patra stated that the current level of policy rate is “probably exactly right” if one factors in the neutral rate. He highlighted there is ample room for foreign investors to invest in G-secs, with the existing pool of bonds under the FAR category and \~INR 4tr of new bond issuances will be open to the FAR route. He added the central bank is reviewing the OIS limit for foreigners

• Banxico lowered its policy rate by 25bp to 10.75%, in a 3–2 vote split at its August meeting. Markets and economists were pricing \~50% chance of a cut. In the statement, policymakers mentioned market volatility and the MXN depreciation, but also highlighted two elements that favored the cut: 1) although supply shocks translated into non-core pressures, core inflation metrics continued to improve gradually and they continue to expect convergence by 4Q25; and 2) the slowdown in growth seen in recent data. Given the focus on activity, our economists see risks to their call that Banxico will only cut in December this year

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
