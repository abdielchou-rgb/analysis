This view is simple, an extension of this prior work and predicated on the following:

• Rates start to fall in 3Q: While our US Economist, Ellen Zentner, has pushed the first rate cut expectation to July (from June) and takes out one cut from this year, she still expects three cuts of -25bps this year and four more in 2025 with the FF target range ending the year at 4.5-4.75% and next year at 3.5-3.75%.

• Bankruptcies peak in 2025: There has typically been a delay from first Fed rate cut to peak bankruptcy in the US. The contrarian view here is the sheer scale of dry powder and private debt facilities which could see bankruptcies pushed further out than prior cycles.

• Stellar vintage expected in 2H25/FY26: In turn, the best Growth Equity vintages even occurred within 11 months of peak bankruptcy. For this to play out will require further repricings, capitulations and consolidations in the meantime. However, the outlook for financing is beginning to become clearer after a 2 year bear market.

Using this logic, 2026 could be a rare vintage indeed. Only two Growth Equity vintages through history have comfortably delivered an >2x net MOIC (Multiple on Invested Capital) back to 1997. These were 2003 and 2009 after exactly the above series of events occurred in both cases. In short, this view further reinforces the more positive stance Bruce Hamilton recently reiterated on EQT and ICP.

The reason we say above that the market is still due (or overdue) a consolidation can be seen both on the supply and demand side of the market. Excess capital invested in sub-par business models is well documented by this point, both in venture and growth equity markets; the core view of this prior report still holds true.

Less well analysed is the addition of >5000 venture funds globally since 2020 (according to Dealroom) chasing allocations in comparatively few game-changing power-law winners. While there may be both survivorship bias and lag effects (realised vs Total portfolio MOIC) in the numbers below, it is nonetheless indicative of the problem of over-supply in both venture and growth-equity markets as the number of sub-scale and sub-par funds has been rising.

As in 2000 and 2008 - leading up to these remarkable vintages - the industry needed to contract by at least half in each case. This consolidation/correction in the number of funds has still yet to happen this cycle. The 2000 was more extreme on this front, and for a number of reasons explained regularly in this Venture Vision series, this cycle has many more resemblances to 2000 than 2008.

## The Fintech Radar (15 Apr 2024)

• #1 Empower Finance to acquire Petal, and completes acquisition of Cashola: This week (9th April), Empower Finance has announced an agreement to acquire Petal, a New-York based consumer credit card and fintech company, expanding the company's products to include the credit card category in the US. Petal, used by nearly 400,000 consumers, provides Visa credit cards that don't require a credit score to qualify, and are instead approved via cash flow underwriting. According to TransUnion, over 45 million consumers in the US are deemed credit unserved or undeserved, making it quite a proportion of the market. According to press reports, in 2022 Petal was reportedly valued at \$800m, although the article notes that given the cash struggles the company has had since then, it is likely the acquisition amount was significantly lower than this. In the same release, the company also announced it had completed the acquisition of Philippines-based consumer credit and lending company, Cashola, which enables it to enter the Southeast Asian credit market.

• #2 B2B BNPL provider Hokodo secures €100m debt facility: This week (8th April), UK-based B2B payment terms solutions provider Hokodo has announced that it has secured at €100m debt facility from Viola Credit, which will be used to help them facilitate more than €1.5bn of B2B transactions across Europe over the next 2 years. Founded in 2018, Hokodo B2B BNPL solution enables B2B buyers in Europe to defer payment by between 30-90 days (or the end of the month following purchase). The company is expanding its operations across Europe (via M&A) and into North America (via its recently announced partnership with Balance, to enable cross-boarder payment terms).

Unless otherwise indicated, the news reports referenced above have not been confirmed.

## Real-time Valuation Monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.

## Valuation Methodology and Risks

## EQT AB (EQTAB.ST)

Our price target is set at 24x PE on 2025e EPS, a premium to the global sector average of 18x given scale, attractive earnings mix, solid earnings growth over the next 3 years and gearing to secular growth areas (infra, Impact investing and wealth). We apply \~33x on management fees (60% of earnings) in line with the average assigned to our US private market coverage, and 10x on performance fees. We use 9% CoE and adjust for the net debt.

## Risks to Upside

n Fund raising could be greater than we expect if strong performance continues to drive market share gains

n Successor Flagship fund launches raise earlier than we expect vs the 4 years base case

n Upside to carried interest

## Risks to Downside

n General macro conditions impacting realization, investment and fund raising activity

n Flagship fund launching later than we expect

## Intermediate Capital Group PLC (ICP.L)

We use a sum-of-parts approach, applying multiples to management and carry fees based on the growth outlook relative to peers and diversification of AuM by asset class and strategy. We apply \~25x P/E to Mar-25 Core Mgmt earnings (adj. for IC staff costs, CLOs dividends & other income), \~8x P/E to Mar-25 performance fee earnings, 5x to CLOs and intra-company fees, 0.75x P/NAV to Sep-23 NAV of £2,046m. We translate to a 12-month PT at \~10% CoE.

## Risks to Upside

n Deal activity picks up, leading to faster deployment/realisations

n Better fundraising pace boosted by seeding of new strategies and LPs penetration

n Benign credit outcome

## Risks to Downside

n Concerns around balance sheet exposure and credit cycle

n Slower deployment; poor investment performance

n Challenging capital markets backdrop from higher rates delays exits & strategic/dealdriven asset flows

n Regulatory headwinds
