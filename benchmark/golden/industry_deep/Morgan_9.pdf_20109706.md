## Great Reset & Great Volatility

## Sho Nakazawa

The biggest concern among market participants is whether pessimism over the US economic outlook has gone too far. TOPIX P/E has tumbled to recession-era levels. We believe the markets will remain highly sensitive to US inflation and job statistics for the foreseeable future. We recommend choosing stocks with consideration of the volatility factor, which should quickly become an effective tool.

Prevailing market strategies abruptly overturned following BoJ’s July 31 MPM: The Nikkei average plummeted ¥4,451 to ¥31,458 on August 5, the single largest drop ever in the index on a closing-price basis. The sharp downturn was triggered by communications highlighting the differences in the direction of US and Japanese monetary policy and the subsequent release of unexpectedly weak US job data. The BoJ retreated from its longstanding accommodative stance at its July monetary policy meeting (MPM), a move known as the BoJ pivot. It hiked rates against consensus expectations despite the softness in consumption and hinted at further increases to come. At the same time, US inflation and labor markets are clearly slowing, prompting the markets to discount even deeper rate cuts by the Fed. The release of the weak US job results amid these developments sparked a rapid unwinding of yen carry trades, leading to a strengthening in the yen and a brutal falloff in Japanese stock markets.

Main market concern for now will be whether US recession fears are overblown; our US economist believes soft landing still possible: The unexpectedly strong ISM nonmanufacturing index on August 5 led to a sweeping rebound of nearly ¥1,500 in the Nikkei futures index relative to the cash closing price. The biggest concern in the markets ahead will be whether fears of a US recession will ease. Our US economists said that, even after the feeble job data, we still expect a soft landing. The determining factor for the start of Fed rate cuts is not a slump in economic growth or employment but disinflation, and spending, GDP and employment stand firm. We maintain our view that the Fed will reduce rates three times this year by 25bp each, starting in September.

Japanese stock drop likely to correct quickly once extreme US gloom fades: The TOPIX fwd P/E slid to 11.5x on August 5, below the assumed 13x in our bear scenario and the average 12.9x in recent recessions. The figure is likely to react sensitively for now to US inflation and job data, but we believe the falloff in Japanese stocks will reverse quickly once the excessive pessimism over the US economy fades.

Yen soared and stocks plunged after BoJ’s July MPM. Market discounting of BoJ rate hikes appears erratic. Key in BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida’s speech on August 7 will be his view on yen rise and stock slump: The markets had discounted two BoJ rate hikes in the coming 12 months in the immediate aftermath of the bank’s July MPM, but that has fallen to one increase in light of the subsequent drop in yields. BoJ Deputy Governor Shinichi Uchida is scheduled to speak in Hakodate on August 7. We expect him to offer detailed remarks on how the yen’s rapid upswing and equity slump since the MPM will affect the scenario envisioned in the bank’s quarterly Outlook Report (e.g., corporate investment and household consumption sentiment, future rate hike path).

If Uchida maintains his hawkish stance from last week’s MPM and offers no damage control regarding current yen and stock movements, we expect a further falloff in Japanese stocks as the markets discount a greater number of rate hikes ahead and the yen continues upward from a further unwinding of yen carry trades.

Volatility factor should quickly become effective gauge in choosing stocks: As of August 5, the CTA strategy position on global shares excluding Japan has decreased from its recent peak of 42% to 6% and its position on Japanese stocks from 4.6% to -0.3%. A significantly higher risk on a multi-asset portfolio, as with the higher volatility of equities, reduces the qualitative appeal of investment from the prospect of recession or poor earnings, which could spur investors to lower their weighting of shares in advanced economies from a risk control perspective. The mix of bullish and bearish forecasts for the US economy could also heighten the volatility in equity markets. In such case, stock picks could change dizzyingly according to the level of volatility. We list low-volatility shares in Exhibit 7 and high volatility shares in Exhibit 8 .

# Comparison with past market downturns via CTA strategy

## Kanji Ohi, Ukyo Haraguchi

A comparison with past market downturns suggests that the market has further downside risk even with the 24% plunge in shares (Topix basis) from the July 11 peak to the August 5 rout. Even as some shares recover on their own strength, the overall market environment could remain weak for some time.

We analyzed investor behavior during previous stock market crashes by replicating a CTA strategy in order to compare the present with past cases and gauge the market environment ahead. While multiple methods are used for actual CTA strategies, including contrarian signaling, we concentrate here on average CTA user trends. We thus assume a simple momentum strategy using equity ETF and consider future market conditions from the relationship between the market climate and position sizes. (Details of our CTA strategy replication can be found in Overview of CTA Strategies in the appendix below).

The CTA strategy position for global stocks excluding Japan slid from the recent peak of 42% to 6% as of the August 5 closing price, and the position for Japanese stocks from 4.6% to -0.3% ( Exhibit 9 ). While the Japanese downturn was triggered by the rate hike at the BoJ MPM and accompanying rebound in the yen, we believe the appropriate periods of comparison would be the pandemic and so-called “China shock” of 2015 given the fundamental factor of the present slowdown in the US and Japanese economies.

The CTA positions for ex-Japan global stocks and Japanese stocks in December 2019, just before the pandemic took genuine hold, were respectively 62.4% and 8.0%. By May 2020, those figures had shrunk to -11.7% and -2.2% ( Exhibit 10 ). In the China shock of 2015, the economic jitters caused by the burst of the Chinese equity bubble led to a downturn in stock markets worldwide. CTA positions for ex-Japan global and Japanese stocks shriveled from 15.5% and 5.3% in May 2015 to -23.8% and -2.3% in October that year ( Exhibit 12 ).

The latter case in particular occurred just after the surge in Japanese stocks markets with the start of Shinzo Abe’s second turn as prime minister and the turn by the BoJ to an accommodative monetary policy stance under the Kuroda regime. This is closer to the current situation. As noted, the CTA Japanese stock short position as of the August 5 closing price was -0.3%, implying further room for decline compared to the level during the China shock. In addition, the mean reversion in volatility, which had climbed in line with the stock market collapse, spurred a rise in short positions under a CTA strategy. That suggests that the expansion in short positions could continue apace amid the comeback from panic selling.

This analysis looked only at investment trends under an average CTA strategy, but we believe it shares aspects with general trend-following strategies and some quantitative strategies. A comparison with past market situations suggests that investors should assume continued downside risk in stocks and thus maintain careful risk control.

Appendix

## Overview of CTA strategies

In replicating a CTA strategy, we used the following nine equity ETFs. For each ETF, we calculated values from one-business-day returns to 260-business-day returns, and took the average signs of the returns as the signal for investment. We used the investment signal based on the closing price of each business day, divided by historical volatility, and applied an adjustment factor to find each ETF’s investment position. The adjustment factor was determined from the standpoint of risk management in CTA strategies as a whole. Our analysis was constructed so that the annualized volatility of multi-asset strategies, including non-equity asset classes, would come to around 8%. Consequently, the absolute value of the CTA position was less important than the comparison of asset classes and changes in chronology.
