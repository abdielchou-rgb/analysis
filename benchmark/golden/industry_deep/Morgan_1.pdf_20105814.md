# NOK, Stokk and Barrel

We recommend long NOK/SEK. Norges Bank is slowing its NOK sales dramatically as the Riksbank's SEK-buying FX-hedging programme is about to end, while Swedish data are underperforming peers meaningfully. Expected rate differentials imply NOK/SEK rising to 1.06.

Central bank footprints are shrinking rapidly: Both the Riksbank and Norges Bank have been active in FX markets, though for different reasons and in different directions – the Riksbank buying SEK and Norges Bank selling NOK. Both flows are set to slow meaningfully, removing a significant NOK/SEK headwind.

Slowing Riksbank SEK purchases... We estimate that on-market transactions from the Riksbank's FX-hedging programme, where it buys SEK versus USD and EUR, should end in the next week or two. The recent rally in SEK has reduced the chances that the Riksbank will extend the hedging programme at its February 1 meeting.

...meet slowing Norges Bank NOK sales: Meanwhile, Norges Bank has slowed its daily average pace of NOK sales from NOK 1,400 million/day to NOK 350 million. This is twice as fast a decline as we were expecting and we think that the much slower pace of sales will continue.

Policy and rate divergence in Scandinavia: The absence of central bank flows suggests that NOK/SEK can trade more on fundamentals – which point to a higher NOK versus SEK. Norwegian data have held up while Swedish data have disappointed the most in the G10. We estimate that Norges Bank-Riksbank policy divergence implies NOK/SEK at 1.06, compared to sub-parity today.

NOK/SEK is uncorrelated to risk: NOK/SEK's correlation to risk assets has fallen to roughly zero. Continued lack of correlation suggests that macro factors like global risk demand won't impact the pair much, rendering it a better reflection of the local stories suggesting NOK/SEK topside.

We recommend investors buy NOK/SEK. Shifts in central bank engagement in FX markets in both Norway and Sweden, coupled with our continued expectation for widening policy divergence, should see the pair return to its previous range above 1.05.

Trade idea: Buy NOK/SEK at 0.99 with a target of 1.06 and a stop of 0.95

SEK buying from the Riksbank is almost done: As discussed further here, the Riksbank's SEK buying and USD and EUR selling is nearly done. The bank had proposed to sell US\$8 billion USD/SEK and €2 billion EUR/SEK. We estimate that the former transaction will be completed in the next week or two ( Exhibit 1 ).

Norges Bank NOK selling is slowing more quickly than expected: Norges Bank's footprint in the NOK market may be shrinking faster than expected, rapidly reducing NOKnegative pressures (see here for a summary of Norges Bank's FX purchase programme).

Norges Bank's NOK selling, in response to elevated petroleum revenues, played an important role in keeping NOK under pressure and suppressing realised volatility ( Exhibit 4 ).

We had expected a pullback in petroleum revenues, coupled with a modestly wider nonoil budget surplus, to reduce Norges Bank's NOK selling by about a third ( Exhibit 5 ). It turns out that the fall is about double in size, thanks to a higher-than-expected denomination of hard currency revenues ( Exhibit 6 ).

This suggests that Norges Bank's ongoing NOK-selling footprint will be far smaller than in the last couple of years, which removes a critical NOK headwind.

Scandinavian policy divergence set to continue: Rate differentials should prove a longterm tailwind to NOK/SEK. While our economists expect both Norges Bank and the Riksbank to cut rates this year, alongside other global peers, Norges Bank's normalisation cycle is expected to be slower, particularly relative to market expectations ( Exhibit 7 ).

Wider-than-expected rate differentials should continue to be aided by data divergence. While Swedish data have been underwhelming expectations, Norwegian data have rebounded versus expectations in 4Q ( Exhibit 8 ).

NOK/SEK is now almost entirely uncorrelated to risk assets: NOK and SEK are often viewed as risk-sensitive currencies and historically their relationship to risk assets has been on the high side. However, both the individual correlations and NOK/SEK's correlation to equities have waned in 2023, and NOK/SEK is now almost entirely uncorrelated to risk assets even despite NOK and SEK remaining risk proxies ( Exhibit 9 and Exhibit 10 ).

This is important because, should this low sensitivity to risk continue, it means that NOK/ SEK can capture alpha between two local stories, rather than being driven primarily by global risk factors.

Key risks to long NOK/SEK include an unexpected extension of the Riksbank's hedging programme, a surprise pick-up in Norges Bank NOK sales, or downside surprises in Norwegian data and/or upside surprises in Swedish data. Positioning is also a key risk; investors are long NOK and long SEK far less.
