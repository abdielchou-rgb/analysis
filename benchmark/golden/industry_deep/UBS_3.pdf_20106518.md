## KRW: Missed a window, or due for a catch-up?

## Korean Won: Due for a catch-up

There’s been no lack of good news for the won lately – robust semiconductor exports growth, improving manufacturing surveys, rising DRAM prices, Kospi earnings upgrades and more recently monstrous equity portfolio flows. These tailwinds lifted Kospi by c.10% in the past three months, which when coupled with the 40bps decline in 2y UST yields since early November should have sent USD/KRW comfortably below 1300. Yet, the pair is broadly unchanged. This underperformance of a well advertised narrative has puzzled many, let alone us. Equally so, we don’t find the poor price action as enough of an evidence to override the ongoing improvement in fundamentals.

We note that Korea's weak Balance of Payments (BoP) have been a headwind for the won since early 2022, including in 2H23 when the tech cycle inflected. This is however changing with the recent trend in modified basic balance surging above 2016-20 average. Fading seasonal dollar demand in the coming weeks, vis-a-vis January, could potentially catalyze won's overdue catch-up. We see scope for a step shift lower in USD/ KRW to 1280-1300 in next couple of months, while sticking with our relative preference for won on crosses - CNH/KRW over a medium term, and tactically in THB/KRW.

## Executive Summary

## Korean Won: Due for a catch-up

There’ve been plentiful of good news for the won lately – accelerating semiconductor exports growth, improving manufacturing surveys, rising DRAM spot prices, Kospi earnings' upgrades and more recently a rush of foreign buying in the equity markets. These narratives, alongside the ‘Value Up’ theme have sent the rolling three month equity portfolio flows to almost a record high. Likewise when coupled with Fed’s insurance cuts narrative, which sent Kospi c.10% higher and US 2y yields about c.40bps lower since early November, USD/KRW should have been much lower. But it is broadly unchanged, sending the cross as screening 3-4% cheap on our models. We suspect Korea's weak BoP position, which has consistently stopped won from delivering on the widely advertised narrative since late 2Q23, could be the culprit here. This is however changing with the recent trend in modified basic balance surging above 2016-19 average. Fading seasonal dollar demand in the coming weeks, vis-a-vis January, could potentially catalyze won's overdue catch-up. We see scope for a step shift lower in USD/KRW to 1280-1300 in next couple of months, while sticking with our relative preference for won on crosses - CNH/KRW over a medium term, and tactically THB/KRW

## Memory cycle comfortably in an upswing. KRW is lagging

Korea’s semiconductor exports growth has accelerated from about 0%y/y three months ago, to 50%y/y currently (85% on 3m/3m saar basis), while semis IP growth surged to 55%y/y. Likewise, the DRAM spot prices have risen by c.25% since the Q3 trough. Importantly, our tech analysts don’t see this as a short-lived optimism. They expect the DRAM prices to rise by 65% in ’24 (80% through 2Q25) while noting a strong contract pricing for Q124 from their channel checks. Likewise, our above consensus tech sector EPS estimates suggest that the narrative has not yet been fully priced in Kospi (Link). Indeed, this optimism has sent the new export orders’ subcomponent in PMI surveys to 95th%ile of 10y range. Per our historical analysis of tech exports vs. KRW, UBS tech upcycle projections point to a 5-6% headroom for won.

## BoP has been a headwind, but it's improving. We are optimistic into ‘24

Korea’s balance of payments have been an immaterial headwind for the KRW in the past couple of years, with systemic net annual dollar supply recording an average 16bn\$/annum deficit in 22-23 - a 30bn\$ swing vis-à-vis 2021. This headwind, however, is fading. Thanks to an ongoing improvement in the CA surplus, and almost halving of resident outflows vis-à-vis 2021, our estimates of the modified basic balance has risen to highest in three years - above 2016-20 average.If the above-mentioned memory prices led improvement to terms of trade extends, we’d expect the BoP flows to turn into a tailwind in the coming months.

## Seasonal dollar demand to fade in coming weeks, a potential near-term catalyst

The organic dollar demand in Korea tends to be seasonally strong at the start of the year, and vice-versa around late Q3. However within Q1 as well, we note that this demand tends to be strong in Jan, and marginally in Feb. This phenomenon tends to fade in March and April, explaining won’s historical tendency to strengthen in March/April. This non-negligible shift in the demandsupply dynamics, in our view, should be seen as an accrual to the recent surge in portfolio flows. Barring any material external shocks, we’d expect this swing to catalyze catch-up gains in won.

## Won’s window for a catch-up is now; we’re conservative on 2H outlook

For two reasons: a) By Q3, our Korean equity strategist sees the re-rating window to have broadly closed as market focus switches to the late cycle. Note: markets typically tend to peak two-three quarters ahead of the actual cycle peak; in this instance we see the peak as 2Q25. b) UBS expectations of a tapering of US consumer demand, which could potentially also act as a headwind for Korean exports in 2H - especially in the autos sector.

## Don’t fret the US exceptionalism; recession is a bigger threat for won

Having over-latched on to imminent rate cuts narrative in 4Q23, markets have so far been focused on trimming such expectations YTD. Equally so however, in the context of - a) Fed’s sustained insurance cuts narrative, b) UBS expectations of US core PCE hitting Fed’s end year forecast of 2.4%y/y by March/April, and c) already elevated real yields - we see the bar for an extended repricing in 2y US yields as high. This is especially the case if CTAs’ dollar buying also stalls here as our models predict. More importantly, this US economy resilience has been a tailwind for Korean exporters (vis-à-vis a step-shift lower in exports to China since the pandemic). In that context, the cyclical won should be more worried about a US recession and not necessarily its consumers’ resilience.
