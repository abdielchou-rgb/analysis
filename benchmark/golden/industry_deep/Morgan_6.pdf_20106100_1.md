# Key Agency Revisions - January 2024

We summarise January forecasts from the IEA, EIA and OPEC. New forecasts for 2025 have been released by the EIA and OPEC. Some upwards revisions to absolute level of both demand and non-OPEC supply leave overall forecasts for 2024 still calling for a balanced market.

## Key Takeaways

■ IEA - Significant upwards revisions to both global demand and non-OPEC supply growth in 2024. Now sees global demand at 103.0 mb/d in 2024.

OPEC - Kept estimates unchanged for demand in 2024 and lowered non-OPEC supply growth estimate by -30 kb/d. Still sees global demand at 104.4 mb/d in 2024.

EIA - Small upwards revisions to demand and downwards adjustments to non-OPEC supply growth estimates for 2024. Now sees global demand at 102.5 mb/d in 2024.

Summary - Upwards revisions to both demand and supply leaves implied stock change estimates for 2024 broadly the same MoM. New 2025 estimates from OPEC and EIA.

This report summarises estimates from the IEA, OPEC and the EIA, and compares them with our latest estimates from The Oil Manual: 2024 Outlook: Balancing Act (3 Jan 2024).

January saw upwards revisions for global 2024 demand growth by the IEA (+190 kb/d) and EIA (+50 kb/d). This takes them to respective global demand growth estimates of 1.24 mb/d and 1.39 mb/d for 2024. Meanwhile OPEC kept their estimate flat at a still elevated estimate of 2.25 mb/d. The IEA's upward revision for 2024 was based on tailwinds from improvement in consensus economic outlook and the softer oil prices seen since 4Q23.

Initial estimates for 2025 demand were published by the EIA and OPEC. OPEC forecasts 1.8 mb/d of global oil demand growth in 2024, while the EIA has a more conservative estimate of 1.2 mb/d. In both cases, growth in non-OECD countries is the driving force of growth. The main discrepancies between the two estimates concern demand growth in China and the Middle East. OPEC sees 160 kb/d more growth in China and 130 kb/d more in the Middle East than the EIA does for 2025.

OPEC-13 crude oil output increased by 73 kb/d over December according to OPEC secondary sources, with a 100k b/d increase from Nigeria offsetting declines in Kuwait and Saudi Arabia. The IEA saw a smaller 50 kb/d MoM increase, also driven by a 100 kb/d jump in Nigerian output. Direct reporting from OPEC members was also consistent with a MoM rise in Nigerian production. However, a deviation from the other sources occurs concerning direct reports of Saudi Arabian production, which indicated a significant increase of 126 kb/d in January.

Minor downgrades for non-OPEC supply growth were made for 2024 by the EIA and OPEC, meanwhile the IEA boosted its non-OPEC supply growth forecast by +160 kb/d. The upwards IEA revision was based on upwards revisions to supply from OECD Americas. This brings the IEA and OPEC estimates closer together, both forecasting \~1.3 mb/d of non-OPEC supply growth in 2024. The EIA however is quite an outlier on this point, only calling for +0.85 mb/d of non-OPEC supply growth in 2024, based on lower estimates for US and Canadian production. The trend in EIA revisions for 2024 non-OPEC supply growth has been quite erratic over the last year, with estimates in September as high as \~1.3 mb/d but now lowered back to 0.85 mb/d over the last 4 months. This takes the EIA back to their early estimates for 2024 published last February. Overall all three agencies converge on an absolute estimate of 70.4 mb/d of non-OPEC supply in 2024.

Initial 2025 non-OPEC supply growth estimates were released by the EIA and OPEC, with OPEC having a more optimistic growth estimate of +1.3 mb/d for 2025 vs the EIA's of +0.95 mb/d. In both cases growth is driven by the same countries as in 2024, namely the US, Canada, Guyana, Brazil, Norway and Kazakhstan.

The EIA (the only agency to forecast OPEC production) dropped its 2024 OPEC production forecast by a further 50 kb/d MoM, on the back of OPEC cut extensions into 1Q24. The IEA saw OPEC+ spare capacity rise +0.1 mb/d MoM to now stand at 5.4 mb/d, with Saudi Arabia holding 60% of this spare capacity.

Overall estimates for the market in 2024 are loosening marginally, with smaller inventory draws/larger builds estimated for 2024 than in the December reports. This month the IEA and EIA reversed the demand downgrades they made in their December reports, once again focusing on better than expected macroeconomic conditions and softer prices acting as a tailwind for demand. However, despite stronger demand and continued OPEC cuts, estimates for 2024 market are becoming fractionally looser. This is down to non-OPEC supply consistently beating expectations, particularly from Canada and the US, which is leading to further upwards revisions to absolute supply estimates.

Consequently, despite the changes to demand and supply growth being a bit of a mixed bag, the agencies still see higher absolute levels of both supply and demand which balance out. Overall, IEA and EIA estimates still centre around the market being roughly in balance in 2024. OPEC's estimate of a large deficit for next year continues to be an outlier, based on their very high estimate for demand.
