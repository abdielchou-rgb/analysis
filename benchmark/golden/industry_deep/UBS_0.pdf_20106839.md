# Global Inflation Strategy Global Inflation Monday

Buying bbb-Breakevens: Budget, bottoming or bouncing? ... and Bernanke-Blanchard

Buy 30y TIPS vs UK linkers. The UK budget is this week. The key headline will just be more gilts, again, in 2024-5. The time for the DMO to shift supply shorter and more nominal will be soon, but we don’t expect a bigger move this year. The new UK 30y linker syndication next week is also reason to fade strength in UK rates outright. But there is curve value in 30y. Buy it on the 10s30s50s barbell (as a forward steepener.)

European inflation for February seem to confirm disinflation bottoming. Bounce risk is ignored, but the ECB's version of the Bernanke-Blanchard model also reminds us not to be too sure inflation will continue to fall easily. Strategically European breakevens still need more risk premium. Euro growth pessimism relative to US is also overdone, but we prefer US breakevens this week. We like euro real rate steepeners tactically and strategically. Core longs still in front end TIPS and periphery.

## UK Budget: More bonds again. Too soon to slash longs & linkers

## Fade long end strength into IL54s

The UK budget will be more about bond supply than measures that change expectations for inflation. (The main such measure would be the freeze in fuel duty, which is discounted.) Our headline write-up is here. In principle the gilt supply story is clearly bearish UK rates. We expect a gilt remit for £258bn gilts and total supply pressure over the year will most likely also include around £35bn of active sales from the BoE's APF. The uncertainty around this headline figure is probably at least £10bn (5%) and probably skewed to the high side. UK real rates look tactically expensive. In the run up to the new 2054s syndication next week, that looks worth fading.

## More (short) gilts, fewer linkers? Maybe not yet

The key risk is that the DMO skews supply more aggressively toward short-end conventionals than it naturally would when planning a bigger total remit. The pensions industry is clearly a less reliable source of demand and, at some point, the DMO will need to focus more on what its other pools of demand prefer. On the other hand, value would suggest fewer mediums and more longs (Figure 4.) For linkers, note that supply has already fallen to a long-term low as a share of overall supply (Figure 6.)

## Pensions priorities are now planning for the next phase: asset allocation, better matching

Overall we expect the DMO to be gradual, calculating that turning off the taps too quickly might risk pushing pensions away more quickly still. We also think pensions will also be gradual, focusing mainly on bucket rebalancing and credit/currency allocation within fixed income over the next year. That probably means some shortening of exposures, since the peak of most pensions' liabilities is likely to be around the 20-25 year area where forward rates are cheap. As shown in Figure 4, this is also the cheap section of the forward gilt curve. The logic to receive in this area looks clear to us.

## New IL54s. Curve value.

## Look for 52s+3.5bp

The syndication is likely to be on Wednesday 13th. The seasonal adjustment adds around 2.8bp relative to the 52s (the 54s real yield should be lower). The market doesn't seem to price on the run long-end gilts any differently to older issues. We would look for pricing close to 52s-3.5bp (or 56s+1bp). 30y linkers have been cheapening a little on the curve, especially 51s and 52s. 55s have reacted a little less, but were already cheap. Longer paper has outperformed.

## Buy 30y linkers cash and duration neutral out of 10y and 45-50y

Extending into the new 54s from the 20y or 10y, and shortening from the long end in a equal proceeds, equal PV01 fly looks attractive. This cash-PV01 neutral fly is equivalent to a forward rate steepener in real rates. Figure 5 shows the history for the nominal and inflation legs of this assuming 34s-54s-10y20y/30y10y. It's not at best-ever levels but that doesn't matter: 140bp is still a good pick up and we expect strategic pensions flows in this direction.

## European inflation momentum bottoms

## Echo of pricing power? Look to March for that. Commission surveys suggest less pricing optimism.

European flash CPIs for February were the most important new information in global inflation last week. They appear to show that disinflation's momentum has been bottoming out.

Last week we said that we should be listening for the echo of pricing power, but didn't expect much evidence: March is the month when annual repricing is most important. The high level data at which the data is released makes it too early to make big claims on a month's data, but it does seem clear that service price disinflation is stabilising. That should be considered against the Commission surveys this week, however, which showed selling price expectations ticking down again after having bounced in the middle of last year. (Figures 7 and 8).

## Breakevens rally on natural gas, not risk premium

We take two key points from these data. First, globally breakevens still deserve some risk premium. Second, the euro-world inflation divergence trade is not for now. Last week that was the main message in this note. Breakevens have bounced quite strongly (EURi 1y1y up 10bp) but probably has more to do with a bounce in natural gas prices. If there was a reassessment it wasn't about the fundamental risks of the inflation process, but about the fundamentals of global demand for natural gas when it's so cheap.

## Growth expectations explain the lack of EURi risk premium

It has been popular to say that European inflation is returning to pre-pandemic patterns. That has probably been led by diverging growth expectations. But the European cycle should be bolstered by employment, the expectation of lower rates, savings, energy, and the US cycle. The relative growth risk seems on the upside for Europe at this point. Such a wide sustained gap in growth expectations would be unusual. The sovereign crisis was the last time US growth outperformed by such a margin over a period of two years - that was a special situation for Europe and the US was still enjoying early-cycle recovery growth. (Figure 9.)

## Update your framework with … Bernanke Blanchard III: 'The Euro Area'

The other must read on European inflation (after the Monday, of course) is the ECB s staff's application of the Bernanke-Blanchard (BB) paper to the Euro Area case. The main conclusion is that inflation risk is not past: hawkish for central bankers, bullish for breakevens. The projections are for sharp disinflation to about 2% this year, followed by a bounce, not returning to 2% before 2028. (Figure 10.)

## Bernanke Blanchard II: 'Haskel the Hawk

The Bank of England applied BB to the UK at the end of last year with similar conclusions. One of the lead authors was Jonathan Haskel and the framework is a key part of his hawkish outlook. His November speech and his slides are useful to anyone interested in the workings of the framework.

## (Featuring Sticky Wages)

A secondary conclusion from the work worth keeping in mind, which stands out in both exercises, is that high wage inflation will be persistent. For those who see the pandemic as an exception in which all forces drove inflation, and that returning to pre-pandemic sensitivities therefore implies more disinflation quickly, the model suggests the opposite.

## The tactical view this week: long US breakevens, US real rates, steepeners

## Long US breakevens. Real rate curve steepeners.

 EURi real rates don't stand out. Last week we focused on European real rates, recommending to be long. After last week's move, euro area real rates don't stand out tactically in the global picture.

US short end real rates and Euro periphery are core longs. Strategically, US and UK real rates are most attractive. Nominal rates are too shy about challenging central bank pricing because negative carry makes timing crucial and we don't see increased pressure for a change in rhetoric now. With carry in mind, we keep our recommendation to be outright long real rates in periphery. We still regard front end TIPS, held on real yield, as a core position.

 Long US breakevens. The dynamic RV chart (Figure 11) suggests value in US breakevens. Still very strong momentum in risk sentiment also supports being long breakevens. Real rates are supported through the more positive outlook fo breakevens.

 The bottom up analysis also supports being long in short-end TIPS. At market pricing, they broadly pick up approximately 25bp over bills. On Alan Detmeister's (not a noted inflation-bull) projections, inflation would remain higher over the middle of this year. The annualised pick-up over nominals is would be around 80bp if that's right.

 Nominal rates look flat, especially in euros. That is partly due to pain in steepeners and some level-driven interest in long-end rates. We also like steepeners strategically and will return to that another week. Real rate steepeners are mostly a nominal trade, but we also like the implied breakeven flattening in the real rate steepener and the risk reward is improved (marginally) because the key risk for the steepener is higher front end pricing, and that is linked directly to inflation pressure.
