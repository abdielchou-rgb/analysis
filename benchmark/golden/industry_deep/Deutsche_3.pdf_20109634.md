# Asset Allocation Investor Positioning and Flows

Date 3 August 2024 Strategy Update

## Sliding Further

Last week we noted that equity positioning had pulled back sharply but was still elevated, and risk appetite in our reading remained high, suggesting buy-the-dip rallies but also larger pullbacks if hitherto missing negative catalysts emerged (Sharp Pullback But Not There Yet, Jul 26 2024). The market selloff in response to the weak payrolls report is not surprising in this context. Equity positioning has continued to slide (z score 0.48, 72nd percentile), especially with systematic strategies cutting exposure as vol spiked higher. While not low in absolute terms, positioning has now caught down to earnings growth which as we note in our takes piece published earlier today, moderated slightly from 11.5% in Q1 to a still solid 10.7% in Q2 (Q2 2024 Earnings Takes: Modest Deceleration, Slow Rotation, Aug 2 2024). Inflows to equity funds (\$8.9bn) continued for a 15th straight week but at a somewhat slower pace, while strong inflows into bond funds (\$14.6bn) persisted.

## Positioning and flows detail

After dropping sharply last week, our measure of aggregate equity positioning continued to fall this week (z score 0.48, 72nd percentile). While discretionary investor positioning (z score 0.68, 86th percentile) went largely sideways, systematic strategies positioning fell to the lowest since mid-March as vol spiked higher (z score 0.46, 67th percentile).

## n Under discretionary investor positioning,

n Total net call volume (call minus put volume, 5d ma) declined further to turn net negative briefly for the first time in almost three months, before edging back to positive again (42nd percentile). Net call volume declined mostly in ETFs and single stock options this week. Within single stock options, net call volume declined sharply in mega-cap growth & Tech and to a smaller extent in defensive stocks but rose modestly in Financials and cyclicals stocks. S&P 500 options skew (3m, 90%-110%) declined this week. The implied correlation among S&P 500 stocks remains very low by historical standards (3rd percentile).

n Investor sentiment (bull minus bear spread) had risen this week prior to the payrolls report (76th percentile). Bullish (77th percentile) and neutral responses (42nd percentile) rose, while bearish responses (31st percentile) declined.

## Under systematic strategies positioning,

n Vol control funds trimmed their equity exposure further to the lowest since last November on the back of rising vol (38th percentile). Their sensitivity to market selloffs has declined after rising sharply in the previous couple of weeks.

n CTAs cut equity longs, bond shorts, raised dollar longs while changes in commodities positioning were mixed. CTAs continued cutting their aggregate equity longs (68th percentile) though they remain above average. The decline was driven by cuts to US (61st percentile) and European equity longs (60th percentile). CTAs also continued cutting their shorts in bonds (23rd percentile). In FX, CTAs sharply increased their dollar longs (73rd percentile) to well above average by raising shorts in the Euro (14th percentile) and in the New Zealand dollar (22nd percentile), and by flipping their Australian dollar positioning from long to a small short. Positioning in the yen remains short but not extreme and that in the Swiss Franc remains very short. Pound longs edged higher towards extremes. At the other end, Canadian dollar shorts moved up towards their alltime highs (1st percentile). In commodities, CTAs left their gold longs flat, while cutting their oil longs and remaining slightly long Copper.

n Risk parity funds cut exposures across most asset classes. Risk parity funds’ equity exposure fell this week and remains below average (37th percentile). Positioning in the US (45th percentile) fell below average, that in other developed markets (30th percentile) remained well below while that in EM remained just below (47th percentile). The funds left their bond exposure relatively flat (17th percentile). They slightly cut their inflation linked note exposure (39th percentile) and cut their commodities exposure (69th percentile) though it remains at above average levels.

n Sector positioning: Positioning in MCG & Tech fell modestly again, and that in Real Estate and Utilities jumped higher. Positioning in MCG & Tech fell this week but remains elevated (z score 1.01, 96th percentile).

Positioning in Utilities continued to climb and is now quite elevated (z score 0.93, 89th percentile). Positioning in Consumer Staples declined this week (z score 0.23, 70th percentile) and that in Financials edged up (z score 0.18, 62nd percentile), but both remain modestly above average. Positioning in Real Estate jumped sharply this week to above average (z score 0.05, 62nd percentile). Positioning in Industrial Cyclicals (z score -0.15, 50th percentile), Energy (z score -0.23, 50th percentile) and Healthcare (z score -0.35, 41st percentile) rose slightly and are modestly below average. Positioning in Consumer Cyclicals (z score -0.44, 39th percentile) and Materials (z score -0.68, 23rd percentile) remained significantly below average. Positioning in small caps continued to rise and is now notably above average (z score 0.62, 77th percentile).

n Weekly fund flows to ETFs & mutual funds: Inflows to equities (\$8.9bn) eased, those to bonds (\$14.6bn) remained robust while money market funds (-\$12.5bn) suffered outflows for a second week in a row.

1 Inflows to equity funds (\$8.9bn) continued for a 15th straight week (total of \$203.6bn) but slowed to the lowest in 5 weeks. Inflows to the US (\$4.5bn) and EM (\$4.5bn) eased but those to broad-global funds (\$2.0bn) picked up. Outflows from Europe (-\$1.0bn) continued for a 11th consecutive week. Japan (-\$0.9bn) also saw modest outflows this week. Within EM (\$4.5bn), Asia ex-Japan (\$6.0bn), especially China (\$4.8bn), continued to get healthy inflows. Broad-EM (-\$1.2bn) and Latam (-\$0.3bn) saw outflows, while flows to EMEA stayed muted. Across sectors, inflows to Tech (\$3.6bn) picked up to a 6-week high. Energy (\$0.7bn), Utilities (\$0.6bn) and Healthcare (\$0.5bn) also got notable inflows this week. Financials (\$0.2bn) received small inflows. On the other hand, Consumer goods (-\$0.7bn), Telecom (-\$0.4bn), Materials (- \$0.2bn) and Industrials (-\$0.1bn) registered outflows. Flows to Real Estate were muted.

1 Inflows to bond funds (\$14.6bn) slowed slightly from last week but remained strong. Inflows to broad-mandate funds (\$5.9bn) and Govt bonds (\$2.9bn) accelerated. Inflows to IG (\$4.4bn) remained robust but those to HY (\$1.5bn) slowed this week. EM bonds (- \$0.2bn) saw modest outflows. Munis (\$0.2bn) and MBS (\$0.6bn) received inflows at a slower pace. Bank Loans (-\$0.2bn) and TIPS (-\$0.3bn) saw outflows.

n Money Market funds (-\$12.5bn) suffered outflows for a second week in a row, albeit at a much slower pace than last week. Europe (-\$8.2bn), US (-\$1.2bn), and Japan (-\$1.9bn) all saw outflows.

n CFTC update (data as of Tuesday): Aggregate US equity net longs fell slightly but remained near the top of their historical range. Nasdaq 100 net longs fell slightly and are in the middle of their historical range, S&P 500 net longs also fell slightly but remain elevated. Russell 2000 net longs rose slightly and are modestly long. Aggregate bond net shorts increased led by net shorts rising in the 5y and 10y, partially offset by declines in 30y net shorts while 2y net shorts were flat. 3m SOFR net longs rose slightly. In FX, US dollar net longs rose as Pound net longs fell from extremely elevated levels, Euro net longs fell almost to neutral, Australian dollar positions flipped from net long to a decent sized net short, and Canadian dollar net shorts continued increasing towards extremes. Partially offsetting these, yen net shorts were cut in half and Swiss Franc net shorts fell but remain elevated. In Commodities, oil net longs fell to the bottom of their historical range while gold net longs ticked up slightly at elevated levels and platinum net longs ticked up just above neutral. Silver net longs ticked town while copper net longs fell sharply and are nearing neutral.

## Aggregate Equity Positioning Indicator

n Our measure of aggregate equity positioning is a weighted average of Zscores across several positioning indicators. The indicator weights are based on the explanatory power of each variable in a regression with movements in the S&P 500.

Below is the list of indicators used in the aggregate positioning indicator

Vol control funds equity allocation (Systematic). Vol funds total equity allocation tries to mimic the equity rebalancing behavior of a sample of 25 Vol funds following a constant target volatility strategy. More details can be found in the report ‘Derivatives Spotlight: Vol Control Products Disentangled: A Driver of Low-to-High Vol Transitions – 24 Aug 2016’

CTAs equity weight (Systematic). These are model-based portfolio weights, and take into account for each asset class the momentum trend signal, volatility, and cross asset correlations. The model is designed to follow movements in CTA benchmark indices.

n Risk parity funds equity portfolio weight (Systematic). These are our model-based portfolio weights calculated to balance the risk contribution across different asset classes (equities, bonds, commodities), using volatility and cross asset correlations. The model is designed to follow movements in risk parity benchmark indices.

Equity futures (Systematic/Discretionary). Aggregate net long futures (long minus short contracts) for Asset managers and Leveraged funds in the S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, Russell 2000, S&P 400 and DJIA index, taken as a percentage of their open interest. Data from CFTC’s Traders in Financial Futures report.

n Net call volume (Discretionary). Total net call volume (call minus put) across single stocks, indices and ETPs. Data from the Chicago Board Options Exchange.

n S&P 500 3m option skew (Discretionary). S&P 500 options skew (90%- 110% moneyness implied volatility spread, 3-month maturity) from Bloomberg. Indicator is negatively correlated with the S&P 500, and the zscore values are multiplied by -1 before inclusion in the composite.

n Investor Bull minus Bear spread (Discretionary). Investor sentiment (Bull minus Bear spread) from the AAII Investor Sentiment Survey.

n Cash equity median shorts (Discretionary). Median of Russell 3000 universe shorted shares, taken as % of outstanding shares. Indicator is negatively correlated with the S&P 500, and the z-score values are multiplied by -1 before inclusion in the composite.

ETFs short interest (Discretionary). Aggregate short interest (price\*shorted shares) of more than 100 largest equity ETFs in the US, taken as % of overall Russell 3000 market cap. Indicator is negatively correlated with the S&P 500, and the z-score values are multiplied by -1 before inclusion in the composite.

n IMX Retail positioning index (Discretionary). TD Ameritrade Investor Movement Index (IMX) which gauges the sentiment of retail investors.

n MFs holdings beta (Discretionary). The equity beta of the holdings of a sample of broad active equity mutual funds (excluding cash) taken from their quarterly 13F filings. The beta of the holdings to S&P 500 returns is calculated over a 3 year window

## Sectors Equity Positioning

n As an extension of the aggregate equity positioning, the sector positioning measure is also a weighted average of Z-scores across different sector level positioning indicators. The indicator weights are based on the explanatory power of each variable in a regression with movements in the S&P 500 sectors. More details can be found in the report 'Investor Positioning and Flows - Looking Across Sectors, Jul 7 2023

Below is the list of indicators used in the aggregate sector positioning measure

n Net call volume. Net call volume (call minus put) across single stocks and sector ETFs averaged out over the last one month. Data from the OCC.

n Sector fund flows: Fund flows to sector based ETFs and mutual funds, taken as a percentage of AUM, and averaged out for the last 13 weeks. Data from EPFR.

n Cash equity median shorts. A de-trended median of Russell 3000 sector shorted shares, taken as % of outstanding shares. Indicator is negatively correlated with the S&P 500 sector performance, and the z-score values are multiplied by -1 before inclusion in the composite.

n Active MFs excess return correlation. Rolling 3-month correlation of the excess returns of a representative sample of US blended mutual funds with S&P 500 sector excess returns (both relative to the S&P 500).

n Analyst consensus target. Analysts current 12-month target price for the S&P 500 sector, taken as a ratio to its 200d ma. Data from Bloomberg.

Macroeconomic fluctuations often account for most of the risks associated with exposures to instruments that promise to pay fixed or variable interest rates. For an investor who is long fixed-rate instruments (thus receiving these cash flows), increases in interest rates naturally lift the discount factors applied to the expected cash flows and thus cause a loss. The longer the maturity of a certain cash flow and the higher the move in the discount factor, the higher will be the loss. Upside surprises in inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most common adverse macroeconomic shocks to receivers. But counterparty exposure, issuer creditworthiness, client segmentation, regulation (including changes in assets holding limits for different types of investors), changes in tax policies, currency convertibility (which may constrain currency conversion, repatriation of profits and/or liquidation of positions), and settlement issues related to local clearing houses are also important risk factors. The sensitivity of fixed-income instruments to macroeconomic shocks may be mitigated by indexing the contracted cash flows to inflation, to FX depreciation, or to specified interest rates - these are common in emerging markets. The choice of the proper fixing (or metric) is particularly important in swaps markets, where floating coupon rates (i.e., coupons indexed to a typically short-dated interest rate reference index) are exchanged for fixed coupons.
