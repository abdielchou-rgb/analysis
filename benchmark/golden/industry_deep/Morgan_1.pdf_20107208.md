• We also tighten our base cases for HY and loans to 350bp (from 475bp) and 425bp (from 525bp). Total returns are positive but look much better for loans (\~9%) than HY bonds (4.6%).

• Across the board, we think that the bear case should be lower than typical recession levels, with the Fed likely to respond swiftly to weaker growth.

• We expect a less pronounced and flatter default cycle. We see defaults peaking in 1H24 and then moderating gradually from there to 3.75% in HY and 4% in loans at year-end.

## IG Curves – Expect Twist Steepening – Front End Tighter, Long End Wider

## Key Takeaways

This is a good backdrop for credit: 1-2% growth, moderating inflation, policy easing on the horizon, low-butimproving PMIs, improving (but not elevated) corporate confidence, strong demand, and modest volatility with high dispersion (allowing credit-picking to ‘work’). Both fundamentals and bond documentation are driving single-name alpha.

Spreads should be richer than average, given this backdrop: We continue to hold a constructive bias, and have our end-2024 forecasts tighter across corporate and EM credit. The challenge is to find value.

In the US, we like loans and CLO equity: Loan valuations have significantly lagged other assets (high yield, investment grade, equities). The asset class would be a major beneficiary of a ‘soft landing, higher-for-longer’ scenario, and should face improving technicals. Having previously been bullish on CLO mezz, we’re now moving down the capital structure.

In Europe, we like IG and loans: EUR IG spreads aren’t particularly expensive (they’ve been tighter \~40% of the time over the last 20 years), balance sheets are heathy and net issuance should be modest. Similar to the US, EU loans (and CLOs) have lagged HY bonds.

In emerging markets, we see range-bound spreads driving good total returns (\~8.5%) and plentiful opportunities for RV. We have a bullish tilt in Africa, and like Egypt, Nigeria and Senegal. We like both Argentina and Venezuela as high-beta credits, and see another 30% upside in the former this year. In lower-beta names, we like Brazil, Chile and Romania.

## Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)

Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "origina issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any premium paid.
