## Top newsflow

## China / Global

• President Xi Jinping vowed to make “high-quality development” the guiding force of the world’s No. 2 economy at the Third Plenum this week, showing few signs that the top leadership is preparing to unleash major steps to boost demand or arrest the property slump. The ruling Communist Party signalled China will stay on course with Xi’s plan to use advanced manufacturing to generate growth. The focus on manufacturing could increase trade tensions, with rising Chinese exports prompting fresh tarifs from the US and the European Union. Oficials normally issue a more detailed report, which typically announces broader policy shifts, several days after the Third Plenum. Specific measures taking cues from that document are likely to come later this month from the July Politburo meeting.

• Second-quarter data undershot expectations this week after retail sales in June rose at the slowest pace since 2022 and real GDP growth slowed to 4.7% YoY.  Oficials put “actively expanding domestic demand” on their to-do list as a near-term task in the communique. Authorities also reafirmed their determination to hit this year’s social and economic goals, which include an annual growth target of about 5%. That marked only the third time in three decades short-term economic goals were mentioned at the plenum, with the other instances coming in 1998 and 2008 when growth was also under severe pressure. As a result, policy stance may turn more expansionary in the second half of 2024 as policymakers have to defend the annual growth target. The real estate sector was mentioned just once in the nearly 4,000-word announcement. It was stated only in the context of managing risk, a signal no dramatic stimulus measures are forthcoming.

• China is expected to strengthen its policies to balance economic growth in the second half to achieve its target of around 5 percent growth this year, chief economists said after the Third Plenum started. The issuance of general and special government bonds reached 46% and 38% of the 2024 quotas, respectively, in H1. Both figures are much lower than average in the past five years. In the second half, China's fiscal budget still has room to use up CNY6trillion including CNY2.9 trillion of fiscal deficit,CNY2.4 trillion of new special bonds, as well as CNY750 billion of ultra-long special treasury bonds. Consequently, the nation is likely to further boost its fiscal policies to stimulate domestic demand.

• The number of new shipbuilding orders in China soared 44 percent in the first six months of this year YoY. China's shipbuilding orders reached 54.2m deadweight tons in the six months ended June 30, accounting for nearly 75% of the global total, up 8 percentage points from a year ago, according to data from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology. The country's shipbuilding output jumped 18.4% to 25.02m DWT, making up 55% of the global total, the data showed. Meanwhile, holding orders at Chinese shipbuilders rose 39 percent to 171.55 million DWT, accounting for 59 percent of the world's total.

## Base/Precious/Lithium

• Lundin Mining Corp. has approached BHP Group about making a joint bid for copper explorer Filo Corp., people with knowledge of the matter said, in a move that could solve fundraising needs for a neighbouring project it wants to build. It’s unclear how receptive BHP is to the proposal. The Lundin family have a 32% stake in Filo while BHP holds nearly 6%, according to Bloomberg data. Lundin has pitched the idea of buying out other shareholders in Filo and then combining the target’s Filo del Sol copper project — located on the Argentina-Chile border — with Lundin’s neighboring Josemaria operation, the people said. Bringing the two assets together would make it easier for Lundin to finance their development while bringing BHP much-needed growth in copper. Talks are at a preliminary stage and there are no

guarantees they will proceed with a bid, the people said. Representatives for BHP and Lundin declined to comment.

• China’s State Reserve Bureau (SRB) is buying up nickel pig iron, sources told Fastmarkets. The SRB plans to purchase 300kt of NPI at a price of 1,080 yuan (\$148) per nickel unit on a delivered, duty paid basis, according to market sources. When the SRB’s specific package and delivery requirements are taken into account, the 1,080 yuan price being ofered is in line with spot market assessments. The purchase will be stretched out over six months starting from July, with an average of 50kt of NPI bought each month, which would not significantly afect NPI availability. This is the fourth time that the SRB has bought NPI since July 2023, with the other purchases happening in November 2023 and April 2024. (Source: Fastmarket)

• The world’s single biggest buyer of copper, State Grid Corp. of China, has slowed its purchases of copper wire this year, while at the same time ramping up purchases of aluminum wire, a cheaper substitute, as copper prices soared to a new record driven by buying from bullish investors. One major supplier to the grid said that his company’s sales of copper cable were down about 20% in the second quarter. The state grid’s insulated metallic power cable tenders slowed since April due to high copper prices, although some activity resumed after prices retreated, said CRU. State Grid’s tenders for aluminum cables rose by 40% to 718kt in the first four months this year, according to SMM.

• CMOC reported copper and cobalt production in 1H24 reached historic highs. Copper metal production in 1H24 was 314kt, up 101% YoY, reaching 58% of the production guidance midpoint and 75% of the annual copper production in 2023. Cobalt metal production was 54kt, up 178% YoY, reaching 83% of the production guidance midpoint and 97% of the annual cobalt production in 2023. It is highly probable that CMOC will exceed the upper limit of the annual production guidance of 520,000-570,000 tons and become the largest incremental source of global copper production in 2024.

• Alcoa noted in its earnings call that San Ciprian alumina refinery (0.5% of world supply) continues to operate while it negotiates a potential sale of the asset. Three potential buyers have expressed interest in the asset spanning financial, strategic and non-traditional buyers. Alcoa continues to work the sale process and aims to bring it to conclusion this year, but noted that a successful sale will depend on Spanish government and union support.

• Serbia and the European Union signed a framework agreement on providing critical raw materials as the Balkan nation seeks to leverage a planned lithium mine to become a regional hub for electric vehicle production. Serbian President Aleksandar Vucic sealed an agreement with German Chancellor Olaf Scholz in Belgrade on Friday to reactivate the \$2.4 billion Jadar lithium project owned by Rio Tinto Group into Europe’s biggest lithium mine. Representatives of Mercedes Benz AG and Stellantis NV, European carmakers that have built EV battery links with a joint venture, also signed a letter of intent in Belgrade to invest in a project to develop EV battery output. EC Vice President Maros Sefcovic, who accompanied Scholz to Belgrade, said that Serbia would “occupy a central place in the most important strategic supply chains.”

• Chinese copper smelters are bracing for a potential shortage of scrap as Beijing’s eforts to create a level playing field have the unintended efect of reducing supply. The Fair Competition Review regulation, due to take efect in August, forbids tax benefits without either approval from the State Council or permission by existing law. Many of China’s copper scrap processors rely on tax rebates from local governments, so may have to cut production, which will hit smelters that need the feedstock to make refined metal. Around 30% of China’s refined copper production used scrap as a feedstock in 2023. The competition policy could

also disrupt production of other metals that use scrap as a feedstock including aluminum and lead.

## Bulks/Steel/Other

• Wildfires in Quebec province have led to the shutdown of operations at Rio Tinto's IOC operations (17.5mt) and Champion Iron's Bloom Lake (14mt) on 12 July. Combined production of 31.5mt = 1.9% of seaborne IO market. Champion Iron subsequently announced a gradual return to operations at Bloom Lake on 15 July, noting recent events did not afect the company's and third parties' infrastructure, which should enable a gradual ramp-up of production and rail shipments.

• Cleveland-Clifs deal to acquire Stelco Holdings for CAD \$60/sh in cash + 0.454 shares of Clifs, a total consideration of CAD \$70 per Stelco share. Transaction EV of \~US\$2.5bn (CAD \$3.bn) for Stelco and represents an acquisition multiple of 4.8x 3/31/24 LTM Adjusted EBITDA with synergies. Clifs' move to buy Stelco comes nearly a year after Clifs began its failed bid to purchase steelmaking competitor US Steel. Japanese steelmaker Nippon Steel is now in the midst of negotiating the \$15bn purchase of US Steel.

• Fortescue announced sweeping job cuts and a slowdown in the development of its green hydrogen business. The world's fourth-biggest iron ore miner embarked on a major expansion in late 2020 to turn itself into a green energy giant, based on a view that hydrogen from renewable power sources would become a major source of green energy. But with power prices in Australia remaining high and strong demand yet to take shape, Fortescue will cut about 700 jobs, or 4.5% of its global workforce. It was unlikely to meet a production target of 15 million tonnes of green hydrogen by 2030, a spokesperson told Reuters on Thursday. Fortescue has guided a cumulative spend of \$6.2 billion to meet its decarbonisation targets by the end of the decade, but has only spent around \$300 million per year so far, which suggests it must step up its spending. It will also have to spend more on sustaining capital and on its Iron Bridge magnetite project, analysts said.

• ArcelorMittal said on Thursday that a 55-day blockade at one of its sites in Mexico ended after workers approved a new settlement that includes an 8% pay hike for employees and review of their collective bargaining agreement. Workers afiliated with the mining union had been protesting since late May against the annual profit distribution to employees decided by the company for 2023, bringing all activities to a halt. In a statement, ArcelorMittal said the settlement, besides the retroactive pay increase, included a bonus of 60,000 pesos (\$3,336) per worker, food vouchers, back pay, and contracting a "reputable tax firm" to determine if there is any unpaid profit sharing diference.
