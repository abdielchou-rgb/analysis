## Position adjustment in preparation for European bond risk and BOJ, slowdown in retail investors’ cash market buying (margin buying expands)

In this edition we briefly summarize current Japan market flows/positioning ahead of the major events, and some points worth noting. We will consider detailed trading tactics, etc in light of the market’s interpretation of the events after they happen. We in the QDS team expect a gradual revival of risk-on momentum after the market’s current stalled phase ends. While the FOMC and BOJ meetings may have an impact on the revival’s timing, we think they are unlikely to have enough clout to force a fundamental revision of our scenario. However, for the time being, we are only concerned about whether Japanese equity players get caught up in a greater-than-necessary sell-off of European sovereign bonds (EU periphery countries) on the pretext of France's political turmoil.

We provide updates on overall market activity (NKY, JGB) and risks in the first section below, overseas investor flows in the second, and retail investor money in the third.

## 1. Europe risk/BOJ weighs on sentiment: Macro hedge funds eliminate Japanese equity longs

In the first half of this week, the Nikkei 225 (NKY) tried to get established at the 39,000 level, but failed. Today (June 12), it is at the 38,800 level, and the stalled market conditions remain unchanged. Of course, most investors continue to wait on the sidelines ahead of the FOMC meeting, US CPI data for May, and BOJ meeting scheduled today and tomorrow (June 13). At the earliest, we think Japanese equity player activity will revive after the BOJ meeting’s outcome is digested.

That is to say, we think it would then become impossible to ignore the fear that if turmoil in European markets spreads, the flow of Japanese stock buying through European markets (mainly Germany) will not only slow down but also go into reverse. In particular, the trend (12-month cumulative change) in Japanese equity flows via passive funds (ETFs) correlates strongly with the country risk seen in the European bond market (as measured by BTP vs. Bunds performance). As the backdrop, we think that when European investors raise the weighting of Japanese equities in passive asset management, it is on the assumption that credit conditions in the region are stable, and at the same time, a European equity rally has already occurred. In layman's terms, we believe that without stability and improvement in the mother market, investment in higher-risk foreign stocks, such as Japanese stocks, is likely to be an afterthought (but this does not necessarily apply to active management).

A similar trend can also be observed in flows through Japanese equity ETFs listed on the US market (Figure 4). The second point is interpretation of the imbalance between BOJ policy normalization expectations, and Japan's economic slowdown. After confirming current (as of June 11) derivatives market conditions, we see a strong need for hedging against downside options for NKY and JGBs, mainly in the three-month tenor. It can be seen as pre-event preparation, but given that this trend is stronger at three-months than at one-month, we think the market is bearing in mind the risk of unexpected “hawkishness” in BOJ-related events, not only this time but also in the near future.

What concerns us, including about this derivatives trading trend, is that macro hedge funds’ (HFs) current positioning is beginning to show signs of bearishness toward Japanese equities. Based on estimates using our QDS team’s HFR data-based HF database, we think it likely that macro HFs have built up close to a record-large bear position in JGBs. We think this is also the case for trend-chasing CTAs. Overseas investors all have bear positions in JGBs from both fundamentals and technical perspectives, and we think they are waiting for developments like the BOJ raising interest rates further.

On the other hand, we estimate macro HFs’ Japanese equity position is currently close to flat after unwinding of longs. We think the short-term trend of macro HFs’ Japanese equity position is liable to be defined by the Japanese economy (as measured by the manufacturing PMI) and USD/JPY changes. Japan’s manufacturing PMI remains weak year to date (partly due to special factors related to automobiles). In addition to the manufacturing PMI, many other macroeconomic indicators have recently disappointed overseas investors, such as consumer spending and real wages.

The imbalance between Japan's macro environment, which can hardly be seen as strong, and the BOJ, which appears to be in a hurry to normalize monetary policy, may be becoming a pretext for macro HFs to reduce their long position in Japanese equities for now, and to buy JPY call options at the same time (= prepare for the yen to appreciate). We will be watching closely to see whether macro HFs change their stance on Japanese equity trades after the BOJ meeting. On the off chance that this pattern continues, we think macro HFs and macrooriented overseas investors may find it difficult to adopt a bullish stance on Japanese equities (above all share price indices) to the extent seen in 2023.

## 2. Weak trading flows from overseas investors: Small-scale bank selling & quality buying in June

In this section we provide an update on cash market flows for overseas investors as a whole. First, trend-following CTAs are maintaining their long accumulation stance in the futures market, but the actual flows are meager. CTAs are also currently in wait-and-see mode. As a flow factor for indices stalling, we think trading on the cash side (by overseas investors) is also mixed at the same time. Looking at most recent flows (up to June 11) by industry, since June started we can confirm modest outflows from banks, into which inflows surged in May. We surmise investors are implementing rotation whereby they shift some funds to semiconductor relateds and individual high-quality names (with stocks included in defensive sectors somewhat numerous).

We think bank stock selling by overseas investors can be seen partly as position adjustment ahead of the BOJ meeting. However, with the exception of the above, while we are seeing cyclical & value stocks peak out overall, in June overseas investors' preference is turning markedly toward growth or quality, especially among defensive stocks. Our impression is that overseas investors are gradually starting to recognize the risk of a slowdown in the US economy, and the view that USD/JPY will peak out.

## 3. Slowdown in cash market buying by individuals: Margin buying (not covered by NISAs) and overseas stock investment trusts remain active

Meanwhile, what are retail investors up to? We certainly get the impression that retail investors still want to buy, but think this is not necessarily due to NISA impact. Looking at overall supply/demand conditions, buying by individuals is a support factor, but given this is mainly short-term money, while long-term money continues to favor overseas stock investment trusts (NISA impact), we believe that a neutral assessment is appropriate at least for the impact from retail investor money.

The percentage of total brokerage trading by individual investors as a whole was 23% in the recent past (up to May 31), and even when excluding seasonal swings, it has showed signs of falling after peaking at the 30% average in January 2024, when the new NISA scheme was introduced. Further, about 74% of this brokerage trading ratio of 23% is margin trading, not cash. Margin trading is not only not covered by NISAs, but is also dominated by trendchasing, swing-oriented, short-term money (such as day traders). The net balance of margin trades has expanded again to about ¥4 trillion (as of June 7), and we think it is highly speculative retail investor money that is active in the stalled market.

Of course, we think active retail investors, mainly cash investors (this is covered by NISAs), may also be continuing to buy in the cash market. Our QDS team estimates that May–June (through June 11) average inflows into popular stocks from active retail investors maintained a monthly pace of about ¥30 billion. However, this represents a slowdown from the about ¥50 billion/month average in January–April.

On the other hand, NISA impact on overseas equity investment trusts remains eye-catching. Large-scale inflows have continued to this point. We can confirm inflows also into some Japanese equity investment trusts, including high-dividend-type trusts, but the inflow pace remains slower than for overseas equity investment trusts.
