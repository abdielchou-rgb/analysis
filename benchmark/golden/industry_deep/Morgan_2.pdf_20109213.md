## Singapore REITs

What to buy on prospects of rate cuts

S-REITs have rallied 6% over last Thursday and Friday (11-12 July), outperforming FSSTI (1%) and SG banks (0%). This is on prospects of earlier Fed rate cuts, with our economists now expecting the first rate cut in September, and quarterly cuts thereafter (link). While this comes ahead of expectations (link), we see prospects of further upside ahead on the back of light positioning and S-REIT underperformance YTD (-10%) versus STI (6%) and other regional REIT markets such as Australia (12%). While we continue to like quality S-REITs such as CICT and FCT, we see near-term outperformance from more beaten down names including MPACT, FLT and MINT. We also upgrade MINT to OW from N with higher PT of S\$2.50, up from S\$2.35.

2019 best mirrors current situation. In the last cycle where there were prospects of a soft landing, S-REITs rallied 0.9% and 2.0% over one- and three-month period after the rate cut in July 2019, respectively, outperforming SG banks (-6.8%/- 1.7%) and STI (-5.9%/-2.1%) respectively. We are two months away from a potential September cut.

Selective appetite. We have received interest from LO investors to increase S-REIT exposure albeit on a selective basis. Key focus is on REITs with a sustainable earnings profile and lower risk of disappointment with preference to avoid shortterm trades. This is likely due to multiple false downs over the past two years and positive near-term earnings momentum for SG banks where average yield of 5.8% is only 0.1% lower than S-REITs. In addition, in the event of modest level of rate cuts, many S-REITs may still face higher borrowing costs/earnings pressure.

Take a balanced approach. We have been recommending investors to focus on Singapore retail and data center exposures given tight supply as overseas properties have seen weakness in income and asset values. YTD, REITs for this thematic namely CICT (+3%), FCT (-1.8%), KDCREIT (+2.1%) have outperformed S-REIT index (-8.0%). While we see merits to continue holding all three given still favorable property fundamentals, more durable returns over a longer time frame and potential uplift from DPU accretive acquisitions, we believe adding underowned laggards such as MPACT, FLT and MINT, that offer \~6% or higher yields, provides greater total returns over the next 3-6 months.

Tactical opportunity in MINT. We see prospects of a rally in MINT as (1) overhang from having \~66% of its debt in USD which has seen faster rate hikes than REITs with SGD debt is lifted on earlier Fed cuts, and (2) uplift arising from the data center thematic (\~55% of portfolio AUM). In addition, MINT’s current yield of 5.9-6.0% is 125bps higher than KDCREIT, its listed data center peer, vs. historical average differential of 85bps, and we see prospects of MINT’s yield closing the gap with KDCREIT. We have received greater interest from investors to rotate into industrial REITs which have lagged the S-REIT index by 2-7% over the past 3 months with preference for REITs with a sustainable earnings profile and lower risk of disappointment. MINT in our view fits this well as we see greater risk of CLAR/MLT missing Street estimates in the upcoming results season (link).

## Play on data center and rate cut thematic - Upgrade to OW

We upgrade MINT from N to OW with higher PT of S\$2.50 from S\$2.35. We see a tactical opportunity in MINT given prospects of September rather than November Fed rate cut (link) (\~66% of MINT’s borrowings are in USD) and uplift arising from the data center thematic (\~55% of portfolio AUM). In addition, MINT’s current yield of 5.9-6.0% is 125bps higher than KDCREIT, its listed data center peer, versus historical average differential of 85bps, and we see prospects of MINT’s yield closing the gap with KDCREIT. We have received greater interest from investors to rotate into industrial REITs which have lagged the S-REIT index by 2-7% over the past three months with preference for REITs with a sustainable earnings profile and lower risk of disappointment. MINT, in our view, fits this well as we see greater risk of CLAR/MLT missing Street estimates in the upcoming results season (link).

More constructive macro backdrop. MINT over the past 18-24 months has had to confront the narrative of being one of the S-REITs that would be more negatively impacted by higher US interest rates which have risen faster than local SGD borrowing costs. This has created an overhang and discouraged some investors from looking at MINT. However, with prospects of a September rate cut (92% probability as per Bloomberg estimates) and in line with our US economist projections, we expect greater interest/fund flows back into MINT.

Data center uplift. Data center exposures has been a key share price driver for many stocks and this should underpin interest in MINT and its share price going forward. While acknowledging risk surrounding MINT’s older US data centers, this is tempered by increasing power constraints and MINT having backfilled vacancies from exit of tenants which should result in occupancy rising to above 91% from 86.2% in the March quarter (link). Furthermore, we see prospects of MINT closing the gap to KDCREIT as MINT’s current yield is 125bps higher than KDCREIT versus historical average differential of 85bps.

Lower risk of earnings disappointment and lighter investor positioning compared to peers. Investors have sought ideas to play the rate cut thematic among industrials REITs which have structural growth exposures. However, there is reluctance to long stocks with risk of earnings disappointment. We see lower risk of MINT missing Street estimates given borrowing costs assumed by the Street is close to guidance of no more than 3.5% (link), lack of China exposure (as compared to MLT) and potential for higher occupancy versus slippage for CLAR due to high base effect. In addition, based on our investor discussions, positioning appears to be lighter in MINT compared to CLAR and MLT.

PT raised to S\$2.50 from S\$2.35 as we lower our cost of capital assumptions to 7.5% from 7.7% to better reflect the improved macro backdrop and tailwinds from the data center theme. There are no changes to our DPU estimates. Our revised PT implies target yield of 5.3% (0.3 s.d below mean).

## Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation

## Investment Thesis

MINT is a multi-asset industrial REIT with primary exposure to data centers in Singapore (\~3% of AUM), Japan (\~5%) and the US/Canada (\~47% of NAV) as well as high-tech buildings (\~17% of NAV). MINT also owns flatted factories (FF, \~16% of AUM), business parks (\~6%), stack-up/ramp-up buildings (\~6%) and light industrial buildings (\~1%) in Singapore. We are positive on MINT as we believe the overhang on MINT’s share price should be lifted as Fed cuts interest rates and it benefits from fund flows as investors seek data center exposures. This should result in tighter DPU yields/share price re-rating ahead. In addition, we see opportunity for MINT to close the gap with its listed data center peer, KDCREIT, as MINT’s DPU yield is \~125bps higher than KDCREIT versus historical average differential of 85bps.

## Valuation

Our Jun-25 PT of S\$2.50 is based on DDM with a 7.5% discount rate and a 2.0% terminal growth rate. Our PT implies FY25 yield of 5.3% (0.3 s.d below mean) and FY25 core yield of 5.1% (0.3 s.d. mean). The implied core yield spread stands at 2.4% (1.6 s.d below mean), using our risk free assumption of 2.75%. We believe a discount to mean yields and yield spreads is justified, given MINT’s change in portfolio mix towards high-growth sectors and freehold properties (>50% of portfolio) away from exposure to flatted factories with short land tenures which historically led to elevated yields.

# Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks

Mapletree Industrial Trust (Overweight; Price Target: S\$2.50)

## Investment Thesis

MINT is a multi-asset industrial REIT with primary exposure to data centers in Singapore (\~3% of AUM), Japan (\~5%) and the US/Canada (\~47% of NAV) as well as high-tech buildings (\~17% of NAV). MINT also owns flatted factories (FF, \~16% of AUM), business parks (\~6%), stack-up/ramp-up buildings (\~6%) and light industrial buildings (\~1%) in Singapore. We are positive on MINT as we believe the overhang on MINT’s share price should be lifted as Fed cuts interest rates and it benefits from fund flows as investors seek data center exposures. This should result in tighter DPU yields/share price re-rating ahead. In addition, we see opportunity for MINT to close the gap with its listed data center peer, KDCREIT, as MINT’s DPU yield is \~125bps higher than KDCREIT versus historical average differential of 85bps.

## Valuation

Our Jun-25 PT of S\$2.50 is based on DDM with a 7.5% discount rate and a 2.0% terminal growth rate. Our PT implies FY25 yield of 5.3% (0.3 s.d below mean) and FY25 core yield of 5.1% (0.3 s.d. mean). The implied core yield spread stands at 2.4% (1.6 s.d below mean), using our risk free assumption of 2.75%. We believe a discount to mean yields and yield spreads is justified, given MINT’s change in portfolio mix towards high-growth sectors and freehold properties (>50% of portfolio) away from exposure to flatted factories with short land tenures which historically led to elevated yields.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

Key downside risks include: (1) weaker-than-expected rents; (2) higher-than-expected borrowing costs; and (3) depreciation of USD versus SGD as \~47% of MINT’s portfolio is located in the US.
