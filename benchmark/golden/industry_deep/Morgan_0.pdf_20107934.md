## April 30

Tsys sell-off after ECI acceleration; USD exhibits broad strength against G10 peers; European rates bear-flatten with better GDP data; ECB's Villeroy calls cuts after June "pragmatic"; Canada GDP is soft, USD/CAD surges; CLP leads LatAm FX losses; DXY at 106.27 (+0.7%); US 10y at 4.680% (+6.6bp).

• Treasuries sell off after the Employment Cost Index continues the string of upside inflation surprises in 1Q24; focus turns to the May FOMC meeting and tone of Fed Chair Powell's press conference.

• US dollar exhibits broad strength against G10 peers as marketimplied Fed rate cuts dwindle to just 28bp by December, after the acceleration in a closely watched measure of wage inflation.

• European duration bear-flattens after better-than-expected preliminary 1Q24 nationwide and Euro Area GDP data.

• ECB Governing Council Member Villeroy says the latest round of inflation data feed into their confidence for a June cut, but the pace of cuts will be “pragmatic” and contingent on inflation.

• USD/CAD (+0.9%) surges after Canadian GDP surprises to the downside in February, with 1Q24 GDP tracking below the BoC's forecast of 2.8% q/q.

• CLP (-1.9%) leads losses in the region as USD strength is compounded by weaker-than-expected activity data and \~2.5% lower copper prices.

## Japan:

In the Tokyo morning session on Tuesday, long-end JGBs rallied after the strength in US and European rates on Monday and the dovish BoJ outcome. On Friday, the BoJ maintained the status quo, without a change to its pace of JGB purchases. BoJ Governor Ueda said current yen weakness will not affect the trend in underlying inflation at this stage, which suggests monetary policy will not be affected by the currency depreciation. These factors supported a restoration in market participants’ duration exposure. However, the bullish momentum abated in the Tokyo afternoon session amid decent selling pressure in the 5y tenor in the broker market; 5y JGBs rallied by 3.5bp d/d to 0.47% on a simple yield basis. 10y JGBs traded resiliently amid strong demand to square short positions.

TONA OIS outperformed JGBs in the long-end. XCCY basis tightened across the curve except for the 10y tenor. After the Tokyo session ended, the BoJ released its plan for JGB purchases in May, without a change from April. This was in line with consensus expectations, and thus the reaction in markets was quite muted.

During a local holiday in Japan on Monday, USD/JPY fell sharply from 159.60 to below 155.00 at one point. Japanese Ministry of Finance officials did not confirm whether yen-buying intervention had occurred; thus, market participants will now await May MoF FX intervention data (Apr 26-May 29), released on May 31, to confirm. However, changes in the BoJ’s current account balance data suggested MoF intervention was JPY 5.0-5.5tr. USD/JPY traded higher from the Tokyo open

toward 157.00, driven by USD strength, but failed to break above this level given concern for potential intervention. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY, but AUD/JPY fell in the Tokyo afternoon alongside surprisingly weak Australian retail sales.

## Europe:

EUR (-0.4%) reversed overnight gains, which followed after stronger-than-expected GDP data, but posted more modest losses against the US dollar than other G10 currencies.

European duration weakness resumed on Tuesday. Weakness in the first half of the London session was due to strong preliminary 1Q24 GDP data across the regions and at the aggregate Euro Area level. The sell-off intensified after a stronger-thanexpected 1Q24 US Employment Cost Index. Overall, European duration underperformed on a cross-market basis, and 10y Bunds closed the London session at 2.58% (+5.11bp). The bulk of the sell-off was in front-end rates; 2y Schatz drifted back above 3.0% and the curve bear-flattened.

Market pricing of total cuts by December 2024 fell firmly below 75bp, as \~8bp of cumulative cuts were removed. Non-forecast meetings are priced at -7bp for July and -6bp for October. Market pricing of the ECB terminal rate continued to reprice higher, with the trough depo rate currently at \~2.72% as implied by Euribor contracts, in line with the highs from November 2023. In consideration of the move in duration, underperformance of cash was reflected in the ASW complex, although modestly. Peripheral spreads widened by \~1bp; the 10y BTP-Bund spread ended the London session at 133bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

The dollar strengthened broadly following an upside surprise in the US Employment Cost Index. Higher US rates, coupled with a deterioration in risk sentiment, pushed the DXY USD Index (+0.6%) back above 106.00 ahead of Wednesday’s FOMC decision. USD/JPY (+0.9%) trended higher throughout the NY trading session, despite intervention risk, as USD/JPY followed the broader US dollar move.

The Antipodeans underperformed amid the decline in global equity markets. Both AUD and NZD fell as much as \~1.3%. USD/CAD jumped after a downside surprise in the monthly Canadian GDP indicator. USD/CAD (+0.7%) hovered \~1.3760 in the NY afternoon.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly amid USD strength (DXY USD Index: +0.2%) before a holiday in many local markets on Wednesday. CNY and CNH weakened after the NBS PMI release but pared losses after the Politburo meeting signaled property support measures. USD/CNH finished the AxJ session 0.1% higher, at \~7.2480, while USD/CNY rose 0.2% to just under 7.24. KRW was the key underperformer in the region despite KOSPI (+0.17%) outperformance. USD/KRW steadily ground higher over the AxJ session to 1,382 (+0.3%). THB and MYR depreciated \~0.2% against USD. Initially, PHP began the AxJ session stronger, but eventually finished 0.1% weaker. INR outperformed; after hovering at 83.52 for most of the AxJ session, USD/INR weakened sharply just after 4:00pm HKT and closed the AxJ session 0.1% weaker, at 83.43.

AxJ rates were mixed. China rates outperformed after the PBoC net injected RMB 438bn of liquidity via OMOs and the Politburo meeting signaled room for further monetary easing. The rally was led by the front-end, as 1y CNY NDIRS yields fell 6bp and the 1s5s curve bull-steepened 2bp. CGBs rallied by \~4bp across the curve beyond the 2y tenor, despite Politburo guidance for ultra-long CGBs to be issued as early as possible and an acceleration of LGSB issuance. Korea rates also outperformed; the curve bull-flattened amid soft industrial production data and a weak KRW. 10y KTB yields fell 5bp and the 3s10s curve bull-flattened by 3bp. Hong Kong rates underperformed, particularly in the front-end; 1y swap yields rose 6bp. India G-Sec held steady ahead of debt sales by state governments. Thailand and India 5y swap yields rose \~2bp, with a similar move in Indonesia 10y bonds.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies weakened in the session as the US dollar strengthened following higher-than-expected ECI data; COP (-1.5%) and BRL (-1.4%) sold off. CLP (-1.9%) led losses in the region as USD strength was compounded by weaker-than-expected activity data and \~2.5% lower copper prices. The weak activity data affected rates, and the CAM curve outperformed peers; the 2y tenor ended 8bp lower. The rest of LatAm rates sold off, mainly driven by USTs. The DI curve underperformed; frontend January 2026 contracts sold off 23bp. The IBR curve closed little changed ahead of the BanRep policy meeting, where the board cut rates by the anticipated 50bp.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• ECB Governing Council Member Villeroy said the latest round of inflation data feed into the ECB’s confidence for a June cut. He added the next data are expected to be volatile, and that the ECB should focus more on the outlook for inflation than hard data. Lastly, he said the pace of cuts will be “pragmatic” and contingent on the inflation outlook

• ECB Governing Council Member de Cos said that in consideration of inflation expectations for 2025 and 2026, if those estimates remain in place, the ECB could consider cutting rates in June

## Emerging Markets

• Minutes from the April BoK meeting revealed all six members expect the underlying slowing trend in inflation will continue, although oil and agricultural product prices are key uncertainties. Two members pointed out the increasing need for interest rate normalization, while another wanted to prioritize price stability even though adverse effects of prolonged high rates are evident. One member noted risks from both the ongoing tightening and easing of tightening have increased, while another noted there is no rush for a policy pivot. Members expect to cut rates once they are confident inflation/ inflation expectations will not be affected by supply shocks, and highlighted the need for easing to be combined with macroprudential policies

• BanRep reduced interest rates by 50bp and set the policy rate at 11.75% (C:

11.75%). In both the statement and the press conference, the board highlighted tighter external conditions were a reason for not accelerating the pace of cuts; this implied that an easing in these conditions could open the board to a quicker cutting pace

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
