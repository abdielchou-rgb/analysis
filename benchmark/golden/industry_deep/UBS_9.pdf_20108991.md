# China Economic Comment Data Preview: Mixed Picture in June, Slower Q2 Growth Momentum

## High frequency data: less bad property sales, robust trade, stable mfg PMI

June PMIs showed a mixed picture again, with NBS manufacturing (mfg) PMI staying soft at 49.5, while Caixin manufacturing PMI edged up further by 0.1ppt to 51.8. NBS non-manufacturing PMI weakened by 0.6ppt to a still >50 reading. High frequency data showed that property sales narrowed y/y contraction in June thanks to a low base, in particular with a notable uptick in the last week of June. Port activities stayed largely robust, including cargo throughput and container throughput. That said, y/y growth of auto retails & wholesales, FTL traffic index and subway passenger turnover slowed in June from May. Please see our UBS China Activity Tracker, latest China Weekly and the Appendix High Frequency Data Monitor for more details.

## Data preview: mixed picture again in June, slower Q2 GDP growth momentum

For the upcoming June data release, we expect smaller y/y decline in property sales on a low base, while its seasonally adjusted absolute level may have improved only slightly from May but still well below Q1 level. We see slower infrastructure FAI growth on a high base, robust manufacturing FAI growth, better retail sales growth (4.8%y/y) and export growth (8.5%y/y) both on a low base, and slower industrial production growth (4.5%y/y) on a high base. Credit growth likely edged down to 8.2-8.3%y/y. CPI inflation likely edged up (0.5%y/y) partly thanks to pork price rebound, and PPI likely turned less negative (-0.6%y/y) on a low base. Q2 average y/y growth of most domestic activities likely slowed from Q1, while export growth picked up. Therefore, we expect a much slower q/q sequential GDP growth in Q2, while a low base likely still underpinned a decent y/y GDP growth at around 5%.

## PMIs and high frequency data update

June NBS manufacturing PMI stayed soft, while Caixin PMI edged up. NBS manufacturing PMI stabilized at 49.5 in June, with slightly softer new orders and production by 0.1/0.2ppt to 49.5/50.6. New export orders stayed unchanged at 48.3. Raw material inventory and quantity of purchase both cooled by 0.2/1.2ppt to 47.6/48.1. Finished goods inventory turned less bad to 48.3. Input and output prices both softened by 5.2/2.5ppt to 51.7/47.9, indicating a bit cooler price momentum. Employment stayed weak at 48.1, reflecting lingering labor market pressure. Meanwhile, Caixin PMI edged up by 0.1ppt to 51.8, with better production index (+0.2ppt) but softer new orders (-0.4ppt) again. As we discussed before, such divergence between NBS and Caixin PMIs in Q2 is not uncommon, largely due to the difference in survey sample selection, while we may need other high frequency data to cross-check the underlying strength of the manufacturing sector (see China Weekly).

NBS non-mfg PMI weakened by 0.6ppt to 50.5, albeit staying >50 consecutively since January 2023. Services PMI edged down by 0.3ppt to 50.2, while construction PMI cooled further by 2.1ppt to 52.3 partly due to heavy rains in South China. By sector, PMIs of civil aviation, post, telecom / TV / satellite, monetary service, and insurance stayed above 55, while that of capital market and real estate sectors remained subdued.

Property sales narrowed y/y decline in June. Our UBS China Activity Tracker shows 30-city new property sales narrowed decline to -20%y/y in June from -38%y/y in May, with the last week of June 24-30 seeing notable improvements in both volume (partly on seasonality, higher than the level in end-May post policy easing) and y/y growth (-3%y/ y). Meanwhile, top 100 developers' contract sales volume saw a smaller contraction of -19%y/y in June (vs -32%y/y before). Secondary listing in tier 1 cities (from BEKE) declined slightly by 0.7%m/m in June, while secondary transaction volume in 12 cities increased notably by 20%y/y (see property note). We think the impact of recent policy easing on reviving home sales and price expectation may take some time, as the progress of property destocking (inventory purchase) has been limited so far.

Port activities stayed largely robust, auto sales growth weakened. FTL traffic index declined further to -5%y/y in the first 26 days from -2%y/y before. Subway passenger turnover growth decelerated to 6%y/y in June from 9%y/y before. Auto retail sales weakened to -12%y/y in the first 23 days from 10%y/y before despite a low base, so did auto wholesales growth (-9%y/y). Port cargo throughput growth moderated to 5% y/y in the first 23 days, while container throughput growth picked up to 11% y/y, indicating still robust trade activities. In the past week (June 24-30), net issuance of CGB slid to a decline of RMB 30bn, while that of special LGB picked up to RMB 123bn. See more in UBS China Activity Tracker.

# Data preview: mixed picture again in June, slower GDP growth momentum in Q2

For the upcoming June & Q2 data release, we expect:

June industrial production growth likely slowed to 4.5%y/y, partly on a high base. PMI readings showed a mixed picture again, with NBS PMI stabilizing at 49.5 but Caixin PMI edging up further by 0.1ppt to 51.8. In particular, production index under NBS PMI edged down by 0.2ppt, while that under Caixin PMI picked up by 0.2ppt. It suggests that sequential growth momentum of manufacturing sector likely changed little, but exports related production may have continued to stay robust. The operating rate of electric arc furnaces averaged 62% in June, +2ppt y/y (vs -1ppt previously). Steel production narrowed decline to -1%y/y in the first 20 days from -3%y/y previously. Operating rates of cement mills edged down to 46%, 1.2ppt below last year (vs -0.6ppt previously), while that of asphalt facilities fell slightly to 24%, 10ppt lower than last year (vs -6ppt previously). Full-Truck-Load traffic index growth softened to -5%y/y (vs -2%y/y previously). Overall, we expect June IP growth to have slowed to 4.5%y/y on a high base, while its 2019-24 CAGR growth likely improved from May.

Property sales likely narrowed y/y contraction on a low base. High frequency data showed 30-city new property sales narrowed decline to -20%y/y in June from -38%y/y in May, and top 100 developers' contract sales volume contracted by less to -19%y/y (vs -32%y/y before), both on a low base. 100-city local land transaction volume turned less bad to -23%y/y from -34%y/y previously, indicating subdued local government land sales revenue and future new starts. Overall, we expect national property sales growth likely improved to -10%y/y from -21%y/y previously thanks to a low base, with seasonally adjusted property sales volume improving only slightly from May but likely stayed well below Q1 level. New starts likely declined by another -18%y/y, while property investment likely stayed subdued at a decline of 10-11%y/y.

Overall FAI growth likely edged down to 3%y/y. Infrastructure investment growth likely cooled further to around 2%y/y in June on a high base. Although issuance of special LG bonds picked up in May and June, it only recorded RMB 859bn in Q2, which was still 85bn weaker than last Q2 following a much weaker Q1 (-723bn y/y). Local land sales revenue declined by -7%y/y in Q1 but slid notably to -24%y/y in Apr-May. LGFV financing also saw continued tight control, with net issuance of LGFV bonds seeing a decline of RMB 174bn in H1 2024. That said, infrastructure projects supported by RMB 1 trillion special CGB issued in Q4 2023 should start construction by end of June, a possible modest support for public investment in June. Meanwhile, manufacturing investment growth likely stayed robust at 8-9%y/y thanks to continued policy support (e.g. in equipment upgrading). Further considering continued weakness in property investment, overall FAI growth likely edged down to around 3%y/y (YTD 3.7%y/y).

Retail sales growth likely rebounded to 4.8%y/y on a low base. Auto retail sales growth slowed to -12%y/y in the first 23 days of June vs 10%y/y before, whilst auto wholesale growth also slid to -9%y/y vs 0%y/y previously. 18-city metro passenger turnover stayed elevated and its growth slowing to 6%y/y from 9%y/y previously. With a low base last year, we expect retail sales growth to have picked up to 4.8%y/y in June, and its 2019-24 CAGR likely improved as well.

Export growth likely improved further on a low base. New export orders in NBS PMI was unchanged at 48.3, while that of Caixin PMI cooled by 0.4ppt to a still robust reading of 50.6. US ISM PMI and Eurozone manufacturing PMI both weakened in June. Export growth of Korea moderated in June on a high base to 5.1%y/y from 11.5%y/y previously, while Vietnam export growth stayed robust at 10.5%y/y (vs 14%y/y before), indicating still decent momentum of global tech cycle. Meanwhile, Korea's exports to China cooled to 2%y/y from 8%y/y previously, while its imports from China also slowed to -7%y/y from -1%y/y previously. Overall, we expect China's export growth to have improved further to 8.5%y/y from 7.6%y/y before thanks to a low base, with a better 2019-24 CAGR as well. Imports may have edged up to 2.5%y/y (from 1.8% previously), leading to a slightly larger trade surplus of \$88bn.

CPI likely edged up to 0.5%y/y; PPI turned less negative. High frequency data showed a continued but slower sequential growth of producer prices in both NBS price basket and MOC price basket. Thus we assume sequential PPI to increase by 0.1%m/m, leading to another notably less negative PPI y/y growth of -0.6%y/y thanks to low base. Meanwhile, non-food price likely stabilized after previous decline in May, and gasoline price was cut twice in mid-May and mid-June before a hike in end-June. High frequency data suggests average food price saw a smaller m/m decline in MOC price basket, with pork price +15%m/m (20%y/y, vs 3% previously), vegetable prices -10%m/m (-15%y/ y), egg price 3%m/m (-7%y/y), while fruit prices -5%m/m (-2%y/y). Overall, we expect headline CPI to have edged up to 0.5%y/y in June.

Credit growth likely slowed. Regulators' recent efforts in cracking down on "idle circulation" of funds and statistical rule changes may continue to weigh on credit numbers for a while. Headline new RMB loans may have printed RMB 2.5 trillion, 550bn lower than a year ago but at par with 2021-22 average level. Meanwhile, net issuance of government bonds likely rebounded notably to around RMB 700bn, modestly higher than a year ago (+163bn y/y). Corporate bonds may have seen a slightly smaller net issuance of RMB 150-200bn than last year (-55bn y/y). Shadow credit may have recorded a small decline of 50bn, slightly less bad y/y. As such, overall new TSF likely printed RMB 3.6 trillion (-620bn y/y), leading to 0.2-0.3ppt uptick in both adjusted TSF and official headline TSF to 8.2-8.3%y/y. Credit impulse likely turned less negative to - 0.7% of GDP from -1.1% before.

FX reserves likely declined by around \$5bn. Capital outflow likely continued in June on depreciation pressure on RMB exchange rate. We estimate the valuation effect from major reserve currencies' movement has likely been a notable loss of \$15-20bn due to weaker JPY/EUR/GBP vs USD, especially the former. Rebound of global financial market may have contributed to a modest valuation gain. Taking other factors into account (slightly larger trade surplus and stale net FDI inflows), China's FX reserves may have declined by around \$5bn to \$3.227 trillion.

Q2 GDP growth likely slowed q/q notably, leading to 5.0-5.3%y/y thanks to a low base. Q2 industrial production growth likely averaged at 5.5%y/y (-0.6ppt from Q1), retail sales growth likely slowed to 3.7%y/y (-1ppt from Q1) in nominal terms and 3.8%y/y in real terms (-1.7ppt from Q1), while nominal FAI growth likely moderated to 3.3%y/y (-1.2ppt from Q1). On the other hand, nominal export growth likely picked up to 5.9%y/y (+4.4ppt from Q1), with a larger trade surplus at \$243bn (+60bn y/y, vs - 16bn y/y in Q1). Property sales may have narrowed their decline to -17%y/y (+3ppt from Q1) and new starts growth turned less negative to -18%y/y (+9ppt from Q1), while their absolute levels were likely still below Q1 levels. Thus, y/y growth of most domestic activities likely slowed from Q1, while export growth picked up. Therefore, we expect Q2 GDP growth likely slowed notably on a q/q sequential basis, while a low base last year may have still underpinned a decent y/y growth at around 5%.
