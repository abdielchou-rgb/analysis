## The world at a glance

 A staggering JPY carry trade unwind, as the Nikkei enters a bear market

● Investors start fretting about Big Tech’s AI spending; Nasdaq enters a correction

 The US jobs report was weak but not disastrous, and just one print. But…

 The Fed is likely to recalibrate, not panic and cut intra-meeting

 Making sense of it all

## It was just one print, but on the other hand…

• The jobs report was weaker than expected, and not just in the headline number

o There was a slowdown in job growth in most sectors, including services and government <sup>1</sup>

o Aggregate hours fell 0.3% m/m and the workweek dropped to 34.2 hours; AHE was also soft

o The jobless rate has now moved up 60bp this year, and 90bp from the cycle lows

• There are reasons to not overreact to one print, as the Chicago Fed’s Goolsbee noted last week

o The 3mma of payrolls is still a healthy 170k; payroll income is still rising 4% (3m saar)

o Hurricane Beryl might have impacted the number, leading to some bounce-back next month <sup>2</sup>

o ISM manufacturing has been misleading the whole cycle; last week’s weak print should be similar <sup>3</sup>

• But bonds’ outsized reaction on Friday shows investors are worried it is not ‘just one weaker data point’

o A number of the softer metrics on the labour market have softened for several months

o The quits and vacancy rates in JOLTS, ECI, NAHB small business survey have all pointed to a jobs slowdown

o That is why markets seem concerned that this might be an inflection point that pushes the US into recession

• Right now, we disagree. The macro fundamentals of the US (including in high frequency data) still seem solid

o Domestic final sales in Q2, the last retail sales print, personal income and spending have all been healthy

o The massive household wealth created (despite August’s drawdown) is still a tailwind for consumption

o Moreover, dollar weakness and the bond rally have eased FCI, despite the equity pull-back

## The Fed is likely to recalibrate, not pivot

• If the Fed had known about this report a week ago, they might have eased in July

o However, markets have to some extent done the Fed’s job; 10y yields rallied 40bp last week.

o Barkin and Goolsbee both cautioned against over-interpreting the July payroll report <sup>1</sup>

• The Sahm rule was triggered or came close last week (depending on if unrounded U3 is used or not)

o Chair Powell played it down, calling it ‘a statistical regularity….not an economic rule’

o The Fed will have two CPI reports and one more payroll report to digest before September FOMC

• Some Street forecasters have called for two consecutive 50bp cuts, with one even floating an intra-meeting cut

o An intra-meeting cut strikes us as highly irregular; short of an imminent financial crisis, it seems very unlikely

o Markets clearly believe in a 50bp September cut right now, with 44bp priced for the September FOMC <sup>2</sup>

o Options have a 20% chance of a 75bp cut in September, and a 25% chance of 150bp cuts by year-end

• We don’t expect Fed officials to push back on pricing this week, even if markets now have 128bp in cuts this year

o Rather, we think the Fed will depend on data to do that job; if the economy holds up as we (and it) expect

o We believe last week’s data will push the Fed to re-calibrate to a softer jobs market, not pivot aggressively

o The Fed won’t be completely immune to market volatility, but so far price action is not enough to act intrameeting

o We now expect the Fed to cut 25bp every meeting starting in September, with 75bp in cuts by year-end <sup>3</sup>

## Making sense of it all

• There seem to be three separate flows playing out in financial markets right now

o Worries about Big Tech AI spend that have pushed the Nasdaq into a correction <sup>1</sup>

o An extremely volatile unwind of FX carry trades, especially against the CNY and JPY <sup>2</sup>

o US bond yields rallying 87bp and 67bp in 2s and 10s from the start of July

• The biggest move by far is an unwind of the JPY carry trade; the move accelerated overnight

o USDJPY has moved an astonishing 9% from early August; USDCNH by 3.6% just last week

o The MXNJPY – a popular carry trade – has repriced 13% this month, leading to heavy unwinds

o The 87bp move in 2y US yields is not too far off the 130bp rally (5.07 to 3.77) during 2023’s bank crisis

• The bond and currency moves both seem outsized to us, at least if our economic outlook is not misplaced

o For example, absent a hard landing, it is difficult to justify US 10y yields below 4% <sup>3</sup>

o Similarly, China data have deteriorated recently; the recent CNY rally is not good news for Chinese exports <sup>4</sup>

• But given the scale of the recent shock, markets are unlikely to reprice quickly even if our view proves right

This week sees ISM services, European IP and Chinese inflation; all will be watched for signs of unexpected weakness

o We think markets will wait until the US CPI print next week, before last week’s ‘fear trade’ starts to dissipate

o But before that, markets will first want to see signs that forced carry trade unwinds are largely over

o We recommend staying in cash as we wait for stressed positions to clear and more clarity from the data
