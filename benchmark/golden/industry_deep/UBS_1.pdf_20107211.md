# APAC Economic Perspectives ASEAN: Monthly CPI read-throughs—Ready for central bank divergence?

## Inflation picks up at a faster than expected pace in February

CPI inflation came in hotter than expected in February. The acceleration was most evident in the Philippines (up 0.6ppt to 3.4% y/y), followed by Singapore (up 0.5ppt to 3.4% y/y), Thailand (deflation narrowed 0.3ppt to -0.8% y/y), Malaysia (up 0.3ppt to 1.8% y/y), then Indonesia (up 0.2ppt to 2.8% y/y). The rise in inflation was due to a combination of base effects, as well as ongoing food price increases (in Singapore, Philippines, and Indonesia) and fuel price increases (mainly in Philippines and Thailand). Core inflation was tamer, and is already at or below inflation targets/historical averages across the region, except for Singapore. Factoring in February's upside surprise, we raise our '24 inflation forecast for Philippines/Malaysia by 0.1/0.2ppt, to 3.7% and 2.4% respectively. We retain our inflation forecast for Indonesia at 3.0% and our Singapore MAS Core Inflation forecast at 3.1%, but lower our Singapore headline inflation forecast to 2.7% in 2024.

## Diverging inflation across ASEAN

On a y/y basis, inflation is likely to remain sticky in Philippines in the near term, but trend down in H2 as base effects turn more favourable. We warn of upside risks from electricity rate hikes in H2'24 as power generation costs are passed on to consumers. This was noted by the BSP in its latest monetary policy report, and at our recent macro tour. A 10% increase in electricity rates in H2 2024, could have a roughly +0.2ppt impact on inflation this year. In Thailand, we expect headline deflation to persist for 1-2 more months, until base effects (from a high base in H1 23) dissipate. Even so, amid slightly weaker labour market conditions (see our latest monthly pulse check here), core CPI barely rose in Jan-Feb. For Malaysia, we expect inflation to step up and exceed 3% in H2 2024, as we assume a \~20% increase in fuel prices from subsidy rationalization. In Singapore, easing wage growth and lower job vacancy rates may reduce the need for further monetary policy tightening, even if growth accelerates in H2 2024 on the back of a recovering manufacturing cycle. Finally, in Indonesia, core inflation has been soft, annualizing below 2% over the past year, amidst relatively tepid consumer expectations and modest consumer demand. We expect y/y inflation to keep within the 2.5–3.5% range in 2024.

## We expect BoT to recalibrate policy early, and a shallow rate cut cycle in ASEAN

1. BoT (Apr'24, 50bp in '24): We expect BoT to cut rates in Apr & Aug. An earlier recalibration of policy when economic activity is still sluggish, is not incongruent with price and financial stability. Indeed, it could forestall expectations of deeper rate cuts down the road, should economic activity take longer than expected to recover. That said, we expect a shallow easing cycle this year (-50bp), as growth picks up alongside accelerated fiscal disbursements after budget approval, as well as an ongoing tourism recovery.

2. BSP (Jun'24, 100bp in '24): Food inflation has remained sticky, largely due to elevated rice prices. Inflation is likely to breach the 4% threshold over Apr-Jul due to base effects, in line with BSP guidance. We expect BSP to start rate cuts in June alongside the Fed, even though inflation will still be slightly above 4% then. We highlight risks are skewed towards BSP delaying rate cuts until August, if upside risks to inflation materialize. Should rate cuts be pushed back to August, we expect BSP to lower rates only 75bp this year.

4. MAS (unchanged S\$NEER policy): As we had expected, January's price adjustments in 'sticky services prices' (e.g. outpatient services and food services) was softer than last year. Accordingly, we expect MAS to look through the February inflation uptick, which was partially driven by lunar new year seasonality, and keep S\$NEER policy unchanged at the April meeting.
