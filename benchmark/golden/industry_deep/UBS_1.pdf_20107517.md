# China Oil and Gas Sector

# Do China oil majors still offer value after rallying for two years?

## UBS raised oil price forecasts; we remain bullish on China oil majors

Since 2022, PetroChina-A/CNOOC-A/Sinopec-A shares have rallied 134%/150%/77%, and their H shares have risen 154%/214%/58%. Following the sizable rally, investor views on the Big-3 oil majors have diverged, but we reiterate our Buy ratings on China's Big-3 oil majors and raise our price targets (we published reports in 2023 -“Why do we remain bullish on Chinese oil majors?” and “Domestic oil companies' valuation potential—historical and global perspectives”. Our rationale for staying bullish now is: 1) UBS remains optimistic on oil prices in 2024. Seeing the oil market tightening further in 2024, UBS global oil & gas team raised 2024E Brent average price to US\$83/bbl and left 2025E/long-term Brent average price unchanged at US\$80/75/bbl, while also lowering 2024E TTF natural gas price to US\$9.7/mmBtu. 2) China oil majors’ H-share valuations remain attractive compared to overseas peers, especially US oil companies (the Big-3 trade at 6.9x 2024E PE and 0.8x 2024E P/BV on average, below the overseas peer average of 10.7x/1.8x and well below the US peer average of 15.7x/2.6x ). 3) China oil majors' results are strong. We expect PetroChina/CNOOC/Sinopec to post 7%/11%/17% net profit YoY growth for 2024, and think their Q124 results could beat market expectations; 4) High dividends. Sinopec/PetroChina/CNOOC’s 2023 dividend payouts were 75% (including buyback)/ 50%/44%; and 5) historically, Chinese oil companies have outperformed the market in a tough macro context.

## Oil majors: 2024-26E earnings revised; Q124 results likely to remain strong

Based on UBS's updated global oil and natgas price estimates, as well as our fundamentals forecasts for upstream, refining and chemicals, we revise our 2024/25/26E earnings projections for oil majors to Rmb137.3/140.9/143.9bn for CNOOC, Rmb172.2/171.2/174.7bn for PetroChina, and Rmb70.5/78.2/89bn for Sinopec. We expect PetroChina, CNOOC and Sinopec to post Q124 net profit of Rmb45/33/19bn, up 53%/26%/153% QoQ, slightly above consensus estimates.

## We still like China's oil majors

We continue to like China's Big-3 oil majors, but prefer the H shares over the A shares. Our order of preference is Sinopec-H, CNOOC-H and PetroChina-H. Sinopec sharply underperformed the other two over the past year, primarily on sluggishness in the refining and chemical sectors. We think the refining and chemicals sectors may improve in 2024, and Sinopec has the highest dividend yield (c.10% in 2024E) among the three. CNOOC catalysts: strong oil prices and strong oil and gas production growth. PetroChina catalysts: strong oil prices and strong results, though the realisation of domestic natgas prices needs monitoring.

## Q: Upstream: Will oil prices stay high and China oil majors record robust earnings in 2024?

Yes. We stay bullish on China's upstream oil companies in 2024. UBS expects the global oil market to remain marginally tight in 2024. In China, we expect domestic refined product demand growth to slow to 2-3% in 2024 after the post-Covid recovery. Yet we expect profitability for refined oil products to remain solid as 2024 could see high levels of maintenance and refineries’ utilisation rates could stay elevated (over 75%).

“Outlook 2024: Balance upstream/downstream; overweight chemicals," 12/08/2023

## Q: Natgas: How will prices retreating from high levels affect Chinese companies?

UBS expects a looser global natgas market in 2024, with TTF/Asian spot (JKM) likely to fall 26%/27% YoY to US\$9.70/US\$10.45/mmBtu. For oil companies, we think: 1) realised natgas prices for 2024 may decline YoY, but the reductions may be smaller than overseas cuts given China’s domestic natgas price adjustment mechanism; 2) their natgas import business losses may narrow, though price competition for domestic natgas sources may intensify.

## Q: Global oil & gas: When will oil demand peak?

UBS global oil & gas team expects global oil demand to peak at c.106Mb/d in 2029 and gradually decline over the 2030s. Despite seeing a sharp drop in road transport fuel demand due to the transition to electric mobility, UBS expects the overall oil demand decline to be much softer, mitigated by rising demand for petrochemical feedstocks, as well as jet fuel demand. UBS maintains a longterm oil price forecast of US\$75/bbl; lower demand in the 2030s does not mechanically mean lower oil prices as natural decline will still have to be offset.

“Global Oil Fundamentals — Tackling the big question: is peak oil in sight?” 03/04/2024

With share prices up 60%-200% since 2022, investor views on China's oil majors are diverging. We remain bullish given: 1) UBS remains optimistic on oil prices in 2024. 2) The Big-3’s H-share valuations remain attractive vs overseas oil companies, especially US oil companies; 3) China oil majors' strong results. Oil prices dropped YoY in 2023, but PetroChina net profit grew 8%, and though Sinopec/ CNOOC’s net profit fell 10-12% YoY, this was still much better than overseas peers (earnings declined 20-70% YoY, see Figure 27); and 4) high dividend payouts.

1) OPEC+ announced it would extend its 2.2Mb/d voluntary production cut agreement to end-Q224.
2) Sinopec/PetroChina/CNOOC payout ratios in 2023 were 75% (including buybacks)/50%/44%.

Though oil prices are a key indicator of oil companies‘ earnings and market attention, we think the stock prices of China's Big-3 oil majors are no longer driven solely by oil price movements, but also their financial strength and shareholder returns (see Figure 2): 1) Higher returns on capital. The Big-3’s ROE is up sharply since 2021 and remains at high levels, benefiting from oil price increases and robust operational cost controls. 2) Maintaining high dividend yields. Since 2014, the Big-3’s dividend payouts have stayed above 40% and we think they are set to focus even more on shareholder returns, given SOE reforms and the inclusion of market cap management in the central SOE's performance assessment. We expect Sinopec-H, PetroChina-H and CNOOC-H to post dividend yields of 9.7%, 6.9% and 6.8% for 2024.

Global oil & gas markets update

# Global oil & gas markets: strong oil vs. subdued gas

UBS recently raised its 2024 oil price forecast (see "Oil price update: faster tightening supports prices", Henri Patricot, 05 April 2024). UBS now expects 2024/2025 Brent prices at US\$83/80/bbl (vs prior estimates of US\$82/80/bbl) and long-term Brent at US\$75/bbl (unchanged), mainly driven by: 1) higher Q124 oil prices (up 13% YTD); and 2) an increase in UBS's Q224 forecast to US\$86/bbl from US\$84/bbl to reflect the sharp oil price rebound YTD and a tighter market. UBS believes that stronger demand growth than anticipated and more limited supply have led to a supply deficit in H124, while heightened geopolitical tensions and the decision by OPEC+ to extend voluntary cuts until at least the end of June have also supported sentiment.

UBS expects a larger oil market deficit in H124. UBS forecasts the oil market supply deficit to increase to 0.9Mb/d in Q224 (previously: 0.8Mb/d), as oil demand bounces back seasonally while supply growth remains limited. UBS has also raised its 2024 demand growth forecast to 1.4Mb/d from 1.2Mb/d on higher GDP growth (global GDP growth of 2.8% in 2024).

UBS sees risk to its forecasts as skewed to the upside in the near-term. The key uncertainty is around the OPEC+ meeting scheduled for 1 June. While UBS models a partial reversal of the OPEC+ cuts from July, the group may decide to further extend the existing production cuts. UBS has also not modelled a further drop in Russian production at this point. These two developments could lift Brent to over US\$90/bbl. On the downside, increased US supply and weaker oil demand could see prices return to UBS’s long-term price forecast of US\$75/bbl faster than expected. UBS sees a faster return of OPEC+ production as less likely at this point.

## China natgas market: 2024E demand to grow 7%-8% YoY

China’s natgas demand grew steadily in 2023, with annual demand up c.7.6% YoY. We expect YoY growth to remain at 7%-8% in 2024. By use case, in 2023 residential consumption rose steadily, transportation consumption grew rapidly (driven by increasing penetration of LNG heavy-duty trucks), and industrial consumption recovered. Supply-wise, we expect domestically produced gas, LNG imports and pipeline gas imports to grow by 6%, 12% and 12% respectively in 2024.

However, based on our expectation that natgas imports will see price declines in 2024, we believe the price competition for gas sources could intensify. SCI99 data shows that most downstream buyers would have increasing buying interest when the spot price of imported LNG is below US\$12/mmBtu. If the price of imported natgas falls in 2024, city gas operators may turn more proactive in seeking low-cost gas from overseas, and price competition for gas sources may intensify.
