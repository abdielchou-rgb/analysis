## February 15

USTs come off post-retail sales highs; Japan enters technical recession, JGBs rally; GBP resilience is despite UK technical recession; ACGBs rally after weak Australian jobs data; 10y BTP-Bund spread sub-150bp; ECB's Lagarde cautions on cuts; DXY at 104.28 (-0.4%); US 10y at 4.230% (-2.5bp).

• Softness in January US retail sales takes US Treasuries to session highs, but the rally gradually retraces, as the curve bull-flattens.

• Japan GDP contracts for the second consecutive quarter, marks technical recession. Our economists cite higher risk of a NIRP exit now in April; a broad rally in JGBs is led by the belly.

• GBP is resilient after UK GDP also contracts for the second consecutive quarter, a technical recession. Our economists view the economy as fragile and the outlook heavily dependent on BoE rate cuts.

• ACGBs stage a double-digit rally across the curve after Australia only adds 0.5k jobs in January (C: 25.0k; P: -62.7k) and the unemployment rate rises to 4.1%, the highest since May 2022.

• Although European rates end modestly weaker, the 10y BTP-Bund spread continues to tighten, below 150bp for the first time since April 2022.

• ECB President Lagarde warns against rate cuts that are too fast and says, “the last thing that I would want to see is us making a hasty decision to see inflation rise again and have to take more measures."

Disappointing data made its way westward with below-consensus January US headline retail sales at -0.8% m/m (C: -0.2%; P: 0.4%), while control sales fell by 0.4% m/m (C: 0.2%; P: 0.8%). Our economists note the role of seasonal factors but point to a broad slowing in consumption after the strength in 3Q23. Indeed, the Atlanta Fed’s 1Q24 US GDPNow estimate fell to 2.864% q/q (P: 3.425%).

The prevailing rally in US Treasuries extended to session highs but ultimately was faded as the bull-steepening from the Wednesday session likely discounted a weaker retail sales report. Overall, the slight long-end outperformance resulted in a mild bull-flattener ahead of the release of January PPI data.

Despite the unexpected growth contractions, both JPY (+0.4%) and GBP (+0.3%) appreciated against the US dollar, which was the top underperformer in the G10 against a broad rally in equities. AUD (+0.5%) shrugged off the weak labor market data and gained against the US dollar alongside other risk-sensitive currencies.

A further surge in the Russell 2000 (+2.45%) took the index above its closing level from Monday and easily filled the gap down which followed release of January CPI data. Corporate credit continued to trade very well as both investment grade (+52bp) and high yield (+342bp) CDX spreads rallied to new 52-week tights.

## Japan:

The combination of a retracement lower in UST yields and weak 4Q23 Japan GDP data led to a broad rally in JGBs. The belly of the curve, the recent underperformer, outperformed, but long-end JGBs lagged ahead of the liquidity tap auction for the 15.5-39y sector on Friday. 7y JGBs rallied by 3.5bp d/d to 0.455% on a simple yield basis while 30y JGBs only rallied by 0.5bp d/d to 1.800%. Market participants were increasingly concerned about weak 1Q24 Japan GDP data, as the Noto earthquake and temporary shutdown of the auto factory may weigh on private demand because of supply restrictions. Market participants seemingly find it increasingly difficult to price in a hawkish BoJ normalization path. TONA OIS rallied, led by the belly of the curve, alongside JGBs. XCCY basis tightened modestly across the curve.

Initially, weak 4Q23 Japan GDP data led to further JPY weakness early in the Tokyo morning, as market participants repriced the BoJ normalization path lower. However, JPY weakness was offset by very weak Australian employment data; AUD/JPY traded lower to 97.30, and USD/JPY ground lower toward 150.00 as USTs continued to retrace the sell-off into the London morning. There may have been repatriation-related activity by the domestic community related to coupon payments.

## Europe:

GBP (+0.2%) gained against USD, despite a technical recession in the UK in 2H23. CHF and SEK outperformed, as USD/CHF and USD/SEK ended the London session 0.6% lower. Lack of Swiss-specific catalysts suggests a partial reversal of CHF weakness from earlier in the week and sensitivity to US rates. The outsized move in SEK was likely driven by slightly more hawkish rhetoric from Riksbank members.

RX futures opened higher and traded in a 30c range for most of the London session.

In the London morning, European duration continued to retrace the post-US CPI sell-off from Tuesday. Data releases remained light in Europe and focus turned to US retail sales, which were below consensus. Treasuries rallied, but the move was short-lived and price action reversed toward the end of the London session, without a clear catalyst. 10y Bunds sold off 2.3bp at 2.36% but outperformed real rates despite a 2% rally in oil (10y breakevens: -1bp). The German ASW complex finished mixed, Schatz ASW cheapened over 1.5bp, while Buxl ASW richened slightly; boxes steepened. The rally in risk assets gained momentum and supported peripheral spreads; the 10y BTP-Bund and 10y Bono-Bund spreads tightened by 2bp and 1bp, respectively.

## Dollar Bloc:

Despite various local catalysts across G10 countries, price action in London hours was largely dictated by the US dollar, with very few deviations from the DXY USD Index. The DXY USD Index (-0.3%) ended the London session lower, driven by lower US yields and below-consensus US retail sales data.

Soft Australian labor market data barely held back AUD (+0.3%), which gained against USD alongside a rally in equities. JPY (+0.4%) gained against USD by London close, despite an unexpected second consecutive quarterly contraction in Japan GDP, which marked a technical recession. JPY strength was more likely attributed to weakness in the US dollar.

## Emerging Markets

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended mostly stronger amid broad US dollar strength. MXN (+0.3%) and COP (+0.2%) ended higher with few local drivers, while CLP (-1.0%) weakened following release of minutes from the January BCCh meeting. The minutes stressed the need to increase the pace of cuts, as inflation has converged quicker-than-expected. This helped the CAM curve outperform peers; the 1y ended 16bp lower. The IBR curve rallied, following growth in Colombia well below consensus expectations (10y: -11bp); DIs ended the session flat. TIIEs had gains concentrated in the long-end (10y: -5bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Riksbank Governor Thedéen claimed while “inflation has fallen and is on firmer ground” risks remain that could cause inflation to rise again and as a result any rate cuts must “be made with caution”

• Riksbank Deputy Governor Breman discussed the currency, geopolitics, and pricing behavior as upside risks to inflation

• ECB President Lagarde warned against moving too fast with rate cuts and said “the last thing that I would want to see is us making a hasty decision to see inflation rise again and have to take more measures […] we do not have enough evidence yet to have the level of confidence that we are going to hit our medium-term 2% target and that it will be sustainably there”

• ECB Governing Council Member de Cos said “the confidence that inflation will continue to slow and converge to the 2% target is what makes us in the Government Council think that the next movement in interest rates will be a cut,” he added “we are not being explicit about when it will occur, I think there is still some time left for that”

• BoE External Member Greene said she needs further signs of price pressures cooling in the UK before she can consider rate cuts. She referenced the relative strength of wage growth and services inflation, compared to international peers

• BoE MPC Member Mann said despite easing price pressures there is still “a long way to go.” She cited services inflation, as still too strong for the BoE’s 2% price growth target and wage growth that is too high

• The Federal Reserve released hypothetical scenarios for its annual stress test. 32 banks will be tested against a severe global recession with heightened stress in both commercial and residential real estate markets, as well as in corporate debt markets. In the 2024 stress test scenario, the US unemployment rate rises to a peak of 10%, house prices fall by 36%, and commercial real estate prices decline by 40%. Elements of a new “exploratory analysis” include funding stresses that cause a rapid repricing of a large proportion of deposits at large banks as well as the hypothetical failure of five large hedge funds applied to the largest banks

## Emerging Markets

• Minutes from the January BCCh meeting continued the tone from the statement and noted inflation has converged faster than previously thought. Because of this, the minutes stated the board deems it necessary to increase the pace of cuts going forward. At the January meeting, the largest reason they did not cut by more was to avoid volatility by surprising the market. One member even discussed possibility of a 150bp cut in January. There was minimum discussion on FX implications, which suggests the board is more comfortable with FX weakness than previously thought. Markets have priced in another 100bp cut for the April meeting

## Economic Release Notes

## Developed Markets

• In Japan, GDP unexpectedly contracted by 0.4% q/q in 4Q23 (C: 1.1%; P: - 3.3%), the second consecutive quarter of contraction. Domestic demand remained weak, especially in real private consumption (-0.2% q/q) and private capex (-0.1% q/q). Although the private capex estimate could be adjusted upward in the revised GDP report on March 11, which will reflect the MoF’s corporate stats from March 4. Real consumption of durables was solid, but other categories (semi-durables, non-durables, and services) declined q/q. Weakness in consumption of non-durables on a real (volume) basis may reflect the adverse impact from high prices

• In Australia, Jobs rose by 0.5k in January (C: 25.0k; P: -62.7k), as negligible growth in jobs and a flat Participation Rate at 66.9% (C: 66.8%) drove the Unemployment Rate up to 4.1% (C: 4.0%; P: 3.9%). The softness was influenced by workers waiting to start jobs and higher annual leave, which implies a rebound in February. Our economists do not change their expected RBA policy path in 2024

• In the UK, GDP contracted by 0.3% q/q in 4Q23 (C: -0.1%; P: -0.1%) to mark a technical recession. The economy has effectively been flat for 2 years and had a meaningful correction in household spending in 2H23, even as real wage growth turned positive. Our economists expect a modest pick-up in growth in 1H24 but view the economy as fragile and outlook heavily dependent on BoE rate cuts, which our economists expect in May

• In Spain, final January Headline CPI came in at 3.4% y/y (C: 3.4%; December: 3.1%)

• In the US, Headline Retail Sales fell by 0.8% m/m in January (C: -0.2%; P: 0.4%), with downward revisions to the two prior months. Our economists note the consumption path in 4Q23 into 1Q24 now appears softer as seasonal factors exaggerated 4Q23 strength and weakness in January spending. Control Group Sales fell by 0.4% m/m in January (C: 0.2%; P: 0.8%), but dining out remained solid. Building materials were deeply negative due to colder weather. Overall, there has been a further slowing in consumption after the strength in 3Q23

• In the US, the Philadelphia Fed Manufacturing Index rose to 5.2 in February (C: -8.1; P: -10.6), but the details were softer. On an ISM-adjusted basis, the Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index rose to 45.2 (P: 41.9). The Empire State Manufacturing Index rose to -2.4 in February (C: -12.5; P: -43.7) and remained very volatile

• In the US, Industrial Production fell by 0.1% m/m in January (C: 0.2%; P: 0.0%), despite a boost from utilities output in a colder January. Manufacturing Production fell by 0.5% m/m in January (C: 0.0%; P: 0.1%), with manufacturing (ex-autos) down by 0.6% m/m. Amid continued weakness in the factory sector, non-auto manufacturing output has fallen 1.3% over the past twelve months. Business equipment output continued to fall at its recent pace (-0.2% m/m) while consumer goods accelerated a bit in the past three months

• In the US, Initial Jobless Claims fell to 212k in the week ended February 10 (C: 220k; P: 220k) and remained in-line with the average over the past four months. Continuing Claims rose 1895k in the week ended February 3 (C: 1880k; P: 1865k) as the mild uptrend continued

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
