# Global Macro Commentary | Global

# February 26

USTs end mildly cheaper in supply-laden session; European duration grinds cheaper, ECB's Lagarde cautions on inflation; EUR/USD rises as US-Europe yield differentials tighten; KRW is muted, despite incentive plan; 10y China yields hit new local low; DXY at 103.79 (-0.1%); US 10y at 4.280% (+3.1bp).

• USTs mildly bear-flatten through record 2y and 5y auction sizes and a continuation of heavy high-grade corporate supply, which sets a February record; UST swap spreads tighten.

• European duration grinds cheaper throughout the London session amid a cautionary tone from ECB President Lagarde, who stresses "we are not there yet" on inflation.

• Tighter US and European yield differentials support a rise in EUR/ USD (+0.3%) to 1.085.

• COP (-0.9%) leads losses among LatAm peers as copper prices fall over 1.5%.

• KRW finishes little changed against USD after the highly anticipated “Corporate Value-Up Program” contains scant details.

• 10y CGB yields fall 2bp to a new low of 2.38% despite an announcement from President Xi on Friday that China will use fiscal policy to promote equipment renewals and trade-in of consumer goods.

## Europe:

Price action across G10 currencies was mixed during London hours amidst a light economic calendar. SEK (+0.4%) outperformed against the US dollar as front-end rates in Sweden continued higher, despite a lack of local catalysts. EUR (+0.2%) strength was driven by tighter yield differentials between the US and Europe, exhibited by \~3bp widening in the 10y UST-Bund spread. NOK (+0.1%) only advanced mildly against the US dollar as oil prices reversed a large part of the selloff from Friday. CHF held firm against the US dollar.

European duration traded heavily during the London session. Hawkish comments from ECB Governing Council Member Stournaras and ECB President Lagarde, who warned “we are not there yet” on inflation, likely contributed to the sell-off. 10y Bund yields closed the London session 8bp higher at 2.44%. In swaps, initially 10s30s bear-steepened but reversed mid-session and the 10y point underperformed (5s10s30s butterfly: +1bp; 10s30s: -0.8bp). 2s10s bear-steepened throughout the session and closed 2bp steeper. Core ASW traded sideways and closed slightly cheaper. The Bund-Buxl ASW box flattened by \~1bp ahead of the auction of 2050 German bonds and the 30y French syndication on Tuesday. Semi-core and peripheral spreads widened from local lows, alongside an equity sell-off and wider credit spreads (10y BTP-Bund spread: +1.5bp).

## Dollar Bloc:

The Antipodean currencies both were 0.4% weaker against the US dollar; AUD was impacted by equity weakness, while NZD weakened, as market participants pared the likelihood of another hike by the RBNZ on Wednesday. CAD (-0.1%) was modestly weaker. Wider US-Japan yield differentials sent USD/JPY (+0.2%) higher. The DXY USD Index (-0.1%) finished the London session lower, despite the rise in UST yields throughout the session.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies generally maintained stability and traded range bound. CNH remained largely stable \~7.2085, even as the local equity market underperformed (CSI 300: - 1.0%). Initially, KRW weakened by 0.1% after the highly anticipated “Corporate

Value-Up Program” contained scant details but then stabilized, and USD/KRW finished the AxJ session flat at \~1,331. IDR underperformed, down 0.2%, amid weakness in local bond and equity markets. PHP fell 0.3% alongside weakness in the local equity market. THB outperformed amid a local holiday, 0.3% stronger, alongside the decline in US yields.

AxJ rates rallied broadly and curves bull-flattened, in line with the move in USTs on Friday. Singapore and Hong Kong rates outperformed given their higher correlation with US rates; 10y swap yields fell by 10bp in both markets. In South Korea, 10y swap yields fell by a modest 7bp. In China, 10y CGB yields fell another 2bp to a new low of 2.38% despite an announcement from President Xi on Friday that China will use fiscal policy to promote equipment renewals and trade-in of consumer goods. India 10y G-Sec yields fell by 3bp as states intend to sell less debt this week; the decline in crude oil prices also provided support. Indonesia relatively underperformed, as the 10y INDOGB yield remained stable.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies finished the session mixed. CLP (-0.9%) led losses as copper prices fell over 1.5%. COP (+0.2%) and MXN (+0.1%) strengthened marginally, while BRL (+0.3%) outperformed peers amid few local drivers. LatAm rates moved higher, mostly driven by the sell-off in core rates. TIIEs underperformed peers, with losses concentrated in the belly (3y: +11bp); DIs sold off by \~7bp across the curve. The CAM curve ended 9bp higher at the 2y tenor, while IBRs sold off and bearsteepened slightly (2s10s: +2bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• ECB Governing Council Member Stournaras said inflation decelerated “faster than our December projections and it is very likely that we will closely approach our 2% inflation target in the autumn of the current year.” He said the “optimal timing” for the first cut may be the end of the first semester

## Emerging Markets

supporting economic activity. The interest rate path will be determined in accordance with the continued convergence of inflation to its target, continued stability in the financial markets, economic activity, and fiscal policy”

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
