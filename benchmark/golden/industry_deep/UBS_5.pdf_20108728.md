# China Equity Strategy

# CFO survey - upstream seem the most upbeat

## A more subdued top down outlook with upstream sectors the most upbeat

UBS Evidence Lab conducted the 13th wave of the China C-Suite Business Survey of over 400 senior executives responsible for decision making within the company they work during 18 Apr - 14 May 2024. In general respondents are slightly more downbeat compared to the previous wave conducted in April 2023. On a net basis 69% of companies expect a YoY revenue growth in 2H24, lower than 84% in the previous wave. 27% of companies on a net basis still expect margin improvement, though this is lower than the 59% in the previous wave. On a more positive note, over half of the respondents expect ROE improvement with more than 50% of the respondents seeing better returns on new investment vs existing ones. In terms of spending, R&D remains a key focus for companies while more emphasis has been placed on greenfield investments and advertising this year. In terms of sector trends, materials and energy companies seemed to have had the most improvement in outlook while consumer discretionary and financials saw the most deterioration.

## Upstream sector outlook looks better than consumer and property

On an absolute level, taking account of the sector outlook on revenue, margin, competition, pricing power and dividend, energy, healthcare and industrials ranked the best while property, consumer discretionary and construction ranked the lowest. Relative to last year, materials and energy showed the most improvement on the composite measure while financials and consumer discretionary showed the most deterioration. Stronger pricing power and robust customer demand are some of the reasons for the positive outlook for energy and industrials. For consumer discretionary (e.g. white goods, consumer electronics, autos), the weaker outlook seems to be driven by higher labour costs and intensifying competition. Historically the Evidence Lab survey respondents in the financials and energy sectors provided the best proxy for listed companies margins while healthcare had the least read-through (possibly due to heterogeneity in the companies).

## Potential impact from upcoming US tariff hike

Over 40% of respondents believe that the US tariff would fall between 30-60% on all Chinese goods in 2025. To mitigate this impact, expanding non-US export markets, relocating supply chain and converting exports into domestic sales are the preferred measures by the respondents. There are also a quarter of respondents that indicate an intention to exit their US business if the impact is too significant. Export business is expected to decline by 17% on average if a 60% tariff is imposed, with electric equipment and consumer discretionary including home appliances, auto & parts and consumer electronics seemingly the most impacted sector. Compared with last year, there appeared to be a visible pick-up in manufacturers looking to move production overseas with hardware tech and energy expecting the most relocations.

## Implication for sector allocation

The survey result suggests a lukewarm top down picture though we think this is broadly reflected in the market valuations with MSCI China trading at 9.6x 12-mth forward P/E, 1.2 std. dev below historical average. The survey result broadly reaffirms our recent sector change to remove most of the consumer stocks and focus on high dividend names as well as internet, property, education, semi-equipment and outbound travel for beta options (see note). We remain somewhat wary of the export oriented sectors given the potential sentiment and earnings impact from tariffs.

## Top down survey results

The key top down conclusions from the CFO survey included:

 A more subdued revenue outlook: 69% of companies expect a YoY growth in 2H24 vs 84% in the previous wave. Meanwhile 10% of companies expect a YoY revenue decline.

 Slower net margin expansion though industry competition is slightly moderated: on a net basis there are still 27% of companies expecting margin improvement in 2H24 however the proportion dropped from 59% in the previous wave. 60% of respondents expect ROE to increase in 2024 vs 2023.

 Slower capex growth: 50% of companies on a net basis expect a higher capex spending in 2H24 which is on par with historical average, though lower than 63% in last year;

 R&D remains a top priority item for capex spend: followed by restocking and equipment upgrade, while staffing (i.e. hiring, training and incentives etc) ranked the lowest in the list;

 More emphasis on advertising vs 2023 survey:　though the top spending priorities remain on market expansion, channel & distribution and sales growth.

## Sector survey results

In the following section, we attempt to glean some of the secular trends as identified by the CFO survey responses. We note that due to sample size, we have grouped some of the sub-sectors to get a more credible representation of overall sector trends. The key highlights include:

 Overall best and worst outlook: on a composite basis, energy, healthcare and industrials ranked the best in terms of overall outlook while property, consumer discretionary and construction ranked the worst;

 Most and least improvement: Materials and energy showed the most improvement in terms of the business outlook in the next 6 months while financials and consumer discretionary showed the most deterioration vs last year.

 The contributors to these varying outlooks cited by the respondents included:

 Energy: benefiting from lower raw materials and interest cost, as well as less intense competition and stronger downstream demand;

 Industrials: expecting stronger pricing power alongside robust end-customer demand;

 Software and internet: lower R&D expenditure and strong user demand;

 Consumer discretionary: citing higher labour costs and intensifying competition;

 Property: most pessimistic outlook on revenue, margin and competition among all sectors

In terms of the predictive power of the UBS Evidence Lab survey by sector, it would seem that historically participants in the financials and energy sectors provided the most credible margin outlook for listed companies while healthcare and internet were the least relevant, possibly due to the differences in homogeneity of the listed companies vs those being sampled;

 The above aggregates certain industries in order to get a larger sample size per industry. For a more granular cut of industry data, some of the highlights include:

 Capex spend: Upstream, education, software internet are more likely to raise their capex spending this year compared to 2023 level while auto, consumer electronics and furniture companies are more reluctant to spend on capex;

 Revenue growth: Most sectors expect to see a YoY revenue improvement in 2H24 though outlook for electric equipment, property and AI related sectors appear the weakest;

 Margin: Upstream sectors, semi and education in general expect a net margin expansion in 2H24 while consumer sectors including auto, furniture, staples and home appliances are less confident to maintain the net margin from current level;

Competition: Net 40% of the companies surveyed expect more industry competition in 2H24 however some also indicate a resilient pricing power including machinery, materials and textiles & clothing.

## Assessment of US tariff impact on Chinese companies

The following set out answers to question relating to a potential 60% US tariff:

 Upcoming US tariff would likely fall between 30-60% with most respondents (>40%) chose this category however only c.10% believes the tariff could go beyond 60%;

 Expanding non-US export markets, relocating supply chain and converting exports into domestic sales are the three most popular in response to a potential 60% tariff;

 Business closure is also highlighted by a quarter of respondents if impact is too significant;

 Respondents expect a 17% decline in export should a blanket 60% tariff is imposed, with electric equipment and consumer discretionary including home appliances, auto & parts and consumer electronics seemed to be the most impacted sectors;

 Fewer companies on a net basis indicate a negative impact on business operation from tighter tech restrictions perhaps due to the fact that companies have adapted to new norms over the years;

 Sector-wise industrials, materials and software & internet are the sectors most disrupted from tech restrictions, while health care and property being least impacted;

 More companies have currently set up production sites outside APAC (in particular Americas and Europe) in the past year;

 On average 45% of production or capex currently occurs overseas, on par with the highest level seen in April 2021.

 Sectors with higher overseas exposures including healthcare and tech hardware have a higher % of capex spending or production outside mainland China currently;

 Notably a higher intention of exporters to move production overseas this year in particular the hardware tech and energy sectors.

## Valuation Method and Risk Statement

For various stocks across the industries we cover in the Hong Kong and mainland China stock markets, we use a variety of valuation approaches, including DCF models, Gordon growth model analysis and relative valuation analysis using various multiples such as PE, EV/EBITDA and P/BV.

We think the risks facing China's equities include a hard landing for the property market, a capital exodus associated with currency depreciation and slow structural reform progress. In our view, any government policies that do not adequately address these risks could result in a shock to the market. For example, an excess of stimulus policies could pose a risk to the transition from an investment-driven to a consumption-driven economy and increase the debt of government and state-owned enterprises.
