## March 20

FOMC keeps median projection for 3 cuts in 2024; Powell says appropriate to slow run-off pace "fairly soon"; Risk premia for 2024 cuts rises, USTs twist-steepen; S&P 500 at new ATH; USD, JPY weaken against risk currencies; UK CPI eases, gilts rally; DXY at 103.40 (-0.4%); US 10y at 4.273% (-2.0bp).

• FOMC maintains the fed funds target range at 5.25-5.50% and maintains a median projection of three 25bp rate cuts for 2024 in the updated Summary of Economic Projections.

• Fed Chair Powell acknowledges discussion on slowing the pace of run-off and states "the general sense of the committee is that it will be appropriate to slow the pace of run-off fairly soon."

The median growth projections were marked higher throughout the forecast horizon, to 2.1% in 2024 (P: 1.4%), 2.0% in 2025 (P: 1.8%), and 2.0% in 2026 (P: 1.9%). The median unemployment rate projection was marked down to 4.0% in 2024, maintained at 4.1% in 2025, and marked down to 4.0% in 2026. In fact, five fewer participants viewed risks to the unemployment rate as weighted to the upside than in December.

The median core PCE inflation projection was marked up to 2.6% in 2024 (P: 2.4%) but was maintained at 2.2% in 2025 and 2.0% in 2026. Three additional participants now viewed risks to core PCE inflation as weighed to the upside than in December.

A main focus of Fed Chair Powell’s press conference was on the balance sheet. Powell confirmed that in-depth discussions on slowing the pace of balance sheet run-off had taken place, but a decision was not made on the size of the taper, although he guided that the general sense on the Committee is that it will be appropriate to slow the pace of run-off “fairly soon.”

In response to a question that asked if “fairly soon” meant a decision could be reached as soon as May, Powell responded “That is the idea. That is what we're looking at. In terms of the timing, I would say "fairly soon." I don't want to be more specific than that. You get the idea.”

We believe this timeline aligns with our view for a taper to be announced in May and for a June start, with the end of QT not until 1Q25. Powell was careful to make a distinction that slowing the pace of run-off does not mean stopping QT. In fact, he pointed out the irony in, by going slower they can get further in their run-off process because it allows for a smoother distribution of reserves across the banking system, which reduces the likelihood of a premature end.

Powell made it clear money market conditions and the distribution of reserves will determine to the Fed when to stop QT. He confirmed where money market rates are trading relative to administered rates (RRP and IORB) will guide the Fed’s thinking on QT, not a specific dollar amount of reserves or percentage of GDP.

We look for additional details on QT in the March minutes, but continue to view the following indicators as signposts for assessing the progress and impact of QT: (1) SOFR-IORB spread; (2) Sensitivity of money market rates to changes in reserves; (3) Usage of the Standing Repo Facility (SRF); and (4) Distribution of reserves and sources of bank funding.

As QT continues to progress, we believe risks to funding markets could emerge in 2H24 once the RRP is depleted, with reserves then expected to decline in line with the pace of asset run-off. Even though Powell characterized reserves as currently “ample or even abundant,” he cautioned that may not be the case for individual banks. The composition and maturity distribution of the balance sheet remain "longer-run" topics of discussion.

A rally in front-end USTs (2y: 4.60%, -8.1bp d/d) drove a considerable twiststeepening of the UST curve as markets incorporated more risk premia for rate cuts this year. The long end (30y: 4.45%, +1.2bp d/d) lagged, in tune with the rise in longrun dot, which led to 6.3bp d/d steepening in the 5s30s curve to 20.5bp.

The S&P 500 (+0.89%) surged to new all-time highs – led by Cyclicals – as Powell leaned on supply-side improvements and said, "in and of itself strong job growth is not a reason for us to be concerned about inflation."

Risk-sensitive currencies led against a broadly weaker USD and JPY, yet JPY pared losses after a media report suggested currency weakness may spur a hike from the BoJ in July or October.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar displayed broad-based weakness after the FOMC painted an optimistic picture about the US economic outlook while Fed Chair Powell reiterated the Committee expects to cut rates at some point this year. The DXY USD Index fell 0.3% and traded below 104.00 in the NY afternoon. JPY underperformed as the only currency that weakened against the US dollar. However, a media report which suggested the possibility of a BoJ rate hike in July or October reversed some of the intra-session JPY weakness. A rally in US equities supported risk-sensitive currencies, with NOK and AUD among the best performers.

## Emerging Markets

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mixed. CLP (-0.2%) ended slightly weaker and recovered from losses of as much as 1.7% during the session. BRL (+1.2%) and MXN (+0.8%) extended gains after the FOMC meeting. In rates, TIIEs followed USTs lower, with gains concentrated in the belly (3y: -10bp). DIs followed suit ahead of the BCB monetary policy meeting. The IBR and CAM curves ended largely unchanged but closed before the FOMC meeting.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Summary of Deliberations from the January BoC Meeting revealed the Governing Council agreed the current stance of monetary policy was likely “sufficiently restrictive” to achieve the Bank’s 2% inflation target. The Governing Council noted future policy discussions would likely shift towards “how long” to maintain the current policy rate to ensure the disinflationary process. However, the Governing Council recognized based on the current information available that “it was difficult to foresee when it would be appropriate to begin cutting interest rates.” The Council stated with inflation still too high, members want to be clear in their communications that they were still concerned about the underlying persistence of inflation

• The FOMC statement was unchanged and the summary of economic projections showed a stronger supply side story, with growth revised up 70bp to 2.1% this year while inflation was only revised up 20bp, to 2.6% 4q/4q. Despite upward revisions to growth and inflation, the median participant still expects three cuts this year and the range on 2024 projections narrowed to 4.4-5.4% in March (December: 3.9-5.4%). Overall, the Fed is capturing the supply side story in their projections

## Emerging Markets

• CNB remained cautious despite inflation at target and cut its key policy rate by 50bp, to 5.75% (C: 5.75%). CZK weakness and concerns about core inflation stickiness likely continue to stop the CNB from delivering larger rate cuts. Our economists continue to expect a steady pace of 50bp rate cuts until November

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
