# APAC Economic Perspectives

# Korea: Growth improvement vs. Re-emerging risk of external challenges

## GDP growth rebounds in 1Q, export momentum seems to last into early 2Q

Korea registered a significant growth rebound in 1Q with GDP expanding 1.3%q/q sa, well ahead of market consensus expectation. Real private consumption showed an uptick, exports continued to be the biggest driver in the economy. The latest first 20 day export data in April seemed to suggest that export momentum remained solid in early 2Q with support from strong semiconductor exports and an uptick in auto exports (Figure 3&4). Strong growth so far supports our recent upgrade of Korea's GDP growth, with private consumption and non-tech exports being key areas to monitor for growth ahead.

## A better inflation and growth combination to offer policy flexibility

Inflation has continued to moderate. Despite a gradual pace, core inflation has maintained a moderating trend, reaching 2.42% in March. Therefore, the gap between policy interest rate and core inflation has widened to the largest since June 2019 - which was the start of the previous rate cut cycle from the Bank of Korea (Figure 6). The growth improvement and moderating inflation may offer the BoK and other policy makers some room to adjust for other issues faced by the economy.

## Risk of external challenges re-emerging

The interest rate differential between US and Korea government bonds has widened further recently given the significant changes of market expectation of US monetary policy. At the same time, the risk of high oil price seems to have increased. The KRW experienced a wave of fast depreciation recently against a backdrop of strong USD DXY index. The combination of high US interest rate, widening interest differential between US and Korea, high oil price and KRW deprecation brought a challenging environment for Korea's economy between 3Q22 and 4Q22; the risk of such a combination seems to be re-emerging recently. Our earlier studies showed that larger KRW depreciation thus higher import price inflation, more transmission from international oil price to domestic inflation are factors driving higher inflation in Korea vs. its peer in North Asia - Taiwan.

## Another delay of BoK rate cut

Given such recent experience and improvement in Korea's current business cycle position, we believe the BoK will take a cautious step towards the combination of heightening risk of higher US interest rate for longer and high oil price. We therefore remove our expectation of a July BoK interest rate cut, now projecting the BoK to only conduct 1 interest rate cut in October, taking year-end policy rate to 3.25% vs. our previous expectation of 3.0%. Further changes in external monetary policy and inflation environment could bring the risk of further delay of BoK rate cut. At the same time, overall domestic stability consideration (e.g. financial stability) may bring risk of faster rate cut if external risk is deemed manageable. We also adjust year-end KRW projection to 1330 (from 1280), recalibrating it to reflect recent shifts of overall macro condition.

Korea’s GDP expanded by 1.3% q/q sa, 3.4%y/y in 1Q24, the strongest sequential expansion since 4Q22. Such growth performance had surprised the market consensus (of a 0.6% sequential expansion) to the upside. Real private consumption registered strongest sequential expansion since 4Q22, moderating inflation and improvements of trade and industrial cycle positions might be supporting private consumption. Export continued to be the biggest driver behind the economy.

Data from the most recent weeks in 2Q continued to point to strong export growth. For the first 20 days in April, daily average export shipment grew by 11.1%y/y vs. 9%y/ y in 1Q24. Tech exports stayed on the upswing to support overall export growth with more than 42%y/y daily average shipment growth in semiconductor exports, and auto exports seemed showing an uptick too. That said, consumer sentiment has remained soft in April after the rebound in Jan-Feb.

## We recently upgraded 2024 GDP

growth projection to 2.3%, above consensus and the central bank’s projection (2.1%) for the first time in 2 years. The strong overall growth performance in 1Q24 and robust export growth into April supports our projection. Further developments in private consumption and non-tech export are key areas to monitor for GDP growth ahead.

Inflation has continued to moderate. Core inflation has softened further to 2.42%y/y in March. Despite it being gradual, the moderating trend of core inflation seemed clear over the past quarters after peaking in 4Q22. As a result, the gap between policy interest rate and the core inflation has widened further to 108 bps, the widest since June 2019 - when the Bank of Korea (BoK) was about to commence its rate cut cycle.

There have been some fluctuations in headline inflation between months, though those fluctuations seemed to have not disrupted the moderating trend of core inflation so far. Headline CPI inflation still managed to moderate to 3% in 1Q24 from 3.4% in 4Q23.

The current growth and inflation combination is visibly better than previous quarters, and may offer some room and flexibility for the BoK and other policy makers to adjust for other issues in the economy.

However, the KRW has recently experienced a wave of fast depreciation recently, against the backdrop of strong USD DXY index and depreciation of many other currencies against the USD. As market expectation shifted significantly for a delayed US monetary policy easing, the interest rate differential between US and Korea 10-year government bond widened significantly recently to the widest level of recent history. At the same time, oil price seemed to pose risk of a more persistent increase.

The combination of high US interest rate, widening interest differential between US and Korea, high oil price and KRW deprecation brought a challenging environment for Korea's economy between 3Q22 and 4Q22. That said, Korea's current overall business cycle position shall be stronger than that in 2H22, with lower inflation, improvements in export and overall growth, stronger trade balance etc.

Korea imports approximately 1bn barrels of crude oil per year. While it is a net exporter of oil refined products, the size of such net export is much smaller compared to crude oil imports.

High oil price would push up the fuel-related trade deficit, and the risk of an overall trade deficit. In the recent episode of high oil price in 2022, Korea registered overall trade balance. With the strong tech cycle now, Korea may still have non-fuel trade balance to abort the fuelrelated trade deficit. But persistently high oil price would result in a a much narrower overall trade balance, reflecting part of the direct cost to the economy.

In the recent inflation acceleration episode in 2022, we observed that Korea's inflation went up much more than peers in North Asia, for example, Taiwan. Our earlier studies showed that one of the key reasons behind such difference is the larger transmission between international energy price increases to domestic inflation. For example, Korea's utility CPI registered around 12%y/y inflation in 2022 and Taiwan only registered a 3% utility inflation. Larger currency depreciation was a also a factor behind higher inflation, as reflected by the much higher import price inflation.
