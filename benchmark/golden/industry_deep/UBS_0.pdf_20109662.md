# Global Rates Strategy

# Rates Map: Our front-end views after US employment and BoE cut

## United States - Summer Fridays are prone to overshooting

 We think the market is now running well ahead of the data, and look to take profits on front end positions like being long 3yrs. Fed funds is now priced for 122bps of rate cuts in three months (starting in September).

 In the past four recessions, in the first three months there were 25bps of cuts in the 1990 cycle, 150bps in the 2000 cycle, 75bps in 2007, and 50bps in 2019 (Figure 3 ). While there is also Mideast tension, we note that oil prices have not shown the same volatility as 2s.

 UBS economics thinks the hurricane depressed July payrolls by about 40k, so August jobs will be inflated by about 40k. That release comes 1.5 weeks before the FOMC meeting.

 Breakevens have fallen sharply, with 5s 65bps below their April levels despite the concerns about tariffs/oil risks. We think this is in large part a summer Friday overreaction (Figure 4).

 2y spreads tightened as investors grabbed SOFR contracts to cover shorts.Yet this hasn't changed the looming shift in 2yr spread dynamics caused by a what should be a Tbill scarcity in 2025. We reiterate our long 2yr spread view.

## Euro area - Closing Dec'25 receiver and Z5Z7 steepener but still long German ASWs

We close our Dec 25 receiver after the contract rallied \~54 bps in July and also close our Dec 27 versus Dec 25 steepener after the spread rose \~19 bps this month (Figure 6). The ESTR 1y1y rate, currently at 2.1%, is close to our market-based estimate of the ECB’s neutral rate of 2% but Q2 growth and inflation surprised to the upside so we see less value in the front end at current levels. The market is pricing nearly 150bps of cuts by the end of 2025, aligning closely with UBS expectations.

We expect European duration to remain supported around these levels as the ECB acknowledges growth risks to the downside and should continue easing as medium-term inflation returns to target. The slowdown in supply in August will offer some seasonal support, but the pick-up in supply in the Autumn could provide further steepening impetus. The lack of progress in forming a French government could keep EGB spreads at elevated levels.

 We see value in maintaining our long bias in 10y German ASWs. The 10y German ASWs appear still attractive from a valuation perspective (Figure 910y GermanASW stilokchp v ud' f), while being long can provide hedging protection against risk-off shocks stemming from potential stock market declines or escalating geopolitical risks.

## United Kingdom - Still value in the front-end, despite the recent moves

 The message we took from the MPC was simple. The central case is bullish for the rates path, so it is the risk case that continues to worry nearly half the members (and still weighs on the minds of some of the rest too). But the probability of the sticky inflation risk case naturally falls as data meets expectations. Real rates have been rising, meanwhile. Net result: further easing is likely.

## Switzerland - SARON 1y1y looks too low

Inflation in Switzerland has been tracking below the SNB’s inflation forecasts. Swiss inflation was in line with market expectations (and UBS) in July at 1.3% y/y, but fell short of the SNB's Q3 forecast of 1.5% y/y. This undershoot in inflation, combined with a generally bullish global sentiment, has further supported CHF rates.

 The SARON 1y1y rate is currently at 0.55%, nearly 45 basis points below the SNB's neutral rate estimate of 1%. While we expect the SNB to cut its policy rate by 25bps to 1% in September, we think that would be increasingly hard for the SNB to set its policy rate into expansionary territory this year.

 The currency remains strong, and the SNB's balance sheet data for this month did not indicate any significant FX interventions.

 Nevertheless, we think that FX interventions may become more significant when policy rates approach to neutral rate, thereby limiting the need for additional policy easing through the policy rate channel.
