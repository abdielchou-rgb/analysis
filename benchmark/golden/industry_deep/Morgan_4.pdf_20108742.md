# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 17

USTs sell off alongside surge in corporate issuance; JGB curve twist-steepens; PBoC maintains rate; Fed's Harker sees one cut in '24 if forecast materializes; euro area retraces risk-off moves; risk-on rally supports oil and equities; DXY at 105.34 (-0.2%); US 10y at 4.281% (+6.0bp).

• USTs sell off in a parallel manner in a low-liquidity session ahead of Tuesday’s retail sales data, alongside a surge in corporate issuance totaling over \$20bn.

• JGB curve twist-steepens as lower expectations for a near-term BoJ rate hike spur a rally in the front end, but uncertainty regarding the reduction of BoJ purchases supports a sell-off in the super-long sector.

• CNH holds steady as the PBoC maintains the 1y MLF rate at 2.5% and net withdrew CNY 55bn of liquidity through MLF operations.

• Philadelphia Fed President Harker says “more data is essential,” noting that one rate cut in 2024 is appropriate if his current forecast materializes, but still sees potential for two cuts or none based on forthcoming data.

• Euro area partially retraces last week’s risk-off move amid reports that the National Rally leader will work with President Macron, should she win; 10y OAT/Bund spread tightens and French stocks rebound slightly (CAC 40: +0.9%).

• Risk-on rally fuels increases in equities with the S&P 500 (+0.8%) reaching new highs, and supports oil prices with WTI crude oil futures (+2.4%) breaking through technical levels.

## United States:

It was a quiet start to the week ahead of meaningful data releases, such as May retail sales in the US, and a plethora of central bank meetings. Overnight, Treasuries largely traded in a narrow range. During the London session, European market participants were no longer as focused on risk-off trades as they were last week, which allowed French bonds to outperform, EUR to strengthen, and EGB spreads to tighten. Further, it allowed USTs to sell off into the NY morning.

The gradual cheapening of USTs continued during the NY morning in a low-liquidity session. Given the short week in the US, there was a surge of corporate issuance with \~19 new investment grade deals that totaled over \$20bn and helped UST yields drift higher. The sell-off was also supported by economic data this week. The Empire State Manufacturing Survey rebounded to -6.0 in June (C: -10.0; P: -15.6), a still-low level but above consensus expectations, though it remains to be a volatile index. Additionally, many market participants are forecasting strength in the May retail sales data to be released on Wednesday, supporting higher UST yields.

Fedspeak also had a hawkish undertone as Philadelphia Fed President Harker reiterated the tone from the June FOMC noting that he is comfortable with just one cut in 2024 if his forecast materializes. He acknowledged the weak May CPI, though continued to stress the need to see more data. Ultimately, USTs ended the day \~6bp cheaper across the curve.

The risk-on sentiment following easing concerns around European geopolitical tensions led to a decline in the USD (DXY USD Index: -0.2%) and gold (-0.6%), but strengthened equity and oil prices. The S&P 500 (0.8%) rose to fresh highs with technology continuing to outperform, but also aided by gains in consumer discretionary and consumer staples. Oil outperformed and WTI oil futures (+2.4%) closed above \$80 a barrel for the first time in a month after prices broke through their 200-day moving average.

## Japan:

The JGB curve twist-steepened in the Tokyo morning. Lower expectations for a nearterm BoJ rate hike as well as the risk-off sentiment that stemmed from the political uncertainty surrounding the French elections supported the rally in the front-end and belly of the curve. However, the super-long sectors sold off, largely due to uncertainty regarding the BoJ’s reduction of JGB purchases, which will be determined at the July monetary policy meeting and could be a “considerable amount,” as BoJ Governor Ueda indicated at the press conference of the June meeting. Ex-BoJ Member Sakura said the BoJ is likely to reduce monthly bond buying by 2 trillion yen, which would bring the monthly pace to 4 trillion yen. He added that another rate hike would be in September at earliest. In the Tokyo afternoon, the trend was unchanged from the morning, and the curve continued to steepen especially in sectors greater than 10y. 2y JGB rallied 1.0bp to 0.285%, 10y JGB rallied 0.5bp to 0.925%, and 30y JGB sold off 3.0bp to 2.12% on a simple yield basis.

TONA-OIS curve bull-flattened supported by better receivers across the curve with demand to square positions given risk-off sentiment. ASW cheapened on broad sectors, and super-long ASW cheapened sharply. JSCC-LCH basis in the long-end

tightened, which suggested decent receiving pressure from the overseas community in the LCH space. XCCY basis widened across the curve. The BoJ will conduct scheduled JGB purchases on Tuesday in the 1-3y, 3-5y, 5-10y, and 10-25y sectors. We do not expect the BoJ to make any changes to the buying size to avoid confusion right after the decision for QT in the June monetary policy meeting.

USD/JPY traded largely in a range between 157.3-157.6 on Monday. A datadependent Fed and lower expectations for a BoJ rate hike in the near term should support carry-focused trades, but the risk-off sentiment may cap the upside. USD/ JPY traded \~157.4 in the London morning. Risk currency pairs such as AUD/JPY traded lower from 104.2 to 103.6 in the London morning alongside weak Chinese manufacturing/property market data.

## Europe:

It was a relatively quiet day in G10 FX amid a data-light calendar on Monday, ahead of a central-bank-heavy week. In Scandinavia, we saw a contrast in which SEK gained +0.1%, spurred on by positive equity performance, whereas NOK fell -0.1% despite positive price action in brent crude oil futures. Sterling gained +0.1% as gilts soldoff more than treasuries, which drove yield differentials in favor of the GBP. In the funders, JPY continued to fund, which drove the currency down -0.2%, whereas investors were more cautious about CHF, which closed flat given uncertainties around the upcoming SNB meeting. EUR lightly retraced some of last week’s losses and was up +0.2% during the London session.

On Monday, the European market partially retraced some of the risk-off move from last week. Bunds extended their decline and French bonds outperformed. The 10y OAT/Bund spread tightened by \~5bp using last week’s benchmark bonds as reference, and the rest of the complex followed the move. In the ASW complex, short, and intermediate maturities followed the playbook and ground tighter. Duration-wise, the 10y Bund sold-off 5bp to 2.41% and the rest of the curve moved in a parallel fashion. In the front-end, market implied policy paths repriced marginally higher and reds underperformed.

## Dollar Bloc:

Dollar bloc moves were also fairly muted ahead of central bank meetings later this week. The biggest mover was NZD, which fell -0.3% and was outperformed by its Australian neighbor, AUD, which fell only -0.1% as the RBA’s cash target rate decision approached. Canadian bond yields rose less than UST, which pushed yield differentials in favor of the USD, and resulted in CAD falling -0.1% during the London session. The DXY USD Index posted a small loss of -0.1% during the London session.

## Asia:

AxJ currencies traded in a narrow range with many markets closed for local holidays. CNH held steady \~7.27 after the PBoC’s on-hold MLF rate decision and a mixed bag of May activity data. THB was the main underperformer in the region, down 0.4% to

36.8 following the 0.5% fall in gold prices and weakness in local equities (SET: - 0.8%). KRW and TWD also depreciated 0.1% to 1,381 and 32.37, respectively, given the weak risk sentiment. SGD held steady at 1.353 amid a local holiday. India, Indonesia, Philippines, and Malaysia markets were also closed for holidays.

AxJ rates exhibited mixed moves in a quiet session. China rates underperformed; 1y CNY NDIRS yields rose 2bp with a bear-flattening bias after the PBoC kept the MLF rate on hold and net withdrew CNY55bn of liquidity. 10y CGB rallied 3bp, but 30y CGB held steady at 2.51% as a PBoC-backed media outlet reiterated that current high returns in bonds are unsustainable. Hong Kong swap rates held stable. Korea was the main outperformer after Presidential aide Sung said on Sunday that an environment in which interest rates can be lowered is being created; 2y KRW NDIRS yield fell 3bp, and the 2s10s curve bull-steepened 1bp. In Thailand, yields fell \~1bp across the NDOIS curve, while 10y ThaiGB yields remained stable at 2.76%.

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
