# Sector Allocation and Focus List Changes: Be More Defensive

We add CCB-H (0939.HK), Petro China-A (601857.SS), MTR (0066.HK), and remove Baosteel (600019.SS) and Trip.com (TCOM.O) to our China/HK Focus List and A-share Thematic List, following sector allocation changes below. We advise more defensiveness, and wait for signs of a policy pivot.

## Key Takeaways

Moving Consumer Discretionary to EW (50 to 0bps) as consumption momentum deceleration persists;

■ Maintaining OW on Materials, but cutting our active risk exposure (100 to 50 bps) given less favorable supply-demand dynamics for steel specifically;

■ Moving Banks to EW (-50 to 0 bps) on further financial risk reduction and topdown preference for stable yield;

Moving Telecom to OW (0 to 50 bps) for more exposure to defensiveness.

We advise to add more defensiveness to China dedicated portfolios, at least in the near-term, as we expect multiple macro, sentiment, and geopolitical concerns to persist: 1) the deflation and macro slowdown debate continues; 2) more disappointing earnings results may come through during the 2Q results season; 3) market expectation towards a policy easing step-up comes and goes, each time weighing on sentiment and liquidity; and 4) US election season approaches. As a result, we adjust our sector allocation preferences and add more exposure to those with more defensiveness and stable cash returns.

We upgrade Telecom to OW and expect a valuation re-rating amid potential Fed rate cuts in 2H24 with attractive dividend yield and high visibility. We upgrade Banks to EW as the ongoing coordinated policy efforts and new monetary policy framework should support banks' NIM and ROE, with attractive dividend yield.

We downgrade Consumer Discretionary to EW, given the weak consumer data print recently, and the macro slowdown starting to broaden out within consumer space with more categories starting to see weakening demand. We cut active risk exposure for Materials from 100bps to 50bps andbecome selective, particularly on potential demand headwinds for steel. The new rebar standard and traditional slow demand season in June-August lead to lower prices and widening losses.

Accordingly, we add China Construction Bank Corp. (0939.HK), and MTR Corp (0066.HK) to our China/HK Focus List, and Petro China-A (601857.SS) to our China A-share Thematic Stock List.

## Sector allocation and focus list changes

## Moving Consumer Discretionary to EW as consumption momentum deceleration persists

We move Consumer Discretionary from OW to EW, considering the weak consumer data print recently. China's retail sales growth in July remained weak at 2.7% y-y with 7M24 growth at 3.5% y-y, the implied recovery pace vs. 2019 retreated visibly to 114%, the lowest YTD (vs. 121% in 1H24), indicating softer sentiment. Our consumer Z-score has shown an accelerated declining trend since April 2024 with broad-based factor deterioration, including air passenger traffic, household loan growth, and retail sales. We see the macro slow-down start to broaden out within consumer space, names with relatively solid demand previously have started to see weakening demand recently - liquor, luxury goods, dairy and online entertainment.

Related China/Hong Kong Focus List change: Remove Trip.com (TCOM.O).

## Maintaining OW on Materials, but cutting our active risk exposure from 100bps to 50 bps

We manage our active risk exposure to Materials and become more selective, particularly on the back of potential demand headwinds for steel in at least the near-term. According to our sector team (China Materials: Growing steel production cuts in China amid losses), a new rebar standard was announced at the end of June and will be effective on 25 September, 2024, thus traders are cutting prices to liquidate inventory which meet the old standards. In addition, with over 60% steel demand coming from property and infrastructure, June-August is the traditional slow demand season. Lower prices and widening losses have led to steel mills bringing forward maintenance or cutting production.

Related China/Hong Kong Focus List and China A-share Thematic stock change: Remove Baosteel (600019.SS).

## Moving Banks to EW on further financial risk reduction and top-down pursuit of more defensiveness

We have reduced our cautious view towards Chinese banks when we published our 2024 mid-year outlook (2024 China Equity Strategy Mid-year Outlook: Seeking Certainty Amid Change) in May, stating better than expected fundamentals and preference for dividend yields as the key reason. As of this report, we officially upgrade the sector to EW in our sector allocation framework for the Chinese equity market, as from a top-down perspective, ongoing coordinated policy efforts to contain and reduce financial system risk should further reduce market concern about bank risk, and the new monetary policy framework (reduced focus on loan and TSF volume growth, and a new interest rate transmission framework) should support banks' NIM and ROE. Morgan Stanley Banks team expect modest NIM pressure in 2025 and some rebound in 2026, which should give the market more confidence in earnings and dividend sustainability. Moreover, H-share banks offer high dividend yields at 7-9%, with SOE banks at around 8% as of August 2024, which provide a solid buffer amid the macro and geopolitical uncertainties. Please see China – Banks: Look for outperformance for the fourth year in a row for MS China Banks team's latest industry views.

The positive developments on both fronts (reduced financial risks and attractive dividend yield) resonate well with our current relatively cautious view towards the overall China equity market, and our preference for more defensiveness and stable cash returns, as we are likely facing a persistent heightening of market volatility given the following headwinds: 1) the deflation and macro slowdown debate continues; 2) more disappointing earnings results may come through during the 2Q results season; 3) market expectation towards a policy easing step-up comes and goes, each time weighing on sentiment and liquidity; and 4) US election season approaches.

Related China/Hong Kong Focus List change: Add China Construction Bank Corp.
(0939.HK).

## Moving Telecom to OW for more exposure to defensiveness

This action is to reflect our existing China/Hong Kong Focus List de facto OW allocation in the Telecom space, with HKT Trust and HKT Ltd. being a core holding since March 2021. The stock has outperformed MSCI China consistently, by a cumulative outperformance of 69 ppts since March 2021. For the HK telecom space, MS sector team expect a valuation multiple re-rating for the sector amid potential Fed rate cuts in 2H24, as forecast by our US economics team, and reiterate HKT Trust as their top pick given its steady and resilient growth, high visibility on dividend (2-3% 2023-26e CAGR), and attractive 2024e yield of \~8% (\~420bps spread). ## Focus List changes

Adding China Construction Bank Corp. (0939.HK) to China/HK Focus List: CCB-H remains defensive with the highest dividend yield at 8% (2024e) among H share SOE banks. We think PBOC’s monetary policy framework shift away from quantitative targets should lead to reduced window guidance on SOE banks, paving the way for more rational loan growth, stabilizing loan yield, less deposit competition and more efficient capital deployment. CCB’s highest NIM among the big 4 SOE banks at 1.57% in 1Q24 reflects a solid client base, on which the bank could potentially have more pricing power to support NIM once non-market oriented window guidance moderates. CCB has also been more rational on balance sheet expansion in recent quarters despite the influence of window guidance, which could help it better control credit risks as SOE banks rationalize credit growth since 2024. Its 238% NPL coverage (1Q24), second highest within big 4 SOE banks, and 5.11% CET1 buffer, highest among big $4 ,$ should continue to offer CCB ample buffer in risk digestion while maintain the highest ROE among SOE banks.

Adding MTR Corp (0066.HK) to China/HK Focus List: Despite macro challenges, MTR’s dividend yield of 5.0% is high and looks secure. Development property (DP) earnings are more resilient than those of developers, and growing with improved visibility. Recurring business (rail and rental) is improving helped by normalizing traffic, fare hikes, and new shopping malls. Leverage should remain healthy despite high capex. Potential US Fed rate cuts will also help the stock. We expect EPS growth of 87% YoY and 61% YoY in 2024e and 2025e. Our HK Property analyst Jeffrey Mak upgraded MTR from UW to OW in April 2024. For more details, please read here.

Adding PetroChina-A (601857.SS) to China A-share Thematic Stock List: Morgan Stanley Energy and Chemicals team's double upgrade PetroChina-A from UW to OW (see China Energy and Chemicals: Multiple Rating Changes; Prefer Upstream Over Downstream), we believe the high yield theme for China energy stocks remains, because they have shifted capital allocation from scale to shareholder returns. We see potential in maintaining an attractive yield, even if the oil price further corrects, according to our pressure test at US\$60 Brent. DPS used to link to EPS with a certain payout ratio, however, free cash flow generation and balance sheet space matter more going forward. In addition, the widening 12m forward risk-free rates spread of China and the US should lead to a higher valuation premium for PetroChina-A share over dual H-share given the lower yield required by A share market. PetroChina-H has been in our China/Hong Kong Focus List since April 2024.
