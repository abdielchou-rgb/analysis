# Venture Vision: Let's Put \$7 Trillion in Perspective

Even if only half of the mooted \$7 trillion figure ends up being raised for AI chip output, the amount would exceed the value of all start-ups globally and all US venture funding...ever. Even if pie-in-the-sky, it reinforces our view that Security capex is a winning theme in a Multipolar tech world

What's happened?: While not the first time, recent speculation in the press with regard to Sam Altman seeking to raise capital for vertically integrated AI through both consumer hardware and upstream chip capacity took an exponential turn last week. The WSJ reported investors being sounded out for between \$5-\$7trn for this chip endeavour. Clearly - or until more is announced - one cannot (1) ascribe particular weight to this figure, nor (2) the likelihood of whether it could even ease mid-term supply bottlenecks or (3) judge whether custom silicon backed by this degree of funding stands a chance of closing the quality gap to the best-in-class GPU hardware.

What can \$7 trillion buy you?: In the spirit of this series of reports, \$7 trillion surpasses funding (in real \$ terms) for >300,000 businesses across 850,000 global funding rounds back to 1980. It is therefore, comfortably more than all the US venture funding ever raised. The figure is >5x larger than Taiwan's PPP GDP. In fact even the reported lower end at \$5trn could acquire every start-up on the planet with another \$2trn in change. The number dwarfs annual Magnificent 7's capex outlays combined (...even the aggregate figure over the past decade). It is double the market value of NVIDIA, AMD, Intel and TSMC combined (shown above).

Is this comparable to venture in any way? Venture is about power laws. Outlier IP backed by - historically - relatively small tickets leading to scale up anomalous exit valuations. It has typically been the domain of software founders predominantly and it thrived on software's inherent scalability to achieve outsized ROI. Clearly then, capital intensive start-ups do not fall into the category resembling "traditional" venture capital. This endeavour - if it were even to materialise - would be best associated with either (1) national security spending (discussed below) or (2) private equity led infrastructure investing. Even still, this figure would be 3x larger than either (1) global military spending or (2) all Private Equity dry powder - per Pitchbook.

Original thesis of Seeking Security in a Multipolar World: China has developed enormous industrial scale and in our framework is leading in 28 of the 47 key security technologies, emerging industries and materials production by market share. What did we estimate the cost of de-risking to be?: An increasingly common question from investors – particularly in Europe – is how large do incentives need to be to reduce our external dependencies on critical products across the security spectrum?

We start with (1) a per-capita spend of \~\$1,300 to re-shore and modernise critical security verticals, as well as (2) Europe's average \~0.7 dependence score relative to the US based on our Security framework. Then we develop a sensitivity analysis for potential subsidies if Europe seeks a level of critical infrastructure security sovereignty similar to the US (Exhibit 6). To level up, we estimate that Europe could be required to offer \$1 trillion in subsidies similar to those in the US. We concede this is simplistic for a number of reasons, but we think it is a starting point for investors to anticipate what might be required if a similar approach is adopted in the multipolar de-risking process.

There is a simpler, cheaper option to propel Europe's critical infrastructure ambitions forward: improved permitting speed and reduced complexity. We have not seen material change on this front in recent years. Yet this, arguably even more than Europe's response to the IRA, seems to us the greatest undiscounted upside risk for European equities – particularly in the industrials and utilities segments.

## Real-time Valuation Monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.
