# Memory – We Doubt This Time Will Be Different

Stocks lead pricing, which leads earnings. Bull markets for memory climb a wall of worry, but that’s the rhythm of memory stocks. What’s the next thing to worry about? Focus on things that we believe will never change – the cycle and how people react.

A breather needed – it's not the end of the world. Sell-off periods during cycle upturns are normal and have historically often turned out to provide valuable entry points for memory stocks. Supply and demand dynamics are tailwinds now, in our view, 4Q is backward-looking and rising production is good news ahead of quarterly double-digit price hikes in memory.

Price matters most – not production. We believe the market may be overly fixated on the belief that higher production raises the likelihood of a memory recession. Instead we believe we need to focus on the big AI/HBM wave across the tech sector. Higher production of advanced 1a/bnm DRAM is needed for companies to meet rapidly surging HBM demand from 2H24 – this is logical, rational and to be expected, in our view. We believe stock price movements are highly correlated with the YoY rate of change in memory pricing (not utilization), which is accelerating on higher output ( Exhibit 3 ). Furthermore, DDR4 production restarts are shifting to DDR5, resulting in lower total wafer capacity compared to 2022. This is what a memory cycle recovery is supposed to look like, in our view.

Recovery gaining momentum in 2024. Earnings do not lead the recovery; it is multiples that rise first. Cycles are largely supply-driven, unless there is an unexpected demand shock (not de-stocking or seasonal weakness). AI is still a key driver generating outsized earnings, and we expect margins to accelerate the most, fueled by hyper growth HBM, a better cyclical backdrop and 'At-Home-AI' giving another boost to increased TAM this year.

We are past the ‘easy money’ stage of the rally but still see 29%-57% upside to fair value. But we think the real story is the earnings growth outlook rather than current stock valuations. We believe the memory cycle has recovered from the 2Q23 lows and is well set up for 2024. Our primary concern is on the timing of an eventual YoY peak in the cycle entering 2H24 where it becomes 'sell the good news' and stocks respond negatively to positive events seen as marking a peak/inflection. We are clearly not there yet, and a lot of the gains in a memory bull market tend to occur toward the final stage of the up-cycle.

## Market Fixating on Cutting Production

Stocks lead pricing, which leads earnings. "I'm going to buy once I know there is a recovery." You have to buy some degree of uncertainty.

It seems like investors are always searching for the next thing to go wrong in memory. It is natural to be skeptical of bull markets – we are sometimes so afraid of being wrong, we don’t want to be right. We may see social media posts, production news headlines, or bullet point analysis ("2Q pricing… up but not that much", etc.) – likely uncorroborated by field checks – prompting investors to sell. This has been a secular problem since the 'Tech Wreck', and was not helped by the pandemic either. Many now think that memory stocks can only go up if production cuts continue indefinitely (Samsung just spoiled that party). In our view, that’s just not the case. We see no correlation between stock performance, DRAM pricing and fab utilization. And this is apparent if you look at past cycle upturns where fab utilization was at 100%. What we believe investors should be searching for at this stage of the cycle is what can go right. To us, it is supply growth with no capex this year – and that bodes very well for returns.

A little unsettled in January... Historically, market bears and their worst-case scenarios have had a much better chance of being right after a so-called 'easy year' of performance. However, we must keep in mind that we are in a cyclical upturn and down days are a natural part of that. Although we believe we have now reached the middle of the upcycle and the easy money stage has passed, we are highly confident that 2024 will again prove profitable.

Investors turned bullish in November 2023 after they saw signs of memory price hikes, similar to November 2021, and we have often seen that once investors start buying after a trough, they search for sector laggards. However, with the recent reversal in 10Y UST yields and China smartphone manufacturers de-stocking in early 2024 following restocking in 4Q23 causing memory stocks to pull back, we believe the memory sector is still some way off optimism and euphoria.

…but DRAM cycle to finish the race. The market can sometimes overexaggerate the accuracy of forward DRAM pricing estimates. We must remember that when someone says a stock is expensive based on this PB or cheap on that PB, they are using a forward pricing estimate (like TrendForce's recent conservative estimates for 2Q24), and sometimes these estimates can be way off. We have seen such estimates rising aggressively on the back of companies delivering above expectations by keeping production well below demand (even if utilization rises). We expect this to continue in 2024. We do not view current PBs as stretched as we believe consensus and buy-side expectations are still to catch up with increasingly optimistic earnings forecasts.

We need to remember our history: we’ve been here before, and stocks tend to outperform as YoY growth accelerates and clearly that is the case in Exhibit 3 . The problem is the lack of visibility beyond the next quarter – but all cycles complete eventually. While they may have different narratives and headlines, ultimately they are all the same and we doubt this time will be any different.

No need for anxiety around increased production. Samsung has been raising production gradually since 4Q23 due to shortages in server DRAM/HBM components but we believe supply is still below 2H24 HBM demand. We believe Samsung is keeping production well below demand and will continue to raise prices every quarter. As Samsung posted US \$10bn of losses in 2023, we appreciate there is a sense of urgency to lift margins again quickly. If, despite this, investors turn bearish and wait on the sidelines, they could end up missing the entire cycle. Raising production is only logical and should be expected rather than viewed as an attempt to curtail the cycle or future cash flows.

• Samsung DRAM production was running at 68% capacity in 4Q23; we expect this to be raised to about 75% in 1Q24. We expect Hynix to lift from 87% to 90% this quarter and Micron from 83% to 86%. We expect NAND production cuts to go largely unchanged this quarter, moving from low 60% in 4Q23 to mid-60%.

• All DRAM production cuts were DDR4 and are being replaced with DDR5. Therefore even at full utilization, effective wafer capacity will be well below that of 2022 given the larger dies and capacity loss from longer production steps for DDR5. DRAM suppliers lost US\$10bn last year and we doubt they will want to earn even less this year.

• Furthermore, the die size for HBM is around 2x bigger than DRAM. This year, DRAM used for HBM is in the ‘low-single digit’ % of total shipments and thus bit consumption by HBM will be a high-single digit %. However, it could account for more than half of all 1 a/bnm DRAMs.

## Channel Checks

HBM. LTAs (long-term agreements) with a large GPU producer were recently completed for 2024 with some price discounts in exchange for some pre-payments. HBM3e qualification this quarter is unlikely to change HBM share dynamics much, with SK Hynix remaining in pole position given its superior thermal performance and strong execution capabilities.

We expect next generation GPUs in 2025 (X100) to continue to utilize HBM3e but at significantly higher content. We estimate that the 32GB based HBM3e die will come in two versions: 1) an 8-layer 256GB HBM and 2) another version at 12-layers 384GB (48GB units x 8 pieces per GPU).

We expect HBM4 in 2026, based on JEDEC standards, and it is not clear at this stage whether or not a hybrid bonding design will prevail given the significantly higher costs involved and manufacturing yield risks. Our best guess is that conventional TCB (Thermal Compression Bonding) will be further extended given its lower costs and greater reliability.

DRAM. Tighter supply has driven up shortages in 1Q so far, with contract prices now likely to inflect even higher in our view (+20% QoQ in 4Q23 and estimated >+15% QoQ in 1Q24). Producers are quoting near +20% QoQ with many expecting 1Q24 contract prices to settle around +15% on a blended basis. It is too early to determine 2Q24 pricing, while recent third-party estimates point to moderating price hikes of around 5%. However, we note that historically once the cycle turns pricing tends to move in a straight line. The exceptions have been when the industry has faced unexpected demand shocks, but we would not classify the relatively weak demand of late as an 'unexpected shock'. We expect double-digit price hikes to continue in 2Q24 and DRAM spot pricing to plateau around +100% YoY by late 3Q24.

We note that one of the largest DRAM module makers started to raise DDR4 and DDR5 prices aggressively (>10-20%+) for the first time this month, implying normalized channel inventory. We see 8GB DDR4 module prices now at US\$15 compared to US\$13 in 4Q23 while DDR5 modules are now closer to US\$26. Note that peak DDR4 prices in the previous cycle were more than double the current price.

NAND. Production remains well below demand with continued aggressive production cuts and fabs utilized at mid-60% capacity vs. low-60% in 3Q23. We view the improving demand environment as driving greater certainty on the pricing outlook into 2024. Suppliers' overall NAND flash inventory has significantly improved in the past two quarters despite still sitting slightly above what we would normally label a healthy threshold.

The contract pricing outlook is similar to DRAM at 20% QoQ price hikes in 1Q24 lead by Chinese mobile NAND and SSD customer outpacing, and we expect a moderate increase in 2Q24 before climbing back to double-digit hikes in 3Q24 on the back of enterprise restocking from US cloud customers in a seasonally strong quarter.

## AI Set To Remake PC and Smartphones

Departing from the norm. While most demand for AI applications has resided in the cloud, demand at the edge will become increasingly common (mobile AI) from 2024, in our view, and may add to the AI TAM. The memory rally is real and we believe will continue to be concentrated in AI supply chain stocks. Of course things will likely overheat sometime down the line but we cannot deny the potential of AI and the earnings power that companies will gain by shifting more to HBM. The potential growth from all of these technological advancements and what AI can bring to DRAM is intriguing. Therefore, in our view, it is worth playing the hot hand and staying with these stocks.

More AI = more money. AI applications have very high memory-bandwidth requirements, since computing layers within deep neural networks must pass input data to a massive number of cores as quickly as possible. For example, when a model is being trained to recognize images or video, all intermediate results in the recognition process such as colors, contours, textures need to reside on DRAM as the model fine-tunes its algorithms. So the more AI we have, the more it is going to be a tailwind for DRAM companies. AI models are only as good as the data that DRAM feeds into processors – this is missioncritical.

Edge AI = more memory. While most demand and AI applications currently reside in the cloud (a network of remote servers), over time we expect demand at the edge to become increasingly common. This trend will increase the DRAM content per edge AI device such as AI smartphone, AI PC or connected cars. We also expect edge computing to emerge in edge devices for applications where privacy issues and data bandwidth are critical. We need to take into account the impact of a US\$10bn HBM TAM in 2024 that was not here a year ago and the sudden birth of AI demand that will likely magnify supply shortages.

## What could go wrong?

The thing that most people focus on is how much stocks are up today and valuations for memory stocks, which is less relevant given the growth outlook for AI. But valuation is where supply meets demand and not a moment pre-ordained by history. We have to reprice future cash flows to account for this newly created HBM TAM and the duration it gives to earnings, which could ultimately result in better-than-expected growth. Furthermore, based on consensus earnings revisions, many stocks in this area are materially cheaper today than they were in the previous cycle. Two areas to watch out for, however:

• Cycle peak. This is the timing of an eventual YoY plateau or peak in the cycle entering 2H24 where it becomes 'sell the good news' as stocks peak on good news and respond negatively to positive events that are viewed as marking an inflection point.

• AI chip demand. A cloud AI environment less favorable to HBM spending would be a headwind. In terms of company-specific risks, an inability to catch up with advanced HBM market growth, and unfavorable FX volatility vs USD would impact earnings.

## Stock Preference

When you have a secular story, one should not be valuation-sensitive and dispute past peak multiples for an AI play. When we think about allocated dollars and capex to AI that memory will capture, if they can have sustained growth, the multiple is going to go up well beyond past peaks. So in our view there is a multiple expansion story on top of an earnings story from a cycle recovery. We also have to be wary that nothing goes straight up, so we can expect many tactical pull-backs along the way. But we would certainly regard those as dips to buy instead of a top.

We counter the view held by some investors that memory stocks have risen so much that they have become unattractive – in fact, many are no higher than they were at the end of 2021. So although they certainly rose significantly in 2023, they were hit hard the year before. In our view, this suggests that these stocks have more fuel in the tank for 2024 ( Exhibit 13 ).

We expect Hynix to re-rate further as growth and margins outperform peers and exceed consensus expectations. Trading in line with mid-cycle multiples on 1.7x 2024e PB, the stock is not pricing in the attractive risk-reward and positive catalysts we see ahead in H2 2024. Samsung. Samsung is on a mission to drive margins quickly with memory pricing, and we believe it will likely benefit from the launch of its S24 edge AI product in 1Q24. The 4Q23 prelim miss is mainly from its appliances and foundry divisions while memory was in-line (Samsung Electronics: 4Q23 Prelim: Slight Miss on Foundry and Appliances (9 Jan 2024)). 4Q is backward-looking and should not matter ahead of double-digit price hikes in memory, in our view. Supply and demand dynamics are a tailwind now and visibility into the company's solid position in AI keeps us solidly OW.
