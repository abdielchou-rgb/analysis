## Ten questions (and answers) on currencies in 2H24

 Dollar to stay elevated due to USD’s improving yield advantage and lingering exceptionalism. Trading approach remains tactical.

 Modest Fed easing isn’t sufficient for outsized USD weakness; a growth pick up ex-US is required as well. Stay vigilant for such outcomes given rich USD valuations. Valley of USD smile exists but will be narrow.

US elections add upside risks to USD and are already impacting markets. Transmission to FX could be via: tariff risk (USD positive), Russia policy (EUR-negative), fiscal (USD positive), dollar policy (could be longer term USD-bearish). The first two could be implemented in a timelier manner.

 Election opportunities: Event pricing is tracking prior cycles with USD TWI, CNH vols close to prior highs, MXN middling, but European FX visibly lagging. Medium-term USD/CNH digital risk reversals appear attractive.

 Rotation away from global carry remains a key theme but will be a process and will require stable/ lower US yields. Some highyielders could recover but risk-reward in global FX carry looks unattractive given yield compression and impending US elections….

 … by contrast, G10 carry should be more resilient so long as inflation remains sticky and Fed is high-for-long.

 Funders to underperform tactically, but tariff risk/ lower yields create uncertainty; performance to be differentiated. JPY weakness to persist on worst real yields globally/ glacial BoJ. CHF to be relatively resilient on yield/ BoP support.

 Central bank policy will impact FX given varying start / size of easing. G10 policy RV involves long USD, AUD, NOK, GBP vs. EUR, NZD, CAD. Watch EUR where sticky inflation could limit easing.

 Outlook for EUR/USD remains bearish with target of 1.05. Range of 1.05-1.10 to hold but implications from US elections bias risks to the downside.

 Geopolitical risks facing CNY will come to the fore in H2. Paucity of risk premium to keep CNY depreciation intact; risk bias to 7.30 target is squarely to the upside.

 FX vol unlikely to trend cleanly higher in H2, despite inevitable US election noise. While VXY is moderately cheap, firm growth, central bank easing, and pre-existing USD length should stymie any outsized vol pickup.

 Housing may provide some relief for early-cutter FX. SEK is our preferred recovery candidate on this basis.

Summary on main themes: We stay long USD in the portfolio vs lower-yielding currencies like EUR & CNH, which should outperform given a Fed that is high-for-long and bullish risks from the elections. Policy divergence is still tradable but needs to be more discriminate; stay long AUD/ NZD & NOK/SEK. NOK also to get a boost if yields continue to normalize and boost global growth in the process. The case against JPY mean-reversion remains compelling as G10 carry can remain resilient on wide yield differentials.

 Look to scale up USD longs where US election impacts are clearest (CNH & EUR), but with expressions where premiums are not punitive.

 No longer at peak US exceptionalism, but the dollar still benefits from US-derived supports.

 Be more surgical in policy r.v. trades.

Lower yields boost growth, and cheap/rate-sensitive G10 FX by extension. The G10 high-beta bloc is already well-priced for a recession, which suggests that these are attractive candidates to go long should the global soft landing play out.

 G10 carry still viable on lesser overall narrowing in yield dispersion; strategic JPY weakness intact but requires nimble management, compared to CHF where tactical weakness runs into tariff risks.

Our view looks for the dollar to stay elevated and is motivated by two pillars: (1) the dollar’s improving yield advantage as other central banks cut rates earlier and more than the Fed, and (2) US exceptionalism continues to linger even with US growth moderating, as recovery in Eurozone and China remains tepid, if not fragile.

• US rate differentials in aggregate should remain relatively elevated in favour of the dollar even if the Fed ends up cutting more than we expect, and this is already discounted to a significant degree in forward OIS curves.

US exceptionalism may be moderating but still pieces remain intact, including the high-for-long Fed, US equity outperformance, stronger US productivity, and bullish risks stemming from the upcoming US election.

Since the launch of the Fed rate hike cycle in March 2022, US monetary policy expectations have been the main driver of USD/JPY. Divergence in monetary policy between the US and Japan to continue. Our baseline scenario is that of a modest decline in USD/JPY driven by monetary policy divergence, but recent developments suggest the possibility of an alternative scenario — outright JPY weakness driven by carry trades and/or domestic capital flight — has materially risen.

One possible explanation for the recent deviation of USD/JPY from rate expectations is booming JPY carry trades. History suggests that the long USD/JPY carry trade tends to remain well supported so long as the US-JP yield spread is above 4%.

Japan’s deeply negative real interest rate has driven underlying JPY weakness in recent years, and there is some speculation that domestic outflows from Japan could even accelerate at some point, even as the Fed / BoJ policy gap begins to narrow.
