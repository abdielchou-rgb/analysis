## Oil Demand Tracker

## Global oil demand expanded 1.9 mbd YoY in April

Our high-frequency demand indicators estimate that worldwide oil consumption averaged 101.5 mbd in April, 200 kbd below our published estimates (Figure 1). On a year-over-year basis, demand grew by 1.9 mbd, below our expectations of a 2.1 mbd increase. In the first four months of the year, global oil demand rose by 1.4 mbd compared to the same period last year. Our projections from last November called for a 2.0 mbd improvement. The underperformance in the realized demand vs. our expectations during January-March period was almost entirely due to warmer winter conditions and the resulting weaker demand for heating fuels. The 200 kbd gap in April was due to weaker US gasoline consumption during the month.

Diesel cracks in the US and Europe fell 4-5% week over week for the week ending April 30 tracking higher diesel and gasoil exports out of China and India (Figure 7 & 8). Meanwhile, Russian diesel/gasoil exports on a four-week average basis improved marginally. Meanwhile, US gasoline cracks rose 3% over the same period as exports to Mexico improved sequentially (Figure 9).

Regionally, the US, Canada, the UK, Brazil, and Japan reported consumption statistics.

• In Europe, the UK reported its total oil consumption for February. Total oil demand was unchanged from year ago levels as declines in diesel and kerosene demand were offset by gains in jet fuel and other products. Europe<sup>’</sup>s oil demand tracker estimate for April continues to oscillate in a -100 kbd to +100 kbd range around our published projection of 14.0 mbd (Figure 4). Germany<sup>’</sup>s truck toll mileage remained weak in April (Figure 22).

Canada<sup>’</sup>s oil demand declined 67 kbd YoY in February led by weak distillates and gasoline consumption. On the other hand, petcoke use improved, and jet fuel demand rose to 98% of pre-pandemic levels.

US<sup>’</sup>s total oil demand expanded a solid 190 kbd YoY in February as per the latest PSM statistics. Petroleum products demand contracted 200 kbd YoY while liquids demand rose 400 kbd YoY. While distillates consumption declined on a year-over-year basis, total distillates demand including biofuels rose 28 kbd YoY with share of biofuels in total distillates sales averaging 7.9% in February.

• Our US gasoline demand tracker estimates that consumption averaged 8.5 mbd in April, 400 kbd below our projections, (Figure 2) likely due to a 20% increase in US retail gasoline prices from January lows.

China oil demand is tracking 16.7 mbd in April, 100 kbd below our estimates for the month (Figure 5). High frequency indicators of demand improved over the week. Trucks on Chinese expressways rose to their highest level in 2024 at 56.3 million (Figure 17) and air travel activity improved ahead of Labor Day holiday. However, Chinese gasoline stocks rose for a second week in a row while diesel stocks remained unchanged week-over-week (Figure 19 & 20).

## Methodology

Our global demand tracker calculates implied demand formulated as:

Daily total oil products demand = total refinery output<sup>(1)</sup> + biofuels blending + daily net imports of products + daily change in products stocks

<sup>(1)</sup> Total refinery output is computed as: daily crude runs \* products yie ${ \mathrm { \Delta \ l d ^ { ( 2 ) } + N G L s ^ { ( 3 ) * } } }$ products yields

<sup>(2)</sup> Product yields are for LPG, Naphtha, Gasoline, Diesel/Gasoil, Fuel Oil, and other products.

<sup>(3)</sup>NGLs are used to generate ethane, propane, butane, and pentane. We have applied different yields based on that for LPG and other products to estimate NGLs derived liquids demand.

Our US gasoline demand tracker calculates daily implied gasoline consumption using the formula:

Implied gasoline demand = daily crude runs \* US gasoline yield + ethanol blending + net gasoline imports

Data source: Refinery runs and outages are sourced from IIR, Product yields are sourced from WoodMac, Trade flows from Kpler, Biofuels from Rystad, and products stocks from Platts.
