## Morgan Stanley

## US Credit Strategy | Executive Summary

In the (GDP) Zone: There is a lot to like about the macro set up for credit – slowing but robust growth (2.1% 4Q/4Q), improving earnings, start of rate cuts, healthy all-in yields, and modest supply. Tight spreads mean that credit may not continue its outperformance vs. other markets, but absolute performance – both excess and total – remain respectable. We have a high conviction preference for Loans through a shallow easing cycle, followed by IG. HY valuations look the richest, but aggregate fundamentals remain healthy.

US Investment Grade – The Yield is Right: We expect spreads to grind tighter to 80bp – levels not seen since before the GFC – generating positive excess returns and strong total returns (\~10%). Demand should accelerate from multiple pockets as fixed annuity sales, pensions de-risking, and asset rotation out of money market funds add fuel to already strong mutual fund/ETF inflows. Across maturities, we like the front end (especially 3-5Y) and expect the long-end to remain anchored. We like low BBBs, money center banks, energy and utilities.

US Leveraged Credit – Rising Dispersion, Loans over Bonds: We expect spreads to move sideways in HY (300bp) and tighter in Loans (400bp) through 2Q25. We like Loans over HY given the better carry profile, improving coverage ratios and more favorable technicals. We close our CCC overweight given YTD compression and a shallower easing path that delays balance sheet repair. We like Single-B Loans and a CCC/BB barbell in HY. We are cautious on smaller B-rated capital structures in HY that have run too far.

A Shallow but Sticky Default Cycle with Lower Recoveries: We continue to anticipate a flatter but longer default cycle that peaks in the near term and gradually declines from there. We expect 3.5% defaults for HY and 3.75% defaults in Loans (including distressed exchanges) through mid-2025. Loose covenant quality and the rising trend of aggressive LME transactions keep us cautious on lower recoveries.

Derivatives: We prefer cash risk to synthetics given healthy fixed income technicals benefitting the former, while the latter is negatively convex at these levels. We like HY senior mezzanine tranches (HY41 25-35%) as a cheap hedge with good convexity.

## Macro Themes | Preparing for a Smoother Landing

• We see the US economy in the sweet spot for credit – not too hot, not too cold.

• Growth was stronger than expectations in 2024, supported by faster immigration and population growth. We think growth slows somewhat (e.g., 1.3%Q in 1Q24), driven by lower consumption, trade, and a drag from inventories. But overall, macro remains resilient with our economists forecasting 2024 GDP growth at 2.1% 4Q/4Q in 2024, and 2.1%Y in 2025.

• Manufacturing PMIs are also showing signs of bottoming out, pointing to a stabilization in the weakest part of the economy.

Macro Themes | Inflation – Preparing for a Stepdown in the Second Half

Despite challenging inflation data in 1Q, our economists believe this bumpy path is temporary and reverses in the back half of the year. The upswing in 1Q inflation was partly driven by residual seasonality, which should revert in 2H24. Meanwhile, rents, goods, and other services should lead the deceleration.

• On a 4Q/4Q basis, we expect core PCE inflation to slow from 3.2% last year to 2.7% this year and 2.1% in 2025, above the Fed's current median forecast of 2.6%.

• Our economists expect the Fed to cut 3 times this year starting in September followed by 4 cuts in 2025. Four more CPI readings before September are enough to give the Fed confidence to lower rates.

## Macro Themes | A Positive Supply Shock from Immigration

• The rise in immigration has helped explain the better-than-expected economic and job growth. Our economists believe that the increased working age population suggests faster overall growth and an upward pressure on the existing capital stock, encouraging new investment.

## Macro Themes | The Rate Cuts are Coming!

• The market hasn’t been fully convinced of sustained improvement in CPI given that the probabilities of a rate cut have traveled sideways since the last print. But our economists project that all 3 categories of CPI (shelter, core services ex shelter, and core goods) should continue their decline, which will help further the case for earlier rate cuts.

• Our rates strategy team thinks that the market has wrongly interpreted the recent strength in inflation. They see the 10y rate rallying to 3.75% by 2Q25 from its current level and hold their long duration bias by continuing to suggest 3m10y receivers.

• Although leveraged credit may appear uninteresting from a top-down view, it is more interesting from a bottom-up lens. Dispersion is high and has risen this year.

Leveraged Credit Positioning | Cautious on Small Single-Bs, Barbell BBs/CCCs in HY

• Cautious on the small-sized single-B cohort. Amid the broader HY rally, size premium has tightened most notably in single-B HY. We measure size by total amount outstanding in the HY index.

• Instead, we prefer barbelling CCCs with BBs. We believe BBs can maintain tight levels given a strong macro backdrop, and they provide good current income and price convexity in the event of a takeout. • Over the past 10 years, the barbell strategy has generated a 12-month return of 5% on average, 0.6pp higher than comparable single-B returns. It has also outperformed single-Bs on a Sharpe ratio basis.
