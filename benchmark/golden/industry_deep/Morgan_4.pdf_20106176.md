# US Credit Strategy Brief | North America

# Fund Flows – Starting on a Strong Footing

Inflows into credit have been strong to start the year, with HY steady and IG accelerating, while Loans have lagged. IG flows have skewed toward ETFs, which have so far seen their best Jan inflows. From here, flows should be most supportive for 1-5Y IG as we approach the start of policy easing.

One of our key themes for 2024 is for credit technicals to improve, as the macro and monetary policy backdrop becomes more supportive. After a nearly two years (4Q21 through 3Q23) in which fixed income returns were underwhelming and active funds saw outflows in aggregate, the tide is turning, in our view. The environment broadly remains supportive for credit risk. Growth should slow per our economists, but not collapse, which is typically good for credit. We expect the start of rate cuts across major DM central banks (Fed, the ECB, and the BoE). We think the rates curve will bull steepen, which helps push marginal investment dollars into credit, both from domestic and overseas investors. And finally, 2023 saw a significant build-up of AUM in ultra short-dated assets (such as money market funds), which we expect will rotate out the curve as competition from "cash" eases.

To get a clearer picture, we want to highlight that Lipper changed its fund classifications, effective September 30, 2023. The definition of the "US IG" category is now narrower, given certain maturity restrictions. Now, the weekly reported IG flow numbers only reflect flows for funds that Lipper classifies as Short/Intermediate IG. Funds classified as longer-dated IG are excluded from the weekly reported headline number that is intended to reflect "US IG Corporate".

The impact of the classification change is bigger for IG ETFs, where \$96bn in AUM that was previously included for most of 2023 in the prior reported universe is now excluded. LQD is the biggest IG ETF not included in the current weekly reported universe. However, LQD saw inflows YTD (of \$4.5bn thru Jan 24). If we add back the mutual funds and ETFs that are currently excluded from Lipper to get a universe comparable to the prior methodology, YTD IG flows add up to \~ \$12.0bn, \$9.2bn driven by ETFs.

Within the Lipper mutual fund universe, we estimate that \~83% are broad fixed income Agg funds, so all inflows do not end up in corporate bonds. Getting granular data for allocations is not straightforward; our sample set of fixed income Agg funds shows that positioning shifted to higher allocations in cash in 2023 compared to 2022. ## Our views reiterated

We expect demand to rebound across the credit spectrum. But investment grade remains best positioned, considering the macro environment of lower growth and policy easing. Within IG, we think the front end should see the most benefit from "cash" rotating out the curve, from both retail and institutional investors. On the flip side, long-end demand has already been very strong, driven by yield-buyer demand, and lack of issuance. We think long-end spreads look rich, with issuance likely to come back as rates fall, and demand slows from a sequentially elevated level. Valuations have gone from rich to extreme at the long end, which makes risk-reward very unattractive.
