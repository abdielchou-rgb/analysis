# Global Macro Commentary | Global

## March 5

USTs bull-flatten as risk appetite wavers; Tech losses drive indices lower; Gilts bull-flatten before UK fiscal statement; Riskoff emanates, JPY and CHF gain; CAD weakens before BoC; 10y CGBs rally on smaller China fiscal deficit guide; DXY at 103.78 (- 0.1%); US 10y at 4.153% (-6.1bp).

• UST duration rally extends alongside an unexpected drop in the US ISM Services Employment Index into contraction at 48.0 (C: 51.4); the curve bull-flattens amidst a rare waver in risk appetite.

• Acute losses in Technology stocks (XLK: -2.46%) invoke concern that the 21% rally in the S&P 500 since October 31 has begun to show signs of exhaustion.

• Gilts bull-flatten ahead of the UK fiscal statement announcement, where our economists cite downside risks to fiscal space and caution about the fiscal drag in the UK economy this year.

• As risk-off sentiment emanates, safe havens (JPY and CHF) exhibit mild outperformance in the G10.

• CAD (-0.2%) flashes modest weakness against USD ahead of the March BoC meeting, where our economists expect the Bank to keep the policy rate at 5.00% (C: 5.00%).

• 10y CGBs rally 3bp, the most since February 7, to 2.33% amidst the smaller-than-expected fiscal deficit announced at China's NPC meeting.

Volatility in the NY session oriented around the 10:00am ET release of the US ISM Services PMI, which fell to 52.6 in February (C: 53.0; P: 53.4). The most concerning aspect of the report for the US economy was the unexpected drop in the employment subindex to 48.0 (C: 51.4; P: 50.5) – the second contraction in three months. Comments from respondents noted “Employers remain cautious about hiring direct employees and are considering utilizing contract labor to cover project and interim work demands as concerns about the economy continue to be front of mind.”

The prevailing rally in US Treasuries immediately extended to session highs. As riskoff sentiment began to emanate across macro markets, the long-end began to outperform and the curve bull-flattened even though duration moved off session highs. As 5y Treasuries (4.15%, -5.8bp d/d) have now fully retraced the post-January CPI sell-off, key catalysts that will shape the next direction of yields will start with labor market data releases and continue with Fed Chair Powell’s Semi-Annual Monetary Policy Report (Humphrey-Hawkins testimony) to the House Financial Services Committee on Wednesday. There is not a scheduled time for the text of Powell’s prepared remarks to be released.

Sharp declines in Treasury yields offset weaker equity prices and left the US dollar mixed against G10 peers and DXY USD Index (-0.1%) slightly lower. This left other safe havens, JPY and CHF, the preferred risk-off expressions. The risk-sensitive Antipodean currencies finished the session with mild losses.

Acute losses in Technology stocks (XLK: -2.46%) invoked concern that the 21% rally in the S&P 500 since October 31 has begun to show signs of exhaustion. The Nasdaq 100 (-1.80%) had its largest loss in over a month and relatively underperformed the S&P 500 (-1.02%).

Focus now turns to the political spectrum as results of the Super Tuesday US primary elections roll in.

## Japan:

Preliminary February Tokyo CPI was almost in line with consensus expectations, but weak price action in USTs overnight on Monday led to a broad JGB sell-off from the Tokyo open on Tuesday. Market participants were focused on the 10y JGB auction, which was surprisingly strong with only a 4c tail from average prices. The resultant demand to square short positions drove a broad rally, led by the belly of the curve. Long-end JGBs continued to lag ahead of the 30y JGB auction on Thursday. 10y JGBs rallied by 1.0bp d/d to 0.700% on a simple yield basis, while 30y JGBs rallied by 0.5bp d/d to 1.76%.

TONA OIS bull-flattened, with the long-end better received. A tighter JSCC-LCH basis suggested the overseas community seemingly received the long-end. Long-end ASW continued to cheapen. XCCY basis essentially traded sideways across the curve.

During Tokyo hours, CCY/JPY pairs traded range-bound despite the gradual recovery in USTs. Preliminary February Tokyo CPI data was almost in line with consensus

expectations, thus the impact in the FX market was limited. Other CCY/JPY pairs also ground lower alongside weak performance of risk assets. Risk-sensitive currencies particularly underperformed, and AUD/JPY sold off to 97.50.

## Europe:

EUR closed flat due to the ISM-driven rally in USTs, after it had traded \~0.1% lower. NOK (+0.1%) gains were hampered by a sell-off in crude oil. SEK closed flat, as its sensitivity to equities counterbalanced its sensitivity to US rates. GBP (+0.2%) continued to outperform ahead of the UK budget announcement on Wednesday. CHF (+0.1%) also gained against the US dollar, supported by a small hawkish repricing of the front-end. Market pricing implies a March SNB cut as 50:50.

In a quiet London session, the rally in European duration was led by the long-end of the curve (EUR 2s10s: -3bp; 10s30s: -0.5bp). In the London afternoon, soft US data spurred the next leg of the rally, and 10y Bunds closed the London session -6.8bp d/ d at 2.32%. Initially, the periphery outperformed core EGBs and the 10y BTP-Bund spread reached 136bp. However, the soft US data drove a sell-off in equities, which weighed on risk assets and BTPs. Overall, the 10y BTP-Bund spread closed the London session 2bp tighter at 139bp. In core ASW, Schatz traded sideways but rallied (+1.5bp) in the London afternoon, likely driven by the equity sell-off. Ahead of the ECB meeting on Thursday, market pricing implies a hold for the March meeting with \~95bp of rate cuts priced in 2024.

## Dollar Bloc:

G10 currencies traded in tight ranges and ended the London session close to opening levels, despite release of the US ISM Services PMI, which induced a shortlived US dollar sell-off. The DXY USD Index (-0.1%) sold off, weighed down by lower US yields despite the equity sell-off.

The Antipodeans ended the session on level terms with the open after a sell-off overnight on weaker sentiment in China and broader Asia markets. US dollar weakness ultimately allowed the Antipodean currencies to retrace losses. CAD followed much of the G10, flat against the US dollar.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly amid risk-off sentiment and as China’s NPC fiscal deficit fell short of consensus expectations. USD/CNH continued to hover \~7.21 despite the sell-off in the Hang Seng Index (-2.6%), as the USD/CNY fixing remained sticky at \~7.10. MYR underperformed, down 0.3% after outperformance in recent weeks. KRW and TWD were 0.2% weaker, alongside the weak risk appetite. USD/ IDR continued to rise, by 0.2% to 15,771, as INDOGB continued to sell-off (10y: +1bp) amid concerns around an easier fiscal stance.

Most AxJ rates rallied given the weak risk appetite. In China, 10y CGBs rallied 3bp, the most since February 7, to 2.33% amid the smaller-than-expected fiscal deficit from the NPC meeting. 30y CGBs remained steady at 2.48%. In India, 10y G-sec yields remained stable at 7.06%, with limited reaction to reports that India was added to the Bloomberg EM local currency index from January 2025. Singapore and

Korea rates outperformed, as 10y swap yields fell 2bp and curves bull-flattened following the trend in US rates. Hong Kong rates underperformed, as 2y swap yields rose 2bp, possibly aided by reports of a potential tech-related IPO.

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
