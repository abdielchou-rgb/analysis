# Global Macro Commentary | Global

## March 1

USTs bull-steepen after ISM PMI drop; Fed's Waller talks UST holdings composition, 2y swap spreads widen; JPY retreats after Ueda comments; US-EU rate differentials tighten, EUR/USD rises; NOK exhibits broad strength; COP lags, despite oil rally; DXY at 103.89 (-0.3%); US 10y at 4.180% (-7.0bp).

• US Treasuries bull-steepen after an unexpected faster pace of contraction in US manufacturing, as the ISM Manufacturing PMI falls to 47.8 (P: 49.1); markets increase odds for June rate cut.

• 2y UST swap spreads widen \~2bp, after Fed Governor Waller advocates for a "shift in Treasury holdings toward a larger share of shorter-dated Treasury securities."

• JPY retraces gains from the prior session after BoJ Governor Ueda says he does not believe the 2% price target is in sight, a different view than BoJ Board Member Takata.

• EUR/USD (+0.3%) rises amid a considerable tightening in US-European interest rate differentials.

• A resurgent NOK leads G10 gains against USD, amidst higher oil prices, a broad advance in equities, and a rise in the Norwegian Manufacturing PMI for February.

• COP (-0.6%) underperforms LatAm peers, despite the 2.2% rally in WTI crude oil.

## Japan:

JGBs rallied from the Tokyo morning following dovish comments from BoJ Governor Ueda. Long-end JGBs retraced some of their weakness from the prior session amidst demand from real money market participants to buy the dip. Strong Nikkei (+1.90%) performance appeared to raise discussion about additional rebalancing from equity into bonds; 30y JGBs rallied by 1.5bp d/d to 1.74% on a simple yield basis. 2y JGBs were also supported as this tenor has cheapened a lot over the past month; 2y JGBs rallied by 1.0bp d/d to 0.175%. The belly of the curve continued to underperform ahead of the 10y JGB auction on Tuesday; 10y JGBs sold off by 0.5bp d/d to 0.715%. TONA OIS rallied broadly and continued to outperform matched maturity JGBs and ASW cheapened on a broad basis. XCCY basis tightened, led by the long-end of the curve.

After BoJ Governor Ueda communicated a different message than BoJ Board Member Takata, JPY retraced its strength from the prior session at the Tokyo open on Friday. Ueda said he still does not believe the 2% price target is in sight. USD/JPY ground higher toward 150.50 into the London morning and AUD/JPY approached 98.00. However, a weak Euro Area CPI reading led to a rally in overseas duration and JPY partially retraced its weakness from Tokyo trading hours.

## Europe:

Price action in G10 currencies was choppy in the London session. NOK (+1.0%) reversed the whole week’s worth of losses, driven PMI data and a significant rally in oil prices. SEK (+0.5%) also gained but did not benefit from the same tailwind in oil prices, as its strength came from stronger PMIs and beta to higher US equities. EUR struggled to find direction before US ISM PMIs came in below-consensus. As UST yields fell, this drove a sell-off in the US dollar and EUR/USD (+0.3%) advanced. CHF (+0.1%) gains were overshadowed by the announcement of SNB Chairman Jordan’s resignation at the end of September.

European duration traded sideways on Friday amid a plethora of data releases in

Europe and the US. Euro Area CPI came in at 2.6% y/y in February (C: 2.5%; P: 2.8%) while Core came in at 3.1% y/y in February (C: 2.9%; P: 3.3%). In the London afternoon, US ISM Manufacturing and Prices Paid surprised to the downside, which spurred a bull-steepening of the US Treasury curve. European duration rallied as 10y Bunds closed little changed at 2.41%; the 10y UST-Bund spread tightened 6bp to 178bp. In swaps, 2s10s was little changed while 10s30s steepened 0.5bp. Schatz ASW richened by 1bp but the rest of the complex continued to cheapen (Bund ASW: -0.5bp). EGB spreads widened, the 10y BTP-Bund spread was 4bp wider to 147bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

Price action in the Antipodean currencies was dictated by the US dollar, with AUD (+0.5%) and NZD (+0.3%) both stronger. JPY (-0.1%) was the only G10 currency which ended weaker against USD. The DXY USD Index closed the NY session 0.2% lower.

## Asia:

AxJ currencies exhibited mixed performance, but most were stable. CNH weakened by 0.1%, despite stronger-than-expected February PMIs and gains in the local equity markets. USD/INR opened \~0.1% lower following India’s strong 4Q23 GDP data released Thursday after Asia market hours, but eventually finished the session unchanged at 82.9. KRW was the main underperformer in the region, down 0.3% to 1,335 amid a local holiday. PHP outperformed, up 0.3% to 56.02.

AxJ rates exhibited mixed performance. China rates underperformed; the 10y CGB yield rose 3bp to 2.38%. 5y NDIRS yields rose 4bp and the 1s5s curve bearsteepened 1bp. In Indonesia, 10y INDOGB sold off 2bp as February headline inflation was higher-than-expected. India rates outperformed despite strong 4Q23 GDP data. 10y G-Sec rallied 2bp; the NDOIS curve bull-flattened, 5y yields fell 3bp.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mixed. COP (-0.6%) underperformed, despite a \~2% increase in the price of oil. CLP (+0.1%) finished largely unchanged after opening the session \~0.6% stronger. BRL (+0.3%) and MXN (+0.2%) strengthened mildly. LatAm rates rallied, driven mostly by US rates. The IBR curve outperformed peers in the belly (5y: -7bp), while CAM had gains concentrated in the front-end (2y: -4bp). TIIEs underperformed peers but still rallied and ended \~2bp lower with few local drivers.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• BoJ Governor Ueda answered questions after a G20 meeting. Although BoJ Board Member Takata said the 2% price stability target has finally come into sight on Tuesday, Ueda said he does not believe the 2% price target is in sight and that he will closely monitor wage growth developments

• RBNZ Governor Orr remarked that monetary policy will need to stay restrictive for some time before normalizing “next year, or towards the end of this year.” Although, Orr did note inflation has declined and is declining further

• SNB Governing Board Chairman Jordan announced he would step down from his duties at the end of September. After 12 years in this role, he cited current price and financial stability in Switzerland as making it the right time to end his tenure

• Fed Governor Waller believes moving the balance sheet more toward T-bills would shift the maturity structure more towards the Fed’s Policy rate

• Dallas Fed President Logan said the Fed should slow asset runoff as reverse repo falls, but reiterated slowing the balance sheet runoff does not mean stopping

• Chicago Fed President Goolsbee said the Fed should keep an eye on housing inflation. Nonetheless, he said he would not be surprised if January inflation was noise and if inflation keeps falling, the Fed must think about jobs

• The Fed released its Monetary Policy Report ahead of Fed Chair Powell’s testimonies next week. Two highlights include: Caution against using shorter term metrics for measuring core inflation such as the six month annual rate; A handful of simple policy rules suggest the Fed’s current stance on policy is appropriate

## Emerging Markets

• In order to address difficulties many tourists encounter when using their own credit cards and currencies, the PBOC recently called a conference of financial institutions to discuss ways to provide more convenient payment methods for foreign visitors in the near future. As the amount spent by international tourists continues to rise, regulations will enhance acceptance of foreign cards at businesses – a major issue in the past. They will also make it easier for foreigners to convert modest quantities of cash at foreign exchange dealers

• RBI Deputy Governor Rao said central bank communication has evolved to become more transparent over the decades and the RBI has undertaken concerted efforts to demystify mandates and functioning of the Reserve Bank. Rao added "expectations management" by the central banks through effective and credible communication can increase the effectiveness of policy measures

• BNM stated it is closely monitoring how MYR weakness is affecting prices. It anticipates inflation will stay modest in 2024, and the ringgit's weakness will not impact the inflation outlook significantly. It noted historically, MYR’s effect on prices has been low, with core inflation y/y rising on average by 0.2ppt for every 5% depreciation in MYR/USD. It also expects easing production costs to partly offset FX pressure on import costs

• BCB Member Galipolo commented the central bank at some point will have to remove the plural from its forward guidance where it has guided toward 50bp cuts in the coming meetings. He also noted it is too early to think about the terminal rate and inflation data will continue to guide them. Markets currently price the slowdown in the pace of cuts at the June BCB meeting (-35bp)

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
