In the NY morning, USTs gradually drifted higher alongside the sell-off in European government bonds. The main event of the session was Fed Chair Powell’s testimony to the Senate Banking and Housing Committee.

Powell’s prepared testimony continued to emphasize the two-sided risks of the policy rate, noting that reducing policy restraint too soon could “stall or even reverse the progress we have seen on inflation,” while highlighting that “elevated inflation is not the only risk we face” given the cooling in the labor market. He reiterated that the labor market is “strong, but not overheated,” while adding that it has come into better balance as he described current conditions as like those in 2019. He continued to emphasize the need to make decisions meeting by meeting; however, his prepared testimony made no mention of the committee remaining “highly attentive to inflation risks,” which was mentioned in the FOMC statement. As a result, many market participants felt the remarks leaned dovish, and USTs briefly rallied following the release of the testimony.

During his testimony to Congress, Powell did not offer any timeline for rate cuts. However, he did state that it is unlikely for the next policy move to be a rate hike. He acknowledged that recent inflation data has shown modest further progress and that the Fed continues to need more good data to strengthen its confidence in inflation returning to 2%. When asked about Basel III, Powell said the Fed and other regulators are close to finalizing changes to the proposal.

Overall, the remarks largely reiterated prior sentiment, but continued to emphasize that policymakers are increasingly wary of potential risks to the labor market. The testimony also did not push back on the possibility of a September rate cut, for which market-implied pricing currently indicates a \~75% probability.

Following Powell’s testimony, USTs continued to gradually cheapen into the NY afternoon and ahead of the 3y auction given that Fed sentiment largely remained unchanged. The \$58bn 3y auction was met with solid demand and stopped-out 0.8bp through the when-issued yield. The strong results mainly stemmed from demand from direct bidders whose allotment grew to 21% (P: 16%). Allotment to indirect participants remained steady at 64%, which left dealers with just 15%, below this year’s 17% average. The strong results supported the front end of the curve, which ended the day modestly richer, while the rest of the curve cheapened.

In the NY morning, long-end ASW spreads widened after media reports that the Fed is mulling changes to G-SIB capital surcharge calculations. Throughout the rest of the session, the knee-jerk move retraced and long-end spreads ended just \~0.5bp wider.

Once again, there was a pickup in usage of the Standing Repo Facility (SRF) to \~ \$100mn, which suggested the presence of a small value exercise by counterparties to test functionality. There has now been a one-day uptick in usage of the SRF every month since February, suggesting banks are continuing to test their systems. Currently, there are 33 eligible SRF counterparties.

Equities gained with the S&P 500 (+0.1%) up for a sixth straight session, the longest streak of consecutive gains since January. Financials led the gains given reports on

potential easing of capital restrictions as well as Fed Chair Powell’s remarks to Congress surrounding potential rule changes to Basel III. Oil futures fell, with WTI crude oil futures down -1.1% after Hurricane Beryl seemed to do little damage to energy facilities.

## Japan:

In the Tokyo morning, JGBs sold off across the curve ahead of the 5y JGB auction and the BoJ’s meeting with market participants. Market participants seemingly had concerns surrounding the supply and demand of JGBs not only in the super longend, but the belly as well amid uncertainty on the BoJ’s bond-buying reduction plan. The 5y auction was stronger than expected, with a cut-off price of 99.93 (C: 99.89), due to demand from real money market participants. The 5y sector started to outperform into the Tokyo close. Meanwhile, long-end JGBs lagged ahead of the 20y auction on Thursday. By the end of the Tokyo session, the JGB curve twiststeepened; 5y JGB rallied 2.0bp to 0.57%, 10y JGB rallied 1.5bp to 1.07%, and 40y JGB sold off 1.5bp to 2.45% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve twist-flattened and outperformed JGBs on the long end. XCCY basis tightened especially on the longer end of the curve. During the Tokyo evening session, the BoJ released the survey results on bond-buying reduction, which included a wide range of options but did not indicate any specific reduction plan.

USD/JPY traded in a narrow range of 160.75 to 161.10 throughout the Tokyo hours. USD/JPY had steadily moved higher in the Tokyo morning, likely driven by demand from real money market participants for USD funding and supported by higher Japanese equity prices. The move higher in USD/JPY was limited ahead of Fed Chair Powell’s semiannual testimony to Congress, with some market participants focusing on a weakening US labor market and any hints in regard to near-term rate cuts. USD/JPY gradually moved lower toward 160.75 into the London morning. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY.

## Europe:

It was a relatively quiet day in European FX, with largely unsensational price action and sparse data. EUR posted a loss of -0.1% despite a broad-based sell-off in EGBs, which outpaced the sell-off in USTs and thus drove a widening yield differential. The biggest mover was found in Scandinavia, where the SEK strengthened +0.4% against the USD and partially reversed Monday’s late, seemingly non-fundamental driven, sell-off. NOK also posted gains but less so and rose +0.1%, thus sending NOK/SEK back toward the 0.9950 level. CHF ended the day relatively unchanged. Sterling showed a little weakness and fell -0.2% following the poor BRC data.

European duration traded weak on Tuesday and the 10y Bund underperformed on a cross-market basis, rising to 2.58% at the close. During the London session, the curve bear-steepened and the cash curve underperformed slightly as the German ASW complex closed marginally tighter. EGB spreads headed wider, led by the 10y OAT/Bund which widened by \~3.8bp to 66.4bp and erased Monday’s gains

## Asia:

AxJ currencies exhibited mixed moves ahead of Fed Chair Powell’s testimony to US Congress. CNY continued to be under some pressure and fell 0.1% to 7.272 per USD, despite the rally in both local equities (CSI 300: +1.1%) and bonds. CNH held relatively steady at 7.289 per USD. THB and PHP were the major outperformers. USD/THB continued to decline for a fifth straight day, down 0.2% to 36.40, after Thailand Prime Minister Srettha announced to disclose digital wallet details on July 24. USD/PHP slipped 0.2% to 58.44, despite the dovish tone from BSP Governor Remolona who said the Philippines could cut rates ahead of the Fed. KRW rallied modestly to 1,383 alongside BoK Governor Rhee’s comments on disinflation. TWD depreciated 0.2% to 32.56 following a third straight day of foreign equity outflows, which amounted to US\$487mn on Tuesday. INR, SGD, and MYR remained steady.

AxJ rates rallied broadly but traded in a narrow range. In China, 1y short-term CGB yields dropped 5bp to 1.54%, while 10y CGB yields fell 2bp to 2.27% as the PBoC and HKMA announced that CGBs under the Bond Connect scheme could be used as collateral for Swap Connect. Meanwhile, the 2s10s CNY NDIRS curve flattened by 2bp. In Korea, 10y KTB yields fell 2bp to 3.20%, while moves in the short-term KTB yields and KRW NDIRS curve remained subdued. India's G-sec yields held largely steady. The Hong Kong swap curve shifted lower by 2bp, while the Singapore swap rate remained unchanged. In Thailand, 2y NDOIS rates fell 2bp to 2.24%; the bond market had a few fluctuations amid Thailand’s digital wallet announcement.

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
