## Executive Summary

This marks our fourth China Equity Strategy Insight report in Morgan Stanley's "China's 3D Journey" series, following analysis of the long-term ROE trajectory of China's equity market, and a detailed comparison between China and Japan regarding stock market implications:

• China's 3D Journey: China's ROE Roadmap: Challenges & Opportunities (27 Sep 2023)

• China's 3D Journey: Japan vs. China: What Can Be Learned from Japan's ROE Dynamics Since the 1990s (15 Oct 2023)

Together with this report, we also publish China's 3D Journey: Going Global: Securing Growth in the New Era, to discuss the bigger picture around opportunities and challenges associated with Chinese companies going global, particularly against the new macro backdrop of slower domestic growth combined with rising global geopolitical complexity.

## Domestic macro conditions and global geopolitical complexity demand a customized investment approach for China Going Global

Overseas expansion – a proven bright spot amid macro deflationary concerns: We believe overseas expansion will continue as a sustainable theme for Chinese companies, which are actively trying to identify top-line and bottom-line growth drivers, given the following headwinds that they face and that we believe could be sustained in the near/ medium term:

1. Slower domestic growth and deflationary concerns: Overseas exposure and expansion has also been proven to be an effective alpha-generating strategy by the Japanese stock market back in the 1990s and early 2000s, and by the Chinese equity market over the past several years as domestic macro challenges mounted.

2. US election year pressure and rising Multipolar World complexity: Rising Multipolar World complexity and global supply chain de-risking demand a more customized stock-picking approach. 2024 is a major election year, adding further uncertainty to the outlook for direct exports given a wide range of trade policy outcomes that could arise during a potential second term for former President Trump.

## Stock-picking methodology – top-down + bottom-up + macro/quant overlay

We believe the best approach to identify the right candidates should be a combination of:

• Top-down thematic (industry dynamics, geopolitical sensitivity) +

• Bottom-up (fundamentals, overseas expansion initiatives) +

• Macro adaptability (factor screening under slower macro growth).

Step 1 – stock nomination based on top-down thematic consideration + bottom-up fundamentals: This first step based on a combined top-down and bottom-up approach yields an initial nomination list of 38 stocks in total with superior returns collectively (Exhibit 3).

Step 2 – quant factor overlay: we identify factors likely to outperform in a slowergrowth, lower yield environment, as forecasted by Morgan Stanley's economics team for China.

• Total Yield, Dividend Growth, and Sentiment Composite showed promising returns and similar results adjusted for volatility in terms of Sharpe ratio (Exhibit 11).

Step 3 – refining the stock nominations by selecting only those with greater adaptability to the new macro environment, based on their alignment with the quant factors: By choosing only companies with a z-score higher than 0, we narrow down the China Going Global selection down to 20 stocks, which constitute our first version (V1) of the Morgan Stanley China Going Global (MSCGG) stock list (Exhibit 12).

## Superior back-test results on a risk-adjusted basis

• Back-testing shows that an equally-weighted hypothetical portfolio based on the 20 stocks would have generated 4,597bps of alpha vs. MSCI China since December 31, 2022 (Exhibit 13).

• On a 12-month trailing basis and measured in USD total returns, the list would have returned 13.1% compared to MSCI China's -14.8% during the same period.

• Our quant factor overlay is able to further improve the alpha-generating capability of our thematic strategy, lifting the alpha vs. MSCI China since December 31, 2022 by 2,355bps (the gap between the 38-stock list and the 20-stock list).

## China Going Global Stock-picking Methodology

Overseas expansion – a proven bright spot amid macro deflationary concerns: As discussed in great detail in our Insight report (see Going Global: Securing Growth in the New Era), which is published together with this Insight, we believe overseas expansion will continue as a sustainable theme for Chinese companies, which are actively trying to identify top-line and bottom-line growth drivers, especially as the domestic market could face near/medium-term growth challenges against the 3D (Debt, Demographics, Deflation) backdrop. This has also been proven to be an effective alpha-generating strategy by the Japanese stock market back in the 1990s and early 2000s, and by the Chinese equity market over the past several years as domestic macro challenges mounted.

## Rising Multipolar World complexity demands a more customized stock-picking

approach: However, a simple approach of investing in stocks with large foreign revenue exposure may not work as well in the future, given rapidly rising geopolitical complexity as the Multipolar World thesis plays out further and supply chain de-risking takes place globally. 2024 as a major election year also adds further uncertainty to the outlook for direct exports, given a wide range of trade policy outcomes from a potential second term for Donald Trump.

Therefore, we believe the best approach to identify the right candidates should be a combination of:

• Top-down thematic (industry dynamics, geopolitical sensitivity exposure) +

• Bottom-up (company specific fundamentals, overseas expansion initiatives) +

• Macro adaptability (factor screening of those better positioned under slower macro growth).

## Step 1 – stock nomination based on top-down thematic consideration + bottom-up fundamentals

We start off with the entire Morgan Stanley Chinese equity coverage, and take the following dynamics into consideration when deciding whether or not a stock should be nominated as a outstanding beneficiary of future overseas expansion initiatives:

• Existing foreign revenue exposure and upcoming expansion plans;

• Whether future overseas expansion efforts could drive a meaningful difference to the company's top-line as well as bottom-line growth, and/or ROE improvement;

• Whether the company is in a sector/industry well positioned for overseas expansion;

• Potential susceptibility to trade/investment restrictions, at either the industry or company level and what the contingency plans are;

• Company-specific fundamentals, business/industry/macro cycles and implications for operations and stock market performance.

For detailed industry-level positioning and future outlooks, please visit the following section in this report: Industry Outlooks – Adapting to Overseas Opportunities .

• On a 12-month trailing basis and measured in USD total returns, the 38-stock list would have returned -2.1% compared to MSCI China's -14.8% during the same testing period;

• The 38-stock hypothetical portfolio offers much higher Sharpe ratios compared to MSCI China on a one-year, three-year, and five-year basis.

## Step 2 – quant factor overlay: identifying factors that tend to outperform in a slower-growth, lower-yield environment

In this section, we try to identify the quant factors that tend to be associated with outperforming stocks in a slower-growth and lower-yield macro environment...

...which is expected to be the case over the next 9-12 months, as forecasted by Morgan Stanley's economics team for China.

The goal is to find those factors and then assess which stock nominations are better aligned, indicating a higher chance of outperforming their peers subsequently, everything else being equal.

Our quant analysis is based on factor back-testing history in China's equity market since 2006. We grouped periods by quarterly changes in two variables:

## 1. Real GDP Growth YoY%

2. China's 10-year yield (China 10Y yield)

The aim: to identify favorable factors that match the base case assumptions of our economists for China.

## Macro indicators and regime distribution

We selected Real GDP Growth YoY% and China 10Y yield for regime classifications. We see Real GDP Growth YoY% as a good representation of overall economic activity and China 10Y yield as a proxy for liquidity and inflation expectations. We used quarterly changes in macro indicators vs. quarterly factor return.

There are a total of 72 quarters since 2006 and regime classifications are distributed quite evenly. We see four fewer quarters under increasing real GDP growth % with decreasing China 10Y yield and four additional quarters under decreasing real GDP growth % with

decreasing China 10Y yield vs. an average of 18 quarter counts.

## Launching the Morgan Stanley China Going Global Stock List – MSCGG V1

We launch the Moran Stanley China Going Global 20 stocks list as MSCGG V1( Exhibit 13 ): These 20 stocks represent our highest-conviction ideas as beneficiaries of overseas expansion initiatives. They are most likely to outperform their peers with strong fundamentals, as well as superior alignment with quant factors that we believe would perform well in the forthcoming macro environment.

## Proven capability of even better alpha generation with lower volatility

We have performed back-testing of the performance of these 20 Going Global stocks as an equal-weighted portfolio with dividends reinvested, both in absolute terms and relative to MSCI China benchmark. The start of the testing period is set at December 31, 2022. The portfolio would be rebalanced if:

1. New stocks are added (listed) and;

2. At year-end over the back-testing period.

The rebalancing is to avoid overly concentrated positions in certain stocks, which is relevant for investors, as their portfolios are often subject to single-stock index weight thresholds. We acknowledge that this exercise is backward-looking – we have selected these stocks now in April 2024, but would like to see how they have performed as a group over the medium-term past.

Our China Going Global stock selection approach has demonstrated solid capability of generating alpha: back-test shows that an equally-weighted hypothetical portfolio based on the 20 stocks would have generated 4,597bps of alpha vs. MSCI China since December 31, 2022: As of April 9, 2024, the list of nominated stocks with global revenue exposure has generated an absolute return of 34.1% since the start of the testing period (December 31, 2022), vs. MSCI China's -11.9% ( Exhibit 14 ). On a 12-month trailing basis and measured in USD total returns, the list would have returned 13.1% compared to MSCI China's -14.8% during the same period.

## The China Going Global theme has a clear skew towards Consumer and IT

Our 20-stock MSCGG V1 (Morgan Stanley China Going Global) list is rebalanced from a sector allocation perspective. Looking into sector allocation of the two groups (original sector bottom-up recommendation of 38 vs. final MSCGG list of 20), the two lists demonstrate relatively similar sector distribution, measured by number of companies (Exhibit 19). Some quick observations and rationales behind this:

• Consumer Discretionary accounts for 32% and 40% for the sector weight of the 38-stock list and 20-stock list, respectively;

• IT accounts for 29% and 35%, respectively;

• Industrials accounts for 18% and 10%, respectively, and;

• Other sectors combine less than 10% for both lists.

The China Going Global theme tends to have a much higher skew towards Consumer Discretionary and IT, compared to the broad MSCI China universe: This makes sense to us given that consumer electronics devices, and tech hardware products have been rising as new dominant export categories. In addition, consumer durable products and ecommerce players have also been accelerating their overseas expansion efforts (as part of Consumer Discretionary).

Industrials follows as the third-largest sector/industry exposure within the theme. Again this makes sense because China has been ramping up its technology, cost, and manufacturing capacity advantage in areas such as EV components, batteries, solar, machinery, and other higher-end industrial products.

What's important to note is that despite a very similar sector distribution, our quant factor screening overlay is able to further improve the alpha-generating capability of our thematic strategy, lifting the alpha vs. MSCI China since December 31, 2022 to 4,597bps, from an already very impressive 2,242bps achieved by the bottom-up nomination list.

## Industry View

In 2023, China exported 4.1mn units of passenger vehicles, growing 64% YoY, accounting for 16% of PV wholesale, while exports accounted for only 11% of PV sales in 2022. Overall, we expect exports and offshore expansion to be a key volume driver for China’s auto OEMs and parts suppliers in 2024, especially in view of slowing domestic demand against a weak macro backdrop.

Growing protectionism from the West: While China's PV exports grew 64% YoY in 2023, we anticipate a bumpy road ahead for Chinese OEMs making inroads into developed markets, especially into the US and the EU. In particular, the EU anti-subsidy probe could cast a shadow over the growth prospects of Chinese EV makers aiming for rapid expansion in Europe, where we have already seen countries like France excluding Chinese EVs from local subsidies.

We expect a potential tariff hike by the EU sometime later this year vs. 10% now: However, based on our conversations with local OEMs, most are confident they can navigate through a sub-20% tariff without meaningfully altering their go-EU strategies, thanks to their cost-competitive supply chains in China and EM, which provide them with a favorable cost structure without government subsidy. That said, Chinese EV makers' "go global" strategy is not just about dumping cheap capacity, but rather an eventual shift towards transplanting and foreign production, as seen in Japanese and Korean OEMs between the 1970s and 1990s. In the near term, we expect Chinese OEMs to pivot to EM markets like ASEAN and Latin America, while accelerating their plans to localize production in Europe. Hence, the EU's action may slow China's EV push, but is unlikely to halt it ultimately, in our view.

Minth (0425.HK) – 35%+ growth of overseas revenue with margin recovery in 2024: Minth expects revenue to grow 20% in 2024, with 10%+ growth from China and 35%+ growth from overseas, respectively, amid share expansion. Given Minth's long-standing ties with German, Japanese, and US OEMs, we remain constructive on Minth's ability to increase value content through cross-selling its diverse product portfolio of aluminum parts, plastic parts, and battery housing overseas.

Amid the push for global expansion, Minth has realized 70% and 50% production localization in North America and Europe and guided that margins will continue to increase with enhanced efficiency. Currently, Minth's factories in Mexico and Thailand are all getting closer to the same GpM levels achieved by their domestic factories, while the US plants for plastic products are still under pressure, hovering in the single-digit range. We expect better scale and improving yield rates to underpin margin recovery to 28-30% for its traditional business in 2024.

Joyson (600699.SS) – overseas share gain and margin recovery in 2024: For Joyson's safety business, we expect share gain and margin recovery overseas to be key drivers in 2024. Joyson has turned profitable since 2Q22, but net margin for its safety business remained low, at <5%. Joyson Safety System's (JSS) North American business was still lossmaking despite improvements in Asian and European businesses in 3Q23. Management sees multiple cost-saving opportunities for its operations in North America in 2024, and if it executes well, we would expect to see a meaningful earnings improvement in its auto safety business from a low base in 2024.

For its auto electronics business, Joyson now has a Rmb22bn 800V-related order backlog (vs. its Rmb19-20bn auto electronics revenue target for 2024), mainly supplying to global OEMs such as Volkswagen, Mercedes, and Volvo's upcoming 800V new models in overseas markets, to support growth beyond 2025. Given Joyson's well-established production plants in the US, Mexico, Brazil, Europe, and Southeast Asia, it is well-

positioned to secure new orders from global OEMs and capture any overseas volume upside in 2024, in our view.

Chinese OEMs and EV startups: We remain constructive on the export theme among most local OEMs like BYD, GWM, Chang'an, Chery, and EV startups in the next 2-3 years, but we expect intensifying price competition in China to remain an overhang near term. Overall, we stay OW on EV startups Li Auto, XPeng, and NIO given reduced expectations and their rapid model cycle. We see divergent short-term or investment opportunities while laggards in the startup group are being shaken out. That said, we are well aware that profitability and cash flow concerns linger among EV startups amid fierce competition and a tight credit environment.

Bear case – full restriction on China-related smart components: While the US Inflation Reduction Act mostly restricts China-sourced battery materials and components, it’s worth highlighting that according to Bloomberg, the Biden administration has been considering restrictions on imports of Chinese “smart cars” and related components, no matter where they’re finally assembled. We think it is possible that non-battery auto components could start to face tighter restrictions from the West. Currently, Chinese auto parts suppliers like Sanhua, Tuopu, Xusheng, Joyson, and Minth all supply to US OEMs either through exports or production in Mexico/US, which could face growing headwinds if the US chooses to limit China-affiliated auto parts in the future. Under this scenario, we would recommend focusing on suppliers with greater domestic exposure like Xingyu and Desay.

## Industry View

The key function of the financial system in China is to support the real economy via credit supply and risk control. Thus the system is generally more domestic-focused by nature, with most business conducted onshore in China. While banks have actively explored global business opportunities, the underlying theme still lies in helping domestic companies to step out of China or stabilizing RMB via financial support.

Futu (FUTU.O) – existing overseas AUM exposure quite small but gaining more importance; the penetration of Japan's retail brokerage market could be the growth driver in coming years: Currently AUM from clients outside of Greater China accounts for only a mid-single-digit percentage of total, but will become increasingly more important for future growth given that onshore China can no longer provide new client and AUM growth due to regulatory reasons. The Japanese retail brokerage market looks to be a major potentia growth driver for Futu's overseas expansion story. There are over 14mn retail investors in Japan and the market is dominated by a small number of incumbent players. Localized strategy will be key because Japan's retail brokerage market has unique features compared to HK and other overseas markets in which Futu has been operating: 1) slightly older demographics, 2) highly concentrated competitive landscape, 3) the likely trend of zero commission, and 4) the importance of comprehensive financial product offerings.

We think Futu management is very dedicated and taking a long-term view in developing the Japanese retail brokerage business, focusing on its competitive strength in good trading experience and enhancing its product offerings. Nevertheless, we think it might take some time to see the strategy to work out and monetization would most likely not be a priority, at least in 2024. We expect Futu to focus on building its user and paying client base with patience.

Futu is showing solid progress in acquiring users for its stock trading app in Japan. Yet it remains to be seen how fast it can ramp up the conversion of users to paying clients in 2024, especially how to take market share from incumbent players with strong ecosystems. There's also debate on future monetization efficiency – brokerage commissions for Japanese stocks are approaching zero, and therefore Futu will have to build out more comprehensive offerings in order to generate meaningful revenue.

## Industry View

In the realm of basic materials, metal miners (copper, gold, and lithium) have been early to step outside of China to look for upstream resources, which provide them advantages in fast volume growth over their domestic peers. Industry leaders in new materials (glass fiber, rare earth magnets) also utilize their cost competitiveness and technological advantages to go global via both exports and building overseas factories. In light of the sluggish building materials demand from domestic property markets, some cement producers are also exploring emerging markets (Africa, Central Asia, Southeast Asia) with fast demand growth and better profitability.

We prefer JL Mag, Jushi, and Huaxin Cement: These companies will continue to benefit from their overseas expansion. We also like Zijin, CMOC, and Tianqi for their overseas growth profiles. We think political and regulatory risks would be major concerns regarding overseas expansion of these materials companies, because their overseas footprints mainly focus on less developed countries.

JL Mag (6680.HK/300748.SZ) – largest rare earth magnet producer globally; Mexican plant from 2025: JL Mag is the largest producer globally of rare earth magnets for EVs (used in drive motors), with \~30% market share in 1H23. The company's customers include all of the top 10 EV producers globally, including BYD and Tesla, and four of the top five wind turbine producers globally. JL Mag's overseas revenue percentage has been growing quickly from 10% in 2021 to 22% in 1H23, following overseas demand from EV and wind power. We expect its overseas revenue proportion to continue to grow, as it is the only Chinese rare earth magnets producer to build a factory overseas, and its Mexican plant will commence in 2025 to better satisfy growing demand from its American customers.

We don’t expect any trade restrictions on rare earth magnets, because China encourages downstream development of the rare earth supply chain to maximize value, while foreign producers find it hard and slow to expand capacity given high production cost and limited supply of rare earth metals.

In addition, rare earth magnets have advantages in both performance and production cost (especially when rare earth prices have come down to reasonable levels). Thus, we do not see the “zero rare earth” alternative solution as a real risk to the industry and JL Mag.

Huaxin Cement (600801.SS) – further overseas expansion planned: As of end-1H23, Huaxin had overseas cement production capacity of 17mnt, compared with its total cement production capacity of 122mnt (including joint ventures). In addition, it will further expand its overseas cement exposure, while maintaining focus on overall risk diversification. Huaxin notes that cement consumption per capita in some overseas countries is not as high as in others, potentially suggesting future demand upside. In addition, we believe that it has the capability to consolidate resources in overseas markets, and construct clinker and cement capacity to match up with local market demand.

China Jushi (600176.SS) – global footprint to capture high-end demand as well as avoid trade barriers: Roughly \~30% of Jushi's revenue is from overseas markets, mainly the EU and US. The company has two production bases overseas, in Egypt and the US, with a total constructed capacity of \~360kt and 96kt, respectively. Meanwhile, the company is mulling whether to construct a new plant overseas to further capture the solid demand in developed markets as well as to avoid the trading barriers imposed on Chinese domesti glass fiber products by the EU and US. Raising foreign exposure would help volume and earnings growth for the company because the products sold overseas are more targeted toward high-end markets with higher prices and margins.

## Other stocks with overseas expansion potential

Zijin Mining (2899.HK/601899.SS) – operates in 16 countries, feeding 40% of incremental global copper demand: Since Zijin's first gold mine, Zijinshan in Fujian, commenced in 1993, the company has transformed from a domestic gold miner into a major global gold and copper producer. Domestic earnings contributions has shrunk from >90% in 2015 to 59% in 2022. This was achieved via a number of acquisitions, including its first sizable purchase – Kamoa in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) via a joint venture with Ivanhoe in 2015. Through developing greenfield and brownfield projects, Zijin's copper output now accounts for 40% of the incremental copper volume globally. Zijin increased its copper output from 110kt in 2012 to 880kt in 2022, ranking it sixth among global copper miners. Currently Zijin operates in 16 countries, mainly gold and copper mines, but it does have some zinc and lithium mines.

CMOC (3993.HK/603993.SS) – two global copper-cobalt projects ramping up: CMOC is currently in the process of ramping up its two global copper-cobalt projects in the DRC. Upon the full ramp-up of these two projects, we expect CMOC's copper and cobalt production in the DRC to increase to 550-600kt and 70kt, respectively, from 250kt and 20kt in 2022, which will help boost CMOC's overseas revenue contribution notably. This also shows CMOC's higher volume CAGR than its global peers. Meanwhile, larger trading business carried out by IXM, CMOC's trading arm, will also lead to higher overseas revenue exposure.

Tianqi Lithium (9696.HK/002466.SZ) – Australian lithium hydroxide project ongoing: Tianqi Lithium is currently in the process of commissioning of Kwinana Stage I lithium hydroxide project (24ktpa) in Australia, and has plans to construct the Stage II project (another 24ktpa). According to IGO (Tianqi's counterparty in the Kiwnana project), it expects production rates at Stage I to reach approximately 50% of nameplate capacity by the end of the December quarter, subject to successful rectification works being performed and a smooth resumption of operations after the major shutdown during the quarter. As of now, Tianqi is currently primarily exposed to the domestic lithium carbonate market, while the ramp-up of the Kwinana lithium hydroxide project shall help it increase the foreign revenue exposure accordingly.

## Industry View

Exploration and expansion of overseas markets is becoming a rising and more important growth driver for the consumer sector, which previously was driven mostly by domestic demand. Having said that, top-down quantification of the implications for the broad sector is difficult at this early phase, given that sub categories and individual companies are at very different stages of going abroad beyond the Chinese market, as shown in Exhibit 62 and Exhibit 19 . As such, the investment thesis is driven bottom-up by the dynamics of companies in terms of business nature, execution capability, competitiveness, and relevance of overseas business to the incremental growth in 2024, as we summarize below.

A mild recovery with a slow start for domestic consumption: Macro uncertainties and weak sentiment are still overhangs in 4Q23/1Q24 Demand/pricing stabilization and more disciplined supply could be positive catalysts for 2Q24 and onwards to support gradual growth recovery. Against such a backdrop, we identify four key investment themes for stock selection with overseas opportunity being one of them: 1) strong pricing power or value proposition to defend against a general environment of deflation; 2) market share gain with superior margin control; 3) operational turnaround via self-help; and 4) overseas exposure.

We believe companies that could benefit from overseas demand growth or gain market share would continue to outperform peers.

Samsonite, Shenzhou, Pop Mart, ANTA, Haier, Midea, Roborock, and Ecovacs are the key picks for the overseas growth theme.

## Industry View

Overall, CXO, API, and Medtech are the segments with the most overseas exposure, largely thanks to their cost and quality edge. Innovative drugs have lower foreign exposure owing to the higher technical barriers. Amid the uncertain macro climate and weak consumption sentiment, we believe the healthcare sector could demonstrate its defensive nature in 2024.

We pinpoint three key criteria in screen stocks under the overseas growth theme:

1. Growing overseas presence in terms of sales and supply chain capability;

2. Ongoing penetration of global top-tier clients;

3. Globally recognized brand with stable operating cash flow and product iterations.

Thus, we have identified Mindray and United Imaging as the most suitable under the overseas growth thesis.

Mindray (300760.SZ) – ample room for growth with an estimated Rmb450bn overseas TAM: We see Mindray – with 39% overseas revenue contribution in 2022 and diversified product mix – potentially outperforming its domestic peers. Management maintains 2024/25 guidance of \~20% sales and profit growth, with similar growth in domestic and overseas markets in 2024.

• Domestic market: Under the anti-corruption campaign in China, many public hospital tenders were delayed into 3Q23. While we saw some positive signals of gradual tender recovery into 4Q23, it is likely to weigh on 1H24 performances given the typically long revenue recognition cycles for large medical equipment. That said, with a broad and comprehensive product layout in three different key segments, consisting of not only equipment but also consumables, we think Mindray should be more resilient compared to pure large equipment players amid the industry rectification. On a positive note, new infrastructure project numbers will peak in 2024-25, so the equipment procurement cycle is likely to remain robust until 2026, per the company. This could support domestic growth for Mindray in the medium term, in our view. This resonates with the results from our Chinese hospital AlphaWise survey showing Mindray as the most preferred medical equipment brand overall, currently and in future, with top market share of 27-28%.

• Overseas markets: With hospitals and medical institute globally turning more budget-conscious, Mindray’s quality yet costefficient products are becoming more appealing over MNC products. As a testament to its overseas development, its PMLS segment alone has tapped into 200+ new high-end customers in 9M23 and have achieved product repurchases with 400 clients. In the IVD market, Mindray expects to strengthen its capability to internally produce and supply raw materials under Hytest and attain 70% of raw material sourcing in-house by 2025. In 2024, emerging markets (e.g., Brazil, Indonesia, Australia, the Middle East, India, Russia) are expected to generate higher growth of \~25%, while developed regions could record \~15% growth. Separately, Mindray's product layout has an estimated Rmb450bn overseas TAM vs. only Rmb12bn ex-China revenue in 2022. This translates into c. 3% penetration, suggesting ample room for growth. Leveraging its integrated "equipment + software" solution, tailored sales strategy in different regions, and strengthening global supply chains, Mindray expects robust near-term overseas sales to offset domestic hiccups. In light of potential tariff hikes in the bear case amid geopolitical tension with the US, we expect manageable impact on Mindray because: 1) it only has c. 7% revenue exposure to North America; and 2) it has facilities in the US whichare capable of producing locally in the country.

United Imaging (688271.SH) – a compelling overseas expansion story: UIH's ex-China sales grew 50%+ with the percentage of total sales rising from 11.7% in 2022 to c. 15% in 2023, per management. A major portion of its overseas revenue was generated from the US (<40%), followed by APAC ex-China (c. 30%) including Southeast Asia, India, Australia, and New Zealand, as well as Europe (c. 10%). It has also identified meaningful potential opportunities in other regions such as Western Europe, the Middle East, and South America, all of which have low revenue bases and considerable headroom for growth. This gives us conviction that UIH's guidance of 50%+ p.a. ex-China growth in 2024 is achievable and that it should help to buffer any impacts domestically caused by the delay in equipment tenders under the anti-graft campaign. We expect UIH's overseas sales to further increase to c. 22%+ by 2027e, translating to Rmb5.9bn+ ex-China revenue in 2027 and a 40% CAGR, 2022-27e.

In addition, because most of UIH's manufacturing sites are located in China, the company has lower labor costs vs. global peers. As evidence, UIH's 64-row above CT scanner and 3.0T MRI scanner are priced roughly 10-25% lower than its global peers' comparable products, per the company and our channel checks. This allows UIH to maintain healthy EBIT margins despite having the highest R&D expense ratio among peers, thus supporting continuous technology iteration.

Recall that the two Biosecure bills (S.3558 and H.R.7085) introduced in December 2023 and January 2024 triggered significant market reaction and debate, and that the allegations in the bills have been refuted by WuXi AppTec and WuXi Biologics through company filings. Industry View

Overseas markets are a key growth driver in 202e: This reflects the modest economic growth outlook and intensified competitive landscape in the domestic market. We identify three key criteria on stock screen under the overseas growth theme:

1. Continued penetration of global top-tier clients with leading positions in key products;

2. Rapid product iteration to defend against intense competition among Chinese and foreign players in overseas markets;

3. Overseas sales/capacity expansion in tandem with downstream capacity relocation to overseas countries.

Hengli Hydraulic, Hongfa, Haitian, and iRay are the key picks under the overseas growth thesis: All of them provide top-quality products among their Chinese peers and are expanding in global markets. As of 2023, overseas sales contributed >25% of their total revenue, and we expect the contribution to continue to increase. Geopolitical concerns are also a consideration with these stocks; however, we think the risk is minor compared to some other areas such as EV and tech, as the share of Chinese capital goods is small in global market. Tariffs and further potential impact could be the top geopolitical risks

Hengli Hydraulic (601100.SS) – leading supplier in top-tier global machinery supply chain: Hengli has a solid overseas sales track record as a key supplier of hydraulic products (cylinders, pumps, valves) for top-tier players in the global machinery supply chain, expanding from excavator makers (such as Caterpillar) to non-excavator construction machinery makers (such as JLG, Genie), and penetrating more agricultural machine makers. We estimate a 25% CAGR for Hengli's overseas sales in 2023-25, accounting for 30% of total sales in 2025, up from 20% in 2022.

In addition, we think Hengli can continue to gain share in overseas markets, by expanding into more global top-tier clients in construction machinery, and by penetrating a new client base (e.g. PIMM, general machine tools and robotics) with new product launches (linear actuators and roller screws)

## China – Petrochemicals

Overseas opportunities for China's petrochemical products still largely depend on exports of downstream products: Although China has built up large petrochemical capacities in the past decade, it is still a net importer of major petrochemical products such as PE, PP, and PX. This is mainly the result of a product structure issue – most domestic capacities focus on low-end products while high-end products still rely on imports. Therefore, to capture the overseas market, China's chemical sector still needs to rely on the exports of downstream products, which is a function of overseas product demand and trade flow. For direct chemicals product exports, we see that Wanhua has been exporting an increasing volume of MDI to overseas markets over the years.

## China – battery value chain

Overseas expansion has uncertainty following the release of foreign guidance from the US Department of Energy, which is not positive for China's battery value chain. This is because according to the new guidance, we believe the following items will not be eligible

for EV tax credits under the IRA:

1. EV battery and component imports from China, effective 2024;

2. Critical battery mineral imports from China, effective 2025; and

3. Batteries/components from companies that are 25% or more controlled by Chinese entities (inclusive of board seats, voting rights, or equity interests).

Licensing arrangements are also involved: FEOCs can also exist in a licensing arrangement, when a Chinese entity exercises effective control over the production of critical minerals, battery components, or battery materials. If other non-FEOC entities are able to "independently operate equipment critical to production, and to access and use any intellectual property, information, and data critical to production," they can be entitled to tax credits. However, we believe this indicates that Chinese entities would have to open up their IP to non-FEOC entities, which is subject to Chinese government approval.

Impact on China's battery value chain: Many Chinese battery value chain players already had overseas plans in order to capture opportunities in overseas markets. Thus, the new FEOC rule could suggest that Chinese players need to adjust their equity stakes in overseas projects in order to fulfill the requirement under FEOC in order to obtain the subsidy. For CATL, there is still quite a lot of uncertainty in terms of whether its technology licensing agreement with Ford can be eligible for IRA credit, not to mention a potential US factory.

Stock implications: We view the latest FEOC news as negative for China's battery value chain as it increases uncertainty for a Chinese company to capture the opportunities in overseas markets. From our channel checks, currently, no Chinese value chain companies are planning new overseas capacities unless there is clarity for the requirement on Chinese companies' equity stakes. The export volume of Chinese battery value chain companies may also be hampered because those volumes will not be entitled to tax credits under the IRA

## Industry View

Chinese semiconductor companies' foreign exposure as a percentage of total revenue dropped 4.5ppt in the past four years, although their absolute foreign revenue scale registered a 13% CAGR in 2019-23. In view of headwinds, given the slower Chinese domestic macro environment and deflationary pressure, we think Chinese semiconductor companies with a global production footprint, consumer-exposed clients or products using mature nodes are facing low policy risks and can further expand business overseas and diversify from weak macro conditions domestically.

As US tech export controls continue to develop, we see chipmakers diversifying their operational footprint to meet customer demands. For example, USI is accelerating its overseas capacity expansion for Apple, its biggest customer. At the same time, foundries like SMIC and Hua Hong are enjoying stronger momentum from Chinese domestic customers given more competitive pricing and decent technology offerings, notably for lower-end chips. We therefore believe further tightening of US tech export controls should further accelerate chipmakers' expansion overseas, and vice versa.

China's IC imports fell 15.4% in value as semiconductor self-sufficiency accelerated amid US sanctions. According to data from the General Administration of Customs of China, China's IC imports dropped 15.4% in value year-on-year in US dollar terms, while the total import volume decreased 10.8%.

We see the growing trend of semi localization. However, if any Chinese semi companies can also expand their overseas markets, that would be a big plus in terms of our stock selection.

customers' need for overseas capacity. During its 2Q23 earnings call, USI suggested that it will continue to accelerate its capacity expansion outside of China – as of 2022, 65% of the capacity is located in China, and the company expects this ratio to decline to 60% in 2026. According to USI management, with an assumption of 50% overall capacity increase during 2022-26, capacity outside of China will grow 85% in the next three years, vs. only 40% within China. Part of the capacity diversification is helped by its acquisition of AsteelFlash (or FAFG), an Europe-based EMS company that accounted for \~10% of USI's total revenue in 2022. Currently, USI is constructing its second factory in Poland, and has confirmed a new site in Mexico for further expansion.

USI's global footprint should provide a competitive edge against its EMS/module assembly peers – USI better suits its customers' needs. The company has executed well on controlling the expenses amid continued diversification of capacity allocation. While USI has been accelerating its overseas expansion in the recent years, driven by acquisition and customers' requests, we don't view this as a particular strength that will drive its share price in the next 6-12 months – a diversified production footprint was already the case as of 2023.

USI's SiP (system-in-package) technology has relatively low risk from US export controls among the stocks under our coverage.

Yangjie Technology (300373.SZ) – diversified revenue exposure and global production footprint: Unlike the majority of A-share semi companies with revenue exposure purely to China, Yangjie's overseas business accounted for 31% of its total revenue in 2022, given its 2015 acquisition of MCC. It sells Yangjie's products overseas under the MCC brand. In 2022, the overseas business gross margin was 14ppt higher than domestically, because in the Chinese market the company needs to offer lower pricing compared to its foreign peers to attract local customers. Besides overseas revenue exposure, the company is also diversifying its production footprint. In April 2023, it raised US \$216mn in capital via GDR issuance in Switzerland; the proceeds will be mainly used for Vietnamese back-end fab production and overseas R&D center establishment and expansion. Yangjie's revenue has been affected by overseas market inventory digestion, but the company expects the digestion to be complete by 1Q24.

We like Yangjie as an underappreciated quality power semi play. We think it is better positioned in China's semiconductor industry because of its diversified revenue exposure and global production footprint. We believe the company has a competitive edge vs. its power semi peers in China, because it already has an overseas brand, MCC, and its back-end fab in Vietnam can facilitate its closed-loop manufacturing for overseas business and meet the growing demand of overseas clients. Thus, we think it is attractive on a 6-12 month investment horizon.

Along with the development of a multipolar world, we believe the suppliers that have further opportunities for business growth in the future are those have a setup with multiple production capacity sites – i.e. "China + 1" capacity expansion. We highlight the key companies among our Chinese tech hardware coverage:

Shenzhen Transsion (688036.SS): In the smartphone segment, we prefer Transsion because the company has 100% exposure to overseas markets. As Africa's largest smartphone player, with >40% market share by volume, we believe Transsion is well positioned to capture Africa's smartphone and IoT growth opportunities. Furthermore, we expect Transsion to continue its smartphone market share gains in other EM regions (e.g., Pakistan, Bangladesh, India, Latin America, and the Middle East). Leveraging its leading market position and large smartphone user base, we believe Transsion's Internet business also has large long-term growth potential.

What’s not priced in? Given the relatively low revenue contribution at the current stage, we believe the market has not yet priced in the significant upside potential from the African Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT) growth opportunities. In our Insight report (Well positioned for significant growth in Africa smartphone/IoT; upgrade to OW), we state that we expect Africa's IoT TAM to triple, 2022-30, reaching US\$13bn, implying a 14% CAGR. Although the IoT demand recovery could lag smartphones, Transsion has made good preparation by developing various new IoT products and expanding its distribution network in Africa during the past several years. Once the industry recovery arrives, we believe Transsion is well-positioned to gain share in the AIoT industry.

Impact from geopolitical risks: With 100% revenue exposure in non-China emerging markets, geopolitical tensions will pose risks to Transsion’s merchandise, production and distribution activities. However, Transsion’s business is well diversified in over 50 countries, so we don’t see significant exposure to any specific country, which could reduce the company’s risk from tariffs. Meanwhile, the company’s multi-brand and localization strategy could also help reduce geopolitical risks in the future.

Foxconn Industrial Internet (601138.SS): FII is a major iPhone casing supplier with \~65% supply share and a key assembly partner for enterprise servers (DELL, Oracle), cloud servers (AWS, Microsoft, Google) and networking equipment (Cisco), with >20% global supply share in each. This leads to diversified market exposure with China \~20% and non-China \~80%. FII also leverages its supply chain management expertise and has built multiple production sites worldwide, including China (\~60%), Vietnam, Mexico, and the US. We expect its business to grow along with AI server upgrades and integrated solution offerings in the long run.

## Lenovo (0992.HK): We like Lenovo in view of:

1. PC demand growth to be driven by Windows 11 and AI PC upgrades starting in 2H24;

2. Our belief that Lenovo should continue to benefit from aged PC installed bases in China, Japan, and LatAm, where it has dominant shares;

3. The launch of Lenovo’s own AI PCs in 2H24, with margins expected to be higher than its IDG (Intelligent Devices Group) average.

In terms of revenue exposure by geography, Lenovo has 75-80% non-China revenue exposure, much higher than that of other A/H share companies in our coverage. This cushions Lenovo against China’s slower macro environment and deflationary pressure.

Impact from geopolitical risks: As the largest PC OEM globally in terms of unit shipment, Lenovo’s revenue mix is well-diversified with \~23% revenue from China, \~17% from Asia (ex China), 24% from EU/Middle East/Africa and 36% from the US. By product, the company is also exposed to servers, software & services, smartphones, etc., aside from PCs, with non-PC revenue reaching 42% of the mix as of C4Q23. We believe this exposure limits geopolitical risks. Both in-house capacity of Lenovo and that of its manufacturing partners are geographically dispersed as well, lowering risks that could arise from import/export policies.

BOE Technology (000725.SZ): In the Greater China Display segment, we have a relative preference for BOE, which stems from:

1. Its strategic position to gain more share in the OLED market in the future (we estimate a \~20% market share in 2023e). It is the largest independent OLED display supplier, which presents no conflicts of interest with its customers in the end market; and 2. The company's superior financial performance and its less demanding valuation vs. its peers.

Impact from geopolitical risks: As the largest panel supplier globally (in terms of shipment), the company is exposed to multiple end applications and across all regions (end product). The diversified regional revenue exposure should limit such risks, in our view. However, we do note that all of its front-end capacity and the majority of its back-end capacity is located in China, which poses some risks should trade tensions intensify and cause changes in import/export policies.

## Industry View

Overall Chinese software and IT service players are currently not competitive in the global market owing to disadvantages in technology (compared with US software players), cost (compared with Indian IT service players), and three years of Covid disruption (could not go abroad to set up teams or marketing).

Most players in our coverage have small revenue exposure to the global market. With the slowing down of the domestic market and enterprise customers going abroad to explore new growth opportunities, Chinese software players are attaching higher and higher importance to the global market. They see less competition, greater willingness to pay, and higher acceptance of public cloud/subscription models in the global market. They would like go to ASEAN as the first stop in globalization, followed by the Middle East, other parts of Asia, and the EU.

The advantages of Chinese software/IT service players in a global market include: 1) home customers going abroad or their leading value chain in China; 2) lower pricing than global peers, which is already much higher than their domestic prices; 3) leading technology in some niche areas.

So far the technology restrictions from the US on software have not been a big issue for Chinese software players except for AI-related areas, given their greater focus on enterprise applications. But most of them have low intention to go to the US market in view of high geopolitical risk.

Among the software/IT service players with relatively big revenue exposure to the global market, we are particularly bullish on Wondershare, Tuya, and GDS.

Wondershare (300624.SZ) – a unique overseas and AI play among China's SaaS providers: We like Wondershare's \~90% overseas exposure, which allows it to enjoy better paying incentives and pricing power in the global market while still benefiting from the cheape labor costs domestically. Wondershare's successful SaaS transition should also start to yield margin benefits as renewal revenue rises in the mix. It should also take steps to reduce its reliance on performance ads in order to increase its operating leverage. AI adoption should help Wondershare accelerate its paying ratio for flagship video editing product Filmora, while new products such as Virgo could also generate new revenue. We also believe the progress of AI multimodel technology like Sora's recent demo can further lower the hurdle for video editing software users and enrich the ecosystem.

## Industry View

## Shipping: limited impacts on global shipping cycles from Chinese companies going global

Chinese shippers actively take part in global competition, so global exposure is high for the shipping segment. However, such high exposure does not necessarily promise a good proxy for China's going-global trend, in our view. China has been upgrading its exports, from low-end products to high-end products, which may not necessarily lead to an increase in export volumes. We remain conservative on containerships with high supply pressure.

We note that increasing car exports have supported the shipping rate for Ro-Ro and CMES has some exposure to that; however, this business only accounts for 8% of the company's revenue and 6% of its gross profit in 2023-25, by our estimates.

## 12-month actionable idea: COSCO SHIPPING Energy (1138.HK)

## Airlines: continued outbound travel recovery to support load factors

YTD, China's air travel demand has received strong support from post-Covid pent-up demand during peak seasons but has also faced headwinds from softer consumption growth amid macro headwinds. We expect non-domestic capacity normalization by end-2024, and we expect non-domestic travel demand to reach full normalization in 1H25. Non-domestic improvement not only contributes directly to flight revenue and commercial revenue but also helps domestic load factors and margins by moving capacity back to international routes.

## 12-month actionable idea: Spring Airlines (601021.SS)

Cross-border e-logistics: J&T to be the bright spot

Cross border e-logistics has four major parts: Chinese domestic leg, cross-border line haul, customs clearance, and overseas local fulfillment. On the value chain, we think cross-border line haul and overseas local fulfillment accounts for over 85% of the cross-border e-logistics revenue.

Line-haul: Air capacity may remain a constraint, leading to air freight rate upside if cross-border e-commerce growth remains robust.

Overseas local fulfillment: J&T is the largest express delivery player in SEA, and growing fast in LatAm and Middle East, along with China's e-commerce players. Express delivery is one of the best businesses in logistics thanks to strong economies of scale and relatively higher margins. J&T has leveraged its experience from the Chinese market and is expanding rapidly overseas.

## Spring Airlines – A consumption downgrade beneficiary

Solid operational performance in 2023: Thanks to its more aggressive capacity expansion compared with the Big 3 airlines, SPA aggressively gained market share during 2020-2022. Although SPA's market share normalized in 2023 as the Big 3 added capacity, it still remained above its pre-Covid level in 2023. In addition, SPA's domestic PLF also remained solid at 90% in 2023, and \~15ppt higher vs. the Big 3's average. The PLF gap between SPA and the Big 3 has widened from 10ppt in 2019 to 15ppt in 2023.

3Q23 earnings returned to well above pre-Covid level... SPA reported Rmb1.8bn net profit in 3Q23, more than double 3Q19's level, despite the soft macro environment, and in contrast to relatively soft performance from its Big 3 airline peers. We think SPA has benefited from market pricing discipline as well as consumption downgrades after China's reopening.

...despite a shortage of pilots: SPA was short of pilots in 2023 as some foreign pilots left China during the pandemic, restricting SPA's effective supply increase to some extent. Its average aircraft utilization was 8.5 hours in 2023, and it aims to increase it to \~9.5 hours in 2024, according to management.

We expect strong earnings growth in 2024: We expect SPA's earnings to stay well above pre-Covid levels in 2024, wth \~50% YoY profit growth, mainly driven by capacity increases. Sustained non-domestic capacity resumption should lead to lower supply in the domestic market, and support yield and margins.

## Industry View

Chinese utilities (mainly independent power producers and city gas suppliers) have limited exposure to overseas revenue, because their key operations mainly focus on the domestic market. Chinese renewable equipment players, including wind turbine (WTG) OEMs, manufacturers of wind components, as well as companies along the solar supplier chain (including polysilicon, wafers, cells, modules, and auxiliary materials, such as solar film), have been continuing to improve penetration of overseas markets.

Wind turbine OEMs: Chinese WTG OEMs still do not have meaningful penetration in the international wind turbine market. With largely improved product quality and extremely low ASP compared with global WTG OEMs, we expect domestic WTG OEMs will obtain more overseas orders. In 2023, MY became the first Chinese WTG OEM to enter the Japanese and Korean markets. Goldwind targets 5% global market share (ex-China) by 2025 vs. \~2% currently

## Wind components:

1. Wind towers and pile foundations: Some countries/regions like the US and EU have imposed anti-dumping tariffs on wind towers made in China. Chinese suppliers like Titan Wind are therefore expanding capacity in Germany to better serve the foreign market.

2. Wind blades: A bottleneck for wind blade exports is transportation, especially with the rapidly increasing size of wind blades. Thus, Sinoma S&T is expanding a new plant in Brazil for higher penetration in overseas markets.

3. Casting parts and main shafts: Most capacity is located in China, and few local companies are able to supply foreign markets.

4. Submarine cables: Foreign markets currently are still dominated by foreign players. Given robust offshore wind demand and relatively tight overseas submarine cable supply, we expect improving overseas order inflow during 2024-25 for top Chinese submarine cable suppliers like ZTT and Orient Cable.

Solar supply chain: The global solar supply chain has long been dominated by Chinese companies. China produced 85% of solar modules globally in 2022, while Chinese solar installations accounted for <40% in global solar installations. Given geographically diverse demand, leading Chinese module suppliers ship >50% of their products to overseas markets, with >90% of their production capacities located in China. In recent years, Chinese solar module producers have been actively expanding capacities abroad, mostly in Southeast Asia, the US, and the Middle East, to cope with import restrictions the US imposed on Chinese made solar products.

However, in view of the extreme competition in China's wind turbine market, we see ongoing market pressure on Chinese wind turbine OEMs and wind components companies in 2024. We like only submarine cable players, while the majority of their businesses still focus on China's offshore wind market in the near term. Meanwhile, solar companies are experiencing an industry down-cycle, as a result of the price wars and capacity oversupply, despite their more significant exposure to overseas markets (China Solar: End of down-cycle in sight (2 Nov 2023)).

Among all Chinese renewable equipment players, we like Jinlei, Orient Cable, and Hangzhou First for their high/quickly increasing foreign penetration.

Jinlei Technology (300443.SZ) – largest wind turbine main shaft supplier with highest foreign penetration: Jinlei is the largest wind turbine main shaft supplier with 50% market share in forged main shafts globally. It has established a strong relationship with international WTG OEMs, and supplies 50-70% of forged main shafts to Vestas, GE, and Siemens-Gamesa. Given complicated production procedures and a long product certification period (normally 2-3 years for overseas WTG OEMs), it is difficult for new players to enter the industry.

Jinlei’s overseas exposure was \~50% over 2015-19, but it dropped to 30-40% during 2020-22, given strong demand in the domestic market and wind power new installation decline in overseas markets in 2022.

We expect its overseas exposure to recover, thanks to:

1. Higher penetration in top international WTG OEMs in 2024; and

2. More order inflows for casting main shafts from overseas clients with more new casting capacity released.

Orient Cable (603606.SS) – quickly increasing foreign exposure with ongoing overseas order inflow: Orient Cable is a top submarine cable supplier in China. It was mainly engaged in the Chinese market before 2022, but in view of potentially strong offshore wind demand in Europe in the long term, it started to expand in overseas markets from 2022. In March 2022, its consortium with Boskalis obtained an order from TenneT to provide 220kV export cables and 66kV interconnector cables for a 700MW offshore wind project in The Netherlands, representing a breakthrough into the international high-end market. The company is producing the sample cable for a 1.08GW offshore project in the UK now. Once the sample cable passes the type test, the company will obtain a >Rmb1bn order in 1H24. Although Orient Cable’s foreign exposure was only 1% in 2021 and 2022, we expect higher foreign penetration thanks to ongoing overseas order inflow. The company targets 10% foreign revenue contribution by end-2025.

Hangzhou First (603806.SS) – higher foreign exposure thanks to rapid overseas capacity expansion: Hangzhou First is the leading solar film supplier and has a global market share of 50%, which is sustainable thanks to its competitive cost advantage, strong pricing power, leading technology innovation, and strong cash position. Backed by dominant global market share, Hangzhou First’s overseas revenue exposure has remained stable at \~20% from 2016. Given the strong demand from foreign markets, the company has quickly expanded its overseas capacity. Its 250mn sqm solar film capacity in Vietnam is under construction and a new 250mn sqm capacity in Thailand was announced in October 2023. Its total overseas solar film capacity will reach 300mn sqm in 1H24 and 600mn sqm by end 2024, vs. 70mn sqm by end-2023. In addition, the company is considering a new capacity in the US. We expect higher overseas exposure for Hangzhou First thanks to its rapid overseas capacity growth.
