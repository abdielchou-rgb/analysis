# Global Rates Strategy March/June Treasury Rolls

## Repo reform should boost the real money long/HF short in US and WN going forward

With mandatory central clearing coming in 2026, we think market participants will be reluctant to do very long repo trades that extend beyond where there is clarity in the structural format of a repo trade. That means real money accounts looking to do the duration overlay trade will need to find avenues other than bond forwards, with WN and US being alternatives.

## Will repo reform impact futures?

While mandatory central clearing of futures will not be effective until mid-2026, we think there are immediate effects. Many ALM accounts/real money accounts running against an index use a duration overlay strategy. Instead of buying an off-the-run Treasury in their index, they buy a portfolio of shorter credit and use swaps, futures, total return swaps, or bond forwards to reach their target duration/curve/bucket risk.

When accounts do bond forwards, they buy a bond and finance it in term repo. They tend to want to have the term repo leg be as long as possible, because there can be P&L recognition when the repo is reset. Accordingly, 2yr or 3yr repo is typically a desired term.

Unfortunately, no one knows what mandatory clearing is going to look like, because the FICC needs to make major rule changes, and there may be significant changes in market structure with competition for sponsors, potential shifts to a securities lending framework, etc. Therefore, we assume that people will be reluctant to enter into very long term repo until there is full clarity on what repo will look like as central clearing approaches.

That means people will look to other ways to get their needed duration, with WN and US being two of the options. We think that will keep open interest trending higher, and real money longs/HF shorts growing further. There is some risk that if the HF short grows significantly, there is a move to limit the size of the HF basis trade. It is premature to price in risk of that, but we are cautious about greater costs for real money to roll futures a year from now if the basis trade is limited.

## WN: Buy June/Sell March

The valuation is reasonably attractive, and the rolling forward of the enormous asset manager long should dominate pricing.

Basis: June is slightly cheaper to its underlying CTD than March.

CTD RV: The May 49s are the CTD for March, while the Nov 49s are CTD for June (with the Aug 49s close to CTD for June). Both the May and Nov issues have comparable Z spreads, so we don't have a strong preference for either issues. If anything the May issue looks slightly cheaper.

Switch value: There is no switch value in the March contract, where June switches to the Aug 49s on a 25bp parallel sell-off. That said, the adjusted duration of the the second CTD is so close to the adjusted duration of the CTD that there is little change in the net basis even on a 100bps selloff. Meaning the switch value is de-minimus.

Positioning: The asset manager long is near highs, and we think that limited capacity to shift from futures to bond forwards will make it difficult for these longs to do anything but roll forward. Accordingly, that asset manager long should dominate the roll.

Open Interest: Open interest is at new highs. We think that some of this is structural, as the move to mandatory central clearing of Tsy repo should make it difficult for certain counterparties to find the long term repo needed to do bond forwards. As a result, more of the duration overlay trade will happen by receiving fixed and by buying WN. If we are correct, WN roll costs are likely to increase until repo clearing begins in mid-2026.

Duration difference: The lower coupon of the June CTD means that investors should buy 6.1% fewer June contracts to match the risk in the March contract.

## US: Buy June/Sell March

We think the US roll will also be impacted by a larger asset manager long that has fewer options for a duration overlay strategy.

Basis: June looks slightly cheaper to its CTD than March.

CTD RV: The Aug 39s are CTD for both contracts, although the Feb 40s are very close.

Switch value: For March, an 11bp parallel selloff would make the Feb 40s, Feb 41s, and May 41s all equally CTD. A larger selloff would anchor the May 41s as CTD. For June, a 10bp selloff would switch the CTD to the Feb 40s, with a 20bp selloff pushing the CTD to the May 41s. Larger selloffs start to have a meaningful impact on the net basis, with the Aug 39s net basis moving to 9 ticks on a 50bp selloff. We expect to see some interest in June shorts as a way to be cheap that protection in a selloff.

Positioning: The asset manager long is very large. We assume the same long term repo driven limitation on bond forwards is playing a role here, putting more pressure on these longs to roll forward.

Open Interest: Open interest is also very high, with cheap optionality and the reduction in bond forwards creating additional interest.

Duration difference: The risk in the two contracts is very similar, with 0.2% fewer June contracts needed to reach the same risk.

## UXY: Sell March/Buy June

Positioning is likely to start playing a larger role in UXY, suggesting investors should roll early.

Basis: There is no significant difference in the net basis for the two contracts.

CTD RV: The 3.875s of Aug 33 are CTD for March, while the 4.5s of Nov 33 are CTD for June. We think the Nov 33s are marginally cheap to the Aug 33s.

Switch value: Neither contract has any switch value.

Positioning: The shift to a very large asset manager long/HF and dealer short in UXT is a new phenomenon. We assume that the same credit overlay strategy that is common in other contracts (real money buys futures and credit instead of off-the-run Tsys, HFs sell the rich futures and buy the cheaper off-the-runs) has arrived in UXY. Meaning the asset manager long will start to be a larger factor in the roll.

Open Interest: Open interest continues to surge.

Duration difference: There is only 1bp of difference in the risk of the two contracts, meaning an equal number of contracts should be used.

## TY: Sell March, Buy June

Given that the June CTD is likely to be the 7yr that gets auctioned next week, positioning rather than value should drive this roll. Meaning we suggest that longs should roll early.

Basis: The basis will depend on the coupon of this month's 7yr auction, as that should be CTD for June.

CTD RV: The March CTD is the 4.875s of 10/31/2030, while the WI 7yr note is likely to be CTD for June. For now we think the March CTD looks cheaper, but we'll have to wait for the 7yr auction to have a better sense of RV.

Switch value: There is very little switch value in March, and no switch value in June.

Positioning: The asset manager long is close to highs.

Open Interest: Open interest is also at lifetime highs.

Duration difference: We will need to see the coupon on the new 7yr before we know what the duration of the June contract will be. For now we suggest assuming June will have about 3.5% more risk, and then doing a clean-up as the 7yr auction approaches.

## TU: Buy March/Sell June

Stop-outs in TU/SOFR spread positions is likely to continue to create volatility in TU in the coming days. That is likely to make the roll quite choppy.

Basis: There doesn’t seem to be a meaningful difference in the net basis between the two contracts.

CTD RV: The 0.375s of 12/31/25 are CTD for March, while the 0.75s of 3/31/26 are CTD for June.

Switch value: A 20bps parallel rally would cause a switch of the March CTD to the 4s of 12/15/25, but that would have such a small duration difference that there is very little switch value. There is no switch value in June.

Positioning: The standard asset-manager long/HF short will play a large role, but we think pricing will be skewed by stop-outs of spread positions in the coming days.

Open Interest: Open interest is near record highs.

Duration difference: June's risk is a large 10.5% higher than March, meaning significantly fewer June contracts will be needed to match the exposure from a March contract.
