## Equity Markets

MSCI EM finished 4.6% higher in February, marginally outperforming MSCI DM (+4.1%)by -0.5% after four consecutive months of underperformance. The outperformance leadership was concentrated in North Asia markets: China (+8.4%), South Korea (+6.4%), and Taiwan (+5.5%). On the global stage, the dollar gained another c. 1%, as excitement around Fed easing continues to be repriced lower. US 10-year Treasury yields climbed up 32 bps, reverting three months of yield declines. Major boost for EM equities were from specific tailwinds for China (better macro data around Lunar New Year, further policy step up) and Korea (government push for a corporate Value-up program).

All EM sectors finished positive this month. Consumer Discretionary (+10.0%), Industrials (+6.8%), and IT (+6.2%) were the best performers, while Materials (+0.2%), Cons Staples (+1.9%), and Energy (+2.4%) posted the lowest gains.

Among regions, EM Asia led performance while LatAm lagged. EM Asia (+5.8%) was the best performer this month, followed by CEEMEA (+1.6%). LatAm finished marginally negative (-0.5%).

Best markets: China (+8.4%), Peru (+6.9%), and South Korea (+6.4%). Worst markets: Egypt (-12.5%), South Africa (-5.4%), and Czech (-4.7%).

Best major market-sectors: South Korea Consumer Discretionary (+18.7%), China Information Technology (+14.6%), and South Korea Financials (+12.8%). Worst major marketsectors: Mexico Consumer Staples (-5.5%), South Africa Financials (-3.9%), and Brazil Energy (-1.9%).

MSCI China posted a strong gain of +8.4% (in \$ terms) in February, the highest monthly since July 2023. Key drivers were better-than-expected macro data: the Lunar New Year consumption data showed number of tourists and tourism revenue exceeded 2019 levels, while January credit report beat expectations with record high TSF flow. These high frequency indicators bode well for a consumption recovery that has so far lagged industrial production post reopening. The PBOC also delivered a surprisingly large 25bps cut in the 5year LPR – the benchmark for mortgage rates. China<sup>’</sup>s national team buying key A-share index-linked ETFs also boosted markets.

MSCI Korea recorded a rise of +6.4% (in \$ terms) in February, driven by a Value outperformance on the back of government initiatives. Following comments on promoting “corporate Value-Up<sup>”</sup> earlier in the year, Korea<sup>’</sup>s Financial Services Commission (FSC) shared guidelines of the program on 26 February, promising bold incentives and tax benefits to corporates embracing the program and the introduction of a

“Value-Up index.<sup>”</sup> As a result, we have noted a sharp rally in low-P/BV stocks with a clear gradation by valuation bands – the cheapest stocks have done best. Institutional investors appear to have driven the move. Both foreign and local institutions have net bought the cheapest stocks in Korea (after a long period of disinterest in this space), while retail investors have been net sellers. Open interest in single stock futures for Value names has risen rapidly in recent weeks.

MSCI Saudi (+6.2% in \$ terms) was among the top 5 emerging markets in February. MSCI Saudi Mid Cap was +8.6%, while MSCI Saudi Small was +9.8%. Brent Crude added +\$1.81 to close at US\$ 84.6/bbl. While the conflicts in Gaza and the Red Sea continued, investor attention was focused more on earnings exceeding expectations outside of the petchem sector. Midway through, MSCI Saudi small cap was tracking 31% earnings growth oya on 14% sales growth. Reports of OPEC+ looking to extend its voluntary cuts into the second quarter was an added boost. The Modern Mills IPO drew \$40bn in orders for a \$320m deal, showing the ongoing demand for Saudi IPOs.

## Earnings Revision

MSCI EM 2024/25E EPS have been revised down by -0.9%/-0.8% in February. All markets saw negative revisions: LatAm (-1.1%), CEEMEA (-1.0%) and EM Asia (-0.9%).

Egypt (+12.5%), Peru (+2.2%), and India (+1.7%) posted the highest positive earnings revisions to their 2024 EPS estimates. On the other hand, S. Africa (-4.8%), Turkey (-4.0%), and Thailand (-3.3%) saw the highest negative revisions.

## Currencies

EM currencies (EMFX Index) finished lower (-1.2%) in February, underperforming the US dollar (DXY Index), which closed +0.9% higher. Mexican peso (+0.9%), Peruvian sol (+0.8%) and Indonesian rupiah (+0.4%) were the best performing EM currencies. On the other hand, Chilean peso (-3.7%), Turkish lira (-2.9%) and South Africa rand (-2.7%) were the worst performers.

## Commodities

Crude oil price gained +2.3% in February, Despite heavy US turnarounds, global refining utilization rates have stayed surprisingly high. Unlike in 2022 and 2023, when surging refined product prices pulled crude higher, oil<sup>’</sup>s \$10 rally since mid-December was led by crude. Although crude supply has tightened on partially delivered OPEC cuts, the main reason behind crude<sup>’</sup>s improved fortunes has been robust demand from refiners, in turn incentivized by rallying product margins and cracks.

The S&P Industrial Metals Spot index declined -1.3% this month. Steel and Iron ore saw a drop of -16.9% and -11.0%, respectively. Supply cuts are changing the narrative in base metals. With this lost supply, balances are moving tighter at a time when incoming data suggests an improving rather than cooling global economy.

Gold declined +0.2%, while silver saw a decline of -2.2%. Gold has remained more resilient than expected over the recent Fed repricing.

## EM Equity and Fixed Income Flows

EM Equity recorded another month of inflows in February (+\$1.6bn, data till 28 Feb) from +\$1.0bn in January. ETFs saw subscriptions continue in February (+\$5.2bn from +\$2.5bn in Jan), while non-ETFs recorded outflows of -\$3.7bn for 8th consecutive month. EM Bonds flows were at -\$2.2bn.

Among regional funds, GEMs (+\$1.1bn) posted strong inflows in February while Asia ex- Japan and EMEA saw inflows of +\$668mn and +\$164mn respectively. Outflows were recorded only in LatAm (-\$311mn).
