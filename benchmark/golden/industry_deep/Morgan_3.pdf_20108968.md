# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## July 1

USTs bear-steepen amid election uncertainty; weak ISM data provide brief relief to sell-off; risk-on move in Europe after French elections; ECB's Lagarde pushes back on July cut; BoJ Tankan Survey shows solid results; PBoC announces bond purchases; DXY at 105.83 (-0.0%); US 10y at 4.461% (+6.5bp).

• USTs continue to bear-steepen on the need for higher term premium amid continued uncertainty surrounding the US election, with market participants pricing in fiscal concerns and higher inflation given potential election outcomes.

• US ISM Manufacturing data come in softer than expected, with notable decline in the Prices Paid Index, which provides temporary relief to the bear-steepening move in USTs.

• Risk-on move in Europe supports a sell-off in Bunds (10y Bund: +11bp), rebound in European stocks (Euro Stoxx: +0.7%), and strengthening of the EUR (+0.3%) as market participants digest the outcome of the first round of parliamentary elections in France.

• ECB President Lagarde pushes back on a July rate cut, saying “it will take time” to gain sufficient data to be certain that the risks of abovetarget inflation have passed.

• BoJ Tankan Survey for June shows solid results, with improvement in large manufacturing business conditions; our economists believe the survey results do not change the BoJ's current positive assessment on the economy supporting a July rate hike.

• PBoC announces it decided to borrow China government bonds from primary dealers in open market operations, as the rally in Chinese bonds given concerns around the domestic economy extends; 10y yields fall 2bp to 2.18%, a record low.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

Global elections take center stage, with increased focus on election outcomes and political uncertainty continuing to drive price action. Over the weekend, the first round of French parliamentary elections took place. The results pointed to an increased likelihood of a "no majority" scenario. While the far-right National Rally (RN) party led the first round of elections, Macron’s centrist alliance and the leftwing Nouveau Front Populaire announced they would pull their third-placed candidates out of the second round for the districts in a three-way tie, which reduces the likelihood of an RN outright majority. The results of the first round of elections supported a risk-on sentiment, which led to a parallel sell-off in Bunds, rally in European stocks, and strengthening of EUR.

In the NY morning, the UST curve bear-steepened in a continuation of Friday’s move amid election uncertainty. Market participants continued to price in the need for greater term premium as well as started to price in continued federal deficits and higher inflation given potential election outcomes.

Data in the US pushed back against the bear-steepening, and ISM Manufacturing data provided temporary relief to the UST move. June ISM Manufacturing data were weaker than expected, with the decline led by production and employment slightly offset by a beat in new orders, though all three remained in contractionary territory. The Prices Paid Index also was softer than expected and fell to 52.1 (C: 55.9; P: 57.0), which supported a rally.

The rally was quite brief, and USTs returned to bear-steepening shortly after the release. The bear-steepening accelerated in the late NY morning alongside reports of the Supreme Court decision that granted partial immunity to US presidents. Ultimately, the move lost some momentum into the NY afternoon, but the 2s30s curve closed another 6bp steeper as the front-end ended the day relatively unchanged while 30y yields were up \~7bp.

Equities closed modestly higher, with the S&P 500 up 0.3% led by a rally in technology stocks, which also pushed Nasdaq to reach a new record high. Oil prices continued their upward momentum, with WTI crude oil futures up 2.3% amid continued geopolitical risks, supply concerns, and weather-related risks, as the strongest-ever June hurricane intensifies, which may indicate a serious hurricane season to come.

## Japan:

JGBs initially sold off and the curve bear-steepened following the sell-off in USTs last Friday that was on the back of higher term premium and inflation expectations amid uncertainty related to the US election. However, 30y JGBs gradually bounced back and outperformed into Tokyo close, which may have been supported by monthend technical factors. 10y and 20y JGBs continued to underperform ahead of Tuesday’s 10y JGB auction. 5y JGB sold off 1.0bp to 0.590%, 10y JGB sold off 2.0bp to 1.060%, 20y JGB sold off 3.0bp to 1.895%, and 30y JGB sold off 1.0bp to 2.240% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS sold off led by the belly of the curve. 10y and 20y ASW cheapened, while 30y ASW richened. XCCY basis tightened across the curve.

Although some market participants are expected to square positions at Tuesday’s 10y JGB auction, we believe the demand may be limited amid the sell-off in USTs and elevated USD/JPY level. We believe longer-term JGBs will be dependent on the US economy and the level of UST yields.

During the Tokyo session, JPY broadly weakened and USD/JPY increased from 160.7 to 161.1, aligning with the sell-off in USTs last Friday. EUR particularly outperformed amid the highly anticipated outcome for the first round of the French elections. EUR/JPY ground higher above 173.0 into the London morning.

## Europe:

EUR strengthened amid news of President Macron’s centrist alliance and the leftwing Nouveau Front Populaire pulling their third-placed candidates out of the second round of elections for districts in a three-way tie, as this reduced the probability of an RN outright majority. The EUR pared its gains despite the weak US ISM data alongside reports of the US Supreme Court ruling that presidents have some immunity, which some market participants interpreted as increasing the probability of a Trump presidency. Ultimately, EUR gained +0.2% during the London session. The funders funded well, and JPY fell -0.3% while CHF fell -0.5%. NOK (+0.2%) continued to outperform SEK (-0.2%), with NOK supported by oil whereas SEK exhibited its usually high beta to the US.

European duration traded with a risk-on sentiment on Monday following the outcome of the first round of parliamentary elections in France. The results pointed to an increased likelihood of having a "no-majority" outcome heading into the second round, a result deemed to be welcomed by market participants. As such, country spreads tightened back on Monday, and the 10y OAT/Bund tightened 5.8bp to 74.2bp while the 10y BTP/Bund closed 7.4bp tighter to \~150bp. 10y Bund yields retraced toward \~2.6%, just 8bp shy from its pre-EU elections levels and underperformed USTs during the London session. Price action remained very weak despite the softer-than-expected ISM manufacturing data in the US and German flash CPI data meeting expectations for June. The curve drifted higher almost in a parallel fashion, with most of the underperformance seen around the 5y point as at the front-end of the market; most of the repricing was seen in the green packs. The ASW complex tightened back as expected given the sentiment.

## Dollar Bloc:

In the dollar bloc, AUD (-0.2%) fared better than NZD (-0.3%), which may have been a result of the strong Australia house prices overnight, which in turn may have increased the probability of an RBA hike. The DXY USD Index ended the London session flat, driven by US dollar strength against most of G10. CAD sold off -0.4% amid political uncertainty around US elections and alongside a Canadian holiday.

• In Japan, the BoJ Tankan Survey for June showed the large manufacturing DI marginally improved to 13 (C: 11; P: 11), while the large non-manufacturing DI worsened to 33 (C: 33; P: 34). Among manufacturing categories, there was a high level of improvement in Textiles, Chemicals, and Petroleum and Coal Products, while the large q/q decline was in Iron and Steel. Among nonmanufacturing categories, there was a prominent level of strong business conditions in Information services, Real Estate, and Accommodations/Eating/ Drinking Services. On a q/q basis, Communications improved, while Retail worsened. The average of the predicted USD/JPY rate for 2024 year-end by large manufacturing exporting enterprise is 142.68. Our economists believe the Tankan survey results are supportive of a July rate hike

Commodity prices “continue to be volatile, especially in fuel, natural gas, aluminum and plastics.” However, steel prices were reported to be

“approaching long-term historical lows.” The S&P measure rose 0.3 point to 51.6 in June. Like the more direct output measures, factory surveys are still giving uncertain indications of activity

• In the US, Construction Spending fell -0.1% m/m in May (C: 0.2%; P: 0.3%). Residential spending fell 0.2%, with single-family residential down 0.7%, a second straight month of payback for extra building during the unusually warm winter. Business spending fell 0.4%, a second straight decline, and public spending continued to rise and increased +0.5%. The construction data are slightly softer than our economists' assumptions for 2Q24 GDP, and they reduced their forecast for nonresidential spending to -2.0%, which resulted in lowering the GDP forecast slightly to 1.8% q/q (P: 1.9%)

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
