# China Property Management Change of investment framework; Top pick CR Mixc

## Capital return matters more

Amid a declining property market, China property managers' earnings slowed in 2023, due to weaker value-added services (VAS) to developers and homeowners. They have guided down their earnings growth outlooks. Amid lower growth and strong cash flow/ cash positions, we think investors look more at capital return policies (i.e. dividend payouts and share buybacks) than growth, and start to value these companies from a dividend perspective. We lower our price targets by 41% on average due to lower earnings growth, and downgrade COPH and Onewo to Neutral, and CG Services to Sell. Our top pick is CR Mixc on less residential exposure and a strong mall pipeline.

## 2024 outlook remain conservative; earnings and rating changes

Companies are conservative on the 2024 outlook. CR Mixc expects around 22% YoY earnings growth in 2024, and Poly Property Services (Poly PM) expects 10%. Onewo and Greentown Service both target 15% YoY core profit growth in 2024. China Overseas Property Holdings (COPH) has mentioned a 15-20% CAGR in 2024-26. We revise down 2024-25E earnings on average by 18-25% due to a decline of GFA expansion and margin. We downgrade COPH to Neutral on slowing earnings growth and low dividend yield, and downgrade Onewo to Neutral on its parent developer deleveraging (see our Vanke downgrade note). In addition, we downgrade CG Services to Sell on potential impairment risk.

## How to think of dividend policy

From the parent developers' perspective, if the shareholding structure is parentsubsidiary, parent developers will need to rely more on managers' earnings growth due to earnings decline of property development business. Thus, we think managers still want to conduct M&A. From the managers' perspective, however, it becomes more difficult to do M&A, as their own valuation multiples got de-rated. Hence, they raised their dividend payouts, trying to support valuation multiples, except for COPH and CG Services, and the parent developers can also benefit from higher cash dividends. Among our covered universe, we think CR Mixc and Poly PM fit better under this framework, whilst CMPO and COPH might have a lower incentive to increase dividend payouts.

## Valuation: Downgrade COPH and Onewo to Neutral, CG Services to Sell

Our coverage universe is trading at 10.3x 12-month forward PE with a 26% 2023-25E EPS CAGR, implying a 0.43x PEG, and 5.3% 12-month forward dividend yield, 4SD higher than historical average since 2018.

## Q: What is the competitive landscape like and what are the supply changes for the primary residential market?

We think the primary residential market is shrinking and expect 2024 property sales (in terms of GFA)/ new starts to decline 7%/7% YoY, which is likely negative for the property management sector. We expect the market split to be 42%/34%/24% for SOE/LGFV/POE developers, from 29%/5%/66% in 2021. The structural shift in the property development industry from POEs to SOEs/LGFVs is likely to benefit SOE managers more than their POE counterparts.

China Property Management: 2023 outlook: Prefer SOE managers over POE (9 Jan 2023)

## Q: Will the existing housing market be a new GFA growth driver?

Yes, due to the shrinking primary residential market, we believe property management companies (PMCs) are expanding into the existing housing market. We think this trend may be sustainable with the increasing penetration of property owners' associations. Compared with non-residential properties, which need particular qualifications and experience, we think the existing housing market has lower entry barriers. In our view, housing in urban areas built after 2000 could be a profitable market for property managers given the prospective market size of 12.85bn sqm. This presents a greater market potential than our estimated China's annual housing demand of 842m sqm in 2023- 30E.

## China Property Management: 2023 outlook: Prefer SOE managers over POE (9 Jan 2023)

Our coverage universe is trading at 10.3x 12-month forward PE with a 26% 2023-25E EPS CAGR, implying a 0.43x PEG, and 5.3% 12-month forward dividend yield. In June 2021, before developers defaulted, the property management sector traded at 30x 12-month forward PE, with a two-year EPS CAGR of 35%, implying a 0.85x PEG. We think the market has priced in: 1) a shrinking primary residential market and slow property managers' GFA growth; 2) a lowered margin due to high competition in existing market.

We continue to prefer SOE property managers over POE managers, and prefer managers with higher non-residential property management exposure over residential property management. On the backdrop of lower growth and strong cash flow/cash position, we think investors place more value on dividend and share buyback policy. Our top pick is CR Mixc on less residential exposure and a strong mall pipeline.

In 2023, most of the managers raised their payout ratios, except COPH and CG Services, and share prices reacted negatively to COPH and CG Services (i.e. COPH dropped 24.5% and CG Services dropped 4.5% on their announcement day ), while positively to other managers.

## 2023 results review

## Slowdown in expansion and conservative 2024 guidance

In 2023, we saw a slowdown in contracted GFA expansion across the sector due to 1) no M&A; 2) slowdown of property sector; 3) high competition in existing market. Our covered managers had an average 14% YoY decline of contracted GFA net addition in 2023 and third party contracted GFA net addition (excluding M&A) had an average decline of 6%. Nevertheless, the performance diverges, SOE property managers continued to outperform in third party contract bidding, with CR Mixc third party contracted GFA net addition increasing 64% YoY, COPH +33% YoY and Poly Property Services +51% YoY.

On the top line, our covered managers had an average of 15% YoY revenue growth in 2023, -15ppt compared to 30% in 2022. Similarly, net profit had an average YoY growth of 17% in 2023 and 14% in 2H23, compared to 23% in 1H23. In the meantime, companies are conservative on the 2024 outlook. Guidance on 2024 earnings ranged from 10% to 23% YoY growth, lowered further from 2023 growth level (see details in Figure 4).

## Price target and rating changes

Our fundamentals scorecard and the methodology used to derive our price targets: We use four metrics in our fundamentals scorecard: 1) growth (20% of weighting): we include managers' 2023-25E EPS CAGRs and 2023 third party share of total GFA under management (50% weighting for each) to reflect their earnings growth visibility and quality; 2) capital return (20% of weighting): we use managers' dividend payout ratio in 2023 to gauge this; 3) liquidity (20% of weighting): the higher the better, as some managers' stock liquidity may be too thin for investors, and we use 2024E earnings to gauge this; 4) competitive position (30% of weighting): the higher the better, and this part mainly based on our comprehensive analysis of managers' competitiveness and associated developers' balance sheet. We prefer CR Mixc and Poly PM due to high non-residential exposure and strong parent, and we also like Greentown Service for its independent position. We put Onewo and CG Services at lowest score to reflect managers' potential downside risks from developers' stake disposals. We derive our final scores from their respective rankings of each factor. Higher scores indicate superior fundamentals for a manager.

Yes, due to the shrinking primary residential market, we believe property management companies (PMCs) are expanding into the existing housing market. ## Valuation Method and Risk Statement

Our valuations for China’s property management service providers are based on PE.

We believe the key risks relating to China’s property management are: 1) potential margin downside risks as it is difficult for management companies to raise fees, while labour costs (typically around 60% of total costs) usually rise 5-8% per year; 2) acquisitions of other management companies at expensive valuations, which would be negative for balance sheets and valuation multiples; 3) a shrinking primary residential market; and 4) potential fundraising risks if associated developers' cash flows are tight.
