## Bull vs. Bear: Is Bad Data Good News Again?

China's growth deceleration continued in July, raising the urgency of more easing. The Politburo has called for faster implementation of announced policies and suggested more might be coming. We expect continued reactive easing and a slight growth improvement in 2H, leaving FY24 GDP growth tracking at 4.6-4.7%.

To bring you a variety of perspectives, we have picked several charts that consider both bull and bear arguments for China's economy:

• Bull arguments: The Politburo meeting offered slightly new and encouraging wordings, raising expectations of incremental support for consumption. Exports remain resilient though there are some signs of moderation ahead.

• Bear arguments: Downward growth momentum continued to build in July. Government tax revenue and the housing market remain subdued (517 stimulus impact has largely faded out). Domestic demand weakness has led to some involuntary build up of industrial inventory, which could weigh on industrial production in the near term.

Bull Arguments

## Politburo Meeting – Some Encouraging Words, Still Reactive in Nature

Reacting to slowing growth in 2Q and July, the Politburo meeting pledged to reach the FY2024 growth target of 5%, which indicates modestly stronger policy support in 2H. We think policy remains reactive in nature. Implementation of predetermined policies is the key, with faster issuance of government bonds in 2H facilitated by more flexible usage, reversing the 1.5-2ppt tightening in augmented fiscal deficit in 1H partly owing to a lack of good projects. While the Politburo also called for more support for consumption and said incremental stimulus could be rolled out “in due course”, the wordings are somewhat vague.

## Exports Still Resilient for Now

Export-oriented Caixin PMI dipped below 50 in July, raising concerns on the sustainability of export strength – arguably the key support to growth. Underlying details reveal that the weakness was driven by domestic demand, as the new export orders subindex was still stable and comfortably above past average. That said, incoming data seems to echo our view that YoY export growth may have closed in on the cyclical peak: global PMI has dipped below 50 in July, shipping prices have eased off since late July, and the US labor market points to a narrower path to soft landing.

Bear Arguments

## Elevated Industrial Inventory

YoY growth of inventory accelerated in recent months, but this is not due to (anticipation of) stronger demand, considering (1) deflation pressures and (2) elevated inventory levels. Instead, we think the restocking is passive/involuntary amid insufficient demand. This means unless final demand picks up from here, elevated inventory would mean continued deflationary pressures (to bring inventory back to desirable levels) and weigh on production.

## Sluggish Tax Revenue Growth Echoes Ongoing Deflation Loop

Both corporate and personal income tax contracted YoY in 2Q. While tax benefits (such as higher deductions for personal income tax) played some role, the weakness echoes the divergence between real GDP growth (which is still solid at 5%) and sluggish sentiment (which depends on profits and income). Moreover, declining tax revenue is constraining the government’s ability to spend and incentivizing activities to “create tax revenues” (such as chasing tax revenue dating back 20 years), which could further dampen sentiment. The deflation loop where almost every entity in the economy is unwilling/unable to spend may only be broken with more deficit-financed spending.

## Impact of “517 Housing Stimulus” Largely Faded

Sales of new and existing homes are back to levels seen in early May. Our property team also notes a faster price decline in July. We think home prices may continue to decline, helping to bring supply-demand into better balance. However, price adjustment alone would be insufficient to stabilize the housing market, as it would also lead to not only hesitancy of home purchases, but also weaker consumer sentiment and higher risk of mortgage delinquency that ultimately translates into weaker aggregate housing demand. The Politburo’s pledge to “proactively support housing inventory digestion” may be part of the solution but implementation may remain difficult without public funds.

## Sluggish Construction Activity

Rebar and cement demand remain at multi-year lows in July, reflecting continued LGFV and housing deleveraging. Robust infrastructure investment growth YTD (7.7%Y) is mainly driven by electricity production and supply (30%Y), yet local government construction remained subdued (-4.5%Y) – the mix is arguably less labor intensive, hence more indirect in supporting wage growth (for the low-income group). Faster government bond issuance in 2H may support construction activity, though probably only marginally amid more flexible usage of bond proceeds.
