## Developed Markets

## United States:

Benign outcomes in the two primary risk events to end the week allowed for strong risk asset performance. The first test to risk appetite came and passed with the status quo BoJ outcome, with respect to the policy rate as well as guidance on JGB purchases. The second test of risk demand came and passed with a less worse than feared March core PCE report, in that a significant upward revision to January was the primary driver of the upside surprise in the quarterly Core PCE Price Index from Thursday, not an elevated March reading. In this backdrop, global sovereign bond yields fell, JPY exhibited acute weakness, and US equities (S&P 500: +1.02%) staged a strong rally.

## Japan:

JGBs opened weaker after another UST sell-off, following strong 1Q24 Core PCE, and traded sideways ahead of the BoJ meeting. JGBs bear-steepened in the Tokyo morning session and underperformed against OIS, after a media report suggested the BoJ would discuss a form of QT in the April meeting. However, after no change was confirmed in monetary policy setting, including JGB purchases, JGBs were supported by demand to square short positions. The belly of the curve underperformed, as market participants increasingly priced in risk of the BoJ falling behind the curve. However, long-end JGBs were supported due to demand to buy the dip from the real money community. 5y JGBs sold by 1.0bp d/d to 0.505% on a simple yield basis, 10y JGBs sold off by 3.0bp d/d to 0.92%, and 30y JGBs sold off by 1.0bp d/d to 1.96%. Initially, TONA OIS bear-steepened alongside JGBs but gradually

recovered led by the long-end after the status quo BoJ outcome, alongside JGBs.
ASW richened in the long-end. XCCY basis continued to tighten across the curve.

It was a very choppy Tokyo session for USD/JPY. In the Tokyo morning session, USD/ JPY traded in a tight range, but surged after the BoJ policy statement was released and reached the 156 level. The BoJ maintained its monetary policy settings and maintained its pace of JGB purchases. Despite rapid JPY weakness, after the BoJ outcome, Ministry of Finance officials were quiet, and USD/JPY continued to grind higher toward 156.20. In BoJ Governor Ueda’s press conference, he indicated current JPY weakness would not largely impact Japan’s underlying inflation, which means the BoJ does not need to move at this stage. USD/JPY reached 156.70 as the press conference ended. During the London session, USD/JPY fell below 155.00 as market participants became worried about potential FX intervention. With that said, the pair quickly recovered above the 156.50 level; it is possible a rate check may have been conducted by the BoJ. Other CCY/JPY pairs continued to climb higher across the board; AUD/JPY approached 102.50. Market participants will closely monitor whether the MoF intervenes during the Golden Week holidays.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar strengthened broadly to catch up with the moves in macro markets from Thursday, which had relatively limited pass-through to FX on Thursday, ahead of the BoJ meeting and discussion of potential FX intervention by the Japanese Ministry of Finance. The DXY USD Index rose 0.4% in the NY afternoon and traded above 106.00, despite a rally in US equities and lower Treasury yields. AUD (+0.2%) outperformed and was the only G10 currency to strengthen against the US dollar. Market participants repriced the implied RBA policy path higher after robust 1Q24 PPI data, which followed a significant upside surprise in CPI data earlier in the week; AUD/NZD (+0.3%) reached a new year-to-date high. JPY was the clear underperformer as market participants continued to test possible intervention levels after robust US data and relatively dovish BoJ; USD/JPY (+1.3%) traded at \~157.70 in the NY afternoon.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

The performance of AxJ currencies was mixed. CNY and CNH remained stable after a strong fixing by the PBoC (7.1056). USD/CNY rose 0.1% to 7.245 while USD/CNH rose to 7.258. KRW continued to weaken for the second consecutive session, with USD/KRW at 1375.30; local equities (KOSPI: +1.0%) rallied. IDR was the major underperformer in the region; USD/IDR rose 0.2% to 16215. SGD weakened after release of industrial production data; USD/SGD rose 0.04% to 1.359. TWD recovered after weakness in the previous session; USD/TWD fell 0.1% to 32.54. PHP outperformed after remarks from BSP Governor Remolona on intervention to stabilize the currency; USD/PHP fell 0.2% to 57.7. THB gained against the US dollar, with USD/THB at 36.94 while gold prices (+0.41%) rose. MYR and INR exhibited little volatility.

AxJ rates continued to sell-off. In Hong Kong, 5y yields rose 7.4bp and 2y yields rose 8.5bp. In China, the 2s5s curve bear-steepened; 10y yields rose 2.1bp to 2.28% and

30y CGBs sold off by 2.4bp. The NDIRS curve shifted higher, with 5y and 10y yields both \~4bp higher. Indonesia bonds were major underperformers as yields rose across the entire curve, with underperformance in the long-end; 7y and 10y yields rose 9.5bp and 4.6bp, respectively. 2y INDOGs were relatively unchanged. Thailand rates rallied as 10y yields rose 2bp. India G-Secs remained relatively stable, with 2y yields up 0.1bp and 10y yields up 0.5bp. South Korea bonds relatively outperformed amid demand from foreign market participants; 2y yields fell 1.8bp and 10y yields were largely unchanged at 3.69%.

## Latin America:

Most LatAm currencies were stronger in the session, despite broader US dollar strength. COP (+1.3%) led peers followed by BRL (+0.9%), although there were few local drivers. CLP (-0.1%) was weaker in the session amidst higher copper prices. LatAm rates rallied; DIs outperformed peers as inflation surprised to the downside (January 2026s: -18bp). TIIEs were marginally lower; the 2y tenor fell 3bp and the IBR curve was \~7bp lower across the curve.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The BoJ maintained the status quo at its April meeting. In the statement, guidance on JGB purchases was unchanged, some market participants had expected this to be reduced. In the economic outlook, the median inflation forecasts were for the most part in-line with consensus expectations. The balance of risks for the BoJ’s FY24 inflation outlook is now also skewed to the upside. The median forecast for the FY24 growth outlook was revised down, given a current slower recovery in domestic demand. In the press conference, Governor Ueda’s remarks were for the most part in-line with his previous comments. While there were no implications of near-term policy changes, he did comment on the neutral rate (for more, read here)

## Emerging Markets

• BSP Governor Remolona said the central bank is prepared to handle any needless movement and extreme volatility in the currency. He also said as tensions in the Middle East have increased, market participants have sought refuge in the safer US dollar. As a result, the dollar has gained against the peso

• MAS said it expects the Malaysian economy will grow, but rising global and domestic cost pressures may act as deterrents to sustain growth. The central bank forecasts GDP growth of 1-3% this year (2023: 1.1%). MAS expects growth could be driven by an upturn in global electronics but expressed caution as persistent inflation could result in a delay of policy rate cuts (for more, read here)

• BCB Governor Campos Neto struck a cautious tone. He acknowledged the process on headline inflation, but expressed concern core services remains elevated. Additionally, he noted inflation expectations in the country remain de-anchored. Markets have already priced a slowdown in the BCB cutting cycle at the May meeting (-28bp) and have only priced \~52bp of cuts for the remainder of the cycle

## Economic Release Notes

## Developed Markets

• In Japan, Tokyo Japan-Style Core CPI (ex-fresh food) slowed to 1.6% y/y in April (C: 2.2%; P: 2.4%). This was mainly driven by special factors, as the MIC reflected the impact of the elimination of high school tuition in Tokyo in the data. Without this impact, Japan-style core CPI would have been \~2.1% y/y. Food inflation including “meals outside the home” continued to decline. Private services have not shown signs of a re-acceleration yet in April, although the results of the Spring wage negotiation were stronger than expected

• In the UK, the GfK Consumer Confidence Index rose to -19 in April (C: -20; P: -21), a two-year high. According to the survey, households had a more positive view of the economy and own finances, as inflation continued to decelerate and an anticipated cut in the rate of national insurance

• In the US, Core PCE Inflation rose by 0.317% m/m in March (C: 0.3%; P: 0.27%) which kept the y/y rate at 2.8% (C: 2.7%). This brought 3m annualized core PCE to 4.4% (P: 3.7%). Lack of improvement in 1Q24

inflation data is notable but our economists expect a reversal in key inflation metrics in April (for more, read here)

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
