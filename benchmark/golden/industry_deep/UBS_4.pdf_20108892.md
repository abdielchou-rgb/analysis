# European Economic Perspectives UBS Evidence Lab inside: Solid sentiment and capex intentions

Economics

## Feeling the pulse of Eurozone corporates

The latest UBS Evidence Lab survey (> Access Dataset) conducted 30 April to 29 May among 540 Eurozone (EZ) firms in Germany, France, Italy and Spain allows us to gauge recent changes in business sentiment, capex plans and corporate expectations for sales revenues, employment and wages, as well as pricing power and profit margins.

## Q2-24 survey results constructive - Eurozone recovery continues

After encouraging signs of corporates turning more constructive in Q1-24, the results of the Q2 survey were also positive. Business sentiment remains reasonably high and capex intentions have strengthened further. Expectations of sales revenue, employment and profit margins were a bit weaker in Q2, but not signalling major concerns. Similar to other data (e.g. GDP and PMIs), the survey responses show that the EZ recovery is under way. Even in the German corporate sector, the mood has clearly improved. Yet, given ongoing headwinds from monetary policy, fiscal consolidation, and a subdued external environment, overall EZ growth is likely to remain moderate and uneven. Following GDP growth of 0.3% q/q in Q1-24, we expect 0.2% q/q for Q2. Our full-year EZ growth forecast is 0.6% for 2024 and 1.2% for 2025 (consensus: 0.7%/1.4%).

## Equity strategy: Cautiously optimistic outlook for pricing power

The survey underscores a cautiously optimistic outlook for pricing power, with varying degrees of confidence across sectors. Profit margin expectations are more tempered, highlighting the need for effective cost management and strategic investments to navigate the challenges ahead. Each sector faces unique dynamics; but common themes of rising costs, economic uncertainty, and the importance of investment in productivity and efficiency emerge as critical factors influencing corporate strategies. Our REMIX model contextualises these expectations, showing that while margin contraction is anticipated, it is moderated by the current economic environment and expected revenue growth. Our REVS framework also provides additional granularity, highlighting sectorspecific strengths and weaknesses that align with broader market trends.

## Key results

Overall: the Q2-24 survey responses were constructive. After encouraging signs of corporates turning more constructive in Q1-24, the results of the Q2-24 survey were also positive. Business sentiment remains reasonably high and capex intentions have strengthened further. Expectations of sales revenue, employment and future profit margins were a bit weaker, but not signalling major concerns. Similar to other data (e.g. GDP and PMIs), the survey responses suggest that following several quarters of stagnation, the Eurozone recovery is clearly underway. Importantly, while previous UBS Evidence Lab surveys showed substantial pessimism in the German corporate sector, the mood clearly improved, suggesting that German economy is through the worst. Nevertheless, given ongoing headwinds from monetary policy, fiscal consolidation, and a subdued external environment, overall Eurozone growth is likely to remain moderate, and uneven across countries and sectors. Following GDP growth of 0.3% q/q in Q1-24, we expect 0.2% q/q for Q2. Our full-year Eurozone growth forecast is 0.6% for 2024 and 1.2% for 2025.

1. Business sentiment - marginally up, at fairly solid levels: Following the strong rebound in Q1-24, Eurozone business sentiment increased marginally further in Q2, to levels that are fairly solid by historical comparison. Encouragingly, and importantly, sentiment picked up visibly in Germany, which had been a clear underperformer in recent quarters. Overall, this supports the view that following very weak Eurozone growth in Q4-22 to Q4-23 and a rebound in Q1-24 (0.3% q/q), the growth recovery will continue in Q2-24 and beyond. However, significant headwinds remain - monetary policy is still restrictive, the external environment is sluggish and fiscal policy is consolidating - hence overall growth is likely to remain moderate: we expect 0.2% q/q (0.4% y/y) for Q2-24 and 0.6% growth for 2024 as a whole, followed by 1.2% fo 2025.

2. Expectation of sales revenues - down: Following a marked rise in Q1-24, firms sales revenue expectations moderated again in Q2, particularly in the industrial sector.

3. Capex plans - up: Encouragingly, aggregate capex intentions have strengthened further, to fairly strong levels - even in Germany.

4. What types of capex are firms undertaking? "Upgrade of equipment & machinery" remains the most important type of capex, followed by "R&D" and "inventory & working capital". The preference for maintenance capex over expansionary greenfield investment is a long-standing feature of the survey. We interpret this as a sign of caution, with firms preferring to increase capacity or extend the lifetime of their existing capital stock rather than setting up entirely new plants.

5. Key drivers of capex decisions: "Shifts in technology" followed by "domestic demand" remained the key drivers of capex, followed by "exports" and "green initiatives". Among the factors having a negative effect on capex, the war in Ukraine was mentioned most prominently, thus taking over from "High inflation and tightening central bank monetary policy" as the biggest worry, followed by "Shortages of available labour" and "Supply chain problems".

6. Access to credit - a bit tighter: At the aggregate level, access to credit marginally tightened in Q2-24, relative to Q1. Yet, comparing the latest responses with those from last year suggests that ECB monetary policy restrictiveness is clearly past its peak.

7. Employment plans - a bit weaker: Following the visible improvement in Q1-24, corporate employment plans moderated somewhat in Q2-24. Overall, though, EZ labour markets are strong, with unemployment at an all-time low of 6.4% in May.

8. Wage expectations - still very strong: Corporate wage expectations have remained unchanged, near historical highs, in a fairly broad-based fashion across countries, sectors, and firm sizes. At the same time, companies have become a bit less confident that they will be able to pass on higher wage costs to their customers via higher prices. Both observations combined should come as a note of caution to the ECB, which has made it clear that the pace of wage moderation and the degree to which corporates will mop up higher wage costs in their profit margins will crucially determine the pace of policy easing. We think the survey results support the expectation that the pace of Eurozone wage moderation and ECB rate cuts are likely to be gradual.

9. Pricing power - a bit better: Overall pricing power expectations picked up again in Q2, from subdued levels in Q1, but with clear country and sector specific variation. (We see a certain tension between corporate responses on (a) pricing power and (b) the ability to roll over higher wage costs via higher prices.)

10. Profit margins - a bit weaker: Corporate expectations of profit margins moderated a bit at the aggregate Eurozone level, with visible improvements in Germany, a clear weakening in France and largely unchanged responses from Italy and Spain. (Here as well, we see a certain tension between corporate responses on (a) pricing power, viewed as being a bit better, and (b) profit margins, viewed as a bit weaker.)

## Equity Strategy

The survey results provide a nuanced view of European corporates' expectations for pricing power and profit margins over the next 12 months. While there is general optimism about increasing pricing power, profit margin expectations are more cautious due to rising costs and external uncertainties.

Firms in the Consumer sector are optimistic about raising prices due to strong demand. However, rising operational and labour costs, coupled with low access to new credit lines, present significant challenges to maintaining profit margins. PMI data indicating weakness in sectors like Beverages and Consumer Services further complicates the outlook. Companies will need to strategically balance price increases with cost management to protect profitability. Consumer Staples and Household & Personal Products score poorly on our REVS framework (Regime, Earnings, Valuations, Sentiment), reflecting ongoing challenges in maintaining profitability.

The Industrials sector benefits from strong business sentiment and substantial investments in CAPEX and productivity-enhancing technologies. These factors support higher pricing power and help maintain profit margins. PMI data shows strong performance in sectors like Machinery & Equipment and Technology Equipment, which are experiencing robust demand and output growth. However, the sector must effectively manage rising labour and input costs, along with potential supply chain issues, to sustain this positive trend. Higher access to credit supports funding these investments, yet the challenge remains to balance the increased costs with strategic pricing. Within Industrials, our REVS framework indicates that Transportation's positive business cycle (regime) and earnings trends support a favourable outlook.

Despite consistently negative expectations for pricing power, Materials companies are confident in their ability to improve profit margins. This confidence likely arises from rigorous cost control measures and strategic investments in efficiency. PMI data supports this view, showing that certain manufacturing sectors have managed to increase output prices. However, the sector remains vulnerable to raw material price volatility, geopolitical tensions, and competitive pressures. The ability to maintain profit margins despite limited pricing power possibly highlights the effectiveness of their cost management strategies. REVS aligns with this, showing strong earnings momentum and favourable positioning based on our proprietary crowding data for Construction Materials in particular.

## Feeling the pulse of Eurozone firms

Since its launch in 2015, the UBS Evidence Lab survey of corporate activity has provided us with valuable insights into the Eurozone business cycle. In 2015/16, we were looking for signs of a self-sustained recovery. In 2017/18, we were looking for evidence of the Eurozone entering a virtuous circle. In 2019, as manufacturing and trade weakened, we were trying to gauge the degree of Europe's resilience. During the Covid and energy crises of 2020-2023, the focus shifted to damage assessment and then to measuring the speed of the recovery. But now, the angle has shifted once again, to gauging the strength of the Eurozone economy at a time when weakest point in the cycle clearly lies behind, ECB interest rates are beyond their peak and household consumption is gaining support from the recovery of real wage growth.

To monitor the latest developments in Eurozone corporate activity across various countries and sectors, and by size of company, UBS Evidence Lab conducted a survey between 30 April to 29 May, based on interviews with:

(1) 540 senior corporate decision-makers (mainly CEOs/CFOs);

(2) Representing 135 companies each in Germany, France, Italy and Spain;

The UBS Evidence Lab survey of corporate activity provides us with valuable insights into the Eurozone business cycle

(3) Mainly from the consumer, industrials and materials sectors; and

The UBS Evidence Lab survey sheds light on many aspects of Eurozone corporate activity, including sentiment, investment, access to credit, employment and wage expectations, pricing power and profit margins

(4) Broken down by size: SMEs, bigger firms, and major companies.

The latest was the 30th survey since we started the UBS Evidence Lab Survey on Eurozone corporate activity in Q3 2015.

Following the strong rebound in Q1- 24, Eurozone business sentiment increased marginally further in Q2, with the net balance of firms expecting an improvement up by 1pp to +30%. ## Equity Strategy

## Pricing Power

Across European corporates, the survey highlights an overall increase in net positive expectations for pricing power over the next 12 months. This optimism is driven by strong business sentiment and sector-specific dynamics, despite economic and political uncertainties, geopolitics, and high inflation. However, PMI data indicates that businesses face significant cost pressures and challenges in passing on higher costs to consumers. This divergence between input and output prices poses risks to profitability and inflation. According to our REVS framework, sectors like Construction Materials and Energy are well-positioned to leverage their pricing power, while sectors like Mining and Tech Hardware face more significant challenges.

The Consumer sector has the highest net positive expectations for pricing power, supported by strong demand for goods and services. However, firms in this sector must navigate increased competition and consumer price sensitivity, which could limit the extent of price increases. PMI data highlights that sectors like Beverages and Consumer Services are showing signs of weakness with lower new orders and future output expectations. Inflationary pressures from energy and labour costs further complicate the ability to raise prices without impacting demand. Consumer Staples, Food, Beverages & Tobacco, and Household & Personal Products score poorly on our REVS framework, reflecting the ongoing challenges in these sectors.

Industrial firms show strong net positive expectations for pricing power. Investments in Equipment & Machinery, supported by positive CAPEX plans, enhance productivity and competitiveness, enabling these firms to raise prices. PMI data shows that sectors like Machinery & Equipment, as well as Technology Equipment, are experiencing strong demand, output growth, and new orders, suggesting robust pricing power. However, managing rising labour and input costs, along with potential supply chain disruptions, remains challenging. Lower ECB interest rates cut should help fuel more business investments, further supporting the sector. REVS highlights Transportation as showing positive momentum, supporting the strong pricing power expectations in Industrials.

The Materials sector continues to experience net negative expectations for pricing power, a trend that has persisted for more than a year and worsened quarter-on-quarter. Volatile raw materials costs, intense competition, and price sensitivity in the market significantly constrain the ability to raise prices. Despite these challenges, the sector remains focused on strategic investments to mitigate the impact of limited pricing power. PMI data indicates that industries like Construction Materials and Paper have managed to increase output prices, highlighting some positive movement within manufacturing. REVS data aligns with this, showing strong earnings momentum and favourable positioning for Construction Materials.

## Profit Margins

Expectations for profit margins are positive, but have declined slightly compared to the previous quarter. This suggests that while firms anticipate some improvement in profitability, they are cautious due to rising costs and external uncertainties, such as high inflation and tightening central bank policies. PMI data underscores these concerns, noting continued cost pressures and challenges in passing on higher costs to consumers. Additionally, our REMIX model shows that margins are expected to contract, particularly in 2025, due to growth resilience. Our REVS framework supports this outlook, highlighting sectors like Media and Real Estate as having positive momentum, while sectors such as Mining and Tech Hardware are expected to struggle.

In the Consumer sector, profit margin expectations are positive, but lower than last quarter. The ability to increase prices is counterbalanced by rising operational and labour costs, which pose significant challenges. Effective cost management will be crucial for maintaining profit margins amidst economic uncertainties and fluctuating consumer spending trends. PMI data reflects lower new orders and future output expectations in sectors like Beverages and Consumer Services, indicating potential pressure on profit margins. REVS data suggests that sectors like Consumer Staples and Household & Personal Products are likely to face continued margin pressures.

There is an overall increase in net positive expectations for pricing power over the next 12 months, also reflected by strong business sentiment and sector-specific dynamics, despite economic and political uncertainties, geopolitics, and high inflation.

Consumer-related firms have the highest net positive expectations for pricing power among the three groups tracked by the survey.

Net positive expectations for pricing power in Industrial firms is supported by PMI data which shows that sectors like Machinery & Equipment and Technology Equipment are experiencing strong demand, output growth, and new orders.

Volatile raw materials costs continue to weigh on pricing power expectations for Materials companies.

While firms anticipate some improvement in profitability, they are cautious due to rising costs and external uncertainties. PMI data supports these concerns, highlighting continued cost pressures and challenges in passing on higher costs to consumers.

Though profit margin expectations for Consumer-related companies is positive, it is lower than last quarter. Our REVS data suggests that come sectors like Consumer Staples and Household & Personal Products are likely to face continued margin pressures.

Profit margin expectations in the Industrial sector are positive but moderate and unchanged from the previous quarter. Significant investments in CAPEX and productivity-enhancing technologies are expected to yield efficiencies that support margins. However, managing rising labour costs and potential supply chain issues will be essential for sustaining profit improvements.

Despite weak pricing power, Materials companies have the highest net positive expectations for profit margins. This optimism is likely due to effective cost management strategies and ongoing investments in efficiency improvements. Favourable market dynamics and a focus on productivity gains help mitigate the impact of limited pricing power on margins. However, the sector remains vulnerable to raw material price volatility, geopolitical tensions, and competitive pressures.
