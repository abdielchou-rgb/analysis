# What happens to EM when the Fed starts easing?

## History may not repeat, but it often rhymes

Sentiment towards EM has improved rapidly since November amid growing expectations of an impending Fed easing cycle. In this note we look back at the last six Fed easing cycles (since 1989) to observe how global and EM assets typically perform into and after the first Fed cut of the cycle. Studying the different fundamental and valuation contexts at the time, we outline implications that we think will help navigate the road ahead. See page 2 for our key conclusions.

## Key questions fielded in this note:

\- How does the strength in EM & risk assets through '23 compare to the onset of prior Fed easing cycles?

\- Who has benefitted more from Fed easing - EM or DM?

\- What variable has helped predict whether EM will outperform?

\- How do EM fundamentals today compare with previous Fed cycles?

\- Which equity markets and sectors enjoyed Fed easing the most historically?

\- Does global fixed income keep rallying, or is the best behind us?

\- How did return profiles differ depending on whether the US entered recession?

## Executive Summary

UBS expects the first Fed cut in March. Looking across previous Fed easing cycles over the past 35 years, we outline 5 market implications that we think will help navigate the road ahead.

The rally in risk assets over the last 6-12m has been atypically strong relative to the onset of prior Fed easing cycles. US equity returns (+23%) over the last 12m, for example, have been almost twice as large as their average leading into prior easing cycles. And US HY credit spreads, in contrast to their historical tendency to widen ahead of Fed easing, have contracted by \~100bp over the last 12m. At 210bp over the next 2 years, markets are pricing 70% of the typical decline in policy rates that the Fed historically delivered, even though these episodes included recession that clearly isn't being priced today. As such, while continued supply-driven declines in US rates/inflation are an upside risk for EM assets again this year, we think markets may have frontloaded much of the boost from Fed easing. This message would be consistent with a) the high level of our EM risk appetite index which, at 0.7 s.d. above 'neutral', is as strong as it was at the height of China reopening optimism 12m ago, and b) foreign flows into EM ex China equities rebounding in Q4 to post their best year since 2014. We prefer a focus on alpha over beta.

FX: Ex US growth typically matters more than the Fed. Stay selective. Historically the broad USD held up well despite Fed rate cuts, rising \~2% on average over the subsequent 6m. This reflected a) US resilience after the 1995 and 1998 "insurance" cuts or b) sharp slowdowns in the rest of the world e.g. Germany contracted for 2 years after the US 2001 recession. By contrast the DXY weakened sharply when Fed easing coincided with robust ex-US growth (e.g. collapse of the Berlin Wall in 1989, or booming global trade/commodities in late '07). Ahead, UBS expects weak but stabilising growth in China/Europe and a mild US recession to drive mild USD depreciation vs. the EUR (1.15) this year. For EM, while stronger fundamentals render long historical comparisons less useful, we note that in the most recent (2019) cycle EM FX stagnated, on average, despite buoyant global risk assets. This likely reflected weak global trade and EM central banks cutting in concert with the Fed - themes that we see being highly relevant again today. While EM trade balances and FX carry are slightly higher today than in 2019, our RAI (+0.26 in July 2019 vs. +0.74 currently) and US recession risk are important offsets. We prefer relative value, seeing PLN, KRW & BRL outperforming CZK, CNH, CLP, COP & ZAR. Seasonal support for EM FX in Dec/early Jan typically fades later this month, and may offer more opportune timing to build hedges via long USD/EM protection.

Equities: We expect limited gains, but hold right-tail optionality amid China easing. MSCI EM typically posted modest returns post the first Fed cut (median 1.6% and 5.0% returns over the next 6-12m) but this aggregate masks strong variation. Returns were negative when the US slipped into recession (though less negative than for US equities), but strong in the non-recessionary periods of 1989, 1998 and H2-2019. Korea and Taiwan historically tended to outperform (Mexico performed well, too, but only when US recession was avoided), while IT, communication services and energy outperformed by sector. Today, mindful of UBS' US recession call, we remain selective and prefer markets with a) supportive valuations b) scope for domestic policy easing cycles to trigger shifts from fixed income to equities and c) proximity to the improving semiconductor cycle. We remain OW Korea, Taiwan, Brazil & Malaysia. We see China internet, supported by light investor positioning, a supportive regulatory and monetary policy backdrop (e.g. strong MLF and PSL expansion in December), and scope for margin improvement, is an inexpensive right-tail candidate for China and global growth performing better than expected.

Rates: Slower gains from here. Add to EM local debt (FX neutral) on dips, credit later. EM local debt was our preferred asset class in '23 and remains so, FX hedged, for '24. Still-high ex ante real rates in EM (3m saar core inflation for GBI EM is just 2.7%), benign global food prices, continued scope for "catch-up" core goods disinflation, and potential for US recession support continued exposure. However the recent rally has been faster than we expected: of the 8-10% total returns that we projected in November, almost half has materialised. We expect slower gains ahead, noting that the bulk of declines in US 10y yields historically came before the first Fed cut of the cycle unless the US fell into recession. We retain a preference for Latam (Mexico, Brazil and Chile) and see Asia lagging (Thailand, Singapore and Indonesia).
