## February 12

USTs close little changed, with muted activity; USD finishes mixed; Equity internals signal caution before US CPI, VIX rallies; CHF mildly weakens before Swiss CPI; European rates outperform cross-market; ECB's Kazaks backs cautious approach; DXY at 104.13 (+0.0%); US 10y at 4.179% (+0.4bp).

• USTs close little changed, amid a sound slate of high-grade corporate issuance and a drop in the New York Fed's 3y ahead median inflation expectations measure to 2.4%, the lowest on record.

• DXY USD Index settles unchanged ahead of the US January CPI release, where our economists expect further core goods deflation and stagnant core services inflation.

• US equity performance divergence – Mag7 losses against Russell 2000 gains – flashes signs of apprehension ahead of US CPI; the VIX Index rises \~8%.

• CHF (-0.1%) ends marginally weaker against USD, ahead of the Switzerland January CPI release.

• European rates outperform cross-market, with outperformance in semi-core and peripheral spreads.

• ECB Governing Council Member Kazaks describes expectations for rates to be cut in March or April as too optimistic and advocates for more cautious approach "until the inflation story is over."

## Developed Markets

## United States:

Activity was quiet across macro markets, with Hong Kong, Japan, and Brazil all closed for local holidays. Rallies across European (SX5E: +0.65%) and US (RSP: +0.68%) equities were led by lower-quality stocks. In particular, the divergence in US equity performance – Mag7 losses against Russell 2000 (+1.75%) gains – conveyed signs of apprehension ahead of the US CPI release, reinforced by the rise in implied equity volatility (VIX: +7.7%).

Gains in broader equities provided for modest risk-sensitive currency outperformance in the G10, led by the Scandinavian currencies. The US dollar finished mixed against peers in the G10, with the DXY USD Index unchanged for the session amid lethargic price action. CHF ended marginally weaker against USD, ahead of the Switzerland January CPI release. In EM, MXN (+0.1%) had a quiet session despite comments from Banxico Governor Rodriguez that downplayed recent strength in headline inflation.

Strength in European government bonds in the London session initially supported US Treasuries, until yields gradually retraced higher in the NY morning alongside several high-grade corporate issuance announcements. Given lack of propensity to take risk, yields returned lower for the session but eventually ended the session little changed across the curve.

Data releases likely went under the radar given close proximity to the first-tier economic data release on Tuesday. The New York Fed’s January 2024 Survey of Consumer Expectations showed median inflation expectations remained unchanged at the 1y (3.0%) and 5y (2.5%) ahead horizons. However, median inflation expectations at the 3y ahead horizon fell to 2.4% (P: 2.6%), the lowest since data collection began.

The Monthly Treasury Statement showed the federal government incurred a budget deficit of \$21.9bn in January (C: -\$21.0bn; January 2023: -\$38.8bn). Through the first four months of fiscal year 2024, the cumulative deficit registered -\$532bn and compares to -\$460bn in the prior-year period. The interest expense on public debt was \$69.2bn in January and totaled \$357bn in the first four months of fiscal year 2024, up 37% compared to the prior-year period.

The Mortgage Bankers Association reported that twenty percent (\$929bn) of the \$4.7tn of outstanding commercial mortgages held by lenders and market participants will mature in 2024. This represents a 28% increase from the \$720bn that matured in 2023. By property type, among loans back by office properties, 25% will come due in 2024 (for our latest views on commercial real estate risks, read here).

Our economists forecast US Core CPI rose by 0.26% m/m in January (C: 0.3%; December: 0.28%) and Headline CPI rose by 0.14% m/m in January (C: 0.2%; P: 0.23%). This would bring the annual rates, to 3.7% (C: 3.7%; P: 3.9%) and 2.9% (C: 2.9%; P: 3.4%), respectively. Further deflation in core goods is expected to be driven by weakness in used cars. Shelter prices likely continue to slow, but car insurance remains sticky. CPI fixings imply Headline CPI at 0.17% m/m in January and 2.94% y/y.

## Europe:

The Scandinavian currencies outperformed; SEK and NOK gained \~0.5% and USD and 0.6% against EUR. CHF ended the NY afternoon unchanged ahead of Switzerland January CPI data. This is an important data point not just to gauge domestic price pressures, but also for SNB intervention in FX markets.

European duration opened higher and then traded sideways in a muted environment. In the London morning, 10y Bund yields fell to 2.33% but sold off afterwards, in sympathy with USTs as Fedspeak weighed on duration. Curves bullsteepened in Europe, 2s10s steepened \~1bp in Germany and 10s30s by 1bp. 10y Bunds closed at 2.36% (-2bp d/d); in money markets, 2025 ECB meetings outperformed (-4bp). Core ASW cheapened, led by Schatz (-1.5bp). Risk asset performed well (Euro Stoxx 50: +0.65%), which pushed peripheral and semi-core spreads tighter. The 10y BTP-Bund spread tightened by 4bp, to 155bp, the tightest in 10 sessions; the 10y OAT-Bund spread tightened by 1bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

G10 FX movements were relatively muted ahead of US CPI data on Tuesday and amid limited macro catalysts on Monday. NZD (-0.3%) underperformed and partially reversed its outsized gain from Friday. Market participants reduced the amount of additional tightening priced in for the RBNZ over the next few meetings.

## Emerging Markets

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies were mostly unchanged on Monday. COP (+0.2%) led the region, despite few local drivers of the outperformance. MXN (+0.1%) and CLP (-0.03%) also had quiet sessions. Andean rates slightly underperformed. The CAM curve had losses concentrated in the belly (5y: +4bp) and IBR ended \~1bp higher across the curve. TIIEs twist flattened (2s10s: -5bp). Brazilian markets were closed on Monday in observance of a local holiday.

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
