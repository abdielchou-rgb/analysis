# China Economic Comment

## Data Preview: Better Y/Y Growth on Low Base in April

## April data preview: better y/y activity growth on low base

April PMIs showed a mixed picture, with NBS manufacturing PMI edging down by 0.4ppt to 50.4, while Caixin manufacturing PMI bounced by 0.3ppt to 51.4. NBS nonmanufacturing PMI softened by 1.8ppt to a still >50 reading. High frequency data showed continued but slightly smaller y/y contraction of property sales on low base, while y/y growth of port activities and FTL traffic index also improved. Auto sales and steel production growth softened however. Korea export and import growth both strengthened in April. For the upcoming April data release, we expect better y/y growth thanks to low base (vs March), while the underlying momentum (on 2019-24 CAGR basis) likely moderated a bit for most domestic activities. We expect slightly smaller y/y decline in property sales, continued deep contraction of property investment, robust infrastructure and manufacturing FAI growth, better y/y growth of retail sales (5%y/y), exports (-1.5%y/y) and industrial production (5.5%y/y). We also see largely stable TSF credit growth despite weak government bond issuance, slightly softer CPI (0%y/y) and less negative PPI (-2.4%y/y). Please see our UBS China Activity Tracker and the Appendix High Frequency Data Monitor for more details.

## April Politburo meeting signalled more policy support and reforms

The April Politburo meeting on April 30 set a more supportive policy tone, notably on fiscal policy and property market. While largely following the policy stance set at the March NPC meeting, the Politburo meeting called for faster issuance of special CGB and warned against late implementation of fiscal support (which was the case in 2023 with a de-facto fiscal tightening in H1), acknowledging weak domestic demand and corporate operating difficulties. It also called for ensuring delivery of homes, adjusting property policies, and preparing measures to help digest existing inventory of properties. Policy tone on monetary policy and consumption remained largely the same. We continue to expect additional RRR cut and LPR cuts in the rest of 2024 and some modest support for trade-in of consumer goods and equipment upgrading. More concrete measures are expected to be rolled out later by various government agencies and local governments. The meeting also confirmed that the 3rd Plenum will be held in July and mainly focus on deepening reforms. Please see our Politburo meeting takeaways for more details. .

## PMIs and high frequency data update

April NBS manufacturing PMI edged down by 0.4ppt to 50.4, after a surprising jump in March. New orders slid by 1.9ppt to 51.1 while production index improved by 0.7ppt to 52.9, indicating a mixed picture of manufacturing activities. New export orders also weakened by 0.7ppt to 50.6, while import index dipped by 2.3ppt to 48.1. Raw material inventory stayed below 50 at 48.1. Quantity of purchase and finished goods slid by 2.2/1.6ppt to 50.5/47.3, respectively. Input price rebounded by 3.5ppt to 54, while output price turned less bad by 1.7ppt to 49.1. Employment index edged down by 0.1ppt to 48, pointing to lingering labor market pressures. On the other hand, Caixin manufacturing PMI edged up by 0.3ppt to a 14-month-high reading of 51.4, with both new orders and output index picking up, especially the former. As we discussed before, such divergence between NBS and Caixin PMIs is not uncommon, largely due to the difference in survey sample selection, while we may need other high frequency data to cross-check the underlying strength of the manufacturing sector.

NBS non-manufacturing PMI softened by 1.8ppt to 51.2, staying >50 consecutively since January 2023. In particular, services PMI slid by 2.1ppt to 50.3, while construction PMI held up at 56.3, reflecting robust momentum in construction activities. By sector, PMIs of railway transport, road transport, post, telecom / broadcast / TV were above 55, while those of capital market and real estate services were much weaker, the latter echoing continued property downturn year-to-date.

High frequency data shows that UBS China Activity Index (y/y growth) likely edged up. Property sales in 30 major cities narrowed y/y decline to -39%y/y in April from -47%y/y in March, thanks to low base. Top 100 developers' contract sales volume also saw a smaller contraction of -42%y/y (vs -47%y/y before). Port cargo and container throughput improved to 3%y/y and 9%y/y, respectively. FTL traffic index growth also improved to -4%y/y from -11%y/y before. On the other hand, steel production cooled to -15%y/y in the first 20 days of April from -7%y/y in March. Auto retail sales cooled to -12%y/y in the first 21 days from 7%y/y before on high base, while wholesales growth edged down to 8%y/y. 18-city subway passenger turnover stayed elevated at over 71 million person\*times per day, with its growth slowing to 6%y/y (vs 7% previously) on fading low base effect. Overall speaking, our UBS China Activity Index (y/y growth) likely improved slightly from the March growth, partly thanks to low base for some index components.

# April data preview: better y/y growth on low base

For the upcoming April data release, we expect:

Industrial production growth likely improved to 5.5%y/y, partly thanks to a low base (on CAGR basis). NBS PMI edged down by 0.4ppt to 50.4, while production index improving by 0.7ppt but new orders cooling by 1.9ppt. On the other hand, Caixin PMI edged up by 0.3ppt to 51.4, with a stronger production index (up by 0.3ppt to 53.1). PMI readings suggest that sequential growth momentum of manufacturing production likely improved a bit. The operating rate of electric arc furnaces averaged 60% in April, -4ppt y/y (vs -7ppt previously). Steel production declined by -15%y/y in the first 20 days from -7%y/y previously. Operating rates of cement mills rebounded seasonally to 45%, 2ppt below last year (vs -11ppt previously), while that of asphalt facilities edged down to 27%, 10ppt lower than last year (vs -5ppt previously). Logistic activities growth likely improved, as the Full-Truck-Load traffic index turned less bad to -4%y/y (vs -11%y/y previously). Overall, we expect April IP growth to have improved to 5.5%y/y, thanks to a low base last year (on CAGR basis), while its 2019-24 CAGR growth likely cooled a bit.

Property sales likely stayed in deep but slightly smaller y/y contraction. High frequency data showed property sales in 30 major cities narrowed decline to -39%y/y in April on low base, vs -47%y/y previously. Top 100 developers' contract sales volume also contracted by less to -42%y/y (vs -47%y/y before). 100-city local land transaction volume cooled notably to -35%y/y from -4%y/y previously, indicating subdued local government land sales revenue and weak future new starts. Overall, we expect national property sales likely stayed in deep contraction of 16-18%y/y, slightly less bad than - 18%y/y previously. New starts may decline by another -25%y/y, while property investment likely stayed weak at a decline of around -10%y/y.

Overall FAI growth likely largely stabilized at around 4.5%y/y. Infrastructure investment growth likely stayed robust at 8-10%y/y in April. Issuance of special LG bond reached only RMB 722bn in Jan-Apr 2024, much weaker than a year ago by almost RMB 900bn. Local government land sales revenue declined by -7%y/y in Q1. That said, RMB 1 trillion special CGB issued in Q4 2023 should help support infrastructure investment in H1 2024, especially hydraulic & water projects and post-disaster recovery construction. Meanwhile, manufacturing investment growth likely held up at 8-10%y/y thanks to continued policy support (e.g. in equipment upgrading). Further considering continued weakness in property investment, overall FAI growth likely largely stabilized at around 4.5% (YTD 4.5%y/y).

Retail sales growth likely improved to 5%y/y on low basis (on CAGR basis). Auto retail sales growth slowed to -12%y/y in the first 21 days of April vs 7%y/y in March, whilst auto wholesale growth only edged down to 8%y/y vs 9%y/y previously. 18-city metro passenger turnover stayed elevated at over 71mn person\*time per day and its growth slowing to 6%y/y from 7%y/y previously. With a low base last year on CAGR basis, we expect retail sales growth to have improved to 5%y/y last month from 3.1%y/y in March, while its 2019-24 CAGR likely cooled a bit.

Export growth likely improved on a low base. New export orders in NBS PMI was 0.7ppt weaker than March at 50.6, while that of Caixin PMI rebounded by 1.7ppt to 53.3. US ISM PMI and Eurozone manufacturing PMI both weakened in April, while Eurozone service PMI edged up. Export growth of Korea (+14%y/y, vs +3%y/y previously) picked up in April, likely indicating better momentum of global tech cycle. Meanwhile, Korea's exports to China picked up to 10%y/y (vs 0%y/y previously), as did its imports from China (5%, vs -14% previously), both boosted by low base effect. Vietnam export growth stayed robust at 11%y/y (vs 13% previously). Overall, we expect China's export growth to have improved to -1.5%y/y from -7.5% before, with its 2019- 24 CAGR also edged up. Imports may have rebounded to 4%y/y (from -1.9% previously) partly thanks to rising oil and iron ore prices, leading to a larger trade surplus of \$72.6bn

CPI likely edged down to 0%y/y; PPI turned less negative. High frequency data showed a mixed picture of sequential change of producer prices, with that of NBS price basket declining m/m while that of MOC price basket increasing. Thus we assume a largely stable sequential PPI, leading to a less negative PPI y/y growth of -2.4%y/y thanks to low base. Meanwhile, non-food price likely rebounded m/m after a sharp decline in March, and gasoline price was hiked twice in April before the latest small cut at end April. High frequency data suggests average food price saw a smaller m/m decline, with pork price +1.5%m/m (+1.2%y/y, vs -5% previously), vegetable prices -5%m/m (-5%y/ y), egg price -3%m/m (-11%y/y), while fruit prices -1%m/m (-9%y/y). Overall, we expect headline CPI to have edged down to 0%y/y in April.

Credit growth likely stabilized. Banks likely continued to offer credit support for the real economy. Headline new RMB loans may have printed RMB 800bn, slightly higher than last year after its significant weakness in Feb-Mar. Meanwhile, net issuance of government bond likely recorded a small decline of RMB 50bn, much weaker than a year ago (-505bn y/y). Corporate bonds may have seen a robust increase of RMB 300bn, largely at par than a year ago. Shadow credit may have recorded a small decline of 100- 150bn, similar to a year ago. As such, overall new TSF likely printed RMB 1.1 trillion (RMB 125bn below a year ago), leading to a stable y/y growth of adjusted TSF and official headline TSF at 8.7-8.8%y/y. Credit impulse likely remained at -1.6% of GDP.

FX reserves likely increased by around \$10bn. Capital outflow likely continued in April, although RMB stayed largely stable vs the USD over April amid the dollar strength. We estimate the valuation effect from major reserve currencies' movement has likely been a notable loss of \$20-25bn, while DM financial market correction may have contributed to additional valuation loss. Taking other factors into account (larger trade surplus and net FDI inflows), China's FX reserves may have increased by \$10bn to \$3.256 trillion.
