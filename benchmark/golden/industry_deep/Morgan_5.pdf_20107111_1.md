# Farewell, NIRP and YCC as Expected: Quick Impression Before Gov. Ueda's Press Conference

## In line with our prior forecast

No surprise: At the March Monetary Policy Meeting (MPM), the BoJ judged it came in sight that the price stability target of 2% would be achieved in a sustainable and stable manner. It decided to terminate its negative interest rate policy (NIRP), yield curve control (YCC), the overshooting commitment on the monetary base (as it "fulfilled the conditions for its achievement"), and ETF and J-REIT purchasing.

The content of the policy adjustment was mostly in line with what we predicted in our preview note (BoJ Watch: March MPM Preview: Farewell, NIRP and YCC(11 Mar 2024))( Exhibit 1 ).

This was not the consensus view when we predicted a policy change at the March MPM as our base scenario back in January (BoJ Watch: Review of the January MPM and Outlook Report (23 Jan 2024)), but the markets have since fully discounted such action in light of the robust shunto wage results and detailed media coverage ahead of the March Monetary Policy Meeting.

The BoJ included in the policy statement the sentence that "Given the current outlook for economic activity and prices, the Bank anticipates that accommodative financial conditions will be maintained for the time being", but we think that we need to pay attention to the expression "Given the current outlook　for economic activity and prices" and "for the time being".

## Changes in the Monetary Policy Framework (March 2024)

As recent data and anecdotal information have gradually shown that the virtuous cycle between wages and prices has become more solid, the Bank judged it came in sight that the price stability target of 2 percent would be achieved in a sustainable and stable manner toward the end of the projection period of the January 2024 Outlook Report. It considers that its large-scale monetary easing measures have fulfilled their roles, including the negative interest rate policy and the yield curve control.

With the price stability target, the Bank will conduct monetary policy as appropriate, guiding the short-term interest rate as a primary policy tool, in response to developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions from the perspective of sustainable and stable achievement of the target. Given the current outlook for economic activity and prices, it anticipates that accommodative financial conditions will be maintained for the time being.

Continuation of JGB purchasing　for Apr-Jun: The BoJ issued guidance stating that it will continue its JGB purchases with "broadly the same amount as before", which is about 6 trillion yen per month. This was in line with our forecast. We think that the move was a means of avoiding a discontinuous upsurge in yields rather than the start of a new monetary easing. The Financial Market Department issued a statement that it will purchase JGBs with "broadly the same amount as before" from April 2024 through June 2024. For Jul-Sep and beyond, the pace will likely be determined at MPMs　ahead.

Abolishing tiered current account structure: As for the guideline for market operations, the BoJ decided to "encourage the uncollateralized overnight call rate to remain at around 0 to 0.1%". The BoJ thus abolished its three-tiered current account structure and will start to apply 0.1% to the whole excess reserves, which was also in line with our forecast. Depending on banks, the payment of interest on the whole excess reserves could significantly boost interest earnings when rates are eventually raised ahead. New loan disbursements under the Fund-Provisioning Measures to Stimulate Bank Lending will be extended only for a duration of one year with 0.1% rate, which we think implies the likelihood of abolishment of these loan programs after one year.

# Governor’s post-MPM press conference: Will he mention on the “appropriate level of interest rates”?

BoJ Governor Kazuo Ueda will hold a press conference at 3:30pm JST.

One focus would be whether he would mention on "an appropriate rate level”. We should also be alert to any mention of details of the new JGB purchasing framework. We wrote the following in our BoJ MPM preview (BoJ Watch: March MPM Preview: Farewell, NIRP and YCC(11 Mar 2024))

"We expect comments by BoJ Gov. Ueda at the press conference to be mixed, including both dovish and hawkish elements.

We think Gov. Ueda is likely to emphasize the stance presented by Deputy Governor Uchida in a February speech that “it is hard to imagine a path in which it would then keep raising the interest rate rapidly. The Bank would, I think, maintain accommodative financial conditions.” Additionally, the real interest rate is currently at a substantial negative level, and Gov. Ueda might state that he does not expect a major change in that situation. The possibility of suggesting a future rate-hike schedule is extremely low, in our opinion.

Regarding the actual short-term policy rate path, meanwhile, we expect the Bank to adopt a stance that it depends on future economic and inflation developments.The Bank is unlikely to rule out rate hikes at a moderate pace in the future. It might adhere to the fundamental approach of “set the policy interest rate at an appropriate level so that CPI inflation will be at around the 2 percent target.” "I

## Limited impact on the economy

Risk of BoJ's policy mistake has declined: We think risk of the BoJ making a policy mistake and a resulting return of the Japanese economy to deflation has substantially declined. The BoJ did not have clear inflation target before 2013 and only finally clarified its understanding of price stability as 1% inflation　for the time being. The lack of a proper inflation targeting led the Bank into hasty shifts to tightening when inflation marginally turned positive. Since adoption of the 2% inflation target (the price stability target) in 2013, such shifts disappeared. The inflation target still ties the hands of the BoJ　to maintain its dovish bias. This time, the BoJ waited to remove the negative interest rate until the Japan-style core CPI YoY remains 2% or above for 22 consecutive months and it confirmed two straight years of strong wage hikes in the spring labor negotiations.

Japan’s monetary policy still accommodative: We expect impact on the economy from the removal of negative interest rate policy (NIRP) to be small enough to ignore. According to the BoJ’s Outlook Report from January 2024, medium-term expected inflation rates are roughly 2% at companies and about 1% at households. The ex-ante real interest rate, defined as nominal interest rates minus inflation expectations, is thus still substantially negative. We estimate that Japan's nominal neutral interest rate is about 1%. A 10bp hike of the overnight uncollateralized call rate from a range of -0.1% to 0% to a range of 0% to +0.1% leaves monetary policy in an accommodative posture.

No immediate impact on home mortgage loans : In Japan’s case, there is a framework that keeps policy rate hikes from immediately affecting variable-rate home loans, and we advise caution toward discussions that ignore this institutional aspect. The first point is that many variable-rate home loans are linked to the short-term prime rate charged by banks. However, the short-term prime rate has not changed at all since 2009, including after rollout of negative interest rates in 2016. We think the short-term prime rate is unlikely to shift until after multiple hikes of the policy rate and hence expect minimal impact on variable-rate home loans ( Exhibit 2 ).

Another point is that many variable-rate home loans contractually apply the so-called fiveyear rule and 125% rule. The five-year rule stipulates that the monthly repayment amount stays the same for five years through use of a reduction of the principal portion payment by an amount that offsets any increase in the interest payment portion. The 125% rule, meanwhile, limits increase in the repayment from the sixth year to 125% of the original repayment amount, although we do not expect the pace of the BoJ's rate hikes to be fast enough to hit the 125% rule.

Overall positive interest income in the corporate sector: As explained in a speech delivered by BoJ Deputy Governor Himino in December 2023, we think short-term impact on the corporate sector should be minor on an aggregated basis.

“Next, looking at the corporate sector, it has reduced its debt, has accumulated funds on hand, and has come to register a net interest income surplus. Therefore, the impact of rising interest rates on the net interest income of the overall corporate sector may be more limited than would have been the case when the sector had more debt and less funds. Of course, as some firms carry high debt while others have a significant amount of financial assets, the impact would vary across individual firms.” (Dep. Gov. Himino, Dec 2023)

While there might be impact on some smaller businesses and companies with large debt levels if interest rates proceed, we do not anticipate the start of a rapid rate-hike cycle by the Bank.
