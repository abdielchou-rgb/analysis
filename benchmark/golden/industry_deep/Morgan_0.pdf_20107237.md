# Asia holds economic, demographic, and emissions significance for global investors

We believe Asia plays a key role in the next advancement of sustainability – because Asia holds economic, demographic, and emissions significance.

Economic significance: Asia Pacific accounts for 36% of the world's GDP on the basis of 2022 data. Morgan Stanley forecasts that Asia ex-Japan's GDP growth will continue to surpass that of major DMs throughout 2025, particularly led by India, Indonesia, and China ( Exhibit 7 ). Separately, S&P Global reports that the share of APAC GDP will further increase and could exceed 42% by 2040, while that of the EU and US will shrink a few percentage points.

The positive outlook for the Asian economies is important, especially because many markets are pivoting toward high-tech green industries as a key growth driver. Within Asia, China's "new three" – solar, batteries, and EVs – are expected by the Chinese government to lead the next growth phrase. while India's latest budget focuses on green economy and climate investments. Even for Japan, where our economists forecast slower growth, the government has placed strong focus on GX – green transformation – as a way to boost economic growth. This pivot towards green industries has also led to substantial cost reduction in renewable technologies. See the below chart or our Bluepaper for more details.

Demographic significance: Asia hosts more than half of the global population as of 2022 and the UN estimates this to continue through to 2050 ( Exhibit 5 ), with the largest populations located in India, China, Pakistan, Indonesia, and Bangladesh ( Exhibit 6 ). Given its large population, we believe Asia will have a significant influence on social trends globally, as well as how investors assess the social issues.

Despite Asia's population dominance, the region will be at the forefront of challenges posed by aging demographics, as discussed in our Asia outlook report. The challenges include shrinking of labour force and therefore labour shortage, especially in the conventionally labour-intensive areas that have relied on people to perform productive tasks. Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo’s research sheds light on the upside. The aging of the population and the slow growth of GDP per capita had no sign of a negative relationship. The countries experiencing more rapid demographic changes are more likely to adopt labour-replacing technologies such as robotics and artificial intelligence, which can increase aggregate output despite reduced labour input. When capital is sufficiently available (or capital is sufficiently cheaper than labour), a shortage of younger and middleaged (20- to 49-year-old) workers can even trigger faster uptake of the automation technologies – so much faster that the negative impact of labour scarcity could be offset or reversed. Asia is rapidly aging, but this may not necessarily impair the region's economic output if invests capital and adopts technology in a timely manner.

Emissions significance: Asia hosts the largest emitters of the globe. The world’s seven largest emitters of greenhouse gases (including fossil $\mathsf { C O } _ { 2 } , \mathsf { C H } _ { 4 } , \mathsf { N } _ { 2 } \mathsf { O } ,$ F-gases, and inventory-based LULUCF CO ) remained China, the USA, India, the EU, Russia, Brazil, and Indonesia in 2021, with the shares of the top three amounting to 30% (China), 11% (USA), and 7% (India) ( Exhibit 8 ). On a per capita basis, the USA and Russia account for greater amounts of GHG emissions ( Exhibit 9 ).

We believe the energy transition opportunity will be huge in Asia, driven by regulatory support (e.g., taxonomies and guidance on transition in various markets), technology and cost improvement (see the Bluepaper above or this report on Japan's decarbonsation). Asian countries are key players in the critical minerals supply chain to drive the energy transition globally.

# The correlation between Asian sustainability stocks and global markets is decreasing

Investing in Asia Pacific sustainability stocks can bring some diversification benefits: We compared conventional MSCI indices and their ESG series for Asia vs. World. Exhibit 11 and Exhibit 12 display one-year historical price performance of and deviation between the ESG indices vs. corresponding benchmarks for reference. In our correlation analysis between Asia Pacific and World using MSCI AC Asia Pacific and MSCI World Indices and their ESG Leaders indices as representative indicators, we found low correlations (0.37- 0.48 in range) in return between Asia and World whether for conventional or ESG and irrespective of the duration of the indices' performance – i.e., one year, three years, five years, or 10 years ( Exhibit 10 ).

Another observation was that the shorter the performance time horizon, the less correlated the indices became; e.g., one-year and three-year AC Asia Pacific vs World coefficients (0.39 and 0.40, respectively) had weaker correlations than five-year (0.48) and 10-year (0.47). Evidently, the diversification benefit of investing in Asia against World, or alternatively, investing in Asia's ESG stocks would hold true.

## Analyst Stock Ratings

Overweight (O). The stock's total return is expected to exceed the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis, over the next 12-18 months.

Equal-weight (E). The stock's total return is expected to be in line with the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis, over the next 12-18 months

Not-Rated (NR). Currently the analyst does not have adequate conviction about the stock's total return relative to the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis, over the next 12-18 months.

Underweight (U). The stock's total return is expected to be below the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis, over the next 12-18 months.

Unless otherwise specified, the time frame for price targets included in Morgan Stanley Research is 12 to 18 months.
