# Seizing le grand opportunity

Chart of the week: Dodge momentum index vs. Legrand's organic growth When Fed Chair Powell said "the time has come for policy to adjust" at Jackson Hole last month, we heard it as "the time has come to upgrade Legrand", which we did earlier this week. The market is currently pricing in over 250bps of rate cuts by the end of next year, which if realized, should help make market conditions more conducive towards moving construction projects forward, supporting Legrand's growth dynamics where non-resi makes up 45% of sales, followed by resi at 40% and data centers at 15%. Our chart this week shows the Dodge momentum index (DMI), which represents the value of non-resi building projects going into planning in the US, and leads non-resi construction spend by a year. The DMI for August rose by 24% YoY and 3% MoM highlighting the optimism building up ahead of the "stronger market conditions" expected for next year. Within the mix, commercial planning grew 42% YoY/2% MoM, supported by broad-based improvements including a pick up in warehouse projects, while data centers continued to dominate the segment. Meanwhile institutional planning was up 8% YoY/6% MoM driven by growth in the healthcare space.

## Last week

Alstom's shares came under pressure on Tuesday as one broker published a note highlighting that the group still had \~€0.5bn of Commercial Paper (CP) as of September 10. While this was factually correct, in our view, looking only at CP is not enough to have a good view on liquidity or on the FCF trajectory: 1/ Alstom also has lots of cash and OPCVM to manage its daily liquidity, especially after the rights issue and the sale of its signalling business 2/ CPs have defined maturities (3-4 months), so even if they generate cash in a given day, CPs are not being adjusted on a daily basis; 3/ CPs are useful as a liquidity tool and there will likely always remain a few hundreds millions on the balance sheet at all times. Overall, net debt is likely to come down to around €1.1bn at September-end and to around €500m six months later. Alstom also reaffirmed all elements of its guidance at a competitor's conference last week. Wartsila held an analyst conference call with its CEO on Tuesday. The general tone was positive. Decarbonization continues to drive Marine and Energy divisions. In Marine, Wartsila continues to see high activity levels with the company well-positioned in green fuels and dual fuel engines with some expansion in yard capacity. In Energy, the company continues to expect a better H2 (previously guided) and see data centres as a growth area (this is new) for power plants. Also on Tuesday, KONE announced a strategic review of its door business to assess structure and synergies including a potential sale of the business. Doors are c2% of group revenue or €220m in size with KONE in a strong cash position (net cash -€417m). The relevant product portfolio is mostly automatic/revolving doors with good profitability but less associated service revenues (vs elevators). No guidance was given on timing for the review. We note that Assa Abloy could be one of the potential candidates for the acquisition of KONE's door business, which would fit well with its strategy to grow the installed base and service part of its Entrance Systems' division. The deal could add nearly 2% of group revenue while diluting margins by c.10bps per our estimates. In an ad-hoc release on Thursday, US peer GE Vernova stated that it now expects the Wind segment to generate around a \$300m EBITDA loss in Q3 due to recent offshore wind blade failures, and to be modestly profitable in Q4. The previous guidance was for the Wind segment to approach profitability for the full-year following a loss of €289m in H1. Vernova's offshore blade issues are company-specific so there is no obvious readacross to Siemens Energy's wind division. On the other hand the readacross for Grid and Gas was very positive, as Vernova noted incremental strength in the Power and Electrification segments, with both trending towards the higher end of its EBITDA margin guidance, offsetting the additional costs in its Wind segment.

## Reports you might have missed

Legrand - Time to get constructive - Upgrade to BUY: Legrand is one of the most sensitive capgoods stocks to the upcoming rate cutting cycle given its large construction exposure (40% resi, 45% non-resi and 15% data centers). Legrand's P/E premium to the sector average has decreased to 10% vs 20% historically and we now expect the stock to gradually regain its lost status as the group's main verticals (housing and offices) should bottom out shortly, raising investors confidence in the group's ability to deliver stronger growth in the future. We like the quality of Legrand's positioning (a "giant in niches") and its ability to leverage the trend towards mass electrification, the smart home revolution, the growing demand for energy efficiency in buildings and the strong momentum in data centers. Rising concerns on the economy also mean that its defensive attributes (high and steady margins, best-in class FCF generation, strong pricing power) should be sought and valued once again. We upgrade our rating to BUY with a TP of €115. See full report here.

Siemens Energy - Still in the early stage of an accelerating investment cycle: Market conditions for the Gas and Grid divisions appear to get stronger by the day, both in terms of volumes and pricing. Recent comments from US peer GE Vernova are very encouraging as it feels like we are still in the early stage of an accelerating investment cycle of what is needed for the energy transition. The path to breakeven for Gamesa will not be linear but the turnaround still looks very much on track for FY26. Overall, we expect ENR to upgrade its FY26 growth and margin ambitions in November. We also see the group becoming a 10% EBITA margin company later in the decade. BUY. See full report here.

Renishaw - Watching developments: Overall, at a headline level, Renishaw's Jun FY24 results (PBT £122.6m vs DBe £127.0m) were below our expectations, although some of this related to one-off charges taken through trading, including R&D impairments of £3.3m. We have reduced our estimates (FY25E EPS c. -11%; FY26E c. -10%), largely reflecting lower operational gearing assumptions on additional sales, given the FY24 outturn and management's comments on further inflationary pressures. This also drives a reduction in our price-target to 4,000p. The weak macroeconomic backdrop, combined with the shares trading on a FY25E EV/ sales of 3.14x and PER of 23.0x (20.9x ex net cash) our revised estimates, means we retain our Hold rating. ## We read in the news

Siemens agrees to acquire Trayer Engineering Corporation to broaden product portfolio for grid modernization "Siemens has announced its agreement to acquire California-based Trayer Engineering Corporation (Trayer), a leader in the design and manufacturing of medium voltage secondary distribution switchgear suitable for outdoor and below ground applications. Trayer specializes in hermetically sealed padmount and submersible switchgear, resistant to extreme weather conditions. The company will provide complementary technologies to the Electrification and Automation Business Unit at Siemens, to further support the market trend of undergrounding overhead electrical networks, increasing resilience of the North American distribution grids. Siemens, as a leading player in the Electrification and Automation industry, will increase the availability of Trayer’s leading submersible and padmount switchgear portfolio by its widespread presence in the market. Trayer has a long history in producing maintenance-free switchgear of the highest quality, designed to thrive in the most challenging circumstances. Its submersible distribution switchgear is perfectly suited for use in areas prone to flooding or extreme weather conditions, providing reliable electrical distribution while being protected from water and environmental damage. Whereas padmount switchgear is typically installed at ground level in residential, commercial, and industrial areas, offering easy access for safety inspections and operation while ensuring reliable, safe, and efficient electrical distribution for underground electrical grids. Trayer was founded as a family business in 1962 and currently employs 130 people. It will continue to operate as a separate legal entity, under the banner “A Siemens Business”. The transaction is subject to regulatory approval, and closing is expected in calendar year 2024. Terms of the transaction are not disclosed." (Source: Siemens, 10 September 2024)

KONE to initiate a review of its Door Business "KONE Corporation, a global leader in the elevator and escalator industry, plans to initiate a review of its Door Business to assess its optimal structure and synergies, including a potential sale of the business. The review aims to evaluate the best environment for growing the business in a way that benefits its customers, employees, and other stakeholders. As part of this, KONE is considering incorporating the Door Business under its own legal structure. KONE Door Business provides maintenance, repair, modernization, and new equipment solutions for pedestrian and industrial doors. In 2023, KONE Door Business accounted for approximately 2% of KONE Group revenue. The contemplated review and actions are subject to local legal requirements and consultation of employee representatives." (Source: KONE, 10 September 2024)

Siemens Mobility to establish America’s first high-speed rail production facility in Horseheads, New York "U.S. Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer, Brightline West and Siemens Mobility announced today that Horseheads, New York will become home to North America’s first high-speed rail production facility. Site represents an approx. \$60 million investment. The first-of-its-kind facility in the United States will produce America’s first high-speed trains, the American Pioneer 220, which will operate on Brightline West’s Las Vegas to Southern California line. Selected for its skilled workforce, industrial history and ability to best meet the needs of Brightline West, Horseheads is located in the southern tier of New York state. Production at the facility is expected to begin in 2026. When fully operational, the new manufacturing facility will span nearly 300,000 square feet and create around 300 jobs – including electro-mechanical assemblers, quality management, quality control, industrial production and test engineers, project management, supply chain management and logistics employees. Just as New York’s rich rail history once helped shape the state, these skilled jobs will help shape high-speed rail production in our country for the very first time. Siemens Mobility also has a memorandum of understanding in place with the International Association of Machinist for union representation at the new site." (Source: Siemens, 9 September 2024)

Schneider Electric to invest £42 million in new manufacturing site in North Yorkshire "Schneider Electric, the leader in the digital transformation of energy management and automation, is investing £42 million in a new manufacturing facility in Scarborough, North Yorkshire. More than 200 jobs will be created to meet the increased demand for electrical equipment to drive the UK’s move to cleaner energy including renewable energy sources, electric vehicles, and intelligent, energy-efficient buildings. The site, which is almost triple the size of Schneider Electric’s existing Scarborough facility, is a blueprint for sustainable design and operations in the manufacturing industry. It is poised to become a net-zero plant and will use modern technology to reduce energy waste and maximise the use of renewable energy. It is expected to be net-zero in Scope 1 and 2 emissions when it opens in early 2025. " (Source: Schneider Electric, 9 September 2024)

Macroeconomic fluctuations often account for most of the risks associated with exposures to instruments that promise to pay fixed or variable interest rates. For an investor who is long fixed-rate instruments (thus receiving these cash flows), increases in interest rates naturally lift the discount factors applied to the expected cash flows and thus cause a loss. The longer the maturity of a certain cash flow and the higher the move in the discount factor, the higher will be the loss. Upside surprises in inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most common adverse macroeconomic shocks to receivers. But counterparty exposure, issuer creditworthiness, client segmentation, regulation (including changes in assets holding limits for different types of investors), changes in tax policies, currency convertibility (which may constrain currency conversion, repatriation of profits and/or liquidation of positions), and settlement issues related to local clearing houses are also important risk factors. The sensitivity of fixed-income instruments to macroeconomic shocks may be mitigated by indexing the contracted cash flows to inflation, to FX depreciation, or to specified interest rates - these are common in emerging markets. The index fixings may - by construction - lag or mis-measure the actual move in the underlying variables they are intended to track. The choice of the proper fixing (or metric) is particularly important in swaps markets, where floating coupon rates (i.e., coupons indexed to a typically short-dated interest rate reference index) are exchanged for fixed coupons. Funding in a currency that differs from the currency in which coupons are denominated carries FX risk.
