# Rates Map: Risk/reward skewed towards lower rates

 Another week of solid data and low conviction pushed US 10y yields to their highest since November. We think the risk/reward is skewed towards lower rates but expect sentiment to remain fragile into US PCE and jobs data next week.

The core PCE deflator rose 3.7% (saar) in Q1, ahead of expectations for 3.5%. This points to the March change in core PCE prices rounding up to 2.8% y/y in today's PCE data, in contrast to prior UBS expectations of 2.7%.

 There was widespread frustration in the market for four months from investors that did not buy US real yields at 2.50% in October, so we think it will be difficult for 10yr real yields to increase beyond 2.40%. That means any dramatic move will rely heavily on moves in breakeven inflation, and they are already within a handful of basis points of their cheapest levels since October.

We think the US Treasury is likely to announce a new buyback program on 1 May, which should help 20s but we do not expect this to be a game changer. In last November's refunding announcement the US Treasury was able to halt the selloff by saying auction sizes would stop rising in May. There are no similar options this time, because the Treasury's buyback program requires more on-the-run issuance to buy back off-the-run issuance. Unlike central bank bond purchases, no duration is removed form the market.

Core bond market divergence has been one of our key themes and clients have asked how the ECB would deal with spillovers from a "no landing" scenario in the US after PEPP reinvestments end. We agree with comments by Bank of Italy governor Panetta that the ECB may have to cut more if global rates are higher on the back of unchanged Fed policy. US and European growth rates and policy stances have diverged for multiple years in a row in the past (Figure 2).

We expect European rates duration to start finding buying support at these levels even if many ECB GC members are hesitant to commit beyond July and UBS economics warns that the Bundesbank wage growth series could jump higher in March. UBS economics still expects the UBS index of monthly Eurozone negotiated wage growth, which excludes one-offs, to ease below 4% in early 2024 from 4.7% in December 2023. UBS projections, both for Germany and the Eurozone, is that wage growth will eventually decline towards 3% during 2025. UBS expects April headline inflation (out on 30 April) to stay unchanged from March at 2.4% y/y and core to drop 0.4 ppt to 2.5% y/y.

 We expect EGB spreads to be range-bound in the coming weeks but remain broadly supported. Focus will be on credit reviews for France by Fitch and Moody today. A negative rating action would exert some pressure on OAT spreads in the short term, potentially widening them by 5-7bps. We think any material widening in 10y OAT spreads would offer an attractive tactical buying opportunity.
