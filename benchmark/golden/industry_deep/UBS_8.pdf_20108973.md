# European Equity Strategy Let the games begin: Earnings season

## Setting the Q2 scene

Just before the Olympics get under way, the European corporate earnings season begins. We have previously noted that, aside from recent election risk premia, Europe has become an increasingly idiosyncratic market. It is therefore likely that this earnings season will offer material alpha opportunities and risks.

Reporting begins in earnest on Wednesday July 17 with ASML. The season will be mostly over by August 8 with the insurers among the last to report. Expectations have been rising in recent months after most companies positively surprised estimates in Q1 and because optimism (PMIs) in Europe has been rising as rate cuts get underway. The April and May sector PMI data shows it is services with strength in output and output prices. Autos and other manufacturers are still reporting weakness.

## It's the outlooks that matter

As we reach the half way point of the year for most companies, their outlook statements become critical. Analyst expectations are typically too high on average even at this time of year - by 12% going back to 1998. In the last 10 years and in years when growth is positive, the overestimation is typically more like 4-6% at this time of year. Consensus SXXP growth for 2024 is a historically low +4% but our models suggest sales growth is offset by margin weakness and we expect another year of 0% growth. 2025 consensus growth is currently +8% but 18-months out from actuals, the bottom-up error is 15- 20% (too high) or more on average!

There are still some striking divergences in the sectors that are forecast to grow (healthcare equipment, retail, cap goods, construction materials and insurance), versus those that are contracting (transportation, semis, software, mining and autos). Revisions in these sectors are also divergent although still quite modest in recent months.

## Stocks to watch for guidance changes

Around 250 companies reported a Q1 EBIT number. For these companies we highlight where the risk of a guidance shift is possible. That is, companies materially missing or exceeding the seasonally adjusted run-rate to consensus and where our analyst has an aligned view that the consensus figure will need to change.

Some of the larger companies highlighted in this screen with potential to disappoint are Siemens Energy, Nokia, Ericsson, Porsche and VW.

Larger companies the screen shows as having UBS expectations above consensus with a Q1 run rate above that required for 2024 consensus expectations include BASF, Orsted, adidas and TotalEnergies.

## It is the outlooks that matter

Q2 earnings are important in that they show whether companies are on track to meet longer term guidance. It is also somewhat difficult to track quarterly earnings in Europe when most companies report detailed accounts only semi-annually or less.

What is clear from history though, is that earnings growth is almost always revised down. Currently, consensus growth for 2024 is an already low 4% and for 2025 a more historically average starting point of 8%. Our analysis suggests that both years may continue to disappoint as modest sales growth is offset by some moderation in margins that remain very high relative to the pre-covid period.
