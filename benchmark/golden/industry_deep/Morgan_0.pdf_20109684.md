## Developed Markets

## United States:

Monday’s session was a tale of two halves, with price action that might have given some whiplash. Overnight, market participants continued to react to the recent weak economic data, including the soft US July employment report. As concerns around the possibility of a recession grew, a risk-off sentiment dominated the price action; global equities tumbled as market participants fled to safe havens such as bonds. Asian equity markets saw particular underperformance. Nikkei fell over 12% in its largest daily loss since October 1987, while Korea’s Kospi index posted its worst decline since 2008 and Taiwan’s Taiex had its worst day on record. European and US equity futures also saw notable declines, and the implied volatility of Euro Stoxx and the S&P 500 reached intraday highs of \~41% and \~65%, respectively.

The risk-off sentiment supported rates with double-digit rallies across the curve in JGBs, Bunds, and USTs. In the NY morning, 2y yields fell \~20bp and the 2s10s curve turned positive for the first time since July 2022. Some market participants may view the re-steepening of the curve as a grim signal, as the yield curve has previously re-steepened to back above zero right around the time an economic slowdown occurs.

STIR pricing also was reflective of increased expectations of a “hard landing.” Market-implied pricing for September and November both rose to 50bp of cuts, while market pricing had 8bp of an inter-meeting emergency cut priced in.

The corporate issuance space also felt the risk-off sentiment, with limited investment grade bond sales on Monday. Around 10 high grade issuers that were looking to sell new US investment grade bonds, according to an informal survey of debt underwriters, decided to stand down amid the heightened volatility.

An upside surprise in ISM services data eased fears of an imminent economic slowdown, calmed the risk-off sentiment, and spurred a reversal of a fair bit of the price action. Headline ISM Services rose to 51.4 in July (C: 51.0; P: 48.8). The prices paid and new orders indices both also rose from slight contraction to growth. More notable was the employment index, which rose 5 points to 51.1, reached the highest level since September 2023, and pushed back against the labor market deterioration that the employment report had suggested. However, underlying details in the employment index still had some worrying signals. The strength resulted from a smaller number of firms reporting lower employment (as opposed to a greater share of firms reporting higher employment), and comments from respondents of the survey included that they “have many open requisitions to fill“ while also noting that they are relying on “higher productivity instead of replacing turnover.”

However, the strong headline figures of the ISM data resulted in a sharp reversal of UST yields, and the 2y yield immediately pared earlier gains. The data also calmed the risk-off sentiment. US equities pared some of their earlier losses, European duration partially reversed its earlier richening, and market pricing for ECB and FOMC meetings retraced some of the additional cuts that were priced in.

The July 2024 edition of the Fed’s Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey (SLOOS) also showed a more positive picture of the US economy. The survey showed broad improvement in bank credit conditions in 3Q24, with much fewer banks tightening standards in 3Q24 than in 2Q24. Additionally, banks reported that after a year and a half of declines, demand for credit was unchanged even as interest rates fell over the period.

Ultimately, the UST curve modestly twist-steepened, with 2y yields up 4bp and 30y yields down 4bp. The inversion in the 2s10s curve returned, and market-implied pricing for an inter-meeting cut reduced to a tail-risk scenario.

While the S&P 500 pared some of its earlier losses, it still fell -3.0% in its largest daily drop in nearly two years. The decline was broad-based; all sectors ended in the red and over 95% of constituents saw declines.

## Japan:

JPY rates rallied strongly on Monday following the weaker-than-expected July US nonfarm payroll data and the subsequent rally in USTs on Friday. Demand to square positions seemingly helped trigger a substantial rally in JGB futures. JGB futures had already rallied by 100c following the NFP data on Friday. During the Tokyo morning session on Monday, JGB futures accelerated the rally and JPX activated a circuit breaker. Accordingly, JGBs sharply rallied led by the belly of the curve. Our strategists believe that the BoJ’s hawkishness amid weak household consumption and deterioration in overseas conditions had generated market concern of a possible BoJ policy mistake, and the concern put downward pressure on term premiums across the curve.

By Tokyo close, JGBs accelerated their rally including the super long end; 5y JGB rallied 21.5bp to 0.360%, 10y JGB rallied 20.0bp to 0.755%, and 30y JGB rallied 16.5bp to 1.910% on a simple yield basis. The TONA-OIS curve bull-flattened and underperformed JGBs. XCCY basis continued to widen on broad sectors led by the belly of the curve.

We expect the 10y JGB auction’s result to be sluggish due to Monday’s rally, while demand to square positions should steadily support demand. Whether the strong demand to square short positions will continue or not will likely depend on expectations around market pricing of the Fed’s rate-cutting cycle.

After the weak US nonfarm payroll data, market participants seemingly started to price in the risk of a US hard landing scenario. Accordingly, USD/JPY continued to trade lower from 146.65 to 141.70 during Tokyo trading hours amid what seemed to be a continuation of JPY carry trades unwinds. The sharp decline in Japanese stock market might also accelerate the unwinding of the Japan’s reflation trades and the risk-off sentiment. The risk sensitive currencies such as AUD, NOK, NZD particularly underperformed during Tokyo trading hours.

## Europe:

Safe-haven European currencies extended their Friday gains amid a broad-based retreat in risk demand. EUR/CHF (-0.5%) fell close to 0.92 at its session low before it rebounded to \~0.93. EUR continued to trade similar to safe-haven currencies and gained 0.4%. NOK underperformed and weakened 1.2% against EUR and 0.8% against USD.

It was an extremely volatile session for European rates, with large intraday swings. European duration opened richer following the big drawdowns in Japanese equities and the 10y Bund touched its intraday low of 2.07% amid the strong risk-off sentiment in global markets. As such, credit spreads and the German ASW complex widened in sympathy with the general risk sentiment; credit proxies such as the BTP/Bund spread touched an intraday high of 154bp. Euro Stoxx implied volatility reached an intraday maximum of \~41%, a level inferior only to the one seen during the Russian invasion of Ukraine headline over the previous couple of years.

Market-implied pricing for the ECB rate reached \~84bp of cumulative cuts through December 2024 in the London morning, but retraced the move in the London afternoon. The stronger-than-expected US ISM services data, where the indexes for new orders and business activity rose from slight contraction to growth – along with the headline number and the employment sub-component solidly above the 50 mark – helped ease some market participants’ concerns of recession and reduced the risk-off sentiment in the market. Following the data, there was a reversal of price action, and the 10y Bund closed 1.6bp cheaper on the day and the curve ultimately slightly bear-steepened. The German ASW complex ended the day broadly unchanged while EGB spreads lagged the move; the periphery underperformed core and semi-core, and the BTP/Bund settled 3.6bp wider at 149bp. Euro Stoxx implied volatility halved relative to its intraday high and closed at 33.4%, which was still above its long-term average of \~28%.

At the front end, whites led the rally as the front-loading of cuts continued, and whites/reds steepened during the day, which led the cheapening on the 5y area in expressions like EUR 2s5s10s (+1.35bp). Long volatility expressions continued to benefit as 5s10s30s closed the day \~3.7bp cheaper at 24.9bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

The DXY USD Index closed down 0.5%, as EUR continued to trade similar to safehaven currencies. AUD (-0.3%) sold off amid the broader risk-off tone ahead of the RBA meeting, at which the central bank is widely expected to keep rates unchanged.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

The carry trade unwind continued in AxJ currencies, which strengthened broadly even as global equity markets sold off amid global growth concerns. CNH rallied 0.6% to finish the Tokyo session at \~7.12 despite the USD/CNY fixing coming in higher than expected at 7.1345. This came alongside a relative outperformance of Chinese equity markets (CSI 300: -1.2%). MYR continued to be the main outperformer and rallied 1.7% to 4.42, the strongest level since mid-April 2023. KRW and TWD exhibited different reactions to the 8-9% sell-off in Korea and Taiwan equities, which may have been reflective of market positioning. KRW initially rose 0.4% alongside gains in AxJ peers but eventually weakened with the equity sell-off and ended the Tokyo session 0.3% lower to 1,364. Korea equities saw \$714mn of

foreign outflows, similar to last Friday. Meanwhile, TWD gained 0.5% to 32.64 despite \$2.1bn of foreign equity outflows. INR also underperformed and fell 0.1% to 83.85. THB and PHP both rallied 0.4% to 35.20 and 57.89, respectively, while IDR gained just 0.1%. Indonesia’s Finance Minister said the 2025 budget, which will be released this month, will be designed to accommodate the incoming administration’s priority programs and measures to spur growth.

AxJ rates rallied broadly as market participants turned to safe havens given the significant equity market weakness. China rates were an outlier and 10y CGB sold off 2bp to 2.14%, amid media reports of state banks selling government bonds. India, Indonesia, and Thailand rates all rallied by a similar magnitude and 10y government bond yields fell 3-4bp in all three countries. In line with their higher correlation with US rates, Hong Kong and Singapore rates outperformed, and the curves bull-steepened following Friday’s move in USTs. 2y swap yields fell 28bp in Hong Kong and 24bp in Singapore, and their 2s10s curves bull-steepened 11bp. In Korea, 2y NDIRS yields fell by a more modest 11bp given the significant amount of BoK rate hikes already priced and the curve bull-steepened 4bp.

## Developed Markets

• The BoJ released minutes from its June monetary policy meeting. While the BoJ maintained the status quo at the meeting, board members agreed it was appropriate to adjust the degree of monetary accommodation if underlying inflation continued to improve as they expected. They also noted that upside risk to the Bank's outlook should be a factor for a policy rate increase, which became a reason for the rate hike in July. One member argued that it was appropriate to keep the current easing monetary policy rate for the time being on the back of weak personal consumption and to support positive structural reforms of companies. Members also discussed the relationship between the recently weaker yen and the upside risk of the price inflation. One member supported a future rate hike from the aspect of risk management approach given that higher potential losses could result from the weaker yen. They also discussed the future JGB purchase plan. All the members acknowledged the idea that long-term interest rates should be freely formed among market participants separate from the BoJ’s purchase operation. Some members argued monetary policy should be controlled only through short-term interest rate manipulation and not JGB purchase operations. Based on these discussions, board members decided to reduce the amount of JGB purchase operations at the next July meeting, taking into account the opinions of market participants

• Chicago Fed President Goolsbee said that the Fed must pay attention to the weakness in the jobs market, but the Fed can wait for more data before the September meeting

• The Fed’s Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey, taken from June 20 to July 8, showed broad improvement in bank credit conditions in 3Q24. For business commercial and industrial loans, much fewer banks were tightening standards in 3Q24 than in 2Q24, and demand was no longer falling after about a year and a half of declines. In CRE lending, the improvement was less pronounced, but there, too, fewer banks were tightening and reporting declines in demand than a quarter or a year earlier. On net, our economists note this is positive for consumption, capital spending, and residential investment growth. Lending standards were not tightening nearly as broadly, and demand not falling as fast. Both household and business borrowing conditions improved after about a year and a half of tightening standards

• San Francisco Fed President Daly said "monetary policy is working in the way intended," adding that the Fed should not react to one data point, but rather the totality of data. She added that underneath the July jobs report, there is "some reason for confidence we are slowing, but not falling off a cliff," and that she will be closely watching the next employment report to see if it reflects the same dynamic

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
