# July MPM Preview: Left to Gov. Ueda's Decision

We think the economic case for a July rate hike is compelling, as more data point to a rise in the underlying inflation trend. We discuss potential implications on rates, FX, and equity markets.

## Key Takeaways

As our base case, we still think the possibility of the BoJ raising the policy rate from 0.1% to 0.25% in July is higher than in September or October.

Key data points　 ahead of the July meeting imply the possibility of increase in “underlying inflation” going forward.

■ We see flatter JGB curve in the case of a July hike as the market will likely speculate it as a coordinated move with the government to prevent JPY weakness.

■ We see further unwinding of JPY carry trade in the case of a July hike. We tactically enter long USD/JPY OTM put option to position for decline in USD/JPY.

If the yen depreciated against USD in the event of a July hike, it is likely that the stock market will see a stronger tolerance of the yen in food and retail.

# July MPM Preview: Left to Gov. Ueda's Decision

Takeshi Yamaguchi, Masayuki Inui

Five reasons why we expect a July rate hike

We still think the possibility of the Bank of Japan (BoJ) raising the policy rate from 0.1% to 0.25% at the July Monetary Policy Meetings (July 30-31) is higher than in September or October. Many sell-side economists and former BoJ officials, meanwhile, forecast a rate hike in September or later amid a lack of clear communications from the BoJ. Nikkei’s latest BoJ Watcher Survey reported that around 70% of respondents expect no rate hike in July.

We believe the current situation is even more of a close call than in the past considering the absence of clear signals from the Bank via speeches and advance reports to lay the groundwork ahead of the policy meeting. From an economist perspective, however, we think the case for a rate hike at the July MPM is compelling.

Reason 1: Recent data on wages and inflation expectations point to a rise in trend inflation, on track with the BoJ's view.

Data showing spread of wage hikes and stronger inflation expectations have increased since the previous June MPM. At the press conference after the June policy meeting, BoJ Governor Ueda commented that “while data have generally adhered to the Bank’s outlook up to now, the Bank wants to confirm some more as to whether the basis is sufficient.” Data disclosed since June has been mostly on track with the BoJ outlook, in our view.

In May’s Monthly Labor Survey released on 8 July, for example, scheduled cash earnings on a common-sample basis posted a robust 2.7% YoY increase, which confirmed broad wage hikes including at small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). We think the strong result has reduced the necessity for the BoJ to wait for the next MLS data for June scheduled for release in August. Furthermore, the BoJ’s “Opinion Survey on the General Public's Views and Behavior (June 2024 Survey)” disclosed on 12 July reported a further rise in household inflation expectations. The Bank’s Tankan Survey issued on 1 July already indicated a further rise in inflation expectations among firms.

## These data strongly suggest that Japan's underlying inflation (trend inflation)

estimated by the BoJ using an economic model is rising (see the next section for a detailed review). Additionally, June’s nationwide CPI results disclosed on 19 July showed resilience in services and household durable foods. Our estimate showed expansion of the positive margin in the weighted median, an important indicator for the Bank in assessing the underlying price trend, in a second straight month to roughly +1.5% YoY. We see risk of re-acceleration of the underlying inflation trend.

Signs of broadening of the wage-increase trend have accumulated too. For example, the BoJ’s branch managers’ meeting and Sakura Report and supplementary document based on meeting content depicted advances in wages hikes among SMEs against a backdrop of structural labor shortages. According to the summer bonus survey’s final compilation for

2024 disclosed by Nikkei on 18 July, the average bonus payment in all industries set a new all-time high. In particular, the average bonus growth among SMEs was strong at +7.84% YoY, more than twice the total average.

Gov. Ueda stated in an interview with Asahi Newspaper in April that increase in the level of confidence would be a reason for moving the policy rate. He also explained that the level of confidence or the probability of attaining the price stability target was 75% at the time of ending the NIRP and an increase to 80-85% would provide a basis for changing the policy rate again. We believe the increase in data suggesting upturn in the underlying inflation trend shown above is enough to lift the Bank’s level of confidence by about 5ppt from 75% to 80%.

## Reason 2: With the current rate level well below the neutral rate, accelerated economic momentum is not a requirement for a policy rate hike.

If the policy rate is close to the neutral rate level, acceleration of economic momentum would likely to be a requirement for a further rate hike. As Gov. Ueda repeatedly explained in his press conference, however, the current policy rate level is substantially lower than the neutral rate. In this environment, adjustment of the policy rate could occur anytime absent significant erosion of economic momentum. In particular, considering a decline in the ex-ante real interest rate amid upturn in inflation expectations, we think it is reasonable to slightly adjust the policy rate for the purpose of avoiding excessive easing.

Data since June have not shown weakening of economic momentum, and Jan-Mar seems to have been the bottom of a mini-cycle, pointing to a recovery　ahead. There are also signs of recovery in sentiment (soft) data, such Consumer Confidence Index and Economy Watchers Survey outlook DI, against a backdrop of wage gains. Furthermore, real imports switched to a recovery in June as a reflection of healthier domestic demand. Despite the unusual downward revision of the Jan-Mar real GDP growth rate in July, the Bank should recognize the large temporary impact of disruptions in automobile production as the main cause of negative growth in the quarter, and we do not expect the backward-looking data to affect BoJ policy. In GDP retroactive revisions, the Cabinet Office uniformly revised up past private capex and put the latest level at close to the all-time high. Our retail industry analyst’s June and July comments seem to indicate solid spending too.

## Reason 3: The fixed-income market already factors in sizable reductions in JGB purchases, and the risk of a steep rise in long-term yields is limited.

JGB market participants have already largely factored in sizable reductions of JGB purchases since the advance notification at the June MPM. We hence do not see much of a hurdle to joint action on reducing JGB purchases and lifting the policy rate. Gov. Ueda commented at his press conference in June that simultaneous reduction of JGB purchases and a rate hike are certainly possible depending on the data.

We expect the pace of JGB purchase reductions to follow the consensus in the bond market as a way of avoiding large fluctuations in the long-term interest rate. According to the Quick Survey of parties related to the bond market, the simple-average projection of purchase value in two years was roughly ¥3trn. While we had previously anticipated cutbacks to about ¥2trn a month in two years, we now expect reduction from the current ¥6trn a month to about ¥3trn a month over a period of two years with the aim of avoiding risk of a sharp jump in long-term yields.

From an economist perspective, we think it would be best to reduce the amount gradually in fixed monthly increments to minimize volatility and circumvent market speculation about a connection to monetary policy. We also believe it is possible to minimize impact on long-term yields by reducing JGB purchases in sectors with tight supply-demand conditions.

## Reason 4: Possible policy coordination with the government on FX

We think there is a possibility of the BoJ engaging in coordinated policy action with the government to prevent the yen from weakening further, although it would be a behindthe-scene factor, not an official reason for a rate hike. The government is concerned that higher import prices as a result of a weaker yen is hurting consumption spending of households. If an administration that puts emphasis on domestic manufacturing emerges in the US, it might raise concern about excessive yen depreciation and threaten tariff hikes against Japan. From this angle, the Japanese government might have an incentive to avoid further yen depreciation ahead of the US presidential election.

We think the departure of Masato Kanda, who serves as Vice Minister of Finance for International Affairs in charge of Japan's FX policy, on 31 July is important as well. If yen depreciation persists through month-end, this is likely to solidify an assessment of the Ministry of Finance’s currency intervention in April and May as a “failed effort.” We think it might be possible that the likely sudden FX intervention in July was not a one-off shot but a part of coordinated actions with the BoJ raising the policy rate later this month as the second arrow.

Note that we also think PM Fumio Kishida's comment at Keidanren (Japan Business Federation) on July 19 is important. He said that "We are also working closely with the Bank of Japan, sharing a common overall economic outlook. The normalization of monetary policy will support the transition of the economic stage, and the transition of the economic stage will encourage further neutralization of monetary policy. We will steadily advance economic management in line with this direction".

While the lack of BoJ's communications aimed at laying the groundwork ahead of the July MPM is a headwind for our July rate-hike outlook, we think this time could be different in the context described above. In other words, providing information to lay the groundwork is likely to result in the market largely discounting the rate hike ahead of the July MPM and thereby increasing risk of the yen weakening on exhaustion of catalysts after the policy meeting.

## Reason 5: Political schedule risk

We think the BoJ wants to avoid having the policy rate hike become politicized as much as possible. Secretary-General Toshimitsu Motegi of the ruling Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) has stated that the party will hold its fall presidential election on a day during 20- 29 September. Some media reports cite 20 September as the leading candidate date. However, this is the second day of the Bank’s September policy meeting.

Furthermore, if the LDP should elect a new president in the party presidential election, there would be risk of the new LDP leader/prime minister dissolving the Lower House and holding a generation election in October during the initial honeymoon period of high public support and prior to the US presidential election on 3 November. BoJ officials might see a certain amount of risk of this type of political schedule interfering with the October MPM too.

## Where we could be wrong

The Nikkei reported on July 22 that there are some voices within the government that if the BoJ tries to raise the policy rate, they may even exercise their right to request a postponement of the decision. If some quarters in the government are strongly concerned about the potential risk of a rate hike hurting private consumption, the BoJ may not be able to raise the policy rate at the July MPM.

We think there is risk that the absence of signals laying the groundwork from the Bank simply might reflect reinforcement of a wait-and-see stance by Gov. Ueda. Despite our view that data up to now sufficiently warrant a rate hike in July, there is a possibility of many policy board members adopting a conservative approach and wanting to wait for accumulation of further data, such as confirmation of positive QoQ growth in personal consumption in the Apr-Jun GDP (TBA: Aug 15).

If the Bank does not rate the policy rate in July, we believe the probability of a rate hike in October is higher than in September　at this moment.

# Our Thoughts on BoJ's Underlying Inflation Measures

Masayuki Inui, Takeshi Yamaguchi

We think the BoJ is conducting policy discussions, with policy analysis reflecting key data release ahead of the July Monetary Policy Meeting (MPM). These key data points suggest the possibility of upturn in the “Underlying Inflation,” which is the Bank’s top focus, going forward. We expect these data to provide a tailwind for a July rate hike.

## The BoJ's Measures of Underlying Inflation (1): Composite Index of Inflation Expectations

The Bank presented multiple measures as new “Measures of Underlying Inflation” in the April Outlook report. It estimates the “Composite Index of Inflation Expectations,” which is one of these indicators, by deriving from the common trend in expectations among households, firms, and experts (e.g., economists/market participants) ( Exhibit 5 ). 　In the latest release of the BoJ Tankan Survey and the BoJ Opinion Survey, survey results indicated that inflation expectations are growing in both the household and corporate sectors (For details, refer to our report: Japan Economics: BoJ Opinion Survey (June): Inflation Expectations Rising (12 Jul 2024)). We think that the BoJ is likely to utilize this updated measure "composite index of inflation expectations" as indication of a further rise in the “underlying inflation” in the policy discussion internally. This is because the sum of weights for households and firms exceeds about 70% in the composite index, which implies that growing inflation expectations of these two economic agents would raise the composite index of inflation expectations.

## The BoJ's Measures of Underlying Inflation (2): Trend Inflation

We have emphasized that the trend inflation estimate is one of the most important indicators in the BoJ’s policy discussion among “measures of underlying inflation measures” because it can allow the Bank to examine changes in the behavior of economic agents in terms of forward-looking aspects more explicitly(for details, refer to our reports: BoJ Watch: BoJ Policy Analysis (Trend Inflation): Our Thoughts on the Policymaker’s View (27 May 2024), BoJ Watch: April BoJ Outlook Report: Our Thoughts on the Policymaker’s View about its Policy Analysis (1 May 2024) ). In fact, when Masa Inui from our Japan Economics team visited investors in NY and London in the last week of June, we heard that the BoJ explained to some investors about the importance of underlying inflation by using its trend inflation estimates. Therefore, it would be quite important to examine the outcome of the Bank’s updated estimate of the trend inflation rate prior to the policy meeting.

The BoJ showed three estimation models for trend inflation. Trend inflation under Model 3 would be particularly important ahead of the July MPM ( Exhibit 11 , Exhibit 12 ). This is because Model 3’s trend inflation estimate can be linked to spring wage negotiation results and may rise further once Shunto outcomes start to be reflected in wage data.

## The BoJ's Measures of Underlying Inflation (3): The weighted median CPI, re-anchoring of inflation expectations

June nationwide CPI implied possible reacceleration of the weighted median　CPI traditionally used as an underlying inflation measures. Rise in weighted median offers important policy implications as it indicates stronger underlying inflation momentum and also advances the re-anchoring of inflation expectations from deflation to inflation near the target level.

We can decompose the re-anchoring process into two stages. When a major cost-push shock occurs, many items see price rises accompanying a large upturn that exceed the median increase as the first stage (i.e., gap between trimmed mean and weighted median expands). In the second stage, impact of exogenous inflation shock starts settling down and the trimmed mean converges toward the weighted median (i.e., the gap shrinks). Once the gap shrinks enough, a re-anchoring process has been effectively advanced.

We estimated the BoJ’s measures of underlying inflation, due for release on July 23 (Tue), reflecting the nationwide CPI results for June. Our estimated weighted median CPI, the most important indicator, was up 1.5% YoY, widening its positive margin for two consecutive months since bottoming at 1.1% in April.( Exhibit 15 , Japan Economics: Nationwide CPI (June 2024): Risk of Reacceleration in Underlying Inflation (19 Jul 2024))

Additionally, amid reacceleration of the weighted median, the gap between the weighted median and the trimmed mean is getting smaller and thereby suggests strengthened reanchoring of inflation expectations ( Exhibit 16 ). We believe this indicates improved sustainability of the stronger “underlying inflation” and is likely to serve as tailwind content for an early rate hike in the Bank's internal policy discussion, in our view.

Meanwhile, the short- to medium-term sector did face a certain amount of repricing pressure in the wake of Minister for Digital Transformation Taro Kono’s July 17 call for a rate hike aimed at countering the JPY’s weakness (see Exhibit 18 ).

Some market participants believe that Kono could be one of the candidate for upcoming Liberal Democratic Party leadership in September, so the aforementioned repricing pressure might have reflected a sense that the central bank could face a stronger push to raise its policy rate.

The BoJ has already signaled its intention to announce a “detailed plan” for tapering back its JGB purchases over the next one to two years at the end-July meeting. The dominant market view is that the BoJ will find it difficult to hike its policy rate at the same time as laying out its quantitative tightening (QT) schedule, which likely goes a long way towards explaining why positioning for a July 31 move has remained so comparatively subdued.

But tapering and a rate hike basically need to be viewed as separate issues. The BoJ has been quite explicit in reframing its monetary policy framework around the uncollateralized overnight call rate while indicating that JGB purchases are to be reduced “to ensure that long-term interest rates would be formed more freely in financial markets”.

Moreover, Governor Kazuo Ueda said in his June post-meeting press conference that a July rate hike for the purpose of adjusting the degree of monetary accommodation will “of course” be considered depending on forthcoming information on the economy and prices.

The Japanese Trade Union Confederation’s final spring wage hike tally and other labor market data have been pointing to smaller businesses joining their larger counterparts in offering very sizable pay increases, while the latest BoJ Tankan survey results show corporate medium- to long-term inflation expectations remaining anchored at around +2% amid increasing bullishness on output prices (see Exhibit 19 , Exhibit 20 ).

Our economists consider such data sufficient to justify a July rate hike. Such a move would come as a surprise to many market participants and might accordingly be conducive to underperformance of the short- to medium-term sector on the curve.

## Impact of JGB purchase tapering

A wide range of views on the pace and extent of BoJ JGB purchase tapering appear to have been expressed at the July 9–10 “Bond Market Group” meetings, but our overall impression is that many investors are now looking for the central bank to roughly halve its monthly purchases (to around JPY3 trillion) over the next two years in quarterly or perhaps half-yearly increments.

Governor Ueda indicated in his June post-meeting press conference that he wants to offer the market a degree of “predictability” so as to avoid a situation whereby the tapering process creates instability in long-term interest rates.

We thus believe the BoJ will likely respect the apparent market consensus when it comes to both the size and the speed of reductions in its JGB purchases. A reduction to JPY3 trillion per month over the space of two years should come as little surprise and have only a limited impact on the interest rate delta. That just leaves the question of how the reductions might end up being spread across the yield curve.

The most natural assumption—and that which is likely being made by market participants —is that the reductions will be biggest in the sub-10y sector where the BoJ’s ownership share is largest (see Exhibit 21 ).

Beta to 10y UST yield (25bp change, bp)

Opinion is more divided when it comes to super-long JGBs, with most at the aforementioned “Bond Market Group” meetings having called for tapering to focus mostly on the short- to medium-term sector. We ourselves envisage something along the lines of offer amounts being reduced by JPY50 billion, JPY100 billion, and JPY100 billion for each operation in the 1y–3y, 3y–5y, and 5y–10y sectors, respectively, if the BoJ opts to cut back its monthly purchases by JPY1 trillion.

More reduction in sub-10y sector should already have been mostly priced in and is accordingly unlikely to have more than a limited impact on ASW spreads through the 10y sector. That said, unchanged offer amounts further out the curve would likely be seen as positive for the super-long sector and might thus cause recent cheapening of long-end JGB ASW to be at least partially reversed.

## Positioning ahead of the BoJ meeting

The BoJ’s rate hike trajectory is liable to be repriced upwards depending on media speculation between now and the end-July meeting as well as what actually ends up being announced on July 31. In particular, the aforementioned comments by Taro Kono—coupled with what appears very likely to have been JPY-supportive intervention from the MoF— are likely to have given many market participants a sense that the central bank will now be working hand in hand with the government to counter the JPY’s slide.

We accordingly see scope for the curve as a whole to face further flattening pressure due to the combination of: (1) a rise in the short-term rates expectation; and (2) a narrowing of the term premium—particularly in the super-long sector—as markets react to the recent weakness in US economic data, resultant declines in UST yields, and also the correction in USD/JPY that has been seen over the past several days.

## JPY FX: This Time Might be Different

Until now we have been arguing that BoJ policy actions should have only a limited FX market impact, citing our analysis that shows the market-implied BoJ rate hike trajectory lacking statistically significant explanatory power for fluctuations in the USD/JPY other than for quite limited periods (see Exhibit 23 , "JPY | What’s Been Driving Movements In USD/JPY?"). However, we do believe that a July rate hike might prove to be an exception.

USD/JPY has finally been facing some correction pressure of late, but remains more than 10 yen above the fair level of around 145 that appears consistent with market expectations for the Fed’s rate cut trajectory (see Exhibit 24 ).

As we argued in "JPY | Strong Demand For Carry?", this “anomaly” appears primarily attributable to the continuing strength of demand for JPY carry trades despite recent signs of weakness in US economic data, with market participants seemingly placing considerable weight on the Fed’s apparent reluctance to cut and also pricing in increased fiscal stimulus under a “triple red” scenario for November’s US elections.

Demand for JPY carry trades does look to remain strong, but may have been shaken to some modest degree by the recent plunge in USD/JPY as Japanese authorities seemingly sought to take advantage of a dip in the greenback after US CPI inflation came in soft for a second straight month on 11 July.

A similarly sharp fall in USD/JPY was also seen the next day, and subsequent analysis of the difference between tanshi money market brokers’ forecasts for the portion of the change in BoJ current account balances attributable to “Treasury funds and others” and the BoJ’s own projections for the same suggests that the MoF may have spent over JPY 5 trillion shoring up the JPY over the course of 11–12 July.

This action appeared to take market participants by surprise (in contrast to previous interventions) as Japan seemingly looked to amplify existing downward pressure on the greenback rather than preventing a sharp rise in USD/JPY.

USD/JPY then fell further this week in apparent reaction to a Bloomberg interview in which former President Donald Trump expressed concern about the weakness of the JPY as well as the Chinese yuan. As our FX strategist colleagues discussed in "USD: Republican Sweep Would Boost USD", the general consensus has been that a Republican sweep scenario in November would boost the US dollar by fueling expectations of inflation being rekindled by a combination of increased fiscal stimulus, a crackdown on undocumented immigrants, and higher tariffs on trading partners.

And there were also factors on the Japan side, with Minister for Digital Transformation Taro Kono suggesting that he and Trump were basically on the same page with regard to concern about the JPY’s excessive weakness. Pressure to unwind JPY carry trades may thus have been exacerbated by hints that Japan and the US could potentially end up working together to arrest the currency’s slide.

Kono went on to call for a BoJ rate hike aimed at supporting the JPY. Kono is considered to be one of the potential candidates contesting the ruling Liberal Democratic Party’s leadership race in September and is thus likely to have the ear of many market participants.

We thus believe that a July rate hike might very well end up being construed as “evidence” of the MoF and the central bank acting in alliance (irrespective of the BoJ’s actual intentions) and accordingly cause markets to start pricing in a steeper rate hike trajectory going forward.

We see ample scope for further unwinding of JPY carry trades amid speculation pertaining to the US presidential race, comments by the MoF and other government officials, and ostensible BoJ support for efforts to shore up the currency, particularly given that markets as yet appear to be pricing in only a roughly 30% probability of Japan’s central bank raising its policy rate on 31 July.

If the BoJ does, in fact, end up hiking at the end of this month, then we would not be at all surprised to see a further USD/JPY drop driven by a further unwinding of carry positions, given that the current USD/JPY level still leaves some distance above its current “fair

value” of roughly 145 (see Exhibit 24 ).

To position for the possible further decline in USD/JPY, we consider long USD/JPY OTM put option with two-week expiry. Given the punitive carry cost to short USD/JPY, we set the expiry relatively short term. That said, two-week expiry covers some major events including the BoJ, FOMC, and next NFP print.

Further unwinding of JPY carry trades would likely lead to a sharp decline in USD/JPY within a short period of time with higher implied volatility. The risk of this trade is either hawkish FOMC or dovish BoJ, as well as strong NFP print. But the downside would be limited within an initial premium.

Strong USD/JPY vs. weak consumption: Will rate hike expectations build ahead of BoJ’s July MPM?

Decisive day is Wednesday: 192 TSE Prime firms will announce their financial results on July 31 (Wednesday), the same day that the BoJ will conclude its monetary policy meeting (MPM) and release its quarterly Outlook Report. Analysts have revised their outlook upward for both domestic- and overseas-oriented firms in a balanced manner. No undue concerns have been raised over earnings as a whole. That said, the most notable falloff in profits has come in the retail and food sectors, reflecting the increasingly cautious spending stance at households. Our analyst Maki Shinozaki, who covers Seven & i Holdings, has pointed out a structural change in consumer behavior since 2H 2023 in the company’s results, noting that consumers who had long tolerated high prices in exchange for high quality are no longer responding to the firm’s appeal for value. We appear a long way from a virtuous cycle in wages and prices that will boost household spending and provide solid support for earnings at domestic-oriented companies.

Still, as the BoJ’s MPM approaches, a correction in the yen’s long slump against the dollar could prompt investors to seek out domestic-demand shares over the short term. As noted, our economists anticipate a rate hike this month. We believe the growing accumulation of positive wage and price data along with the rise in inflation expectations justify a fine-tuning in the effects of the bank’s monetary easing.
