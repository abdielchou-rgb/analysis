# Target price changes for Japan and China

We raise our Topix base case target for end 2024 to 2,800 (our prior bull case and +11% upside vs. spot). Our new MSCI China target is 53 (+2% versus spot), Hang Seng target 16,000 (+1%) and CSI 300 3,500 (+7%).

Our thesis of a new era for Asia / EM equities continues with divergence in performance year to date reflecting shifts in geopolitical, macro and earnings fundamentals. This is driving investor portfolio re-allocation.

For Topix we raise our EPS forecasts moderately for both 2024 and 2025 based on incoming macro data and earnings guidance. This is broadly in-line with a gradually rising trajectory for consensus. However, the major driver of our target upgrade is our view that the market can now sustain a 15.0x forward P/E multiple (vs. 14.0x previously and current consensus of 14.4x) given recent and prospective strong investor flows consequent upon buy-in to the secular bull market thesis for Japan, which we laid out in our Blue Paper thesis last October - Japan Equity Strategy: Japan Secular Bull Market Arrives: Here is How to Play It (15 Oct 2023). We continue to prefer domestic sector exposure in Japan including Banks and Real Estate.

Within the China market, we continue to prefer A shares to offshore China given the sector skew to manufacturing, industrial and clean energy sectors, as well as its historical record of greater reaction to onshore policy-easing, which we expect to incrementally step up during 2024. For MSCI China we now expect 7% EPS growth for 2024 and 9% for 2025 (cumulatively 21% below consensus). We now also assume that China's 3D challenges (Debt, Demographics and Deflation) and multipolar world dynamics - including rising allocation to EM and Asia ex China mandates - keep the valuation multiple at 9.5x and at an approximate 15% discount to MSCI EM where we continue to assume 11.0x.

In response to client enquiries and in light of ongoing changes in investor benchmarking, we also show how our market preferences map to a market allocation on a pan-Asia / EM basis for the top 15 markets by capitalization that we have under coverage.

The major risk to our continued bullish view on Japan equities would be a sudden hard landing in the US / global economy and simultaneous strengthening of the Yen. The major upside scenario for China equities would be a shift in policy towards direct and significant consumer stimulus, which would simultaneously raise growth expectations, improve consumer confidence and directly impact the equity market, which is heavily skewed to domestic sectors.

# Earnings Outlook - continues to favour Japan versus China

Reflation and strong nominal GDP growth in Japan and a relatively weak profile for the Yen are driving best in class estimate revisions trends globally (see Exhibit 10 , Exhibit 14 and Exhibit 15 ). Meanwhile, the opposite trends are causing China to lag, particularly in the offshore market.

Factoring in recent trends top down, the output of our earnings model (which includes Global PMI, Tankan survey, and JPY REER) as well as input from our bottom-up teams, we raise our base case Topix calendar year 2024 EPS estimate from 172 to 173 (versus consensus of 174) and our 2025 EPS estimate from 186 to 188 (versus consensus of 189) for two back-to-back years of EPS growth at around 9-10%.

In contrast, as the deflationary pressure persists at least in the near-interim term, a similar exercise of top down and bottom-up recalibration leads us to cut our MSCI China and Hang Seng EPS forecasts to further below consensus. Our new base case 2025 MSCI China EPS estimate is 5.6 (or +9% yoy) versus 5.8 previously, which is now 21% below consensus at 7.0 (which implies EPS growth of 15% this year and next, an outcome that we view as unlikely).

![](images/caa5d969c258992f9e08fff6162564ab83b8b49a1f41f08f4f19832e8c8d0e1a.jpg)
Source: CEIC, Morgan Stanley Research

For overall EM and AxJ we make no change in overall EPS forecasts at this juncture as downward revisions in China are being offset by positive trends in other markets, particularly India.

For Japan the major downside risk to our more bullish earnings profile would be a hard landing in the US which accounts for around 20% of Japan listed equities revenues directly. All the more so if the Yen simultaneously strengthens. This scenario last unfolded for around 2 months in Q2 2020 at the height of the impact of Covid on the global economy. The major upside would lie in a persistently weak Yen - to 150 or beyond allied with a re-acceleration in global growth in 2H 2024.

For China the major upside risk to our slower earnings growth profile would be a shift in policy towards direct and significant consumer stimulus (of the order of 2% to 3% of GDP) which would simultaneously raise growth expectations, improve consumer confidence and directly impact the equity market which is heavily skewed to domestic sectors and in particular consumer discretionary in the form of E-Commerce and other large cap internet plays. Downside would arise from deepening deflation and our economist's bear case scenario of nominal GDP growth moving to 2% or lower allied with persistent downward pressure on the currency. Whilst monetary policy stimulus has been ongoing for some time - and we note yesterday's further action by the PBOC - we think that at this juncture there needs to be a more holistic approach including fiscal support of the consumer and debt restructuring of the property sector and LGFV debt to work together in support of aggregate demand recovery. See Asia Economics: The Viewpoint: China – What’s holding policymakers back from aggressive easing? (22 Jan 2024) for more details.

# Valuation assumptions - further expansion likely in Japan whilst China likely stays at low levels

We raise our Topix Target forward P/E multiple assumption for year-end 2024 to 15.0x from 14.0x (versus spot on consensus of 14.3x), whilst cutting further our China multiple target assumption to 9.5x (or 15% below our assumption for MSCI EM overall).

The bullish thesis on Japan which we laid out in our Blue Paper revisit last October - see Japan Equity Strategy: Japan Secular Bull Market Arrives: Here is How to Play It (15 Oct 2023) - appears to be gaining traction and is leading to rapid fund inflows during the current re-allocation season. This follows around US\$43 bn of global inflows to Japan equities last year. It is too soon to have solid numbers for the impact of the NISA reforms which launched 1st Jan but we expect these are also supporting the market multiple.

Our new multiple assumption is 0.6x SDs above the 5 year average and as shown in Exhibit 16 would be consistent with how the Japan equity market traded on a number of occasions in the 2013-17 time frame. However, it is well below the 18.0x forward at which Topix traded in early 2021 and is still around 5 P/E points below the multiple for the S&P 500 currently.

The risks to this new assumption on the multiple side lie in a sudden de-rating of global markets consequent upon either a sustained rise in real yields or geopolitical risk event. We also note that our US team do expect the SPX multiple to de-rate towards 17.0x this year. If it were to do so, it may be difficult for Japan equities to sustain a lower than average multiple discount to US equities, albeit that improved fundamentals in Japan can be argued to be consistent with such an outcome.

The reason for adding this lens to our coverage is that increasingly we find a multiplicity of benchmarks being used by clients in a manner consistent with our overall thematic of a new-era for Asia / EM investing.
