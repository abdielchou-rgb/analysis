# Japan Economics Mid-Year Outlook | Japan

# The Year of Normalization

The Japanese economy has been shifting to a normal economy with positive nominal GDP growth. We expect the BoJ to start to normalize policy at a measured pace.

## Key Takeaways

A momentous shift in Japan's nominal GDP growth trend to be confirmed

Private capex to exceed its bubble-era peak in 2024

Income recovery to support gradual recovery in private consumption ahead

Neither a return to deflation nor runaway inflation

BoJ to normalize policy at a measured pace

## The Year of Normalization

## A momentous shift in Japan's nominal GDP growth trend to be confirmed

Japan has been shifting to a normal economy with positive nominal growth after decades of range-bound nominal GDP. We expect Japan’s nominal GDP growth to remain above 3%Y in 2024-25 on the back of sustained domestic inflation. The outlook is supported by sustained wage growth, improved domestic demand　including private capex, and even upside risk to exports.

## Exhibit 13: Era of New Industrial Policy: History of METI's industrial policy

1. Cultivation of designated export industries (1940 to the 1960s): Targeting policy, heavy chemical industry cultivation, infant industry protection, and excess facility disposal

2. Stable growth and international friction (1970-84): Transition to knowledge-intensive industries, energy national security, addressing pollution problems, and response to trade friction

3. Structural reforms (1985-2008): Structural reforms aimed at “small government" and enhanced market functions, including deregulation, liberalization, and privatization, in light of the Maekawa Report and Japan-US structural talks

4. Emergency escape measures (2009-12): Emergency responses addressing external shock from the global economic crisis and Great East Japan Earthquake (eco-car subsidies, corporate financial facilitation for small companies, etc.)

5. Abenomics (2013-20): Three arrows (bold monetary policy, dynamic fiscal policy, and growth strategy that stimulates private investment)

## Income recovery to support gradual recovery in private consumption ahead

Japan’s consumption recovery paused temporarily in 2H23, but service consumption remains relatively solid. The expected rise in electricity charges by the summer should delay the timing of real wage growth turning positive, but the overall recovery trend in consumption will likely remain on track on the back of the outlook of higher wage growth and a one-off income tax cut from June 2024. Inbound consumption by foreign tourists will likely remain solid.

## Neither a return to deflation nor runaway inflation

We expect headline and Japan-style core CPI (ex. fresh food) to approach 3%Y in summer 2024 on the back of a rise in utility prices and yen depreciation before stabilizing slightly below 2%Y in 2H25.

Exhibit 24: Sustainable inflation has come into sight: Japan's various core CPI measures (%YoY)

Japan’s underlying inflation should be supported by the virtuous cycle between wages and service inflation, though it will likely take time for Japan’s long-term inflation expectations to be well anchored at around 2%Y. Compared with our previous forecast, we now expect marginally higher inflation in 2H24 and 2025, reflecting the impact of a weaker yen.

## BoJ to normalize policy at a measured pace

The BoJ will be able to confirm broad wage increases, including at SMEs by the summer. We expect the Bank to implement the first hike to 0.25% in July 2024, followed by an additional hike to 0.50% in January 2025.

## Our base-case BoJ call:

July 2024: A rate hike from 0.1% to 0.25%

Oct. 2024: JGB purchasing pace reduction from Y6trn/month to Y5trn/m (June 2024 is our risk scenario, not our base case)

Jan. 2025: Additional rate hike from 0.25% to 0.50%. Given our inflation forecast, our end-2025 rate forecast is 0.50%.

April 2025: JGB purchasing pace reduction from Y5trn yen/m to Y4trn/m Oct. 2025: JGB purchasing pace reduction from Y4trn/m to Y3trn/m

If inflation and wage growth turn out to be stronger than our forecast, we see risk of the BoJ raising the policy rate again to 0.75% around July 2025, though this is not our base case. We would characterize the measured pace of rate hikes as 'less easing' rather than 'tightening,' as we estimate that Japan's nominal neutral rate is located at around 1% or marginally higher.

As our base case, we expect the BoJ to gradually reduce the pace of gross JGB purchases from October 2024. We expect a very moderate pace of reduction as the BoJ has an incentive to minimize the risk of a sharp increase in long-term yields.

## Yen weakening does not undermine Japan's macro stability

Concerns about Japan's macro stability risk on the back of currency depreciation are unfounded, in our view.

In some emerging economies where the public sector or private firms have foreigncurrency liabilities, currency depreciation would immediately destabilize the economy by damaging their balance sheets.

However, Japan's external positions are strong. Income receipts from foreign assets have increased with a weaker yen, expanding the current account surplus. With a weaker yen, aggregated corporates profits are at a record-high level. The government's fiscal deficit has been shrinking with higher tax revenue.

## Risks and potential catalysts

Upside risks include stronger-than-expected growth in China and the US accelerating Japan’s exports with the yen remaining weak, stronger consumption growth on the back of the wealth effect, and larger gains from friendshoring/reshoring.

Downside risks include a delay in real wage growth turning positive adversely affecting sentiment and political uncertainty. Potential catalysts include households shifting away gradually from cash/deposits with higher inflation expectations and the government’s initiatives for a shift from savings to investment, improving labor market mobility especially among younger workers, a five-year plan for nurturing start-ups, and a potential snap election.
