## Take me higher: 'Higher for longer' narrative taking hold again

We suggested at the start of this year that investors were too quick to fully price the Fed pivot. Fast forward, 'higher for longer' and reflation narratives now top of investors' minds again following hotter NFP and CPI data earlier this month. Alan Detmeister (US Economist) expects another hot CPI print for this month; our latest Nowcast update also suggests upside surprises for CPI and NFP ahead of a key March FOMC meeting. Reminds us a lot of one year ago when inflation breakevens rose sharply post the surprise 500k+ NFP print...

## What's cooking?: Market Cookbook suggests Energy vs. Utilities upside

...thus we use early 2023 (pre-SVB) as a playbook for what 'higher for longer' could mean for relative equity market returns. We update our Market Cookbook framework ahead of a slew of key macro data in early March – and the results are clear (and perhaps unsurprising): strong tilt towards cyclical Value over Defensives. Sector-wise, the model suggests selling upside in Utilities vs. buying upside in Energy, particularly Oil & Gas E&Ps (Figure 1).

## I've got the power: Energy upside for a reflationary environment

Josh Silverstein (US Energy Analyst) has a neutral view on the Energy sector broadly (in line with recent sentiment), but sees positives under the hood. In his view, the space could benefit if we have sustained move above \$80 in WTI. He flags two potential tailwinds: 1) If the Tech rally starts to fade, broadening breadth could favor Energy, and 2) Investors have been buying WTI ahead of the SPR replenishment in March 2024, which could set a theoretical lower bound to US oil prices. He notes concentration risk in integrateds post-key M&A in the broader Energy sector (XLE), and instead sees E&Ps (XOP) more geared towards the pure oil trade.

## Don't get so defensive: Relative parameters favor XOP/XLU call switches

Oil & Gas E&P (XOP) vs. Utilities (XLU) screens as an attractive relative value pair geared to higher real yields. Defensives have underperformed significantly of late, by virtue of their bond proxy nature and coincident rotation into cyclicals amidst fading recession fears. Relative to Utilities, E&Ps are seeing stronger earnings momentum, trade at a \~5pt. valuation multiple discount, and exhibit a smaller volatility premium vs. history (Figures 2-12). Investors may consider XOP/XLU Jun call switches sized for flat premium. We also screen for US single stock candidates most sensitive to rising US breakevens and real yields (Figure 13 + top EU candidates here).
