# India Economic Perspectives Real GDP growth above 8% in Dec quarter, lift FY25 growth forecast to 7%

## India's real GDP growth surprises on an upside in the December quarter

India’s real GDP growth picked-up further to 8.4%YoY growth in the December 2023 quarter compared to 8.1% YoY and 8.2%YoY registered in the previous two quarters. The robust growth in this quarter was a surprise and was well above our (6.7%) and consensus expectation (6.6%YoY). On a seasonally-adjusted (SA) sequential basis, India’s real GDP was up 2%QoQ in the December quarter compared with 1%QoQ in the previous quarter. The details show that while consumption growth remained tepid, the investment growth was robust in the December quarter. Notably, India's growth on gross value added (GVA) basis, decelerated to 6.5%YoY in Dec quarter from 7.7%YoY and 8.2%YoY in the previous two quarter. The difference between GDP at market price and GVA is net indirect taxes which were up 32% in December quarter vs. decline of 3%YoY same time last year and hence explain this upside surprise in headline GDP growth. Overall, the Ministry of Statistics have revised full year FY24 second advance estimate for real GDP at market price to 7.6% (from 7.3% earlier), implying imputed growth of 5.9%YoY in March 2024 quarter. Nominal GDP is estimated to grow 9.1%YoY with GDP deflator of 1.4% in FY24 (CSO second advance estimates). (Details inside)

## We lift our FY25 GDP growth (at market price) forecast to 7%

Our lead indicator, the UBS India’s Composite Economic Indicator (UBS India-CEI) on a seasonally adjusted sequential momentum was up 1.1%MoM in January 2024 compared with 0.1%MoM and -0.3%MoM in the previous two months. Incorporating better than expected real GDP growth in Dec quarter (8.4%YoY vs. our estimate of 6.7%) and signals from our lead indicator suggesting resilient economic activity, we raise our India’s FY25 real GDP growth to 7%YoY (from 6.2%YoY earlier). Going into FY25, we expect consumption growth to marginally recover (with urban demand normalising but premium/affluent segment continuing to do well and rural demand recovering on normal monsoons). The investment recovery will likely become more broad-based. While growth in public capex will likely moderate, we expect residential housing to remain supportive and revival in private corporate capex to gain traction post elections. Lastly, we forecast exports (of goods and services) to improve marginally on improvement in global goods import volume and resilient services exports.

## Will fading fiscal impulse drag growth? Is there scope for RBI to cut rates?

In the the recent union budget for 2024-25, the central government pursued a counter cyclical fiscal policy which led to many investors questioning if this could lead to a potential drag on India's growth. Historically, during a period of economic slack, a 1% point rise in fiscal deficit increases GDP by 43bps cumulatively over four quarters (RBI's estimate). However, in a period of economic expansion (which is the case right now), multiplier values turn negative, signifying the detrimental impact of expansionary fiscal policy on growth. Fiscal consolidation, thus, becomes a necessity to create room for private investment. Overall, we believe India's economic growth seems to have entered a sweet spot with contained current account deficit and inflation being on a decelerating trend. The key question is if there is a need for RBI to cut policy rates at all in FY25 in lieu of global monetary easing. The robust real GDP growth suggest that policy conditions will remain tight in the April policy and RBI will likely shift its policy stance to "neutral" in the June policy. While we maintain our call for a shallow rate cut cycle in FY25 (cumulative 50bp), following the Fed pivot in May (UBS forecast) and as India's real policy rate also starts inching into restrictive territory (amid faster-than-expected disinflation), it seems the MPC may not be in any hurry to change policy settings.

## By industry: Agriculture down; manufacturing and services were supportive

On a real gross value added (GVA) basis, Indian economy grew 6.5%YoY in the December quarter (7.7% in the previous quarter). Looking at the breakdown, agricultural & allied sector contracted 0.8%YoY (vs. 1.6% in the September quarter). This was in line with the first advance estimates of summer crop (kharif) production released by Ministry of Agriculture, which declined 4.6%YoY, due to weather related aberration (read El Niño). The industrial segment growth decelerated but remained strong at 10.1%YoY (from 13.2% in the September quarter) largely led by manufacturing (11.6%YoY vs. 14.4% in the September quarter) and construction (9.5%YoY from 13.5% in the September quarter) segments. The trend in manufacturing growth largely moved in tandem with buoyant operating profit growth of BSE 200 manufacturing companies (up 29%YoY vs. 61% in the September quarter) on reduction in input costs (Figure 8). Growth in mining sector, on the other hand, decelerated to 7.5%YoY (vs. 11.1% in the September quarter). The services sector growth picked up to 7.8%YoY from 5.8% growth registered in the September quarter. This was largely led by pickup in growth of 'financial services, real estate & professional services' (7%YoY vs. 6.2% in the September quarter) and 'trade, hotel, transport & communication' (6.7%YoY vs. 4.5% in the September quarter) segments. ## By expenditure: capex up, consumption down; net exports improved

India’s real GDP (at market prices) grew 8.4%YoY in the December quarter (vs. 8.1% in the previous quarter). Looking at the breakdown, while private consumption growth continued to remain tepid at 3.5%YoY (vs. 2.4% in the previous quarter), government spending declined 3.2%YoY (vs. a strong growth of 13.8% in the previous quarter). The fixed capex growth remained robust at 10.6%YoY (vs. 11.6% in the September quarter) largely supported by double-digit growth in capital expenditure by central government and robust residential housing activity in the December quarter. The order inflow for engineering & construction goods companies (E&C), on the other hand, was largely flat in this quarter (Figure 10) . Net exports contribution was -1.2ppt as imports grew (+8.3%YoY vs. 11.9% in the September quarter) more than exports (+3.4%YoY vs. +5.3% in the September quarter) in the December quarter.
