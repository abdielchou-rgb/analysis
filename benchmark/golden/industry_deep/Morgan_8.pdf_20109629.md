## Developed Markets

## United States:

A high-profile corporate lay-off announcement Thursday evening was a real-time reminder of the non-linear perils in the labor market right before a weak July nonfarm payrolls report on Friday.

Non-farm payrolls only rose by 114k compared to consensus expectations of 175k, with a cumulative 29k in downward revisions to the prior two months. The unemployment rate rose by more than expected to 4.3%, compared to consensus expectations of 4.1%. In addition, average hourly earnings were lower-than-expected and the average workwell fell – a concerning signal for July consumption.

Treasuries discounted this clear slowing in the labor market swiftly as 2y yields fell nearly 30bp at session lows. Short-term interest rate markets assigned a higher probability to a now 50 basis point rate cut at both the September and November FOMC meetings, with over 40bp priced in for each meeting and nearly 125bp of easing this year. The trough fed funds rate for the easing cycle repriced from \~3.50% a week, to 3.00%.

As the Treasury curve continued to bull-steepen, the US dollar was unable to maintain its appeal as a safe haven currency and was the top underperformer in the G10. Instead, JPY – fresh off a hawkish hike from the BoJ earlier this week – and CHF were the top performers. US equities continue to slide, with the economicallysensitive Russell 2000 (-3.52%) once again leading the losses at the index level.

## Japan:

JGBs rallied strongly across the curve on Friday. JGB yields traded lower in the Tokyo morning session following the rally in USTs overnight on Thursday, driven by weak initial claims and ISM manufacturing. Rising concern about a weaker US economy and expectation for a more dovish Fed led to risk-off sentiment in the market, which also may factor into the BoJ’s thinking about its policy rate path. Against this backdrop, demand to square short positions supported the rally, especially in the front-end of the curve. The super-long sector was also supported by lower term premium and the attractive level of yields. The BoJ conducted monthly JGB purchases for tenors under 1y, 1-3y, 3-5y, and 5-10y; the amount of purchases was decreased as announced at the July meeting. Overall, those sectors auctioned around market levels and the rally momentum continued in the Tokyo afternoon session. Super-long sectors performed especially well; 5y JGBs rallied by 7.5bp d/d to 0.575%, 10y JGBs rallied by 7.5bp to 0.955%, and 20y JGBs rallied by 7.0bp d/d to 1.72% on a simple yield basis. The TONA-OIS curve bull-flattened and underperformed JGBs. XCCY basis widened, driven by the front-end as positive carry trades were squared.

Despite the sharp sell-off in global equities and larger Fed rate cut pricing, USD/JPY had traded relatively resiliently \~149.00. At 11:00am JST, Japanese Finance Minister Suzuki commented on the current FX and equity movement, but the impact on the FX market was limited. Prior to the July US non-farm payrolls report, market participants seemingly found it difficult to trade in either direction under the current high volatility environment.

## Europe:

G10 FX price action was dominated by US labour market data, following a downside surprise in July non-farm payrolls. However, the way the data drove returns was in some cases surprising, as only certain safe havens (JPY: +1.7%; CHF: +1.6%) strengthened and despite increasing growth concerns some risk-sensitive currency performance was less intuitive. Instead of weakness in risk currencies, as equity prices and oil prices fell, risk currencies gained as the US dollar was weighed down by lower US yields. SEK (+1.5%) outperformed whereas its Scandinavian peer, NOK (-0.2%), as the weight of the oil sell-off counterbalanced the broad USD weakness. Both EUR (+1.1%) and GBP (+0.5%) gained as yield differentials moved heavily in their favor. Overall, the DXY USD Index (-1.1%) was sharply lower.

## Dollar Bloc:

The balance between USD weakness and risk-off sentiment was present in the Antipodeans as well. AUD (+0.1%) and NZD (+0.1%) gained while CAD closed flat.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies rallied broadly for the second consecutive session even as equities sold off, mainly driven by a squaring of carry trades. CNH retraced losses from Thursday; USD/CNH fell 0.7% to near 7.20, below its 200-day moving average, even as the USD/CNY fixing was at a new recent high of 7.1376 (P: 7.1323). This allowed MYR outperformance to continue, as the currency rallied for the tenth consecutive session. USD/MYR weakness accelerated, 1.6% lower, to an 11-month low of 4.4970. THB also benefited from the strong CNH and higher gold prices, 0.7% stronger, its strongest since March. USD/KRW was 0.3% lower despite the 3.7% sell-off in KOSPI, aided by CNH strength and measured foreign equity outflows (\$731mn). TWD, in contrast, was the main underperformer in the AxJ region, 0.1% weaker, to 32.8 amid \$3.1bn of foreign equity outflows, the second largest daily outflow in the history of the series. INR relatively underperformed amid a weak USD, stable at 83.75.

AxJ rates rallied broadly in the risk sell-off. China rates underperformed on a relative basis, with 1y CNY NDIRS yield unchanged and 5y NDIRS yield down just 1bp. Similarly, yields on the CGB curve fell by just \~1bp. This came amid comments from PBoC MPC member Huang that authorities should focus more on boosting consumption, avoid overly focusing on fiscal health, and set a 2-3% inflation target. In Korea, 1y KRW NDIRS yield fell by a moderate 3bp as July inflation was higher than expected. The 1s5s NDIRS curve bull-flattened 2bp. In Hong Kong, IRS yields fell almost as much as the move in US yields overnight on Thursday, with 1y yield down 14bp and 5y down 9bp. Singapore rates also outperformed, with 1y IRS yield down 10bp; the 1s5s curve bull-steepened 2bp. Most other rates (Thailand, Malaysia, and India) rallied \~4-5bp on the 5y tenor of their swaps curves, as curves bull-flattened. In Indonesia, 10y IndoGB rallied 4bp.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• BoE Chief Economist Pill urged against cutting interests too quickly and claimed the BoE should not be complacent with regards to UK inflation

• Chicago Fed President Goolsbee said markets should not conclude a trend from one data point. When asked what could get the Fed to cut by 50bp in September or have an inter-meeting cut before the September meeting, he said the Fed will get a lot of information between now and the September meeting, and what will determine the size, or if there is a Fed move at all, will be the economic conditions

## Emerging Markets

• PBoC MPC Member Huang said authorities should change their strategy of focusing on investment and neglecting consumption, highlighting cash handouts to households, and improving migrant workers’ access to urban public services would help boost consumption. He called for more policy support to improve businesses and consumers’ expectations, and said an excessive focus on fiscal health could hinder the economy and eventually erode the room for future policy action. Huang also called for greater attention on falling prices, advocating setting a hard CPI inflation target of 2- 3%

• BI Governor Warjiyo said in light of persistently high UST yields, the central bank will exercise caution while reducing interest rates to prevent capital outflows. He noted while lower inflation should translate into lower borrowing costs, BI will need to ensure global risks are manageable before cutting rates. He reiterated BI’s focus is on maintaining IDR stability, with still-high UST yields, risk of capital flow reversal and unsynchronized global monetary policy the primary risks for IDR

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.

Central bank decision: The BanRep cut rates by 50bp, in line with consensus expectations. The decision was not unanimous, with five members favoring a 50bp cut and two favoring 75bp. Market reading (EM strategy): From a market standpoint, the July BanRep decision and statement were broadly a non-event.

## Argentina Economics and Strategy: Mind the Sequencing; Adjustment Still on Track 30 July 2024

Policy makers seem to be preparing the ground for a future FX unification. Although FX reserve accumulation will likely stall in coming months, conditions for an FX unification might improve by 4Q24 as tax incentives drive inflows. With valuations cheap, we suggest staying long bonds.

"Gaining new insights through revising old material". We review what happened in 2019 and believe that the market could be too bearish on CNY in early 2025, but not now.

## LatAm Macro Strategy: Not Carried Away 29 July 2024

We explore the drivers of the recent sell-off in LatAm currencies and we maintain bearish views on both MXN and BRL. While a tricky call, in an environment where our optionsbased positioning indicator now points to more neutral JPY positioning (from short), we think that a more dovish FOMC that prompts the pricing of additional cuts could be a more important driver for LatAm FX this week, in addition to local central banks.

## Global EM Strategist: Hedging Requests 29 July 2024

Weaker data outside the US support our lower yields and long USD bias. Rate cuts are around the corner and could lift spirits, but as growth risks combine with the US election, investors are likely to request hedges in case of more volatility.
