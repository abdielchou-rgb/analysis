# Latest in AI: Meta Llama 3, Mistral \$5bn valuation and UK regulation

Meta’s release of Llama 3, its next generation large language model (LLM), is not just an aggressive strategic move, but also a significant strike for open-source AI in the existential clash with proprietary models from OpenAI and Google.

In this second edition of our “Latest in AI” newsletter, we explain why this matters. We also discuss the implications of Mistral’s potential \$5bn valuation just a year after it was founded, and the significance of the UK’s changing attitude towards AI regulation.

While open-source models typically offer a smaller, lighter, customisable alternative to their proprietary cousins, Llama 3 is set to go head-to-head with them, offering not just 8bn and 70bn parameter versions but, soon, a 400bn colossus. This matters in a world where parameters – the variables that an AI model learns during training – are a proxy for capabilities, and where big is usually better.

## Technology

Meta releases Llama 3, its biggest open-source model by far, and says it will integrate it into the virtual assistant Meta AI

## What happened?

n Meta has upgraded Llama 3 to a level that makes the open-source generative AI model competitive to proprietary, closed-source rivals. The next generation AI model is just a taster, with a much bigger version to come that will likely be multilingual and multimodal. We explain what multimodal means in our first newsletter edition here.

n The LLM supports Meta AI, a virtual assistant that is embedded in feed, chats and search on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp. Meta AI is also a standalone website and powers the tech behind the Ray Ban sunglasses with a camera. It is currently only available in a small number of countries including the US, South Africa and Singapore.

n Llama 3 will soon be available to developers on all major platforms. As an open-source model, it can be downloaded and adapted by anyone. The original Llama was released a year ago and quickly became the leading foundation for customised models.

## This matters because:

n Meta is leveraging the 4 billion monthly active users<sup>1</sup> of its family of apps to supercharge adoption of its generative AI model. Access to distribution, data and computing power at scale are rapidly becoming the prerequisite for providers of AI.

n The quickest way to introduce generative AI to a wider audience is to have it embedded in existing products. Meta is doing just that, while also offering suggestions for what you can ask the AI as soon as you open a chat window.

n The release strengthens the capacity of open-source models to compete with proprietary models. Indeed, the upcoming much bigger Llama 3 version with 400bn parameters is expected to be three times as big as GPT-3.5, the most advanced free model from OpenAI. There is mounting evidence that open-source models can be as good in some ways as proprietary LLMs if they are finetuned with specific, reliable data.

## Our take:

n Meta is now putting itself in direct competition with the other Big Tech companies that have been leading on generative AI developments, notably Microsoft and Google.

n While even the 400bn parameter Llama 3 will likely be smaller than the state-of-the-art leading AI models (OpenAI's GPT-4, Google's Gemini 1.5 Pro and Anthropic’s Claude 3, none of whose size has been disclosed), it still holds its own in benchmark tests, Meta says.

n Indeed, Meta says the Llama 3 version with 70bn parameters scores slightly better on knowledge and code generation benchmarks than Google’s recently released flagship model Gemini 1.5 Pro.<sup>2</sup> Meta says Llama 3 is considerably more efficient than earlier models, in part through having been trained on around 50 times as much data as GPT-3.5.

1 Llama 3 has a number of unique features that make it sticky, including generating personalised animations and high-resolution images as you type.

n Another unique element is that it retrieves information from both Bing and Google, and decides which one of the two search engines to use depending on the question. This gives it extra flexibility over the other tied models.

n While these features may not be groundbreaking, they are accessible and available on existing applications in your pocket. Also, anyone with the desire and a modicum of skill can build products using the model for free. Compare that with the limitations of generating images with OpenAI’s most advanced conversational program ChatGPT, which is behind a paywall.

n Meta AI is not yet available in Europe, where it would have to jump over more regulatory hurdles. These hurdles will only increase with the imminent introduction of the EU AI Act, the world’s first comprehensive regulatory framework. This may give ammunition to open-source advocates who argue that tougher regulation imposed too early can stifle innovation and growth.

## Companies

## European AI challenger Mistral said to be seeking \$5bn valuation

What happened? One-year-old Mistral, France's great hope for a European AI champion, is reported to be in discussions to raise more funding that would lift its valuation from \$2bn to \$5bn.

n The round may amount to “several hundred million dollars”, according to The Information. That could propel the company, which has just recently begun to generate revenue, to overtake Germany’s Aleph Alpha as the most funded generative AI company in Europe. It would also set Mistral further apart from other start-ups on the continent, where funding has been much weaker than in the US.<sup>3</sup>

n The identities of potential investors have not been disclosed. Previous investors have included US venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz and Microsoft.

This matters because Mistral has only recently gained unicorn status and raised an undisclosed sum from Databricks only a month ago. The staggering speed at which the start-up is growing underlines the significant appetite from investors to find winners while the market is still fluid - particularly in more heavily regulated Europe where government backing may be critical.

## Our take:

n If Big Tech companies are among the investors in the new funding round, this may attract antitrust attention. Consider that when Microsoft invested EUR 5m in Mistral in February, the European Commission (EC) said it would look into the partnership to see if it constituted a concentration of power.

n Even if there are new antitrust investigations against new investors, it is possible they will not lead to anything substantial. Last week, the EC said it will not, in the end, open a formal investigation into Microsoft’s \$13bn investment in OpenAI because the investment did not constitute a full takeover. Separately, Microsoft likely avoided additional regulatory scrutiny through its ‘acqui-hire’ of Inflection’s Mustafa Suleyman.

1 Mistral's reported funding round is more evidence that open-source models are becoming serious competitors to proprietary models. ## Regulation

## UK considers introducing new laws on AI

What happened? The UK is reported to be considering regulating AI with new laws that may oversee the technology’s growth and influence. There may be new rules around how the algorithms of LLMs are shared, as well as rules that could make safety checks a legal matter. They would build on the agreement between the US and the UK for their new AI safety institutes to work together to develop safety tests for the most advanced AI models.

This matters because it would mean a change in approach by the UK government as to how it regulates AI. Previously, it appeared to prefer a more laissez-faire approach, applying existing regulatory frameworks in an attempt to balance innovation and safety. It was only a few months ago that Prime Minister Rishi Sunak warned that the country’s approach should not look “alarmist”. Now, lawmakers and regulators want to be seen to be considering a more active approach amid increasing concerns around the risks of AI.

## Our take:

n This shows that the voices within the industry which are more concerned about AI risks have resonated with lawmakers. Meanwhile, Paul Christiano, a veteran of OpenAI’s safety team who is seen to be cautious on AI, has been appointed to head the US AI Safety Institute.

n The new UK laws, if agreed and implemented, would bring it closer to its EU neighbours, where regulation on AI is set to be stricter.

n The particular concern the UK may share with other European countries is around the market dominance of large technology companies. This implies that the antitrust movement against Big Tech that has gathered strength in the US and continental Europe may extend to other countries.

n Regulation will play an important role in the speed of AI growth in one region, or country, versus another. As the EU AI Act will apply extraterritorially, no company with a European nexus will be insulated from the stricter rules.

n OpenAI's Sam Altman, Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis, and other key AI leaders attended the AI Safety Summit in the UK last November. Their attendance reinforces how tech companies want to be seen to be ready to collaborate with legislators and regulators.
