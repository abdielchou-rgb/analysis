## March 4

Fed's Bostic repeats caution on upside risks; USTs bear-flatten;
Periphery outperforms, 10y BTP-Bund spread at new local lows;
GBP gains before UK Fiscal Statement; MYR strengthens on verbal directive; CNH ends flat despite renewed uncertainty;
DXY at 103.84 (-0.0%); US 10y at 4.213% (+3.3bp).

• Atlanta Fed President Bostic reiterates the tone of "expectant optimism" from business decision-makers holds "potential to unleash a burst of new demand" and create upward pressure on prices.

US Treasuries extended to the cheapest levels of the session alongside release of prepared remarks from Atlanta Fed President Bostic. He reiterated he needs “more progress” that inflation is on a sustainable path to averaging 2% over time. Only then will he gain confidence that "the time is right to begin lowering the federal funds rate to dial back restrictive monetary policy.”

Bostic discussed the delicate balance between keeping interest rates elevated for too long and unwinding policy restriction too soon. And once again, Bostic cautioned around “upside risks” to his outlook, as the tone of "expectant optimism" from business decision-makers holds "potential to unleash a burst of new demand" and create upward pressure on prices.

Overall, the Treasury curve bear-flattened and the front-end led the sell-off (2y: 4.60%, +7.1bp d/d); late whites/early reds sold off \~8bp. STIR markets now priced in \~73% chance of the first 25bp rate cut by the June meeting and a total of 83bp of rate cuts by December.

Although lacking traditional economic data releases, the New York Fed’s Multivariate Core Trend of PCE Inflation increased to 3.04% in January (December: 2.56%). The model measures persistence of inflation for the major sectors of the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) index. Housing accounted for 0.54ppt of the increase in the MCT estimate relative to its pre-pandemic average, while services (ex-housing) accounted for 0.7ppt.

Moves across G10 currencies were muted amid the limited catalysts, with GBP (+0.3%) the main outperformer. Alongside an apprehension in equity markets (outside of the cryptocurrency ecosystem), the risk-sensitive Scandinavian currencies showed modest weakness alongside modest S&P 500 (-0.12%) losses.

Catalysts for the week kick off with the Super Tuesday US primary elections on March 5.

## Japan:

Despite the rally in USTs on Friday, JGBs retraced prior strength after Japan quarterly corporate statistics showed a strong acceleration in capex during 4Q23. This was in contrast with what the preliminary 4Q23 GDP data had suggested. JGBs sold off, led by the long-end of the curve ahead of the 30y JGB auction on Thursday. A weak BoJ purchase result in the 10y+ sector seemingly accelerated long-end underperformance; 30y JGBs sold off by 2.5bp d/d to 1.765%. The belly of the curve outperformed and retraced last week’s underperformance despite the 10y JGB auction on Tuesday. We expect the 10y auction will be weak, given lack of appetite among domestic market participants to buy this sector before BoJ normalization.

TONA OIS twist-steepened alongside JGBs but continued to outperform cash JGBs.
The belly underperformed on the XCCY basis curve.

It was a quiet start in FX markets ahead of an eventful week. During Tokyo trading hours, USD/JPY traded range-bound \~150.00, while other CCY/JPY pairs also traded sideways. A media report suggested the government in Japan had mulled calling an official end to deflation over the weekend, but the response in markets appeared muted. As UST yields ground higher from the London open, USD/JPY ground higher toward 150.50. Other CCY/JPY pairs also traded higher, mainly driven by European currencies. EUR/JPY broke above 163.00 and ground higher toward 163.50.

## Europe:

GBP outperformed amid a sell-off in front-end UK gilts, ahead of the UK Fiscal Statement presentation on Wednesday. The Scandinavian currencies underperformed, weaker by \~0.3% against USD and by \~0.5% against EUR.

10y Bunds closed the London session little changed (-2.2bp d/d) at 2.39%, amid no economic data releases in Europe nor the US. The 10y sector outperformed in swaps (against 6m) as the 5s10s30s butterfly richened 2bp and 10s30s bull-steepened by 1bp; the 2s10s flattened by 2.5bp. BTPs and the periphery performed well, and the 10y BTP-Bund spread tightened over 5bp to a new multi-year low. Core ASW continued to cheapen, led by Schatz (-2bp), while 10y OAT ASW richened by 0.5bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

G10 currencies remained range-bound and the DXY USD Index hovered \~103.80 ahead of the release of several US labor market indicators later this week. The risksensitive dollar bloc currencies moved lower amid relatively few macro data releases.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies gained alongside a rally in equity markets, although moves remained modest. USD/CNH remained anchored at \~7.21, even amid renewed concern about property developers and as China skipped the Premier’s National People’s Congress press briefing for the first time since 1993. MYR outperformed in the region, and extended its recent rally by 0.5% to 4.72, after the Financial Markets Committee on Friday urged state-linked firms to convert foreign income into local currency. KRW and TWD gained 0.3% amid strong foreign equity inflows of US \$743mn and US\$1.2bn, respectively. IDR was the outlier, 0.3% weaker to \~15,740 amid the continued sell-off in INDOGBs (10y yield +2bp).

Most AxJ rates rallied with a bull-flattening bias. Indonesia and India were the exceptions as rates sold off. The INR NDOIS curve shifted 1bp higher while 10y G-sec yields fell by 1bp. Hong Kong and Singapore outperformed following the UST move from Friday. The HKD swaps curve shifted 6bp lower from 2y sector out, while the SGD swaps curve fell 2-3bp. In China, the rally in 30y CGBs resumed, as yields fell by 2bp to 2.47%, and partially retraced losses from Friday. The 1s5s CNY NDIRS curve bull-flattened by 2bp. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-flattened by 2bp.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies strengthened on the margin during a quiet session. MXN (+0.3%), COP (+0.1%), and BRL (+0.2%) ended stronger while CLP (-1.0%) weakened substantially, in a session with few local drivers throughout the region. LatAm rates ended the session higher, largely driven by USTs. TIIEs and IBRs had losses concentrated in the long-end; both 10y tenors ended +4bp. DIs outperformed peers and rallied in the belly (January 2030s: -2bp).

## Developed Markets

• In Japan, the Ministry of Finance’s 4Q23 Corporate Statistics showed capex increased 16.4% y/y, or 10.4% q/q, on a nominal basis. This is in stark contrast with the 4Q23 GDP data. It is rare for capex in the corporate statistics to diverge to this extent from the preliminary GDP data. For capex estimates in the first preliminary GDP release, the Cabinet Office uses supply-side data, such as shipment statistics. The revised 4Q23 GDP estimates, released on March 11, will include demand-side capex data taken from the corporate statistics

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
