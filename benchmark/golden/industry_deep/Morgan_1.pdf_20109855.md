# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## August 14

Cooling CPI supports imminent Fed cuts; USTs sell off on uptick in OER but pare losses; gilts outperform due to low UK services inflation; RBNZ cuts rates and offers dovish guidance; CGBs rally; USD/ZAR falls; DXY at 102.61 (+0.0%); US 10y at 3.835% (- 0.8bp).

• In-line US inflation data with Headline CPI slowing to 2.9% (P: 3.0%) and Core CPI cooling to 3.2% (P: 3.3%) continue to support the belief that the Fed will soon begin cutting rates.; equities rally on the back of reinforced confidence for a September rate cut (S&P 500: +0.4%).

• USTs sell off following an uptick in US rent, OER, and core services ex-shelter inflation, but they partially pare losses as market participants await further clarity on the US growth outlook with retail sales data ahead.

• Gilts outperform relative to USTs and EGBs after a downside surprise to UK inflation driven by weak services; market pricing implies \~2 BoE cuts through the end of the year.

• After market pricing implied \~60% probability of a cut ahead of the meeting, the RBNZ delivers a 25bp cut alongside dovish guidance from Governor Orr that the RBNZ is now “confident” about inflation returning to target; NZD weakens 1.3% against the USD

• Despite a resolute warning from the PBoC around disruptions to the bond market, CGBs rally amid reports that market participants in China are paying a premium to buy CGBs from parties other than the big state banks.

• USD/ZAR falls -0.3% amid improved risk sentiment after strong June Retail Sales data in South Africa support the narrative of stronger recovery in the economy.

Market pricing readjusted near-term easing expectations marginally higher, with \~50bp of cuts being priced for the remainder of the year. In the US, CPI was fairly in line with expectations and continued to show a disinflation trend. Core CPI rose 0.17% m/m (MSe: 0.19%; C: 0.2%; P: 0.1%) driven by a continued decline in core goods. However, Powell’s closely watched core services ex-shelter measure rebounded to 0.21% m/m after two consecutive months of disinflation. Additionally, rent and OER prices accelerated in contrast to the recent trends of slow declines. The Cleveland Fed trimmed mean edged up to 20bp (P: 16bp). Our economists forecast core PCE rose 0.18% m/m in July, which would raise the y/y rate from 2.6% to 2.7%, but still continue to show sustained disinflation, adding to the Fed’s confidence that inflation is moving toward target.

USTs had initially rallied ahead of US CPI following the move lower in gilts alongside the downside surprise in UK inflation. Following the release of US CPI, front-end USTs sold off, seemingly in response to the uptick in rent inflation and Powell’s core services ex-shelter measure. However, the sell-off faded as market participants further digested the data. Overall, the in-line release does not push back against the imminent Fed rate cuts that are currently priced, and continues to add evidence to the Fed that disinflation is continuing and moving toward the 2% target.

In STIR, market participants marginally lowered the probability of a 50bp Fed cut in September, with \~31bp of easing priced. USD struggled to find its footing following the data release and ended the day relatively flat.

In the second part of the New York Fed’s analysis of the meaning of “ample reserves,” the authors propose a suite of complementary indicators of reserve ampleness that can help policymakers “ensure that reserves remain ample as the Fed shrinks its balance sheet.” The indicators should increase as aggregate reserves transition from being abundant to scarce. The indicators include late interbank payments, banks’ intraday overdrafts, domestic borrowing in the fed funds market, and upward pressure in repo rates. The real-time data provided seemingly indicated that currently only late payments are increasing.

The in-line US CPI data, which showed the continued disinflation trend, added more confidence to the Fed that would allow them to begin cutting rates. Equities rallied on the back of the reinforced belief of more imminent rate cuts, and the S&P 500 gained 0.4%. The gains were led by financials and tech shares.

According to the weekly EIA Crude Oil Inventory Report, US crude oil inventories increased by 1.4 million barrels in the week ending August 9. WTI crude oil futures unwound some gains and fell -1.8%.

## Japan:

Following the bull-steepening trend in USTs on Tuesday, JGBs began to rally led by the belly of the curve. However, gradually there were some concession efforts ahead of 5y JGB auction and as a result, the JGB curve twist-flattened during the Tokyo morning.

The 5y JGB auction was a bit weaker than market participants had expected, but it was digested smoothly. The JGB curve began to bull-flatten into Tokyo close; 5y JGB rallied 1.5bp to 0.420%, 10y JGB rallied 3.5bp to 0.805%, and 20y JGB rallied 4.5bp to 1.635% on a simple yield basis. There also seemed to be a demand to square positions ahead of the US CPI data release.

TONA-OIS curve bull-flattened and outperformed JGBs except for the 20y sector. The JSCC/LCH clearing spread tightened particularly in the super-long sectors, suggesting decent receiving pressure from the overseas community in the LCH space. XCCY basis widened especially on the belly of the curve. On Thursday, there will be BoJ’s JGB purchase operations in the 1-3y, 5-10y, 10-25y, and 25y+ sectors.

Initially, USD/JPY moved higher from 146.80 to 147.20, along with a rise in the domestic stock market. However, during the Tokyo morning session, USD/JPY sharply fell toward 146.08 amid headlines that Prime Minister Kishida would not run for the next term as the leader of Japan’s ruling party. Although higher volatility may lead to further unwinds of carry-focused trades, USD/JPY gradually bounced back toward 147.50 into the London morning alongside steady performance in the TOPIX. The risk-sensitive currencies such as SEK, NOK, and EUR broadly outperformed into the London morning, while NZD underperformed on the back of RBNZ’s rate cut. From the London morning, EUR/USD accelerated its increase, and accordingly USD/ JPY moved lower on the back of the weakening US dollar.

## Europe:

It was another choppy day of price action in European FX. Sterling yielded to the US dollar and fell -0.3% as UK inflation data surprised to the downside, with particular weakness in services which had previously been a pain point for the BoE. Elsewhere, market participants were somewhat unsure of how to digest US CPI, which ultimately resulted in a 0.2% gain in EUR but a -0.1% fall in CHF during the London session. NOK and SEK ended the London session flat, despite SEK posting early gains following the upside surprise in local inflation.

## Dollar Bloc:

The biggest mover in G10 FX was NZD, which receded 1.3% against the USD following an RBNZ rate cut accompanied by dovish remarks. AUD lost -0.5% to the USD despite risk sentiment (as proxied by equities) holding up. The currency was seemingly weighed down by weakness in commodities instead. The rest of the dollar bloc traded choppily in response to the US CPI data. CAD fell -0.1%, and ultimately the DXY USD Index ended the day relatively flat.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies maintained the upside momentum amid a downside surprise in USI PPI and stronger equities. USD/CNY retreated 0.2% back below the 7.15 level, with the gap between USD/CNY spot and fixing (7.1415) narrowing further. USD/ CNH was also down 0.1% to 7.14. CNH was a relative underperformer along with INR and PHP, which held steady even amid a weak USD. USD/THB spiked higher by 0.6% amid reports that Thailand’s Constitutional Court voted to remove Prime Minister Srettha from office, but eventually pared back the gains to finish the Tokyo session lower by 0.2%. IDR led regional gains; USD/IDR fell 1.0% to 15,675 amid foreign inflows into local equities and bonds. USD/KRW also fell 0.9% to a new recent low of 1,354 as equities saw \$349mn of foreign inflows, the largest since July 29. MYR and TWD gained by 0.6% and 0.5%, respectively, alongside gains in local equities.

AxJ rates continued to rally for a second session. In China, CGBs advanced amid media reports that market participants in China are paying a premium to buy mainly 7y and 10y CGBs from parties other than the big state banks. 7y and 10y CGB yields fell 3bp, and 10y CGBs gave back half of the rise in yields since the August 2 low. This came despite the fact that PBoC-backed Financial News published an article which stated China will resolutely crack down on unlawful activities that disrupt order in the bond market; the PBoC’s Shanghai branch reportedly asked some local financial institutions to explain their bond trading and better manage their investments in long-tenor CGBs; and the National Financial Regulatory Administration reportedly urged banks’ wealth management units to strengthen their risk-management mechanisms. CNY NDIRS yields were largely stable ahead of the PBoC’s MLF operation on Thursday. Thailand rates outperformed; 5y NDOIS yields fell 6bp, and the 1s5s curve bull-flattened 1bp amid heightened political uncertainty. In India, 10y G-sec yields fell 2bp, and 5y INR NDOIS yields fell 5bp and the curve bull-flattened even as July WPI came in higher than expected.

## CEEMEA:

The benign US CPI data release had limited impact on local rates in CEEMEA. In Poland, rates sold off and 2y yields rose 4bp after a stronger-than-expected 2Q24 preliminary GDP data, with the details of the release set to be published on August 29. It seems to us that consumption growth was potentially the main driver given the current lackluster external demand. In South Africa, the curve bull-flattened with the 2s10s curve 4bp lower following strong retail sales data. In the Czech Republic and Hungary, rates rallied across the curve, with 10y yields lower 5bp and 8bp, respectively.

In CEEMEA FX, EUR/CZK was lower by -0.2%, EUR/HUF was up 0.2%, and EUR/PLN was flat. The risk sentiment in South Africa continued to improve, with USD/ZAR down -0.3%.

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
