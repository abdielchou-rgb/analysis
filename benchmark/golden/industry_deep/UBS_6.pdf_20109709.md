# Global Strategy

## Are these the late stages of EM underperformance?

## Path for strong EM returns still looks narrow over the next 1-2m

We have held a counter-consensus defensive view on EM, in general, this year e.g. "Will a looser Fed make EM's year?" and "Be careful what you wish for". The historical EM gains associated with Fed easing had already played out in 2023, and there appeared limited runway for US rates to further decline without being vol accretive and hence challenging to EM. While we expect the US to avoid recession, the orderly correction in EM thus far and weak China growth suggest a narrow path for strong EM returns over the next 1-2m. For instance, while US rates appear to be pricing in high probability of recession (207bp of easing priced over the next 12m; 275bp delivered on average in the first 12m of the last 4 recessions), this degree of caution isn't reflected in EM FX implied vol, credit spreads, or EM valuations/earnings expectations. Risk/reward is better balanced after this selloff but we'd still advise a defensive stance on cyclical EM assets over the next 1-2m.

## Navigating the near term in EM assets

Assuming the US is cooling and not contracting (UBSe: 1.1% Q3 & 1.2% Q4 real GDP growth) we think: 1) Front end EM rates are the clearest EM beneficiaries. Pricing in parts of EM has already run a long way (e.g. Korea, S. Africa), but could still have substantial scope to fall in Latam. We see asymmetric risk/reward in Brazil Jan 26s, and steepeners in Mexico and Chile. 2) In EM FX, while risk/reward has likely improved from 3-6m ago, we see limited value today. CNH, COP, CEE3 and, increasingly, THB look unattractive; against this we prefer TRY and are tactically long TWD. 3) MSCI EM hasn't fallen into deep value territory through this selloff and earnings expectations for 2025 present a high bar for strong near-term gains. China and staples/ utilities are likely outperformers; India, Korea and financials appear tactically more vulnerable. 4) Credit spreads appear priced for a soft landing and are in disagreement with high equity vol - we see value in tactical CDX EM protection and prefer IG over HY.

## The good news: USD smile may be less potent for EM than in prior cycles

While any broadening of US growth risks will likely hurt EM near term, we are cognisant that EMs' limited external imbalances, high real rates and light foreign positioning may make for a shorter transit through the left-tail of the dollar smile than in previous cycles. The US election in Nov has potential to mark an inflection point. Regardless of who wins, our economists see sub-trend '25 and '26 growth, steep (200bp) rate cuts next year, and much wider fiscal deficits medium term. This could present a higher hurdle for the USD and US equity outperformance than the last 3 years. An EM turning point would be more likely to materialise under a Democratic win, in our view; local debt and parts of EM equities should see rising prospects for outperformance in this scenario.

## The key impediment for an EM inflection point? High China tariffs

The main hurdles for an EM comeback likely revolve around China. Through potential import tariffs on China, Trump’s trade policies could materially impact not only Chinese growth but, indirectly, the rest of EM. China has already shown symptoms of exporting an increasingly deflationary impulse into the rest of the world, particularly EM, and we think this pressure would rise under a high tariff scenario. Financial conditions for EM would also likely be tighter under a potential Trump win: a cocktail of high China tariffs, deregulation and corporate tax cuts, for example, would likely slow the decline in US inflation/rates/the dollar relative to the counterfactual. We present a macro screening tool inside to help gauge spillovers to the rest of EM from potential China tariffs.
