## February 13

OER drives above-expectations US January CPI; USTs bearflatten, market-implied cuts recede; DXY USD Index hits a new local high; CHF losses come amidst Swiss core CPI deflation; EGBs sell-off; Riksbank's Jansson talks pauses in cutting cycle; DXY at 104.85 (+0.7%); US 10y at 4.314% (+13.5bp).

• US Core CPI comes in well above expectations, with broad-based strength in almost all core services categories; the main driver was a re-acceleration in OER that contributes 19bp to core.

• USTs sell off aggressively and bear-flatten after the rebound in inflation. Markets reduce the amount of implied Fed rate cuts to 86bp this year, with less than 25bp now priced in by the June meeting.

• USD exhibits broad-based strength against its G10 peers alongside the relentless rise in UST yields; the DXY USD Index rises 0.7% to 104.85, to a fresh local high.

• Pronounced CHF (-1.3%) losses against USD come despite its safe haven status after Switzerland core CPI deflation during January.

• European curves flatten with front-end underperformance, in line with the moves in USTs.

• Riksbank Deputy Governor Jansson believes continuous rate cuts are improbable and an unpredictable krona remains a concern for the central bank as it evaluates how to move forward.

## United States:

Yet the main driver of the core services strength was re-acceleration in owners’ equivalent rent (+0.56% m/m), which contributed 19bp to the core reading. On its own, owners’ equivalent rent (OER) strength was impressive (the highest monthly reading since April), but it was even more interesting, given the divergence with rent inflation (+0.36% m/m).

Rent and OER, components which typically move closely together, now showed a contrasting picture; rent continued its cooling trajectory, while OER re-accelerated. In terms of how this impacts the CPI basket, OER has a \~27% weight with rent an \~8% weight.

Although this month provided a clear counter to complacency about the path of inflation back down to the Fed’s 2% target, whether this relationship returns to normal will be important to monitor. Especially as the BLS New Tenant Repeat Rent Index (NTRR) has shown new tenant rents below the pre-pandemic levels – a negative sign for shelter inflation over coming months.

US Treasuries sold off aggressively, with distinct front-end underperformance as the curve bear-flattened. Rates markets reduced the amount of implied Fed rate cuts to 86bp this year, with less than a cumulative 25bp now priced in by the June meeting. Further out along the STIR curve, a steep sell-off in reds (\~25bp) helped 2y UST yields (4.66%) post the largest single-session rise (+18.4bp) since late March 2023.

USD was the clear outperformer in the G10 and the DXY USD Index rose 0.7%, to 104.85, to a fresh local high. Pronounced CHF (-1.3%) losses against USD came despite its safe haven status after Switzerland core CPI deflation during January. Losses against USD were most pronounced among the risk-sensitive Scandinavian and Antipodean currencies amidst the decisively risk-off sentiment.

Losses in US equities were widespread, as 90% of S&P 500 (-1.37%) closed the session in the red. Losses were most pronounced in lower-quality pockets of the market and among stocks sensitive to higher interest rates from a credit perspective (Russell 2000: -3.96%). In particular, regional banks (KRX: -4.49%) hit new local lows, while REITs (XLRE: -1.83%) closed just above local lows.

Both investment grade and high-yield corporate credit CDX indices widened amid the broader risk-off tone, but spreads only came off just established new 52-week tights.

## Japan:

It was a relatively quiet Tokyo session, after a long weekend in Japan. JGBs initially rallied, led by JGB futures, but the rally gradually lost momentum amidst decent selling pressure across the curve. JGB futures closed the Tokyo session only 1 tick higher, at 146.31. Both front-end and long-end JGBs lagged; the former encountered selling pressure in anticipation of BoJ normalization, while the long end sold off ahead of a heavy supply calendar (liquidity tap auction for 15.5-39y on Friday and a 20y auction on Tuesday). 2y JGBs sold off by 1.5bp d/d, to 0.125%, and 30y JGBs sold off by 0.5bp d/d, to 1.79%. On Wednesday, the first 10y climate transition bond

(JPY800bn) will be issued. Given limited supply, eligibility for the BoJ’s funding supply operation, and use of proceeds to help climate change, we believe it will trade at a premium against JB373. TONA OIS bear-flattened, the front end remained better paid. XCCY basis widened across the curve.

Although UST yields traded sideways throughout Tokyo trading hours, USD/JPY ground higher, driven by broad USD strength; USD/JPY rose above 149.50. European currencies rallied after strong UK wage growth data; EUR/JPY rallied above 161.20 instantaneously. However, the rally was short-lived, except for GBP and EUR/JPY retuned to the 161.00 level.

## Europe:

SEK (-1.9%) underperformed the most against USD, while NOK (-1.8%) followed close behind. The Nordic sell-off was driven by sharp weakness in risk assets. CHF (- 1.3%) depreciated against USD, despite its safe haven status, after Switzerland CPI came in lower-than-expected. This led to discussion of CHF sales by SNB and earlier rate cuts. GBP (-0.4%) ended the London session mildly weaker against USD, supported by strong UK wage data which tilted risks toward a more hawkish BoE. The rest of G10 currencies underperformed against USD to varied degrees, based on their respective sensitivities to risk sentiment and US yields.

European duration rallied in the London morning, amid limited data releases. Price action reversed early in the London afternoon, alongside the US CPI release. Core CPI increased by 0.39% m/m in January (C: 0.3%; P: 0.28%); core goods prices fell, while services accelerated substantially. Core CPI increased to 4.0% (P: 3.3%) on a 3- month annualized basis, back to levels from 1H23. This drove a bear-flattener in the US, which spilled over to Europe. In money markets, late whites/early reds led the sell-off (+10bp), while pricing for the June ECB meeting fell below 20bp and the cumulative amount of cuts priced in 2024 fell to 108bp. In swaps (against 6m), 2s10s and 10s30s flattened by 3bp, while the 5s10s30s butterfly sold off over 1.5bp. Core ASW and peripheral spreads were under pressure after the CPI data; however, this was short-lived. The German ASW complex closed mixed, while OATs and BTPs were little changed against Bunds, Bonos slightly outperformed.

## Dollar Bloc:

US developments dominated G10 FX price action. The DXY USD Index rallied 0.7% after a sharper-than-expected increase in m/m inflation data, which sent yields across the US curve higher.

## Emerging Markets

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended generally weaker, driven by risk-off sentiment following the higher-than-expected US CPI. MXN (-0.7%) and COP (-0.3%) weakened, while CLP (+0.0%) outperformed and was flat for the session despite few local developments. Rates in the region sold off, mostly in line with USTs. TIIEs ended with losses concentrated in the front end (2y: + 20bp), while the belly underperformed on the IBR curve (5y: +19bp). Brazilian markets were closed on Tuesday in observance of a local holiday.

## Developed Markets

• In Japan, the Corporate Goods Price Index was flat m/m in January (C: 0.1%; P: 0.3%) and rose 0.2% y/y (C: 0.1%; P: 0.2%). # G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
