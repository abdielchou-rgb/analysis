# China Economic Perspectives China by the Numbers (April 2024)

## Our guide to Chinese monthly data

What the numbers are, what they mean, and the outlook going forward.

## Q1 GDP growth beat expectation

Q1 GDP growth came in better than expected at 5.3%y/y and 6.6%q/q saar, both stronger than Q4 2023. However, Q1 nominal GDP only recorded a 4.2% growth. The upside growth surprise can be largely attributed to export recovery and better-thanexpected service sector value added growth, while property activities stayed in deep y/y contraction. In addition, the NBS revised down quarterly sequential growth in H1 2023, as well as the base level for FAI and property activities. In March, a higher base weighed on the y/y growth for most indicators, though annual average growth since 2019 suggests stable retail sales momentum and stronger FAI growth, while export recovery is still in place.

## Forecast revisions: stronger real growth, weaker nominal growth and CNY

In light of stronger external demand and better Q1 growth, we upgrade 2024 export growth forecast to 3.5% (from 1.2%), while revise down domestic consumption growth to 5.1% (from 5.5%) considering more persistent drag from the property downturn and still subdued consumer sentiment. We upgrade 2024E real GDP growth from 4.6% to 4.9%. However, we downgrade our nominal GDP growth forecast from 5.1% to 4.9% considering inflation is likely to be weaker than previously envisaged. We also revise end-2024 USDCNY projection from 7 to 7.1-7.2 and see the risk of weakening more in the short term, on account of higher US rates than earlier expected and stronger USD.

## The government may be reluctant to roll out additional policy support

We think the property market may need additional measures to help it stabilize. However, those may only be triggered by worse economic data later and/or a more damaging credit event. The stronger-than-expected Q1 growth may reduce the government’s likelihood of rolling out any additional supportive macro policies. Therefore, we remove our earlier MLF rate cut call but LPR cut is still likely. We also see downside risk to overall fiscal expansion and infrastructure investment later in the year. The PBC may consider restarting trading of CGB in the secondary market as part of its Open Market Operation tools. Meanwhile, the authorities’ focus on supporting corporates and investment at a time of domestic demand weakness may lead to increasing debate about China's excess capacity issue.

## Tracking corporate hiring and household sentiment

The latest UBS Evidence Survey on corporate hiring suggests that hiring momentum is set to moderate in Q2 2024, while wage growth momentum may soften. Manufacturing sector showed the strongest hiring intention in Q2, partly thanks to exports. Echoing the firms' expectations for a modest labour recovery and softer wage growth in Q2, UBS Consumer Sentiment Survey also showed subdued income expectation from households. Macro level data showed stable labour market with lingering pressures, while residents continued to accumulate excess savings in Q1. In the near term, property downturn may continue to weigh on employment and income of related sectors, while recovering export demand could offer partial offset. In H2 2024, we expect property market to stabilize, helping to underpin labour market and prevent household confidence from sliding much further.

## UBS Activity Indicators

 UBS Physical Activity Index (3mma) softened modestly to 4%y/y in March, vs 6.5%y/y in Feb, with industrial activities, construction activities, transportation activities and power generation all decelerated from February, the latter two were partly due to a high base last year.

 UBS Expenditure index moderated in March, primarily dragged by softened net exports. Consumption and investment growth both edged down on 3mma basis, compared with February.

Subdued y/y growth for most economic activities in the first 20 days of April. Our high frequency data tracker shows continued deep y/y contraction of 30-city property sales in the first 24 days of April (-42%y/y), vs -47%y/y in March and -40%y/y in Jan-Feb. Port cargo throughput and container throughput both edged up to 2.6%y/y and 8.5%y/y in the first 24 days despite a higher base, vs 2%y/y and 7%y/y in March, respectively. Subway passenger turnover remained elevated, with growth edging up to 7.7%y/ y in the first 22 days from 7.2%y/y in March, despite a higher base. In sum, y/y growth of most high frequency indicators remained subdued in the first 20 days of April (see UBS China Daily Activity Tracker).

## Business Indicators

March NBS manufacturing PMI improved more-than-expected by 1.7ppt to 50.8, the first >50 reading after five months in contraction, thanks to acceleration in work resumption post CNY and partly due to seasonality. Both demand and supply seem to be improving - new orders and new export orders bounced strongly by 4ppt and 5ppt to 53 and 51.3, respectively, back to expansionary territory. Production index recovered by 2.4ppt to 52.2. Raw material inventory edged up by 0.7ppt to 48.1, finished goods improved by 1ppt to 48.9, and quantity of purchase jumped by 4.7ppt to 52.7. Input price edged up by 0.4ppt to 50.5, but output price edged down by 0.7ppt to 47.4, suggesting deflation headwinds in the industrial space still linger. Employment index edged up by 0.6ppt to 48.1 but still remained below 50, reflecting continued pressures on the labor market. The improvement was across different sizes of firms, most notably for small firms (+3.9ppt to 50.3), the latter recording the first >50 reading since Apr 2023. On the other hand, Caixin manufacturing PMI edged up by 0.2ppt to 51.1 in March, remaining >50 for five consecutive months and recording the highest print since March 2023. Output, new orders and new export index all improved.

NBS non-manufacturing PMI improved further by 1.6ppt to 53 in March. In particular, services PMI improved further by 1.4ppt to 52.4. PMI of postal, telecom, monetary and financial services stayed above 60, and that of wholesale, railway, leasing and business services improved to >53. On the other hand, PMIs of the catering and property sectors dropped below 50, consistent with subdued property activities. Construction PMI rebounded by 2.7ppt to 56.2, reflecting the accelerating pace of construction activities amid warmer weather.

## Property

Property activities stayed in deep y/y decline in March and Q1. March property sales declined by -18%y/y from -21%y/y in Jan-Feb, while new starts contracted by another -25%y/y (albeit smaller than -30%y/y previously thanks to a low base). After seasonal adjustment, we estimate that property sales and new starts volume were both a bit lower than Jan-Feb. Property investment stayed weak at -10%y/y (vs -9%y/y previously). In Q1, overall property activities stayed in deep y/y contraction, with sales volume declining by -19.4%y/y (vs -11.5%y/y in Q4), new starts -27.8% (vs -9.4%y/y in Q4) and investment -9.5%y/y (vs -11.4%y/ y in Q4). Note that the NBS revised down 2023 level base of property sales and investment both by around 6% in Q1, which partly explains the difference between official statistics and much weaker sector-level data.

Expect property sales volume to stabilize at low levels in the coming months; downside risks remain. The current sharp property downturn was driven by a combination of weakening fundamentals, previous policy tightening, and sliding home purchase intention. The government started to ease policies in Q4 2021, and more notably after July 2023 Politburo meeting. Q1 average of seasonally adjusted property sales volume improved slightly from Q4 while new starts volume has been largely stable, though it is too early to call the bottom on both. We expect property activities to stop falling on a seasonally adjusted sequential basis within the next few months, with the help of additional property policy easing and improvement of developer financing. We believe the government needs to ease property related policy further, including injecting more PSL, boosting developer financing and purchasing existing home inventory directly, though these may need to be triggered by worse data and/or credit events. Overall, we expect a smaller fall in property sales/starts/investment, by 5%/10%/5%, respectively, vs -8.5%/-20.4%/-9.6% in 2023.

## Fixed Asset Investment

Overall FAI growth edged up more-than-expected in March and Q1. Infrastructure growth cooled marginally to 8.6%y/y in March but CAGR since 2019 showed an improvement, with Q1 momentum picking up from Q4. While the issuance of special LG bonds was much weaker in Q1 than a year ago, all funds from the RMB 1trn special CGB issued in Q4 2023 have been disbursed. Meanwhile, LGFV financing has been restricted. Manufacturing investment grew 10.3%y/y in March and 10% in Q1 (vs 7.2y/y% in Q4). However, bypassing the uneven base effects, Q1’s 5-year CAGR since 2019 showed a visible deceleration from Q4 2023. Overall FAI growth strengthened to 4.7%y/y in March and 4.5%y/y in Q1 (vs 2.7%y/y in Q4). Note that NBS continued to revise down 2023 FAI level base by 13% in March following its previous cut of 9% in Jan-Feb (overall revision -11% in Q1), which contributed partly to the stronger growth numbers.

Expect both infrastructure and manufacturing growth to slow in 2024. The central government plans to provide more explicit support to infrastructure FAI, with RMB 1trn of special CGB and RMB 3.9trn of special LG bonds in 2024. However, LGFV financing will likely be restricted, especially for new investment projects in 12 high-risk provinces, which means infrastructure investment growth is unlikely to accelerate. We expect full year growth slowing to 5-6% in 2024 (vs 8.2% in 2023) with downside risks. We see more growth potential in new energy investment, hydraulic & water projects, city-cluster transport networks and public utility and facilities. In the manufacturing sector, FAI in the sectors along the property supply chain will likely be held back by weak demand and profits, although FAI related to the green transition should stay robust while FAI along the tech supply chain could improve. Overall, we see manufacturing investment growing by 5-6% in 2024E (6.5% in 2023). See more in our 2024-25 outlook.

## Industrial Production

Industrial production growth slowed in March while improved slightly in Q1. The official seasonally adjusted IP level declined marginally by -0.1%m/m in March, following positive sequential growth in the last 10 months. Despite a much higher base, value added growth of auto managed to grow by 9.4%y/y in March. Upward tech cycle and related export orders supported a resilient VA growth of communication equipment (10.6%y/y, vs 14.6%y/y in Jan-Feb). Meanwhile, production of raw materials remained weak, with cement production declining sharply by -22%y/y (on a high base) and steel production down by -8%y/y. Industry data indicates that post-CNY construction work resumption was weaker than the seasonal pattern in Q1, which likely depressed demand for related construction materials in March. Nonetheless, Q1 average IP growth improved slightly to 6.2%y/y from 6% in Q4.

 China's inventory growth edged up further in Jan-Feb, to 2.4%y/y in nominal terms (vs 2.1%y/y previously) and to 4.7% in real terms (vs 4.4% previously). Industrial inventory to sales ratio (seasonally adjusted, 6mma) has risen notably since 2022, now still well above the historical average, which means it takes a few more months to destock.

## Consumption

Retail sales growth cooled y/y in March and Q1 due to a high base. March retail sales growth softened to 3.1%y/y from 5.5%y/y in Jan-Feb, due to a high base and weaker CPI. We estimate that the seasonally adjusted retail sales level (in real terms) was largely at par with Jan-Feb average, although the official NBS data indicates that nominal retail sales picked up sequentially. The 5-year CAGR of nominal retail sales stayed stable at 4.5% in March. By key category, goods retail sales growth decelerated to 2.7%y/y from 4.6%y/y on a high base, with weak auto sales dragging down headline growth by around 0.4ppt. Sales of property related products saw mixed result, while that of going-out related products (cosmetics, clothing, sports) reported resilient y/y growth despite higher base. Meanwhile, higher base led to decelerating catering sales and services sales. Overall Q1 retail sales growth softened to 4.7%y/y on higher base, while the seasonally adjusted real retail sales improved slightly from Q4.

Consumption recovery should continue at a slightly slower pace in 2024. Post-Covid consumption recovery in 2023 lagged that in other economies due to subdued income growth recovery and weak consumer confidence. Our estimated real retail sales and the official consumer confidence both bottomed around Q323 and have been improving but only slightly since then. Labour market remain stable with lingering pressures, while surveyed firm hiring expectation softened for Q224. Later in the year, the expected stabilization in property activities should prevent household confidence from sliding down further. That will help to support continued post-Covid consumption recovery along with the economy and labor market, though at a slower pace. Household saving rate stayed above pre-Covid 2019 level by 2ppt in Q124, indicating lingering cautious consumer sentiment and further accumulation of excess savings. Overall, we expect annual real consumption (GDP basis) to slow to 5.1% in 2024E from 6.4% in 2023.

## Inflation

CPI softened to 0.1%y/y in March from 0.7%y/y in February. The slowdown is mainly due to fading CNY distortion. Food price declined -3.2% sequentially and -2.7%y/y, on seasonal demand softness and warm weather. Non-food price declined by -0.5%m/m and slowed to 0.7%y/y, with touring and outgoing prices declined by -14.2%m/m after 13%m/m in February, as holiday demand fades. Meanwhile, global prices pushed up domestic price for fuel and gold. Excluding food and energy price, core CPI slid to 0.6%y/y from 1.2% in February, same as Q4.

 PPI edged down by 0.1ppt to -2.8%y/y in March, and dropped by 0.1%m/m on a sequential basis. Subdued PPI reflects supply resumption and slow demand recovery post CNY. Investment and consumer goods price within PPI both edged down. The weaker PPI does not bode well for core CPI ahead.

We revised down 2024 inflation forecast given year-to-date weakness. China saw weaker-than-expected CPI growth of 0%y/y and PPI growth of -2.7%y/ y in Q1, echoing still soft demand recovery but robust supply of goods and services. We continue to expect non-food consumer goods prices to increase slightly and pork prices to bottom out gradually in the rest of 2024. That said, we revise down full-year CPI forecast to 0.4% in 2024 from 0.5-0.8% previously projected, given YTD softness. Meanwhile, manufacturing and consumer goods under PPI may stay soft in the coming months given relatively low capacityutilization rate. Raw materials prices may stabilize gradually after a sharp decline since end 2023. We therefore expect PPI to stay in y/y contraction in most of 2024 and only turn positive y/y in early 2025. We revised down full-year PPI forecast to a decline of 1-1.5% from a decline of 0.5-1% previously anticipated.

## Money & Credit

Credit growth slowed further. Headline new RMB loans picked up to RMB 3.09trn seasonally in March from the previous notable weakness , but fell short of a year ago by RMB 800bn and much weaker than expected. Both M&L household loans and M&L corporate loans were weaker than last March. New TSF recorded RMB 4.87trn (RMB -514bn y/y), slightly better than market expectation, mainly dragged by weaker-than-expected RMB loans and softer government bond issuance (as expected). Apart from a high base, the weakness of credit growth in Q1 may have also reflected soft domestic credit demand and normalization of credit supply in Feb-Mar after an unprecedented strong January. As such, official TSF growth and our adjusted TSF growth both slid by 0.3ppt to 8.7-8.8%y/y. Credit impulse declined more to -1.6% of GDP in March from -0.8% before.

Credit growth to edge up in the rest of 2024E, with some downside risk. New issuance of government bonds in Q1 has been much slower than a year ago (RMB -469bn y/y). We expect government bond issuance to pick up in the rest of 2024, as the new budget plan set a modestly larger overall deficit in 2024 than 2023 (general fiscal + govt fund budgets). Issuance of special LG bonds remained weaker than a year ago so far in April. New bank loans may also improve slightly after significant normalization in Feb-Mar. In particular, we expect property sales and mortgage loans to stabilize gradually in the coming months, and corporate credit demand to improve later in the year as growth momentum stabilizes and equipment upgrading demand materializes. As such, we expect credit growth to edge up in Q2-Q3 and decelerate slightly in Q4. That said, given year-to-date weakness in Q1 and still subdued property activities, we revise down our end-year credit growth forecast to 9.3% from 9.5% previously.

## Base Money & Liquidity Operations

 Base money growth slid by 1.6ppt to 5.3%y/y in March (3mma) from February, after adjusting for RRR cuts (e.g. Jan 2020, July/Dec 2021, Apr/Dec 2022, Mar/Sep 2023, Feb 2024 and various targeted RRR cuts) and changes from other liquidity facilities.

The PBC withdrew liquidity in the interbank market in March. The PBC withdrew liquidity by RMB -551bn through OMO, RMB -94bn through MLF and RMB -32bn through PSL in March. Nevertheless, as seasonal liquidity demand fades, interbank rates remained largely stable. The PBC cut RRR by 50bps on Feb 5, which brought down the the overall weighted average RRR for financial institutions to 7.0% from 7.4% and released base money liquidity of around RMB 1trn, helping to keep overall liquidity ample (see RRR cut comment).

 Expect continued accommodative monetary and credit policy, but no aggressive easing. We expect another small 25bps RRR cut in the rest of 2024, together with other liquidity facilities, to accommodate the expected large bond issuance. In addition, our baseline assumes PBC to inject another RMB 300-500bn PSL this year to support social housing and urban village renovation.

## Interest Rates & Bond Market

 REPO rate unchanged in 2024. Interbank rates typically move up near monthends on seasonal liquidity demand and edge down at the start of the following month. PBC cut REPO rate by 20bps in 2023 and kept it unchanged in 2024. DR007 averaged 1.94% in 2023 and hovered around 1.8-1.9% lately.

Yields edged down as property downturn deepened. 10-year CGB yield trended down since Q2 2023 due to subdued growth momentum, except for a period of modest rebound in Aug-Oct 2023 on signs of growth bottoming. Since late 2023, 10yr CGB yield has trended down again on expected policy easing and investors’ desire for safety assets, hovering around 2.2-2.3% lately. Corporate yields dropped as well, and corporate credit spreads narrowed modestly. March issuance of government bond was softer than a year ago while that of corporate bonds rebounded, as expected.

Likely no policy rate cut in 2024. In 2023, the PBC cut MLF rate by 25bps, 1yr LPR by 20bps and 5yr LPR by 10bps. ## Trade

Exports dropped back to 7.5%y/y contraction in March, and imports also dropped to 1.9%y/y contraction. Monthly trade balance narrowed to \$59bn from \$63bn in Jan-Feb. High base due to reopening boost in 2023 is a key reason; taking 2019 as the base, exports and imports grew by 7.1% and 5.8% in March, vs. priors of 8.4% and 5.2% respectively. Full year exports contracted by 4.6%y/y and imports contracted by 5.5%y/y in 2023, after growing 5.6% and 0.7% in 2022. Real exports and imports expanded by 4.6%/2.9% in 2023.

Export growth to Asia held up better, IT export growth accelerated. Shipments to the G3 (the US, the EU & Japan) registered wider y/y contraction at 10.4%. Export growth to Asian economies held up much better, while shipments to other EM economies seemed to perform much worse with double digit y/y contraction. Consistent with the tech cycle improvement, shipment growth of the IT basket accelerated to 7.4%y/y in March (from prior of 2.2%), thanks to supports from mobile phone and PCs exports. Shipments of consumer goods swung down sharply to 17.2%y/y contraction from 20.3%y/y in Jan-Feb.

A slight trade recovery in 2024. Hard data and survey data suggest that improving external demand condition has been supporting exporters in the region. Despite the disruptions in headline growth rate due to high base, most of our estimates suggest export level has continued to expand over March. Furthermore, we observed sequential improvements in exports over the quarter. That is, China is benefiting from external demand recovery too. That said, a gradual recovery is still more likely than a boom for China’s exports given only modest global growth recovery projected ahead. We revised our full year export projection to 3.5%y/y growth from prior of 1.2%. In the near term, the high base in April may still lead to y/y export contraction (see latest view).

## FX Reserves and Capital Flows

 Headline FX reserves expanded by \$19.8bn to \$3.24trn in Mar vs. Feb, following contraction in Jan and expansion in Feb. China’s FX reserves increased by \$110bn in 2023, declined by \$123bn in 2022, increased by \$34bn in 2021, following gains in 2019-20 and losses in 2018.

Valuation effect remained negative, widening trade balance provided support. We estimate the negative valuation effect from major reserve currencies' movement has likely been around \$3bn due to incremental USD strength at end-Mar vs end-Feb. Global financial market fluctuations might have brought additional valuation losses too, but widening trade balance from February has been a contributing factor to the improvements in the headline. Overall capital outflow pressure has likely continued. That said, the balance of total foreign holdings of China's onshore bonds registered another expansion of RMB 328bn in 1Q24, after RMB 474bn expansion in 4Q23. Foreign holding balance recorded a RMB 282bn gain in 2023 (contractions in most of the early months but turning positive since Sep), after a RMB 616bn loss in 2022 (including a sharp loss of RMB 740bn in the 10 consecutive months over Feb-Nov 2022).

We expect the new normal to continue, with larger 2-way capital flows. The rise in capital outflows will mainly reflect non-government entities’ accumulation of overseas assets, especially as controls on capital outflows gradually ease over longer term. Recent sharp drop in FDI into China is partly due to decline in retained profits and high USD costs, but also reflects pressures of decoupling. We expect FDI to recover in 2024-25, but not back to its pre-pandemic levels. Meanwhile, we believe China's continued financial market opening and decent economic growth potential should continue to attract more inflows (see our deep dive).

## Exchange Rate

RMB depreciated by 0.4% against the USD by end-Mar vs. end-Feb, and weakened further to the range of 7.24 in recent days in April. The strengthening USD DXY index is a key driver behind the CNY depreciation against the USD, while the CNY appreciated against the CFETs basket over Mar. Full year RMB weakened by 2% vs USD while strengthened by 1% against the basket in 2023. This follows 9% depreciation vs the USD and 2.4% weakness vs the basket in 2022.

RMB exchange rate has been driven by various factors. Resilient trade balance is a supporting factor, while the negative China-US rate differentials and concerns on China's growth momentum slowdown are main headwinds. The PBC lowered RRR for FX deposits by 200bp/100bp in Sep/Apr 2022. The SAFE raised the macro-prudential adjustment parameter for cross-border financing from 1 to 1.25 in Oct 2022, and further to 1.5 in July 2023.

RMB may strengthen to 7.1-7.2 range by end 2024E. Similar to the recent depreciations, the dynamics of the interest rate differentials, USD index, as well as China's growth data and property market weakness, may still bring episodes of CNY weakness ahead. That said, PBC will likely use various tools (including counter-cyclical factor in daily fixing) to prevent further CNY weakness much beyond 7.3 amid rapid episodes of depreciation and significant increase of volatility. Given our expectation of a stabilization of China’s growth momentum as well as the expected Fed interest rate cuts, we continue to expect RMB to strengthen a bit by end-2024E. That said, we recently revised end-2024 USDCNY projection to 7.1-7.2 from 7.0, given signifiant changes of market and UBS expectations about Fed rate cuts and US treasury yields.

## Fiscal

Both fiscal revenue and expenditure declined y/y in March. Headline fiscal revenue declined by -2.4%y/y In March following -2.3%y/y in Jan-Feb (-2.3%y/y in Q1. On a comparable basis (after controlling a high base due to delayed SME tax collection and carry-over of tax cut since mid-2023), the underlying fiscal revenue grew 2.2%y/y in Q1. Fiscal expenditure also turned into a decline of -2.9%y/y in March from 6.7%y/y in Jan-Feb, recording +2.9% y/y in Q1. Corporate income tax revenue grew by 7.3%y/y in March (1%y/y in Q1), while personal income tax jumped by 75%y/y in March on a low base partly due to later CNY timing (though still a -4.5%y/y decline in Q1). As such, Q1 saw a fiscal deficit of RMB 898bn (vs RMB -557bn in Q1 2023). Local land revenue declined by another -19%y/y in March after printing 0% in Jan-Feb, recording -6.7%y/y in Q1 (vs -4.3% in Q4).

Further progress in addressing LGFV debt issue, but not yet the long-term solution. Local governments issued RMB 1.39 trillion of special refinancing LG bonds to swap for qualified LGFV debt and/or pay arrears to corporate suppliers in H2 2023. In 2024, we expect the government to issue additional special refinancing LG bonds and encourage banks to increase restructuring of LGFV bank loans. That said, we think the long-term solution (e.g. adjustment of central-local fiscal relations and debt structure) may not come out soon (see NPC takeaway).

Modest fiscal support in a more explicit way in 2024. As expected, the government announced a headline general fiscal deficit of 3% of GDP, additional RMB 1 trillion special CGB and RMB 3.9 trillion special LG bonds. The combined budget deficit (general fiscal + govt funds) on a cash basis is estimated to be 8.2% of GDP, or 1.2ppts larger than the outturn of 2023. We expect the Augmented Fiscal Deficit (AFD) to expand by 1-1.2ppt in 2024 after the 0.2ppt increase in 2023. Overall fiscal support will be more explicit in stronger on-budget spending, especially from central government. We think local government land sales revenue may continue to decline by another 10% in 2024, while off-budget LGFV financing will likely be muted amid the ongoing emphasis of controlling implicit debt risk (see NPC takeaway).
