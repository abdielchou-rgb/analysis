## March 12

CPI strength lacks trend, OER-Rent wedge narrows; USTs selloff in parallel fashion; EUR/GBP rises after soft UK wages data; Periphery outperforms core and semi-core; ECB's Holzmann says June cut more likely than April; PHP outperforms peers; DXY at 102.93 (+0.1%); US 10y at 4.151% (+5.2bp).

• US February CPI comes in strong. The OER-Rent wedge narrows, but the acceleration in rent reverses four months of progress; 3m annualized core CPI rises to 4.18% (P: 3.97%).

• Treasuries sell off after CPI, which neither encourages the Fed to cut earlier nor faster; US duration ends \~5bp cheaper across the curve after the \$39bn 10y re-opening auction tails by 0.9bp.

• EUR/GBP (+0.2%) rises after UK wage growth comes in soft, our economists note the totality of labor market surveys continues to suggest an ongoing build-up of slack.

• Peripheral duration outperforms core and semi-core European duration; the 10y BTP-Bund spread tightens by \~5bp to 128bp.

• ECB Governing Council Member Holzmann believes rate cuts are more likely in June than April, and says "If and as the data are strong enough for the ECB to cut first then it could happen."

• PHP (+0.1%) outperforms regional peers after the trade deficit is smaller than expected, and the US Commerce Secretary says US firms will invest over US\$1bn in the Philippines.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

Risk assets welcomed February US CPI data that did not encourage the Fed to cut earlier, or faster but also kept prospects of hikes limited to tail probabilities.

Core CPI increased by 0.358% m/m (C: 0.3%; P: 0.39%), which only brought the annual rate down to 3.8% (C: 3.7%; P: 3.9%). But a measure of the more recent trend of inflation, 3-month annualized core CPI, ticked up to 4.18% (P: 3.97%). Core goods (+0.11% m/m) turned positive, led by used cars and apparel, but core services (+0.46%) registered a notable deceleration.

Although February inflation data was strong, there was lack of a clear trend in underlying components of the basket. The OER-Rent wedge was under close scrutiny after it had widened to 20bp in January, following a recent methodological change. Although the OER-Rent wedge narrowed to -2bp in February, and returned in line with its historical trendline, rent (+0.46%) surprisingly now accelerated up to OER (+0.44%) and reversed four months of progress.

Rents were stable across three regions and increased the most in the Northeast – typically the noisiest of the four regions – which raises the possibility that the uptick in rent was noise. In the big picture, the New Tenant Repeat Rent Index shows new tenants rents below pre-pandemic levels, a negative sign for shelter inflation over the coming months.

The Cleveland Fed’s trimmed mean CPI measure came in at 0.31% m/m (P: 0.47%), in line with the average over the past six months, and an important drop from the prior month. Incorporating CPI inputs, our economists preliminary forecast core PCE inflation increased by 0.30% m/m in February (P: 0.42%), which would keep core PCE nearly flat on a y/y basis in February.

The mild post-CPI Treasury sell-off built upon weakness from the prior session. Yields rose in close to a parallel manner (\~5bp) across the curve through an uneventful \$39bn 10y re-opening auction, which produced a 0.9bp tail. The Monthly Treasury Statement reported the federal government incurred a budget deficit of - \$296.3bn in February (February 2023: -\$262.4bn). Through the first five months of fiscal year 2024, the budget deficit stands at -\$828.1bn. This is mainly driven by a 41% increase in interest costs, which totaled \$433.4bn over the first five months of FY24, and now comprise 16% of total outlays.

Higher Treasury yields in response to CPI proved little impediment to risk appetite. Both investment grade and high yield CDX spreads tightened to new 52-week tights, at +48.4bp and +323bp, respectively. Technology (Nasdaq 100: +1.49%) stocks led the broader market higher (S&P 500: +1.12%) as artificial intelligence beneficiaries received renewed demand. Caught in between the rise in Treasury yields and a durable risk-on tone, the DXY USD Index (+0.1%) finished little changed.

## Japan:

JGBs steepened alongside decent support in the short-to-medium sector. Market participants were focused on how much usage there will be in the BoJ’s loan support program on March 14, since it may be the last long-term funding supply operation before BoJ lift-off. Markets participants believed banks may rush to apply for this operation to fund long-term (4y maximum) cash at 0%. Accordingly, market participants anticipated there would be decent demand for collateral in short-tomedium-term JGBs. Indeed, the 5y JGB auction was digested smoothly, and the 5y sector traded resiliently post-auction; 5y JGBs rallied by 0.5bp d/d to 0.370% on a simple yield basis. 30y+ JGBs lagged without support from domestic market participants; 30y JGBs sold off by 0.5bp d/d to 1.82%. TONA OIS twist-steepened, with strong receiving pressure in the medium-term sector. This was likely due to bank receiving demand related to ALM against usage of the upcoming loan support program. XCCY basis widened across the curve.

During Tokyo trading hours, JPY weakened broadly as comments from BoJ Governor Ueda in Congress appeared to be interpreted dovishly. He did not give any indication of policy action for the March meeting, but media reports emphasized his comments on weak household consumption. However, there were other various media reports that suggested the BoJ would mull NIRP removal at its March meeting if Rengo wage data was strong and the reaction in markets seemed limited. If anything, price action seemingly responded to any dovish developments as it had already discounted a decent chance for March lift-off. Other CCY/JPY also retraced recent weakness, and EUR/JPY recovered the 161.00 level.

## Europe:

EUR/GBP (+0.2%) strengthened after UK pay growth was below expectations. The Norwegian wage Committee kept its 2024 CPI forecast unchanged at 4.1% – lower than the latest Norges Bank projections from December. NOK underperformed in G10. EUR/NOK (+0.6%) found resistance at 11.5000 while USD/NOK rose 0.6%.

The focus of the London session was on the US CPI release. Headline CPI was in line with consensus expectations, but higher-than-expected core weighed on duration on both sides of the Atlantic. 10y Bunds sold off by 3bp. to 2.33%, outperformed 10y USTs by \~3bp, and curves bear-flattened. In swaps (against 6m), 2s10s flattened by over 2bp. while 10s30s steepened by 1bp. In money markets, reds led the sell-off (+6bp), which brought the cumulative amount of rate cuts priced by end-2025 below 170bp. The sell-off in core ASW gained momentum, led by Bobl and Bund ASW (-1.6bp). The periphery continued to outperform core and semi-core, and 10y Bono-Bund and 10y BTP-Bund spreads tightened by over 1.5bp and by 5bp, respectively.

## Dollar Bloc:

A stronger-than-expected CPI report supported the US dollar, but opposite moves in USTs and US equities limited USD’s gains. US yields moved higher as core inflation measures cast further doubt on whether price pressures would continue to ease over the coming months, but US equities remained resilient. The DXY USD Index rose 0.1%.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies exhibited mixed moves, but most were largely stable ahead of the US February inflation release. CNH remained \~7.1774 despite a continued rally in the Hang Seng Index (+3.1%), and the PBOC set the USD/CNY fixing at the lowest since January (7.0963). PHP outperformed, up \~0.1% to 55.3, after the January trade deficit was smaller than expected, and the US Commerce Secretary said US firms will invest over US\$1bn in the Philippines. THB was the main underperformer; USD/ THB rose 0.4% to 35.58 and tracked the decline in gold prices (-0.3%) ahead of US CPI. KRW fell \~0.1% to 1,311 alongside foreign equity outflows of US\$193mn.

Most AxJ rates sold off. Singapore and China rates underperformed. In China, 5y NDIRS yields rose by 2bp and the 1s5s curve bear-steepened. 30y CGBs sold off by 8bp to 2.55% amid the continued rebound in local equities. In Singapore, the swaps curve shifted 2-3bp higher following the overnight move in USTs. In India, although the central bank took delivery of US\$5bn USD/INR swaps, which injected INR liquidity, NDOIS yields were largely unchanged ahead of the release of February CPI for India. Korea was the main exception to the broader trend, as yields fell 1-2bp across the NDIRS curve even though several BoK members highlighted in the February meeting minutes it is too early for a policy pivot.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended mixed following a volatile session after US CPI data. COP (- 0.2%) lagged peers, while CLP (+0.7%) outperformed with few local developments in either market. MXN was little unchanged, while BRL (+0.1%) strengthened slightly. Rates mostly followed USTs, with the CAM curve 9bp higher at the 2y tenor, while the IBR belly underperformed (5y: +13 bp). DIs performed better than peers (January 2026s: -3bp) despite slightly higher-than-expected inflation data (for more, read here).

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
