# Rates Map: Trades we like as central banks adjust policy

Gilts look cheap across the curve and also compared to US and euro area at the long end. UK inflation will likely fall below US inflation by April, and the divergence in growth outlook is set to persist.

## Longs attractive again but how dovish are central banks?

We are holding on to our early March views that longs across the curve are attractive again, especially in the front-end and in the UK (Figure 2). UBS does not expect a US recession in 2024 anymore but we see downside risks to our growth forecast. We also continue to see downside risks to the ECB's growth forecasts for 2025-26. The FOMC sees stronger growth and the long-run dot ticked up to 2.6%, from 2.5%, but our US economists emphasize that a big "clump" remains on 2.5% (Figure 3). The market reaction to the BoJ decision to end negative interest rate policy and yield curve control was dovish as governor Ueda provided no hint on further tightening. The BoE MPC had a dovish shift in the vote and we see risk of a cut in June. The SNB surprised with a cut.

## Data dependence and balance sheet reduction limit dovish messaging

Yields fell on dovish surprises of central banks but policymaker continue to emphasize data dependence, which keeps curves from steepening (Figure 4). The SNB cut generated a dovish market reaction, but UBS expects just two more cuts as the SNB pointed to the risk of tighter monetary policy "in some countries" and noted that academic work suggest a neutral real interest rate of 0-0.5% for Switzerland. This would put the nominal neutral rate in Switzerland at 1-1.5% based on the 1% mid-point of the inflation target range. Turning to the US, Fed Chair Powell suggested that the Fed will not simply wind down QT and stop later this year, but could explore how small its balance sheet can get before reserve scarcity leads to stress in money markets. ECB president Lagarde anchored expectations for a first cut in June at the ECB watchers but also emphasized the need to see further progress on wages, profit margins and productivity. We see the optionality in the future introduction of new structural longterm refinancing operations and bond portfolio as a positive but ECB GC member Schnabel said that the higher supply of liquidity could pose longer-term risks to financial stability by distorting market prices and inducing excessive risk-taking.

## What does BoJ normalization mean for bonds markets?

UBS does not believe that repatriation flows will sink global markets but a key question is what foreigners will do as BoJ auction plans suggests some QT (Figure 5).UBS believes that complete normalization of the BoJ is still far away. We expect the next BoJ hike in October followed by further hikes to 1% in '25. Since the BOJ meeting, FX basis tightening has worsened JGB levels for foreign investors, while improving the potential pick up for Japanese investors purchasing long-end euro area bonds.

## What about monetary policy and higher long-term real rates ?

Fears about a shift to a new regime of higher long-term real rates and the implications for monetary policy persist, with ECB GC member Schnabel arguing that policymakers need to tread carefully (Figure 6). We have argued in essay 2 of our 2024 outlook and previously that policy rates need to be lower if term premia are higher or financial conditions will be too tight. One can certainly argue that monetary policy is not neutral and drives expectations of future real rates. An increase in the policy rate could also prompt investor to reduce duration, lowering the demand for long-term bonds and increasing long-term interest rates and term premia. A big challenge for bonds has also been that they have not been a good hedge for equities following the recent inflation surge. But that correlation should change again as inflation settles lower.

## What is the ECB watching?

ECB President Lagarde pointed to three key factors to watch to gain confidence on disinflation; wages, profit margin, and productivity growth. Nevertheless, the ECB also sees falling forecast errors on inflation even if euro area labour markets have remained stronger than expected. Recent forecast errors on inflation have been mainly due to surprises in energy prices and supply chain disruptions and a key question is if we could see a return of the negative correlation between core and energy inflation often observed in the post-EMU period (Figure 7US andEuro eiflt ).

 Wages. Following a jump in our monthly tracker of negotiated wages to 4.7% y/y in December, our economics team sees signs of a slowdown in early 2024.UBS expects euro area wage growth to ease below 4% in early 2024 if the December one-off in Italy were to drop out in January. The ECB will receive data on negotiated wage growth in Q1 of 2024 at the end of May.

Profit margins. The compression of profit margins has allowed wages to catch up without accelerating inflation, but President Lagarde warned that if firms were to regain pricing power as the economy recovers and profit margins increase 1 ppt more than current ECB projections, inflation would be 2.7% in 2025 and 2.4% in 2026. Nevertheless, Belgium provides some evidence that mark-ups are not constant and unit profits fell after mandatory indexation increased wages in 2022 and 2023 to keep track of cost of living increases (Figure 8). Belgian CPI inflation fell to 1.35% y/y in December 2023 before rising to 3.18% y/y in March.

## Buy German swap spreads as scarcity concerns have eased

Short 10y German asset swap spreads (ASWs) as bonds underperform swaps was one of the top trade ideas for 2024 in our Global Outlook but we believe that swap spreads will now widen to our fair value levels of around 50 bps. 10y German ASW spreads have cheapened by more than 30 bps since our outlook and are close to pre-pandemic levels of around 30bps. Our parsimonious model, which includes a German bond free float measure and the 3m ESTR vs Bubill spread as a proxy for capturing scarcity premium and funding stress, explains nearly 72% of the total variation in our sample (Figure 9 and Figure 10). Monthly securities lending from the ECB was €85 bn in Feb 2024, below the peak of €124 bn but above the pre-2022 lending limit of €75 bn.

## UK curve attractive for multiple reasons

We think the strong performance of UK rates compared to other markets will persist for at least five reasons. First, we expect UK inflation to further ease in the coming months with differentials turning negative (Figure 11 and Figure 12). Second, there has been a shift in the MPC with 2 hawks no longer voting for hikes and the Committee acknowledging the recent decline in services inflation and moderation in wage growth. Governor Bailey also said that a June cut is in play. Third, the UK spring budget was relatively uneventful and we think that the fiscal risk is relatively higher in, for example, the US due to the upcoming elections. Fourth, the market is pricing in cumulatively fewer BoE rate cuts for 2024 and 2025 compared to the Fed, despite policy rates at the same level (Figure 13 and Figure 14). Fifth, cross-country spreads are also wide (Figure 15 and Figure 16).

## Receive ESTR against SARON with UBS expecting just two more SNB cuts in June and September

We expect money markets to reflect a narrowing policy rate differential between the ECB and the SNB (Figure 18). Our baseline assumes 75bps of cuts from the ECB in 2024 and an additional 100bps of cuts in 2025 compared to two more cuts from the SNB. Chairman Jordan noted in the press conference that some academic studies suggest a neutral real interest rate of 0-0.5% for Switzerland. Using a mid-point of the inflation target range of 1%, this points to a nominal neutral rate of 1-1.5%.
