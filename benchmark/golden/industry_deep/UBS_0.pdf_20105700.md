# European Leisure Sector

# Outlook 2024 - Likely more of the same just at a slower pace...

## While positive momentum to slow we think profit growth will continue…

2023 saw positive share price performance in the European travel space although share price performance in the leisure space was more mixed. While we think earnings momentum will slow in 2024 (a more normal year with base impact recovered in 2023), we remain selectively positive on opportunities in the travel and leisure space. We think certain themes from 2023 such as inflation will likely become lower concerns for investors in the travel and leisure space as the year progresses while others such as consumer health will remain a topic of debate. On balance sheets we see strength with the focus on capital returns through special dividends and buybacks. If the environment becomes more certain and interest rates fall we could see momentum return to accretive M&A in the T&L sector which will accelerate earnings. The sector in general is below long term average multiples and Eurostoxx.

## Hotels - Europe RevPAR benefiting from international recovery/events

We continue to see buoyant ADR above the pre-pandemic level (past 28-day moving average ADR growth vs 2019 +23% in Europe, +31% in the UK, +23% in Ireland). However, with an economic backdrop of recession concerns, there is some concern on continuance of ADR growth due to its negative correlation with unemployment which may rise (UBS economist have benign unemployment levels in key travel markets). However, low level of construction activity and pipeline support pricing/ADR growth (here). We expect net unit growth of +2-5% in FY24E for the different operators but with the potential for falling interest rates, we could see developers commit to new hotel projects. Conversions are expected to continue to aid the brand portfolios.

## Catering - best positioned to pass on inflation

We think catering/concessions should continue to deliver mid to high single digit organic growth on the back of inflation, net contract wins and in the case of concessions travel recovery (We have bottom up European airlines delivering 7% traffic growth in 2024). We continue to think in leisure that contract catering is best positioned to manage inflation on way up and down based on the nature of the contracts and managing past inflation. Inflationary trends seem on the downside and we continue to believe that contract catering companies could see a margin boost for a limited period of time until pricing resets.

## Gambling - continued significant growth in the US

Much of the debates in gambling continue to be on the US where we continue to see strong growth (+c50% Jan-Oct'23 in online sports betting and +23% in iGaming). We estimate the US market size to reach \$25bn by 2025 (here). FanDuel remains the clear leader with 40% GGR market share in online sports betting (47% NGR share) and 23% in iGaming in 3Q23 although its market share reduced slightly whilst DraftKings increased slightly. BetMGM saw some deterioration. Overall, we continue to think that Flutter is strongly positioned in the US sports betting market and will retain a high market share over the long term. We rate both Flutter and Entain Buy and we view risk/ reward more balanced with Flutter even though we have higher upside at Entain.

## Travel budgets expected to increase by c10% yoy in 2024

## UBS Economists forecasts don't suggest a recession…

UBS economists expect global growth to be 2.6% in 2024 i.e. ½ ppt lower than 2023 which is mainly due to weaker growth in the US and China. In the US, the economy was more resilient than expected in 2023 as the strong consumer spending growth sustained the business sector and kept the labour market expansion brisk. However, as nominal wages are high, UBS expects some monetary tightening, which could impact the US economy with an already eroding excess savings and tight credit and lending markets, therefore, UBS expects a few quarters of negative growth. For China, UBS expects weak property market sentiment, oversupply and weak household income growth to slow China GDP growth to 4.4% in 2024. In Eurozone, UBS expects restrictive monetary and fiscal policy, and weak external environment to likely impact GDP moderately, and forecasts growth at 0.6% in 2024. ## GBTA expects global business travel to recover to its pre-pandemic level in 2024

The Global Business Travel Association (GBTA) expects global business travel to recover to its pre-pandemic level (\$1.4 trillion) in 2024. ## Key Data - Hotels (3): Supply

The data: We look at UBS Evidence Lab US Construction Activity Monitor tracks total construction activity in the US - including for hotels and motels - on an aggregate and state-level basis, STR data on ADR, policy rates and Lodging econometrics' pipeline estimates. The construction activity dataset measures total project value, which is the value of a project that is awarded to a general contractor once it wins a bid (i.e. proposed value) while the ADR growth representing the relationship of pricing to occupancy, serve as a leading indicator into construction activity for hotel developers. The relationship between construction activity and ADR while also looking at the policy rates indicate supply prospects.

UBS view: Hotel supply is inherently linked to demand fundamentals, although it adjusts lower with a lag (reflecting long construction times). Before the construction of a hotel, developers typically look for a specific market ADR to earn a sufficient return and we continue to see upward ADR pressure. Nevertheless, we think IRR is under pressure due to inflation of materials and higher interest rates and the UBS Evidence Lab construction activity monitor which tracks project value, remains under pressure. While negative for system growth this likely means a tight supply environment for next year and beyond.

## Valuation Method and Risk Statement

The hotel sector is economically sensitive with business activity influencing the degree of corporate travel and consumers' discretionary spending influencing leisure spending. Hotel demand can be affected by local health concerns, terrorism, travel disruptions, sporting events, trade fairs, and the like. On a company specific basis, demand is affected by brand positioning and brand standards, and the trade-off of room price and perceived value which in turn is somewhat affected by levels and consistency of maintenance capital spending. Hotel supply depends on the construction cycle and local incentives. The magnitude of each company's profit cycle is somewhat dependent on the company's mixture of owned and company operated hotels, management contracts, and franchise arrangements as well as the brand positioning. We value hotels using EV/EBITDA and SOTPs.

For contract catering, the risks include deterioration in employment levels, continued selfisolation measures in the US and Europe, which together account for the majority of sales. There could also be an increase in people working from home. As wages and food costs represent over 70% of costs, an unexpected rise in either would have a negative impact on our forecasts. We value the catering companies on a DCF basis.

Betting has a high short-term trading volatility depending on the outcome of individual races. While in the long term this volatility tends to average out, short-term surprises are not uncommon. The UK industry is highly regulated by the Government. It is therefore susceptible to changes in Government thinking that are wholly unpredictable and we are currently awaiting the results of the Gambling Act Review. Regulation changes can manifest themselves through higher duty regime, restrictions on the amount that customers are allowed to stake on a single bet, monthly limits etc. that can have an impact on the profitability of the business. The online market is competitive and requires significant marketing costs. The business can be affected by local health concerns (ie lockdowns), macroeconomic headwinds/lower discretionary spending by consumers, integration issues, regulation/tax changes in the different countries in which the business operates, regulatory/ speed of online gambling legalisations in the US on a state by state basis, interruptions of information technology & communication systems, competition. We value the gambling operators on a multiple based SOTP.
