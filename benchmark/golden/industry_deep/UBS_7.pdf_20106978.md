# How do credit markets perform in a no and soft landing scenarios?

We discuss three key themes from investor meetings in a backdrop where the contour of the US economic outlook seems to be shifting with each data release: first, is a US nolanding scenario actually bad for credit? Second, in a softer landing scenario can spreads tighten from current levels? Third, what are the catalysts for spread widening and what risks are more likely? We have three key conclusions from our analysis: 1) in a no-landing scenario spreads widen moderately, mainly in Q424/Q125, with single B leveraged loans outperforming; 2) in a softer landing outcome spreads are rangebound with little upside, and loans again outperform; 3) we empirically review key catalysts for spread widening and believe a US growth slowdown and credit defaults/downgrades are higher probability risks in the next few quarters.

## In 'no landing' spreads widen, mainly in Q4: single B loans outperform (Figs 1-7)

In collaboration with our US economics team we constructed a ‘sustained strong growth/ no landing” scenario assuming above-trend US real GDP growth of 2.75%, monthly payrolls of \~200k and a bit stronger population growth for Q2-Q424. In that context core PCE finishes Q4 ’24 at 2.3% and accelerates to 2.8% in Q4 ’25, and the Fed cuts twice in H2 before starting hikes again in June ’25 – moving 25bp at every meeting in H2 ‘25. Our rates team projects US 10yr rates finish at \~4% in Q4 ’24, then rise sequentially close to 5% in Q4 ’25. We see credit spreads holding in through the summer (IG/HY/LL at 95/300/425bp in Q2 ‘24) as base effects and falling OER support a couple Fed cuts, in addition to HY/LL credit metrics having some cushion to higher rates (excluding the lowest rated cohorts). But as inflation base effects fade, stronger inflation data becomes apparent in H2 and post-election the Fed starts to pivot guidance. We see spreads widening mostly at the very end of 2024 or early 2025. We forecast US IG, HY and LL spreads at 120/400/550bp, respectively, in Q424, and rest of year total returns of -1%/1%/2%, respectively (Figs 1-7). In this scenario single B LL outperform.

## In 'softer landing' spreads are a bit tighter: B/CCC loans outperform (Figs 8-11)

For our softer landing scenario we use consensus economic forecasts calling for q/q real GDP of 1.1-1.5% for Q2-Q424 and monthly payrolls of \~100k. Core PCE is stable at 2.3%, the Fed cuts 100bp to 4.5% by end-24, and 10yr rates finish at \~3.8% in Q4 ’24. We expect credit spreads to trade in a narrow range through Q4, with IG/HY/LL closing little changed at 100/325/450bp at end-24. We do not see spreads moving materially tighter. US growth is projected to rebound, but moderately, unlike in Q2 ‘21 when IG/HY spreads touched 80/268bp for one month as real GDP was running at 6.2% q/q and the Fed had been buying corporate bonds. For US HY part of the headwind is historically wide CCC spreads, which are unlikely to compress to prior tights without strong growth.

Pre-COVID, US IG/HY spreads traded below 90/300bp in two main periods: 1) H1 ’07, when IG/HY spreads averaged 89/263bp as new products boomed and leverage was much more readily available, compressing risk premium; and 2) 1997-Q1 ‘98, when IG/ HY spreads averaged 61/258bp but both indices were materially higher rated; the IG index had 27% more AAA/AAs vs. 25% less BBBs, while the HY index had 8% more Bs vs. 8% less CCCs. Using today’s weights US IG/HY spreads in 1997 would increase to 79/295bp, respectively. In our softer landing scenario IG/HY/LL total returns for the rest of 2024 are 6%/7%/7%, respectively, with B and CCC LL outperforming (Figs 8-11).

Other events include deterioration in the earnings outlook, US/China trade tariffs, and frozen capital markets. In the current backdrop we see the risk of Fed tightening/inflation as a lower likelihood per our analysis above. Since November spreads have decoupled from rate volatility, resilient in the face of \~3 cuts being priced out of the market. Credit spreads have become negatively, not positively, correlated with rates as core PCE has moved below 3%, and investors grow confident in falling OER and base effects to extend the trend. We believe this regime will hold assuming expectations of core PCE (also proxied by 2yr breakevens) don’t rise materially above 3%. The risk of a systemic sovereign default, previously Russia and Greece, also seems to be a comparatively lower risk given the market’s resilience through the Russia/Ukraine conflict and with US/European banks better capitalized; US and European bank CDS are trading at 55/60bp, respectively, or 12/19bp off their tights since 2014.

In our view the higher probability risks stem from a more severe deterioration in the US growth outlook, specifically the US consumer, or an uptick in corporate defaults/ fallen angels. On the former our credit-based recession gauge remains elevated, consistent with a potential slowdown but no recession, at 33%; our economists’ broader recession signal is fading somewhat, but stands at 54%. Our toolkit of consumer credit cycle indicators remain fairly stable since our last update, but we’re watching closely for rising signs of stress. On the latter we continue to see mixed, but incrementally improving, signs for corporate defaults/downgrades; the latest default data suggest improvements m/m for HY and private credit, but some residual pressure in LL. Up/downgrade rates are stabilizing in LL, but in IG we see greater fallen angels in 2024, mainly in cable/mediawhich seems largely priced in but could cause some volatility in HY BBs. Lastly, geopolitical events and US/China trade relations will be increasingly important as we move through 2024.
