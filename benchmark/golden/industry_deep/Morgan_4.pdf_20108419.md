# Making progress in AI semis – China HBM and CPU

## Key Takeaways

■ China state-backed investment fund to accelerate its AI semi localization

## China national fund

• China sets up third fund with US\$47.5 bn to boost semiconductor sector. China 2023 semiconductor self-sufficiency ratio only rose to 20% fell short of our previous expectation of 23%. Nevertheless, China has set up its third planned state-backed investment fund to boost its semiconductor industry, with a registered capital of Rmb344 bn (US\$47.5 bn). Key focus will be: 1) leading node foundry, advanced packaging, 2) memory, especially highbandwidth memory (HBM), and 3) semi equipment and materials. (Reuters, May 27, 2024)

## China AI semi – HBM and CPU

• China may start to produce HBM2 by 2026. China’s largest DRAM chip manufacturer, ChangXin Memory Technologies, is collaborating with chip packaging and testing company Tongfu Microelectronics to develop HBM chip samples,. On the other hand, Wuhan Xinxin Semiconductor Manufacturing is constructing a 12-inch plant with a monthly capacity of 3,000 wafers, which is planned to manufacture HBM chips. Per the corporate registration documents, the plant is expected to commence operations in February this year. China supply risk is lingering for the memory industry (Reuters, May 15, 2024)

• Huawei and OrangePi launch new dev board with mystery CPU and AI processor. The Kunpeng Pro development board uses a custom Huawei Kunpeng CPU that's paired with an AI FPGA processor. The AI processor provides 8 TOPS of AI computing power. (Tom's Hardware, May 11, 2024)

## Technology export controls

• Qualcomm can no longer ship to Huawei. The US has revoked licenses that allowed companies including Intel and Qualcomm to ship chips used for laptops and handsets to Huawei Technologies. (Reuters, 9 May 2024). MediaTek's China smartphone customers (e.g., Honor) may gain market share if Huawei cannot get sufficient chip supply (see link).

Stock implications: China's third big IC fund is positive for all China semi companies. Specifically, China HBM development is positive for equipment vendors, e.g., Naura

## China Localization Progress

We expect China's semiconductor self-sufficiency ratio to reach 25% by 2026e, up 5ppt from 20% in 2023. China semiconductor self-sufficiency ratio only increased to 20% in 2023, which fell short of our 23% expectation. We attribute it to three reasons:

• Weak consumer and industrial demand, as Chinese semiconductor vendors' exposure to consumer market is relatively higher, thus the weak consumer and industrial demand as well as the end customer inventory digestion affects the revenue growth in 2023.

• Limited break through in logic chips using leading node foundry. China's selfsufficiency remains low in CPU and GPU. Even though Huawei's 5G smartphone using locally produced chips the capacity is limited.

• Chinese memory vendors face tough restriction from the US, and they have no presence of high growth areas like High bandwidth memory (HBM) in 2023, thus despite capacity addition, these companies were impacted more during the industry downcyle in 2023.

## By segment:

• We are still positive on upstream vendors like equipment suppliers and EDA, they are likely to achieve a higher self-sufficiency ratio, as these are the chokepoints in China's semi localization and local development of these industries has become a strategic imperative for China.

• We are positive on the self-sufficiency ratio of semiconductor components increasing using mature nodes, such as image sensors and power semis, given local foundry capacity expansion

• While we are relatively cautious on logic semis using advanced nodes, like CPUs and GPUs, given US export restrictions.
