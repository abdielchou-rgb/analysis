## April 11

Treasuries twist-steepen, focus hones on PCE; ECB remains on hold, signals cut; EUR slips; USD/JPY remains around 153 after MoF remarks; JGBs sell-off on BoJ implications from FX weakness; THB lags alongside lower gold, equity prices; DXY at 105.28 (+0.0%); US 10y at 4.587% (+4.3bp).

## Japan:

The significant sell-off in USTs overnight on Wednesday, which came after the strong US CPI data drove broad weakness in JGBs as market participants grew increasingly concerned about a possible near-term BoJ rate hike to counter the rapid FX move. 20y JGBs particularly underperformed on the curve ahead of the 20y auction. Although the 20y auction was in line with already low expectations (tail price: 99.15), 20y JGBs further sold off post-auction with the bearish sentiment in duration. Given a lack of specific buyers of the 20y sector, this sector tends to trade cheap on the curve in a bear market; 20y JGBs sold off by 7.5bp d/d to 1.645% on a simple yield basis. 5y JGBs also traded relatively weak as dealers seemingly struggled to liquidate inventory after the 5y JGB auction; 5y JGBs sold off by 5.5bp d/d to 0.48%.

TONA OIS bear-steepened alongside JGBs, but tenors less than 10y broadly outperformed JGBs. Some market participants may have speculated that the BoJ may reduce its regular JGB purchase size to counter recent JPY weakness. We do not believe this will be the case, as the BoJ views its policy rate as its primary tool for monetary policy adjustment. XCCY basis widened on a broad basis alongside the global risk-off move.

Surprisingly strong US CPI data led to broad USD strength, as market participants pushed back both the timing of the first rate cut as well as the eventual terminal rate; USD/JPY surged above 153.00 overnight on Wednesday. During the Tokyo morning on Thursday, a Japanese Ministry of Finance official reiterated they will take appropriate action against excessive FX moves. Thus, USD/JPY struggled to break meaningfully above 153.00. Other CCY/JPY pairs also ground higher from the London morning; Antipodean currencies particularly outperformed.

## Europe:

After a busy London session for the G10 which featured the ECB central bank and US PPI data, G10 currencies closed near where they opened. EUR (-0.2%) weakened after the ECB remained on hold and indicated June remains the base case for the first cut. CHF (+0.2%) displayed slight gains. In contrast, risk-sensitive currencies sold off, led by SEK (-0.3%) and followed by NOK (-0.1%). GBP finished the London session flat.

European duration traded sideways ahead of the ECB meeting. The ECB remained on hold. Our economists noted the language highlighted progress made toward the 2% inflation target was strengthened by note of an easing in underlying inflation measures, a moderation in wage growth, and firms’ absorbing part of the rise in labor costs in their profits. Also highlighted in the March statement, services inflation was characterized as high and warranting caution. The data-dependent approach was maintained. However, indication of an imminent first cut was included in the statement. Subject to updated projections (the next staff projections will be presented in June) which will provide the Council with a "further increase in confidence that inflation is converging to the target in a sustained manner," thus the "current level of monetary policy restriction" could be reduced (for more see here).

Initially, this left markets little changed. Ahead of the press conference, US PPI data came in weaker and spurred a rally led by the front-end. Duration later pared gains and curves bear-steepened. Overall, 10y Bunds yields closed the London session 3bp higher at 2.46% and 2s10s steepened 3bp in Germany while 10s30s was unchanged. In the London afternoon, media reports of tensions between Iran and Israel weighed on risk sentiment and drove the 10y BTP-Bund spread 4bp wider and Schatz and Bobl ASW \~2bp richer.

## Dollar Bloc:

The DXY USD Index (+0.1%) rise came alongside higher US yields. The US dollar move came amidst significant downside surprises to PPI, which counterbalanced a higher CPI pass-through to PCE and led to an equity rally. The Antipodeans diverged from the rest of risk currencies; AUD and NZD both gained +0.2%. USD/JPY crossed the 153 mark from above and below, seemingly without engendering MoF intervention as it closed flat at 153.21.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly against the US dollar after strong US inflation data. CNH was the exception and outperformed peers. USD/CNH fell 0.1% to 7.2550, aided by a strong USD/CNY fixing (7.0968) and tighter offshore liquidity; 1m CNH HIBOR rose 25bp to 4.44%, the highest level since October 2023. KRW led the losses, down 0.7% to 1364, due to its high beta. TWD declined 0.7% to 32.23 alongside foreign equity outflows of US\$160.5mn. THB (-0.7%) underperformed amid lower gold and equity (SET: -0.5%) prices and retraced most gains from last week. SGD remained relatively stable ahead of the MAS monetary policy meeting. This helped Singapore S\$NEER rally \~1.72%, above the midpoint of the MAS policy band. PHP held steady at 56.50. India, Malaysia, and Indonesia FX markets were closed.

Most AxJ rates sold off following the surge UST yields on Wednesday. In Korea, both 5y and 10y NDIRS yields rose 10bp while the 3s10s KTB curve shifted 8bp higher. In Hong Kong, the 2s10s swap curve bear-flattened 4bp while 2y swap yields rose by 17bp. In Singapore, the 2s10s swap curve rose 14bp. In Thailand, the 2s10s NDOIS curve bear-steepened 3bp; 10y swap yields rose 17bp. 3y and 10y ThaiGB yields rose 14bp and 10bp, respectively. China rates relatively outperformed. The 2s5s CGB curve bull-flattened 2bp while the CNY NDIRS curve had limited changes. In Indonesia, 1y INDOGB yields fell 3bp to 6.35%.

## Latin America:

Most LatAm currencies weakened in the session. COP (-0.4%) led losses with no clear local driver, while BRL (-0.4%) fell. MXN was largely flat and CLP (-0.1%) ended only slightly weaker after losses of \~0.6% earlier in the session. Most LatAm rates were higher in the session. After a volatile session, TIIEs ended lower in the front-end (1y: -7bp) and the IBR curve twist-steepened (2s10s: +7bp). The CAM curve underperformed in the front-end (1y: +7bp) despite few relevant local developments.

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
