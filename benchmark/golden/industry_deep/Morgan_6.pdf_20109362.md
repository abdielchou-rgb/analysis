## Carry vs. Rates Momentum in G10 FX

• Despite a negative performance in the last two weeks, G10 carry remains among the most profitable strategies year to date with returns close to 10%. This performance questions the relation between FX and rates in G10 space. Indeed, G10 carry has maintained its year to date gains while rates were “directionally” turning against the strategy. The examples on Figure 2 and 3 are striking: (i) USD/JPY has seen several weeks of decorrelation with its USD-JPY yield differential and (ii) NZD rates are now trending lower (highest yielder in G10) while JPY rates are trending higher (lowest yielder in G10).

• In a nutshell, we can think about two simple but different cross-sectional strategies for FX based on rates market. Either one can buy/sell currencies based on yield gap (carry) or based on local rates market momentum; i.e. directional strength/following the rate trend (with for instance standardized signals on rates changes to correct for different volatilities and magnitudes). The two approaches are very different and currently involve opposite G10 FX portfolios.

• Year to date, G10 carry has been predominant due to (1) large historical yield gaps in G10 and (ii) a lower level of central bank activity. Indeed, the latter is a key component for rates trends to play out. During the 2022-2023 hiking cycle, the rates momentum currency strategy led to very consistent returns. Conceptually, rotating into long currencies at their peak hiking cycle vs. other G10s was a strong approach during this period (and independent from the yield gap, i.e. carry).

## USD weight in economic momentum framework

• Our USD growth strategy currently has a +40% weight allocated to USD. The weight last reached this level on 20-Mar, and has fluctuated between -60% and +20% since then. As a reminder, this strategy allocates a positive (negative) weight to USD if the breadth of global growth momentum screens relatively more negative (positive), in sympathy with the dollar’s anti-cyclical properties. We consider levels within the +/- 50% threshold as neutral overall.

• 7% of countries are exhibiting significantly positive growth momentum. The two countries with momentum above +1 sigma are both in Europe: EUR & GBP.

• 21% of countries are exhibiting significantly negative growth momentum. The six countries with momentum below -1 sigma are INR, RUB, CLP, USD, JPY & SGD.

• Despite the positive weight of USD in our growth strategy, we don’t consider the current level of +40% as a strong indicator. For the moment, the number of countries with growth momentum below -1 sigma is not sufficient to justify a large defensive position in the dollar. Fed pricing remains the main driver.

## FX Valuations

• USD still screens as the richest currency globally on long-term valuation metrics at +16% rich relative to the 15yr average of its REER CPI & REER PPI. The level of richness peaked on 1-Jul at +18% and has fallen steadily since then.

• EUR also screens rich on this same metric but less so at +7%. Notably, EUR now screens rich on both the PPI & CPI measures, which marks a shift from when EUR richness stemmed entirely from the PPI component.

• Aside from USD and EUR, the remaining G10 currencies screen as fair to cheap. JPY cheapness (-28%) continues to stand out in a global context. The Scandis are the next cheapest G10 currencies (NOK -9%; SEK - 8%). Meanwhile, the Antipodeans & CHF screen as about fairly valued.

• MXN now screens as the richest EM currency on long-term metrics (+15% rich), followed by CZK (+14%) and SGD (+11%).

## Inflation analytics for FX

Among the most liquid currencies, 29% are showing a recent increase in their seasonally and jump-adjusted headline inflation trends. This metric was at 55% as recently as 1-Jul and is now at is lowest level since March. The trend is calculated using the 6m/6m run rate with seasonality and jump adjustments (1wk moving avg). We look at the trajectory of the signal between the last two CPI points to qualify as an increase/decrease.

• Relatedly, 32% of countries show an increase when measuring inflation in core terms. This is the lowest level since 3-Jun.

• Our metrics indicate that inflation is still running above respective central bank targets in 7 of the G10 economies. NZD, NOK & GBP appear to be the furthest from their respective CB inflation targets (with the caveat that AUD & NZD are impacted by the quarterly frequency of CPI reports).

## Single factor’s performance

Global carry baskets only show a tiny rebound after the end of May/beginning of June collapse (+4% ytd). We don’t rule out positive returns going forward, but the risk reward for the overall factor is now unattractive due to: (i) compressed yield on the basket vs. (ii) further risk of funders repricing on Fed easing and potential lack of appetite for EM high betas through US elections. However, other signals delivered decent returns on global portfolio year to date such as growth momentum (using J.P. Morgan FRIs) or long term price momentum.

• For EM portfolios, carry remains weaker with other signals delivering stronger returns between 5% to 10% year to date: commodity momentum (via ToT), long term value (REER based) or persistence in FDI and equity flows.

• It is worth noticing that strategies based on the relative performance of local equity markets are still posting a significant drawdown. As discussed in last year outlook (other signals) , this is mainly due to a higher level of central bank activity in the post Covid era (and very different from the local equity-FX relation of the pre-covid era).

## Fast T.E.A.M. allocation

In 2022, we introduced our fast T.E.A.M strategy (see A faster TEAM\*). Since then, several modifications of the strategy have been implemented. Notably, the current account balance metric has been replaced by our commodity terms of trade signal and we added a portfolio optimization on top of the signal. The key takeaways from the last T.E.A.M allocation are the following:

• Our T.E.A.M. strategy maintains large positions in Asia high yielding currencies such as INR and IDR. However, the long weight in Asia has been reduced as more LatAm currencies are re-integrated in the top ranks; albeit in limited size post optimization. Interestingly, EUR is the 4<sup>th</sup> largest weight in the long portfolio.

• The short basket is still very concentrated in CHF, CNY and NOK.

## Optimization details:

• Maximizes portfolio composite multi signal score under minimum variance constraint.

• Correlation matrix uses currency TWIs to better assess USD in the cross section. Window is long term (5Y) to keep historical correlations of currencies during volatility events.

• Currency weight cannot exceed +/-20%. Maximum regional exposure constraints are also implemented.

## Carry screener

Table 1 in this section is the result of research of highest carry differentials in FX across all possible crosses. We then apply multiple filters to find the most attractive carry opportunities with a limited beta to equity markets. To implement a trade, we recommend checking the table recommendations against our analysts’ views and entering both legs of a potential trade vs. USD or EUR for unconventional crosses.

The research process is the following:

• Compute nominal yield differentials for all crosses (1-month forward on 27 currencies) and filter for pairs with carry above 4%.

• Second filter for real yield core differential to remove significantly negative real carry positions.

• Discretionary filtering is then applied to beta to equity and other columns to identify most relevant trade opportunities.

• The 3M spot entry point column checks the entry level of the long high yield vs short low yield currency pairs. For instance, if the column indicates 1 for BRL/JPY, it means that BRL/JPY spot is at its highest level in three months. If the column indicates 0, it mean BRL/JPY is at its lowest level in three months.

• Seasonality since 2010: hit ratio and P&L for the 4 weeks ahead.
