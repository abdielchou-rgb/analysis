# Hawkish Rate Hike: July MPM Review　After Gov. Ueda's Press Conference

## Takeshi Yamaguchi, Masayuki Inu

## BoJ’s rate hike to 0.25% and its JGB purchase reduction plan were mostly　in line with our prior forecast

July rate hike as we expected: The Policy Board of the Bank of Japan decided, by a 7-2 majority vote (Mr. Nakamura and Mr. Noguchi), to set the guideline that the Bank will encourage the uncollateralized overnight call rate to remain at “around 0.25 percent” by changing from “around 0.1 percent.” A small number of market economists expected a July rate hike, but the results turned out to be in line with our out-of-consensus call (BoJ Watch: July MPM Preview: Left to Gov. Ueda's Decision (22 Jul 2024)).

The BoJ gave these main reasons for a July rate hike: (1) Japan's economic activity and prices have been developing generally in line with the Bank's outlook and moves to raise wages have been spreading; and (2) the year-on-year rate of change in import prices has turned positive again, and upside risks to prices require attention. The first reason was no surprise from the perspective of economists, as we discussed in our MPM preview. On the other hand, the second point was interesting to examine in terms of how FX matters. In contrast to some pre-MPM media reports, the BoJ stated that “private consumption has been resilient despite the impact of price rises and other factors,” indicating that the BoJ kept its constructive views on consumption.

Reduction in the pace of JGB purchases was also mostly in line with our forecast: Regarding the reduction of its purchase amount of JGBs, the Bank decided on a plan to reduce the amount of its monthly outright purchases of JGBs so that it will be about JPY3 trillion in Jan-Mar 2026, from the current amount of JPY5.7 trillion. The amount will be cut by about JPY400 billion each calendar quarter in principle. We think the pace of the reduction is mostly in line with the market's expectations, thereby no major surprises.

We think the economic impact of this rate hike to 0.25% will likely be negligible: Inflation expectations among firms are currently located at slightly above 2%, and those of households are also rising to somewhere at 1.0-1.5%, in our view. They indicate that exante real interest rates, calculated as nominal rates minus expected inflation, remain deeply negative. Ex-post real interest rates are even lower, with the headline inflation excluding imputed rents remaining above 3%. Meanwhile, we think real neutral rate levels are at least around 0%, which implies that current levels of real interest rates are much lower than the neutral rate. We think the BoJ has a similar view.

## BoJ mentions the impact of FX on inflation in the Basic View

More explicit expression about FX impacts on prices, possibility of further findings of the Bank's internal analysis: In the Bank's view (front page) of July Outlook report, the Bank added a comment "In particular, with firms' behavior shifting more toward raising wages and prices recently, exchange rate developments are, compared to the past, more likely to affect prices," which was not included in the previous Outlook report.

Similar expression was also added in the last paragraph of section 3. B. "Risks to Prices." This addition indicates that the Bank more explicitly shows its concern about effects of FX on prices than that in the previous outlook report, because the Bank had used the more moderate expression "how these factors will affect Japan's prices requires due attention" at that time.

Given that the Bank used a more explicit expression on FX and prices in the Outlook report, we think that the Bank's internal policy analysis could have already found further empirical results indicating FX impacts on prices intensifying.　This point could also relate to the Bank's emphasis on upside risk on prices both for fiscal 2024 and 2025 in the July Outlook report.

## Shifted to hawkish stance in the Gov Ueda's press conference

Hawkish press conference: Gov. Ueda's press conference after the July MPM included more hawkish comments than expected, whereas his stance had tilted toward the dovish side in past press conferences. The key comments were as follows.

Various comments suggesting future rate hikes, implying that Monetary Affairs department could more actively discuss possibility of further rate hikes with the BoJ policy board: In the press conference, Gov. Ueda stated “If the economy and prices move in line with our projection, we will adjust a degree of monetary easing by raising policy rate” and also answered “we will continue to raise interest rate” and “0.5% is not the ceiling for rate hikes” when Gov. Ueda was asked about his thoughts on the policy rate path.

Gov. Ueda stated that the economic impact of this rate hike would not slow down the economy on the back of continued growth in wages and prices. Also, he pointed out that from a long-term perspective, we can avoid the risk of having to make drastic rate hikes in a short period of time, by raising rates from very low levels and adjusting the degree of monetary easing gradually. In this sense, he suggested possible net positive impacts arising from this BoJ’s policy revision.　This implies a major shift of Gov. Ueda's policy stance from his previous comment that "the cost of waiting is not high" in April 2023.

These Gov’s Ueda’s comments seem to imply that the Monetary Affairs department could more more actively discussing the possibility of further rate hikes with the BoJ policy board.

Limited impact on the economy of rate hike at a level much lower than neutral interest rates, internal policy simulations on rate hike paths may be getting more active: As neutral rates, Gov Ueda indicated that his understanding on high uncertainty in its estimate has not changed, but the bank needs to work on the issue of at what level of interest rate the rate hike will stop when it actually reaches the level near the neutral rate. These responses suggest that internal rate-hike path simulations may be getting more active. It should be noted that the effect of the rate hike on the economy is limited, as “the current situation is an adjustment of the degree of monetary easing at the level of interest rate considerably lower than the neutral interest rate.

Confirmation of sustained wage growth would be a key ahead, thereby implying the importance of Rengo’s wage hike target for the 2025 spring wage negotiations, which will be announced this fall: When Gov. Ueda was asked about the rate hike path and the impact on the economy, he repeatedly stressed an expression of “on the back of the sustained wages growth.” In the May Monthly Labour Survey, scheduled cash earnings accelerated from 2.2% YoY in April to 2.8% YoY, and we think that the Bank expects nominal wage growth to rise above 3% YoY ahead, reflecting strong Shunto outcomes. Gov Ueda’s comments imply that the Bank’s internal policy discussion may assume sustained and strong wage growth in the medium and long term. Thus, important data points would include (1) sustained nominal wage growth on a common-sample basis, (2) whether real wages maintain positive growth once turning positive, and (3) Rengo’s wage hike target for the 2025 spring wage negotiations (its basic policy for 2024 Spring wage negotiations was released on October 19th last year)

## Hawkish tone in the "future conduct of monetary policy" section

That said, the tone in the future conduct of monetary section looks somewhat more hawkish than before:

"As for the future conduct of monetary policy, while it will depend on developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions going forward, given that real interest rates are at significantly low levels, if the outlook for economic activity and prices presented in the July Outlook Report will be realized, the Bank will accordingly continue to raise the policy interest rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation. With the price stability target of 2 percent, it will conduct monetary policy as appropriate, in response to developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions, from the perspective of sustainable and stable achievement of the target".(emphasis is ours)

We agree that real interest rates are at significantly low levels, but combined with the wording that the Bank will "continue to raise the policy interest rate" in the section on the future conduct of monetary policy, we think they indicate BoJ's more hawkish stance. We think it may raise the risk of the BoJ moving early ahead.

In the previous paragraph, the Bank added that "real interest rates are expected to remain significantly negative after the change in the policy interest rate, and accommodative financial conditions will continue to firmly support economic activity." That said, the Bank removed the expression that "it anticipates that accommodative financial conditions will be maintained for the time being," which was included in the previous Outlook Report in April.

(Previous Outlook report in April 2024) "As for the conduct of monetary policy, it will depend on future developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions. Uncertainties surrounding these economic and financial developments at home and abroad remain high. If the aforementioned outlook for economic activity and prices will be realized and underlying inflation will increase, the Bank will adjust the degree of monetary accommodation, while it anticipates that accommodative financial conditions will be maintained for the time being. With the price stability target of 2%, the Bank will conduct monetary policy as appropriate, in response to developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions, from the perspective of sustainable and stable achievement of the target." (Note: bolded emphasis above is ours)

## How the results affect our BoJ outlook

We would like to maintain our existing BoJ outlook at this moment, though we may adjust our forecast depending on communications from the BoJ including speeches by Gov. Ueda or Deputy Gov. Uchida (not confirmed yet). As our base case, we expect the BoJ to raise the policy rate to around 0.50% in Jan 2025 and remain on-hold for the remainder of 2025. Our existing risk scenario has been the BoJ raising the policy rate further to around 0.75% in July 2025 in the case of Japan’s inflation remaining elevated. Our base-case forecast of the policy rate remaining at around 0.50% at end-2025 is based on our price outlook in which we expect inflation to stabilize from mid-2025 under our FX team’s view of appreciation of the yen (USDJPY at 146 at end-2024 and at 136 at end-2025).

That said, we now see new risk of the BoJ bringing forward the timing of the next additional rate hike from Jan 2025 to Dec 2024 or even Oct 2024 when the BoJ publishes the next Outlook Report. Asked about the possibility of a rate hike within this year, Gov. Ueda said in the press conference that “it will depend on data. If they remain on track with or exceed our existing outlook, I think it means that a further adjustment is possible”. In this risk scenario, policy rate levels at end-2025 could reach around 0.75%- 1.0% in the case of higher inflation.

We would like to emphasize that a rate hike in Oct 2024 is currently our risk scenario, not our base-case forecast, as we think there are several hurdles. For example, (1) there are high uncertainties at the time of the BoJ’s Oct MPMs (30-31 Oct) such as the US presidential election and Japan’s political schedule. (2) If the current yen appreciation continues, upside risks to prices from import prices (“the first component”), one of the reasons behind the July rate hike, may stabilize by then. Meanwhile, it may take additional time to confirm that “the second component” (or the underlying inflation) is rising further, given our estimate that the weighted median CPI from the Tokyo preliminary July CPI (released 26 July) slowed again. (3) BoJ may not be able to accumulate enough data to gauge the impact of the July rate hike.

## Koichi Sugisaki, Hiromu Uezato, Hiroki Yagi

## JPY Rates: Sticking with our flattening view

The BoJ announced its first full-fledged rate hike in around 18 years on July 31. Such a move had only been around 30-40% priced in, so the surprising decision ended up having quite a significant market impact.

The central bank continues to focus its spotlight on wage hike trends (including for smaller businesses) and fundamentals (such as services inflation), but some market participants appear to view the JPY FX as the most important input to its policy response function.

The basic expectation was seemingly that while the BoJ would eventually find itself forced into a hawkish rate hike aimed at curbing downward pressure on the currency (see Exhibit 5 ), it had no particular need to move in July given that MoF intervention has recently served to arrest the JPY’s slide (see Exhibit 6 ).

Markets are liable to sense a coordinated effort to shore up the JPY, given that the BoJ rate hike follows so soon in the wake of MoF intervention, as well as expressions of concern about the currency’s weakness from a number of government officials.

The quarterly “Outlook for Economic Activity and Prices” update saw an increase in the number of policy board members characterizing risks to FY2025 inflation as skewed to the upside, while the official statement declared that “upside risks to prices require attention”, given that import costs are now rising year-on-year once again.

Moreover, at the press conference, BoJ governor Ueda acknowledged that upside risk for prices due to higher import prices was one of the reason they hiked the policy rate now, although he said that this was not the main reason.

We thus see potential for a near-term repricing at the short end of the curve reflecting expectations of a more hawkish BoJ rate hike trajectory. BoJ also hinted at further rate hikes in their statement.

“Given that real interest rates are at significantly low levels, if the outlook for economic activity and prices presented in the July Outlook Report will be realized, the Bank will accordingly continue to raise the policy rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation”.

Ueda also reconfirmed the point that the current policy rate remains low vs the estimated range of a neutral rate.

Private consumption, meanwhile, continues to be depressed by rising prices (with the notable exception of spending by visitors from abroad) (see Exhibit 7 ). A number of media reports claimed that some within the BoJ had wanted to hold off a little longer on a rate hike for that reason. However, the Outlook Report ended up sticking with an assessment that “although private consumption is expected to be affected by the price rises, it is projected to increase moderately, mainly reflecting the rise in wage growth”.

That said, this weakness in household spending could lead markets to speculate that the BoJ’s rate hike has been motivated primarily by the JPY’s weakness and, as such, might ultimately prove to have been a policy mistake.

Our economists expect the Fed to kick off a rate cut cycle from September given that the US labor market and inflation are now showing clear signs of cooling, and the US stock market has been in correction mode of late with the high-tech sector faring particularly poorly.

This combination of a deterioration in overseas conditions and (perhaps ill-advised?) BoJ hawkishness are liable to generate downward pressure on the term premiums priced into super-long JGBs, and we also expect this part of the curve to be supported by demand for carry heading into the summer vacation period.

As we discussed at some length in "Japan | Preparing For A Summer Rally", we expect life insurers to ramp up their purchases of super-long JGBs going forward. The BoJ, meanwhile, is set to leave its purchases of super-long JGBs unchanged for the time being (see below). Taking all of this into consideration, we suggest sticking with 5s20s JGB flatteners.

## QT plan: Dovish pace

The BoJ has also delivered its promised “detailed plan” for quantitative tightening (QT). Monthly purchases of JGBs are to be cut back to around JPY3 trillion within two years, in line with the market consensus, but the stipulated pace of reduction (JPY400 billion per quarter) is perhaps somewhat more gradual (or “dovish”) than many had envisaged (see Exhibit 8 ), which might help to explain why JGB futures and USD/JPY initially surged on the news.

## FX: Further unwinding could be possible

As discussed above, markets are liable to sense a coordinated effort to shore up the JPY, given that the BoJ rate hike follows so soon in the wake of MoF intervention, as well as expressions of concern about the currency’s weakness from a number of government officials (including newly appointed Vice Finance Minister for International Affairs Atsushi Mimura).

Our US economists, meanwhile, expect the Fed to start laying the groundwork for a September rate cut at the July FOMC meeting, amid continuing softness in labor market and inflation data. Our US rates colleagues thus see scope for a deeper rate cut trajectory to be priced into the US rates market. Media reports have also indicated a much closer

race in the November presidential election now that the Democrats have opted to back Vice President Kamala Harris as their candidate.

As we discussed in "JPY | This Time May Be Different", the Fed’s professed reluctance to cut, coupled with expectations of massive fiscal stimulus under a possible Republican administration, had appeared to keep JPY carry trades in vogue even as a deeper rate cut trajectory started to be priced in.

But we are now beginning to see stronger headwinds on both sides of the Pacific as various assumptions seemingly need to be recalibrated, which might change market sentiment.

USD/JPY implied volatility has risen sharply since the MoF’s mid-July JPY-buying interventions (JPY 5.5tn in total), while USD/JPY carry has plunged as deeper Fed rate cuts have been priced in (see Exhibit 9 ). The net upshot has been a dramatic erosion of volatility-adjusted USD/JPY carry and, according to CFTC data, a massive unwinding of speculative JPY-short positions (see Exhibit 10 ).

Carry-minded investors who had been looking for volatility to decline into the summer vacation period have basically now had the rug pulled out from under them. We are thus mindful of the possibility that USD/JPY, which is still almost 7 yen above its “fair value” suggested by the market-implied Fed rate cut trajectory, could continue correcting towards 143 in the near term (see Exhibit 11 ).

## Definition of terms

Buy/Long: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be positive over the relevant time period.

Sell/Short: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be negative over the relevant time period.

Selling protection or Buying Risk: The analyst expects that the price of protection against the event occurring will decrease over the relevant time period.

# Japan Equity Investment Strategy: BoJ pivot begins

Sho Nakazawa, Ayako Tanaka

BoJ decided to adjust easing stance in July meeting, in line with our macro team’s forecast: The BoJ decided at its July 30-31 monetary policy meeting to guide its policy rate to around 0.25% (previously: 0-0.1%) and indicated that it would lower its JGB purchasing in stages from the present ¥6trn to around ¥3trn by end-FY25. As discussed in BoJ Watch: July MPM Preview: Left to Gov. Ueda's Decision (July 22, 2024), we cannot rule out that the government and BoJ have joined hands to bring about a correction in the slumping yen. Atsushi Mimura, MoF’s vice minister for international affairs, told Bloomberg today that the demerits of the weak yen were outweighing the advantages, citing the impact of higher import prices on households, and that forex issues have become a matter of keen concern for citizens. Outwardly, it almost seems as if the BoJ raised rates in response to government demands to take action on the yen.

Real rates remain negative; still overweight on Japanese vs. Asian EM shares (Topix target for end-June 2025: 3,200, bull case 3,700): A key point for the stock markets is that real rates remain in negative territory even with the BoJ’s hike in the short-term rate target to “around 0.25%”. That is, BoJ monetary policy remains accommodative. Some media sources characterized today’s move as the start of a quantitative tightening (QT), but the bank simply decided to pare back its JGB purchasing. It is not changing course and actually selling off its bond-holdings. Rather, it has done little more than adjust the degree of its monetary easing in the sense that it is continuing its massive buying of JGBs. It would be misleading to say that it is laying the grounds for a QT policy, in our view.

However, we would caution on the possibility that rising expectations over the BoJ conducting additional rate hikes may lead to intermittent share price declines: Regarding its monetary policy stance ahead, the BoJ indicated that if the economy and inflation outlook outlined in the Outlook Report is realized, it will continue to raise policy interest rates and adjust the degree of monetary easing. This is similar to what was outlined in the April Outlook Report, but in the July Outlook Report, the mention about how the BoJ believes that an accommodative financial environment is expected continue for the time being has been deleted in the discussion about monetary policy management.

The July 31 BoJ rate hike was implemented amid lackluster wage data recently. At the press conference, Gov Ueda seemed to stress that gradual rate hikes would reduce the cost of rapid policy adjustments. All of these appear to suggest a retreat in the BoJ’s accommodative stance (i.e. BoJ pivot). If the data accumulated ahead are in line with the BoJ’s outlook, we should note that the prospects of additional rate hikes as the quarterly Outlook Report meeting approaches could weigh on share prices in the equity market.

Sluggish consumption: Focus on domestic-demand driven sectors like retail, food and services makes sense in light of yen’s correction, but hopes of a consumption-led boost in the earnings of these domestic-oriented sectors may be premature. One reason that a July rate hike was not the consensus expectation is that consumption has now fallen for four successive quarters. However, cost-push inflation stemming from the weak yen is seen as a factor behind the weakness in spending. The BoJ may have worried that a failure to boost rates this month might have sparked a renewed upswing in the USD/JPY, aggravating forex-related inflation and pushing spending down further. The bank appears to believe that recent wage hikes and flat cut in personal income taxes will prevent an outright collapse in spending, but we anticipate only a slow comeback given the sustained uptrend in prices confronting households. Even if real wages turn positive, it could take time before household sentiment turns around and consumption begins to grow. Consequently, while domestic-oriented sectors like retail, foods and services are logically attracting attention as the yen strengthens, it may be premature to assume that increased consumption spending will translate to higher earnings in these areas.

Capex remains robust: Capex growth should spur interest in related shares. The sluggishness in consumption stands in contrast to the bullish outlook for capex. We believe after the release of the preliminary GDP estimates for 2Q 2024 on August 15 that private capex will surpass the previous record high of 1Q 1991 ( Exhibit 13 ). An expansion in capex should lead to a solid rise in nominal GDP, underpinning corporate EPS growth ( Exhibit 14 ). Machinery orders and construction starts, considered leading indicators for capex, are both performing strongly ( Exhibit 15 ).

We believe the rise in capex is being supported by increasing domestic and foreign demand; labor-saving investment in the face of the severe worker shortage; informationrelated investment for digitization needs; construction of distribution facilities in connection with online shopping; construction related to redevelopment of offices and commercial facilities; and semiconductor spending aimed at fortifying the supply chain.
