# Biweekly Perspectives: Fed Pivot Playbook For Asia/EM Equities In 2024

The market is pricing in a front-loaded and steep Fed pivot in 2024, but its timing and pace are unclear amid sticky service inflation. Here we study market performance around the eight Fed pivots since the 1970s to help investors navigate trading patterns.

QUANTWISE 图

## Key Takeaways

■ History suggests market performance before and after a Fed pivot is contingent upon whether it aligns with a hard or soft landing scenario.

■ In a hard landing, Defensive Value continues outperforming even amid a Fed pivot.

■ In contrast, during a soft landing, the rotation between Value and Growth has been relatively modest in the early stages.

Factor behavior in equity markets implies a soft landing scenario, but fixed income markets seem to expect a hard landing.

– We expect a soft landing and see US dollar and UST yields rising in near term. We remain neutral on Value vs. Growth, and prefer Quality.

Our Fed Pivot Playbook: We identify eight Fed pivot scenarios since the 1970s and conduct a detailed study of market and style performance vs. the corresponding macro environment. We found that: (i) UST 10Y yield showed a consistent downward trend prior to previous Fed pivots, with an average decline of 80bps; (ii) If the Fed initiates a pivot when the policy rate is >5%, rotation from Value to Growth is likely to occur at a later stage, (iii) Historically, Quality and Large Cap stocks performed the best with high consistency before and after Fed pivots.

Latest Market Dynamics & Our Thoughts: There appears to be a disconnect between equity and fixed income markets now - with rates and FX traders expecting six Fed rate cuts in 2024, with outstanding short positions in the US dollar the largest since 2020. These are typical price actions if investors assume a hard landing scenario ahead. In contrast, equity price actions have not responded much to the recent moderations of US dollar and UST bond yields. There is also no clear sign of rotation from Value to Growth, and Momentum stocks with leadership in price return and consensus revisions continuing to offer more alpha. Such market behavior is typically seen amid a soft landing scenario, which is in line with our house view and style recommendation.

## Fed Pivots Playbook - A Study Back to the 1970s

Over the past 50 years, we have identified eight Fed pivots on rates, which we have studied to understand market trading patterns and factor implications before and after. It is interesting to note that the implications depended on whether the pivot was done in a hard or soft landing scenario in the US.

In this section, we look at Morgan Stanley's quant database stretching back to 1971 to summarize the historical factor performance and discuss how these factors correlated with macro data, e.g. US dollar, UST bond yields. Our goal is to explore where we are now, and where the market is likely to move in 2024.

## Market Beta Performance Amid Soft vs. Hard Landing Scenarios

Our analysis found that the first step is to divide these historical pivot scenarios by the economic outlook - soft vs. hard landing. Exhibits 4 & 5 show the different market environments amid soft and hard landing scenarios. Here we show the performance of "Equity Beta" factors before and after the Fed pivots, after fully hedging out sector/ style exposure.

## The key messages here are:

(i) In a hard landing scenario ( Exhibit 3 ), if a Fed pivot was done to save the economy or equity market amid a systematic risk event, the market tends to keep declining even after the pivot. That means the early stage of a Fed pivot did not help reverse market risk sentiment in this case. Market volatility remained elevated overall.

(ii) In a soft landing scenario ( Exhibit 4 ), if a Fed pivot was done to support economic growth amid a cyclical slowdown with inflation moderation, then markets tend to perform well after the pivot. Even before the pivot, the Fed's forward-looking guidance or messages also started to help restore market risk sentiment. Market volatility was more stable than in a hard landing scenario.

## Implications for UST Bond Yields

Historically, the changes in UST bond yields have been a good indicator of the Value vs. Growth rotation in equity markets - as rising bond yields tend to favor Value outperforming Growth, whereas falling yields see Growth outperform Value. Such correlation has been particularly significant in the past two years amid an environment of rising bond yields globally. Thus, we firstly look at the historical trajectories of UST 10Y yields around Fed pivots.

Exhibit 7 shows the Fed pivot scenarios after 1984, which we believe are more relevant to today, as US inflation then was no longer above 10%.

Here we see that, historically, UST 10Y bond yields tended to decline three months ahead of Fed pivots, with an average decline of 80bps. If we take the current Fed Fund futures implied rate cut timing - March 2024 - into consideration, the UST 10Y yield moderation from 5% to 3.8% in 4Q23 would seem to be an overshoot. Such a trajectory only happened once previously, during the Fed pivot in 1995, where the UST 10Y yield started to decline 6 months earlier than the pivot, falling by almost 2% in total. However, we note that the Fed policy rate at 6% then, was 4% above US PCE (at 2% YoY), which compares with the current gap between policy rates and US PCE at only 2.3%.

## Implications for Value vs. Growth Rotation

Style rotation has also behaved differently around Fed pivots historically. In hard landings, Value continued outperforming Growth despite easing financial conditions. This is well explained by the defensiveness of Value and Income stocks, which helped outperformance amid a declining market.

The exception was the 2007-08 GFC - most Value stocks were well owned as they offered high Beta trades from 2005-07; therefore, when the market started to turn around, these Value stocks were unwound the most and underperformed.

In soft landings, the historical relative returns between Value and Growth were marginal. We believe this was because the Fed policy rates on average were still high in absolute terms during the early stages of the Fed's pivot (>5% in all scenarios, except 2019). Therefore, the duration effect was not yet meaningful to help Growth outperform Value. We believe that we will experience a similar situation in the upcoming Fed pivot too. Given history, we might see a muted relative performance between Value and Growth during the first four rate cuts, and any meaningful outperformance of Growth over Value might only come if the market starts to price in further rate cuts. So Growth investors will likely need to be patient during the early stages of the pivot.

## Implications for Quality Stocks

Many long-term fundamental stock pickers focus on the performance of high Quality stocks around Fed pivots. Our study shows that Quality stocks tended to outperform market before and after Fed pivots; the situation in 1974 was the only exception, as market risk sentiment was in deep capitulation, which might not be relevant to the current market situation.

Looking at the other historical situations, we note that the outperformance of Quality stocks were also accompanied by low volatility in a soft landing scenario, but more volatile in a hard landing. Given our base case scenario is a soft landing of the US economy in 2024, we believe Quality factor can keep outperforming at least in 1H24, and we reiterate that Quality exposure is our strategic preferred style for now.

## Implications for Large vs. Small Cap

2023 saw large cap outperformance in global equities as well as increasing investor debate on whether to rotate from large to small caps in 2024 or not. Here we have some interesting observations to share from market history.

In general, Small caps have tended to underperform Large caps in the global equity space after Fed pivots, particularly during soft landing scenarios. However, 2001 was an exception as Small caps outperformed after the pivot; we believe this was because of the significantly crowded large cap tech stock trade unwinding amid market capitulation. This is an extreme market dynamic of a valuation bubble bursting and therefore don't think it is wise to reference it amid the current environment

If we carve out EM from global equities and run the same analysis, we still see a similar pattern of Small Cap underperformance post Fed pivots ( Exhibit 14 ). Thus, we continue to recommend EM investors that Small caps are not a good exposure to take at the current stage, even if the Fed pivot might drive some fund flows from USD-based assets back to EM.

## Market Dynamics & Style Implications

## Latest Style Performance in Asia/EM

Relative returns of Value vs. Growth remain marginal and have been trading on reversal basis in APxJ/EM and Japan: Our factor monitor shows that APxJ/EM equities were led by Value stocks in December, and Growth stocks in January. The market expectation of Fed rate cuts and the timing of these remains unclear, resulting in a fastchanging macro environment and volatile Value vs. Growth relative performance. We note similar observations in Japan, which was also led by Growth stocks in December, but Value stocks in January. FX continued to play a big role in driving the style rotation.

"Revisions" & "Low Beta" factors led alpha from December to January MTD: The macro backdrop continues to be uncertain, making it difficult to have a preference for Value or Growth in the near term, but the alpha efficacy of "Consensus Earnings Revisions" and "Low Beta" factors remain resilient. Meanwhile, the "Quality" and "Price Momentum" factors also added steady alpha in APxJ/EM equities.

Our style preference remains neutral between Value and Growth: For investors who have been OW Value, we suggest they may look to take profits from Value stocks if UST bond yields trend higher tactically in 1Q24, and would turn neutral on Value/Growth style. For investors with a longer investment horizon of six to 12 months, we suggest buying any dip on Quality Growth to position for the early stages of the Fed pivot.

Our model continues to classify the macro backdrop as being in the early stages of financial easing. Fed fund futures markets priced in more Fed rate cuts in December and the latest futures curve implies six rate cuts by end 2024, while the Fed dot-plot only guides for three cuts. Concurrently, UST bond yields have moderated with UST 10Y yields below 4% now. These are typical expressions by investors of a potential Fed pivot, but the risk for now is whether these "Fed pivot trades" have gone too far or not.

If we refer to the 1980-90s, there is the potential for the Fed to keep policy rates 3-4% above reported core inflation for a longer period. The latest reported US inflation data remains on a disinflationary trajectory ( Exhibit 6 ), but the gap between policy rates and core inflation is only 1.5-2.5%. We believe there is potential for the market to price out some rate cuts which would see UST bond yields trend higher in the near term. Our US economist also shared a similar view and believes financial markets have run too far with the assumption that Fed cuts will be steeper and front-loaded. Neither coincident nor forward-looking data suggest that deep cuts are needed.

We reiterate our neutral stance between Value and Growth in Asia/EM, because rates and FX markets might be volatile before the Fed clarifies the extent of its pivot.

## Factor Monitor – Biweekly Update January 12, 2024

## Methodology

Factor return calculation: To evaluate historical factor performance, we rank stocks as at month-end within defined universes, and form quintile portfolios based on the underlying factor values. We go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile (i.e., long the top 20th percentile of stocks within the defined universe and short those in the bottom 20th percentile), rebalanced monthly. We then calculate the performance of the time series of this L/S market-neutral portfolio over various time horizons.

Composite formation: To calculate the composite factors, we first z-score the individual factor values at each monthly observation within the defined universe. Individual factor zscores are then averaged into composite scores for each stock on an equal-weighted basis.
