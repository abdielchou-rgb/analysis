Long and winding road: Fed pivot becomes 'higher for longer' becomes stagflation?

While no one wants to say the 'R' word anymore, risks of stagflation and a nonconsensus Fed hike have been the topics of conversation recently. The path to \~1 cut from multiple cuts fully priced in at the start of the year has been long and winding, though equity markets have largely digested the 'higher for longer' narrative. We don't think they can escape scot-free if stagflation risks actually come to fruition, though not our base case. Negative price action post-Q1 GDP, PCE, and ECI the last two weeks suggests fears brewing. In addition to any potential deterioration in labor market data, we suggested reflation/stagflation are the core risks at the margin for volatility to remain elevated. Downside hedges have been left for dead, but if the volatility reaction in early April was any indication, investors will likely look to hedge stagflation risks when they need to, not necessarily when they can. More details in Figures 3-8.

If you can't stand the heat, get out of the kitchen: Market Cookbook suggests a strong Defensives vs. Cyclicals bias for a stagflation scenario

We last updated our Market Cookbook framework for a 'higher for longer' scenario which has since played out. Both the US Energy vs. Utilities and EU vs. US Banks themes have run, but we think a stagflation scenario could derail a lot of the 'higher for longer' beneficiaries here and now. We update the model for a stagflation scenario ahead of a slew of key macro data in early May – and the results are clear (and perhaps unsurprising): Strong tilt towards Defensives and hard commodity-centrics over Cyclicals in both the US and EU. More details in Figures 1-2 and appendix<sub>.</sub>

Sector/industry-wise, the model suggests:

 Long put candidates (US): Consumer Discretionary, Financials, Materials

 Short call candidates (US): Utilities, Consumer Staples, Retail, Banks

 Long put candidates (EU): Banks, Industrials

 Long call candidates (EU): Utilities, Resources

What's Mine is yours: Miners vs. Miners put switch an attractive pair trade to hedge against stagflation risks

Cyclicals outperformance appears stretched. We remain bullish on gold and Gold Miners (here, here). Off the back of our Market Cookbook output, Gold Miners (GDX) vs. Metals & Mining (XME) screens as an attractive relative value pair geared to economic cyclicality and gold 'alpha.' The former has picked up steam as reflation concerns have come back into the narrative, while the latter could be at risk from a stagflation-driven cyclical downturn (again, not our base case). Relative to Metals & Mining, Gold Miners are seeing stronger earnings momentum, trade at a roughly equivalent valuation multiple, and exhibit a larger relative volatility premium vs. history. Gold Miners are the only candidate in the 'buy calls' quadrant, though we would alternatively favor selling puts in GDX to fund XME puts in this context – investors may consider XME/GDX Jul put switches sized for flat premium. More details in Figures 9-18
