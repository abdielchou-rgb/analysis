# Asia FX: US Exceptionalism fading but not reversing, yet

## Jumping back into Asia FX after the squeeze, playing laggards and regional differentiation

• USDAsia has re-aligned with short-term valuations as markets squeezed out cheapness in low-yielding funders and Trump premia faded.

Differentiation remains across the region, with high yielders lagging on expectations of deeper easing cycles, while TWD and MYR are cheap and rich, respectively.

• Flows could drive another leg lower in USDAsia, with rising Asian investor hedge ratios in North Asia and exporter conversions in ASEAN a key risk.

Fading US exceptionalism means a shallower 3-5% valley of the USD smile, but structural issues in the Eurasian continent preclude a deeper valley and 10% + selloff. Structural issues in China preclude Asian exceptionalism and should keep the RMB underperforming.

The TWD and PHP are our top picks to play catch up, particularly versus SGD and THB which may underperform going forward. IDR will remain high beta, swinging between monetary and fiscal policy cycles.

The INR may underperform given its defensive characteristics, while the MYR has a long way to go if Malaysia can deliver on prudent but unpopular fiscal reforms.

## US Exceptionalism fading

USDAsia’s rich valuations have re-aligned: Asia FX has traded cheap relative to rate differentials this year, with our model misalignment hitting +1.5z mid Jun as rates began to respond to softer US data. As we highlighted in late Jul, markets have often mean reverted from the +/-1.5z threshold, barring some sort of regime shift. Cheapness in low yielding funders had been driven by positioning, with cyclical carry not working as well in the rest of EM, but markets leaning on defensive carry as the USD continued to deliver returns vs the JPY. Last week’s CFTC data confirmed that FX traders indeed got carried away, with a 5y Z-Score of USD longs vs low beta (ie JPY and CHF) collapsing to neutral after hovering between +2 to +3z for most of 2Q. USDAsia currently looks much fairer to us at +0.3z, with the misalignment correcting meaningfully after hitting an extreme of +1.8z in late Jul, aided by a sharp move in the US front. This suggests that the easy part of the move may be done, and fading US exceptionalism - as proxied by rate differentials - is somewhat in the price. Having managed to avoid most of the squeeze in funders before, we jump back and re-engage with Asia FX.

Differentiation has emerged across the region, having been broadly cheap before: Not all USD are the same, as the USDLatam and USDAsia clusters have de-correlated in the past month, largely driven by the unwind of carry trades. Within Asia, high yielders have lagged the move, while cyclical low yielders have mostly caught up to fair value. Across Asia, the HY cluster - INR, PHP and IDR - lags the most, while amongst low yielders, TWD and JPY continue to trail, contrasting MYR which has flipped into the richer side of tactical valuations. With domestic inflation dynamics remaining mostly benign in Asia, the FX rally is opening up degrees of freedom for policy makers who had to focus on external stability at the expense of domestic financial conditions. This potentially explains why the HY cluster is lagging, with Philippines, Indonesia and India offering some of the highest real yields in the region, which could translate into the deepest easing cycles in the coming months. With rate cutting cycles now priced in the cyclical LY cluster, however, this suggests to us that any attempts to fade the recent USD selloff on a resurgence of US exceptionalism are best expressed in the rate sensitive low yielders like MYR, THB or KRW.

Flows could drive another leg lower in USDAsia. Further outperformance of Asia FX could come from several channels. On the left tail, a deeper slowdown in the US or a non-linear deterioration in its labour market likely motivate a deeper Fed cutting cycle. Markets would likely associate this with increasing growth risks, however, which may be an offsetting factor that swings FX towards the left tail of the USD smile. Asia FX could continue to be more resilient in such a scenario, having lost much of its cyclical beta this cycle while retaining its rate sensitivity. Asia’s turn for significant outperformance would likely need an associated selloff in energy prices, with the initial selloff potentially being more shallower amid an already soft non-tech export cycle and less FX sensitive portfolio inflows that could be reversed. Such a scenario would likely drive the THB to outperform, particularly with tailwinds from gold and seasonal strength typical into year end. As of right now, however, this is not our base case, and we focus instead on risk positive drivers of USDAsia lower.

Barring a recession, another leg lower in USDAsia would likely require significant flows, with two potential sources in focus. Firstly, local real money investors (particularly in North Asia), have been running lower hedge ratios with their US fixed income investments, owing to an inverted US yield curve that offers no term premia, and wide rate differentials in the front end. From an Asian perspective, the strategy of running FX hedged investments in US duration still offers no pick up, but the spread has been narrowing in the past year. With rate cuts likely reducing hedging costs and returning term premia to the US curve, Asian real money are likely to raise their hedge ratios over time, particularly if downward momentum in the USD picks up as rate differentials start to narrow. Within Asia, the TWD (and TWD implied yields) could likely see the biggest shifts due to this.

Secondly, exporters in the region have been holding on to their USD holdings this cycle. This dynamic has been motivated by rate differentials sometimes for carry, and sometimes in anticipation of USD denominated debt coming due. This presents another segment of local market participants that could be motivated to sell down their USD if firmer momentum erodes narrower rate differentials. We find these dynamics to be relevant across the region, but most acute in China, Malaysia and Indonesia, with FX deposits of 2-4% GDP in the mainland, and excess FX holdings at 2.8% and 1.6% GDP in the latter two, respectively.

Chinese corporates have unsold USD in the past few years of up to \~\$300-600bn, but have yet to sell at scale despite narrower rate differentials, as geopolitical concerns have left them patient for a little longer. Thus far, USD selling flows from corporates have been more measured than expected and their sentiment tends to be less reactive than that of financial investors. In fact, the latest FX conversion data from SAFE suggests that Chinese corporates net bought \$45bn dollars in last months reversal, the largest monthly buying since 2015. The net FX settlement ratio stayed around multi-year lows (Figure 3) as a result. Aug data due mid-Sep will provide more insight into sentiment shifts, but we are mindful that China’s higher exposure to tariff risks may mean corporate dollar selling may not accelerate meaningfully until the dust settles on the US election.

In Malaysia and Indonesia, this dynamic of FX hoarding has a flip side that’s required greater FX intervention from central banks. With FX reserves now looking low, the need to re-build reserves could thus serve as a limiting factor for any rally, effectively transferring FX from private sector to public sector. They key here will be the price at which policy makers want this to happen, with optics around longer term currency valuations having been topical in both places. Its also worth noting that Malaysia and Indonesia also have structural factors - around market psychology and origin of market participants - that may mitigate some of the cyclical turn in in rate differentials.

Regionally, we think ASEAN could be a greater beneficiary from this dynamic, as North Asian economies tend to be demographically older with excess savings and higher production costs. The build up of FX in the likes of China, Taiwan and South Korea could thus be released by outbound FDI into belt and road initiative countries, semiconductor fabrication plans in Germany or Japan, and Auto manufacturing plans in ASEAN, respectively. Still, timing plays a role in all these countries, as exporters have historically sold more USD into the fiscal year end. Our analysis suggests this seasonality tends to present most strongly in Oct, though this year may be different given the sharp moves of Aug.

## China’s structural issues preclude Asia’s exceptionalism

Can USDAsia smile? As the world’s dominant funding currency, the USD cycle is not always about the US business cycle. While the narrative of de-dollarization has been growing, USD money markets remain dominant as a conduit for funding and financial conditions, supporting a deep and liquid pool of assets for a variety of investors and borrowers alike. US exceptionalism drawing capital from the world has swung FX markets to the right tail of the USD smile, while the recessionary left tail is more often associated with a flight to quality USD bid as liabilities are covered - particularly against cyclical currencies.

A key question for for Asia FX is whether the happy medium or valley of the USD smile is deep enough for a sustained move lower, with the latter often requiring growth differentials in favour of the rest of the world. Considering the USD’s longer term cycle and deviation from Fed policy cycles over the last two and a half decades, 2004-14 stands out as a period of dislocation between the two. Whether owing to China’s emergence into the global economy or an attendant commodities super cycle as a source of USD supply to emerging markets, the broad USD traded significantly weaker than the Fed cycle would imply for most of the decade - buried deep in the valley of the USD smile.

After the shale revolution in 2014-15, however, the USD has continued to strengthen on the US’s shifting relationship with energy, coinciding with structural growth concerns in Europe and China to drive US exceptionalism that has intensified until recently. US growth is running flat to the average of the two, with history suggesting a simplistic threshold of 2-3% against the US being necessary for the USD to underperform the Fed’s policy cycle. The combination of rate normalization state side and a re-balancing of growth differentials could drive a 10%+ move lower in the broad USD, which would already be a shallower valley than the 2004-14 cycle. The more likely continuation of structural issues in the Eurasian continent, however, likely means we remain on the RHS slope of the USD smile, with historical sensitivities suggesting a 3-5% USD selloff with normalization of Fed policy to a 3% terminal rate.

## EMAX differentiation remains a theme for FX

EMAX laggards may have reasons to catch up, but some are happy to stay put: Amongst the laggards in EM Asia, we have maintained a more constructive view on INR and PHP given the current account improvements and their domestic orientation. For now, India’s defensive characteristics are likely to see it underperform the rally, making the PHP our top pick to play catch up amongst the high yielders. Amongst the low yielders, while we have been tactically cautious on TWD before, we turn bullish with the dividend season largely passing and valuations looking cheap after the rally.

The Philippines current account continues to improve, both due to softer cyclical investment and the seasonal return of remittances, which could see an uptick from Aug to Sep. A less interventionist central bank and a more adequate FX reserve position also argue for PHP outperformance in an environment where the USD remains benign.

Having closed out our PHP longs (vs TWD) before to avoid a squeeze in funding, we reinitiate PHP longs vs THB to tactically play catch up to the rest of the Asia FX complex, with the THB having been a major outperformer and the Bank of Thailand beginning to pivot in a dovish fashion.

Seasonals and the RBI leave us sidelined in India. Into year end, India’s trade deficit is likely to widen with Diwali festivities and wedding season gold imports, making carry less ideal despite improved fundamentals. Seasonal patterns suggest late 4Q is the ideal time to return to carry in India, given the natural improvement of the current account into the fiscal year end (ie 1Q), which may also see USDAsia positive risks around the US elections, bringing defensive carry back into vogue.

Indonesia’s re-rating story could come back into focus, but fiscal is a concern. A decade of sound policy making has anchored prices and structurally improved Indonesia’s trade balance. Prudent fiscal policy has saved gains and further reduced Indonesia’s reliance on foreign financing, but the associated compression of risk premia has left little for investors wanting to participate in a potential re-rating story. With core rates coming lower, markets could re-focus on Indonesia’s positives, which offer one of the highest real rates in Asia alongside manageable supply dynamics given fiscal improvements. The risk for IDR remains concerns around potential fiscal expansion under the new president, as well as a need to rebuild FX reserves at the central bank, which may limit the rally down the road. If BI maintains its cautious approach to easing and awaits further FX momentum before buying USD, the IDR could continue to do well, leaving fiscal deterioration a trade for 2025 instead.

Valuation and rate differentials argue for TWD strength. Rate differentials have been the one factor model for TWD, motivating both local real money and foreign equity investors to shift their hedge ratios, while driving retail outflows via Taiwan listed ETFs. Sharp outflows from equities in summer have passed, and the TWSE dividend season will be maturing in the coming weeks. Valuations look cheap relative to the rest of Asia, but also by the CBC’s preferred metric, relative to the NEER’s 3y MA.

This suggests that the TWD has room to play catch up, and policy support is likely to prevent further underperformance from here. TWD outperformance is likely to be driven by continued flow from lifers, as well as a seasonal uptick in exporter conversion around the annual handset release cycle. Over the US elections, a shift from tariffs and trade policy towards FX policy could be a right tail that puts pressure on CBC to revalue the TWD as well.

We initiate TWD longs vs SGD, as a proxy for the TWD NEER, as a catch up play positioning for these dynamics.

Singapore’s idiosyncratic story is easing, but not quite done. Labour markets remain unbalanced in Singapore, though labour supply has been rebounding from its pandemic era slump. The price-insensitive onshoring of services due to geopolitical concerns elsewhere has been a major shock to labour demand in the city state, driving domestic prices higher, particularly in services. Strength in the SGD REER - which is at all time highs - is justified by gains in productivity on the margin, but high prices will begin to cure high prices, hollowing out the more price-sensitive components of the economy. Global monetary tightening has helped on the margin by slowing labour demand from manufacturing, but new orders to inventory ratios are beginning to swing the other way, and may soon feed through into hiring intentions. MAS has remained hawkish but patient, with little signs of a dovish pivot and its entire focus on unit labour costs despite swings in the DSPI and core inflation momentum. These dynamics are likely to keep the SGD NEER supported at the top of the band, but in an environment where the USD is weakening and growth is resilient, MAS’ patients likely leaves the SGD an underperformer, particularly versus the higher beta cyclicals like MYR, KRW, and IDR.

The MYR remains cheap despite a short term overshoot. The ringgit has been a major and idiosyncratic outperformer in recent weeks, with markets waking up to prudent fundamental reforms on the fiscal front. While short term valuations have outrun rate differentials, longer-term valuations remain cheap, owing to a break in market psychology and mandatory conversion rules from 2016-21. Administrative measures to encourage income repatriation have certainly helped, but similar to the rest of Asia FX, we think investor UWs are largely covered at this point. FX reserves present a looming issue for BNM, with net reserves sitting 3% GDP below the IMF’s ARA metric. In terms of sequencing, we think Asian central banks will generally want to see some currency appreciation before adding to reserves in a big way, as optics have become a challenge given significant undervaluation from long term fundamentals. The importance of FX reserves may also be de-emphasized if the USD cycle continues to turn, delaying the urgency to re-build buffers in the near term. Nowhere is this more relevant than Malaysia, where simplistic (and broken) regression models of USDMYR suggest continued misalignment from longer-term fundamentals, even on a 3 handle.

With a long way to go and politics stable despite prudent but unpopular reforms, we think it is worth taking a positive view on optimism in Malaysia, and we initiate MYR longs vs the SGD, which offer a simplistic proxy for the MYR NEER.
