# Global Exposure Guide 2024 – Europe

Our Global Exposure Guide is a proprietary database that quantifies the regional revenue and cost exposure of European companies. This product is as important as ever as investors navigate the year of elections and the broader geopolitical transition towards multipolarity.

![](images/dcb7e3c731af5777746b272c896c52b522a2fb2775141b0226f4c8c53832062e.jpg)

## Key highlights:

What is the Global Exposure Guide? A proprietary annual product that quantifies the geographical revenue and cost exposure of European companies based on a survey of over 70 Morgan Stanley stock analysts. This database also forms the basis for our suite of 24 Global Exposure stock screens, which are increasingly being used by investors to track exposure to global macro and political themes.

Why the Global Exposure Guide Matters: Geopolitical uncertainty, a busy election cycle and supply chain disruptions mean that understanding companies' revenue and cost exposure is as important as ever. This product provides useful colour on which areas of the market have the broadest geographical mix of revenues and costs and hence are most sensitive to changes in regional economic momentum/growth.

## What the 2024 edition covers and our key conclusions

1) Geographical revenue exposure – European companies generate more of their revenue from overseas than other major areas. In nearly three decades of running this data, Europe's domestic exposure has never been as low as it is today. European companies' foreign exposure is even higher on a free-float market cap basis. EM is the most important foreign region, contributing 31% of total European revenues, followed by North America at 22% . On a free float market cap weighted basis, Europe has \~25% exposure to the US and \~32% exposure to EM.

2) Geographical cost exposure – Over 50% of Europe's cost base is located inside Developed Europe. In aggregate, the US accounts for a greater proportion of the European company cost base than Asia, contributing around 18% of total costs vs 10% for Asia.

3) Global Exposure Screens – The Global Exposure Guide forms the basis for our suite of 24 regional exposure screens, which our clients are increasingly using to track exposure to macro and political themes. Notably, Corporate Exposed names have been the best performers over the last 12 months and now rank amongst the most expensive names across our suite of screens. On the other end, the China Consumer exposed names have lagged meaningfully in the last year, down 15% relative to the market. Domestic France exposed names rank cheapest across our suite of screens, on a blend of PE, PBV & PD relative to their own history. The recent sell off following uncertainty around the snap election has pulled valuations for this group down to a 40% discount on N12M PE.

## What is the Global Exposure Guide?

A proprietary product quantifying the geographical revenue and cost exposure of European companies. The Global Exposure Guide is our team's flagship annual publication, which quantifies the geographical revenue and cost exposure of European companies. What makes this database proprietary is that it is compiled entirely using Morgan Stanley analysts' estimates, rather than relying on reported company data, which tend to be much less granular and consistent across different companies. Currently in its 27th year, this Guide has historically been one of our most popular and highly requested products. Below is a summary of what the 2024 edition contains:

Geographical Revenue Exposure – Our geographical revenue exposure database has revenue breakdown for 561 European companies (\~90% of Europe's market cap) across 17 different regions/countries. With data going back to 1997, we also have the ability to see how European companies' exposure to different regions has evolved over time.

Geographical Cost Exposure – Introduced in 2017, our geographical cost exposure database provides high-level estimates of European companies' supply chains. This database has the regional cost split for European companies across 5 different regions: the UK, Europe ex UK, US, Asia and RoW. We use the data in conjunction with our revenue exposure database to identify potential cost vs revenue mismatches across different regions.

The database forms the basis of our Global Exposure Stock Screens. The Global Exposure Guide forms the basis for our suite of 24 regional exposure stock screens. Our clients have increasingly been using these screens in recent years to track exposure to macro and political themes, something that would be hard to do using traditional market/sector indices. These screens are also useful in identifying the current consensus geographical themes in the market, and we also track their historical performance, valuation and earnings trends to highlight interesting thematic opportunities in Europe.

Europe remains one of the most global stock markets within Developed Markets (DM) , generating 56% of its revenues from outside its home region ( Exhibit 3 ), and \~60% of revenues on a free float market cap weighted basis. Emerging Markets (EM) remains the most important foreign region, contributing 31% of aggregate European revenues and 32% on a free float market cap weighted basis.

At a sector level, Real Estate, Utilities and Retailing are the most domestic sectors in Europe. Most of Europe's high EM exposure comes from the Semis, Commodities, Pharma and HPC. China exposure is highest for Semis, Autos and Luxury. The Health Care

sector (i.e. Pharma & MedTech) has a disproportionately large exposure to the US at \~40%, compared to just \~20% for the European market.

At a country level, Italy is the most domestic stock market in Europe. EM exposure is highest in Portugal, Austria and Norway. The Netherlands, Ireland and Switzerland are some of the most US-exposed countries in Europe.

UK companies are less domestic than their Europe ex UK counterparts. Around 29% of UK PLC's revenues are derived from within the UK, while Europe ex UK companies derive 40% from within Europe ex UK. Emerging Markets are the most important region for UK companies' revenues, contributing around 32%, followed by North America at 22% and Developed Europe ex UK at 13%.

## Geographical Cost Exposure Summary

Our analysis of European companies' regional cost exposure suggests that 54% of cost bases are located inside Developed Europe, of which 40% is based in Europe ex UK and 14% in the UK. The US accounts for 18% of total European companies' cost bases and Asia is at 10%.

Note that costs exposures are calculated differently to our revenue exposures. Given the difficulty in estimating cost exposures with granularity, we collect these estimates across the following wide ranges: 1-25%, 26-50%, 56-75%, 76%-100%. The mid-point of these exposures is used to calculate aggregates. Hence, all mentions of cost exposures are rough approximations and not comparable to revenue exposures, which are more precise.

## I. Geographical Revenue Exposure by Region

We make four points about European companies' aggregate geographical revenue split:

1) European companies derive over half of their revenues from outside Europe. This number rises to over 60% on a free-float market cap weighted basis. – As we show in Exhibit 6 , 44% of European companies' revenues come from within Developed Europe. This number falls to 39% on a free float market cap weighted basis. Emerging Markets are the most important foreign region for European companies, contributing 31% of the market's aggregate revenues. North American revenue exposure stands at 22%.

2) In nearly three decades of running this data, Europe's domestic exposure has never been as low as it is today – Our data suggest that European domestic revenue exposure is expected to return to 2022 levels this year, after an uptick in 2023. This decline likely reflects the normalization in revenues from domestically-exposed utilities companies following outsized revenue growth from the sector in recent years.

3) EM revenue exposure higher in 2024 - Our analysts are expecting European corporates' revenue exposure to Emerging Markets to rise in 2024 and to account for 31% of European revenues in aggregate, up from 29.7%. The uptick in estimated EM exposure is fairly broad based, with exposure to EM Europe, Latin America, and China all forecast to increase in 2024. The latter is largely driven by methodology changes, noted below. Exposure to the Middle East and Africa is expected to fall to 4.6% from 5% in 2023.

China exposure up as well but largely driven by methodology changes: We have adjusted our revenue exposure methodology this year to improve the way we capture indirect exposures (e.g. through joint ventures and minority stakes). This change in methodology contributed 50bps to the rise in EM exposure ( Exhibit 9 ) and 100bps to the pick up in both Developing Asia and China exposures ( Exhibit 11 and Exhibit 12 ). Without these adjustments, European companies' revenue exposure to Emerging Markets, Developing Asia and China would be 30.8%, 14.5% and 7.4% respectively (versus our new 2024 estimates of 31.3%, 15.5%, and 8.4%, respectively).

4) North America expected to contribute \~22% of European sales – The importance of North America to corporate Europe's revenues is expected to continue rising in 2024 ( Exhibit 9 ). Our analysts' estimates suggest that North America will account for \~22% of European revenue exposure in 2024; a historical high, on our data. The US and Canada are estimated to account for 20.3% and 1.5%, respectively. Europe's exposure to the US and Canada is slightly higher on a free-float market cap weighted basis at 24% and 1.7%.

Pulling all this together, the expected increase in EM exposure in 2024, coupled with North American exposure remaining high, means European domestic exposure is near an all-time low on the 27-year data history we have available to us. ## II. Geographical Revenue Exposure by Sector

Financials and Defensives are the most domestically exposed sector groups in Europe, Commodities the most global. As illustrated in Exhibit 13 , Financials derive 62% of their revenues from within Developed Europe, the highest of all sector groups in the market. Defensives in aggregate have 50% exposure to the region. On the other hand, Commodities are the most global, deriving 74% of their revenues from outside Developed Europe. Asia Pacific is the most important international region for these companies, contributing 30% of total revenues. At 43%, European cyclicals have an average level of domestic revenue exposure in aggregate but tend to have an outsized exposure to North America at 23%.

Below we run through the key conclusions from our analysis at an individual sector level.
Note that, within this section, 'domestic' refers to the whole of Developed Europe.

Most domestic sectors in Europe: Real Estate, Utilities & Retailing. Real Estate and Utilities are by far the most domestic sectors in the market, with over 70% exposure to the continent. Retailing and Banks are highly domestic too, with over 65% domestic revenue exposures to the continent.

Most periphery-exposed sectors: Utilities, Banks and Telecoms. Although peripheral countries (i.e. Italy, Spain, Ireland, Portugal and Greece) contribute around 7% of total European revenues, some sectors are much more exposed to the region. For example, Banks and Utilities have over double the market's exposure to the region at 27% and 21%, respectively, followed by Telecoms which has \~13% exposure. Most EM-exposed sectors in Europe: Semis, Energy, HPC and Food, Beverage & Tobacco. Semis is the most EM-exposed sector in the market, with 65% of sales generated there. Energy companies derive 60% of their revenues from the region. After Semis & Energy, Household & Personal Products and Food, Beverage & Tobacco are the two sectors with the highest exposure to EM at 47% and 44% respectively. China exposure is the highest in Semis, Autos, and Luxury Goods. The top 5

sectors in Europe with the highest exposure to China are Semis, Autos, Consumer Durables, Materials and Energy . China contributes about a quarter of Autos and Semis companies' demand. Consumer Durables are estimated to generate \~18% of revenues from China i.e. about double the estimated European average of 8%.

Food, Beverage & Tobacco companies' LatAm exposure is over double the market average. Companies in the Food, Beverages & Tobacco sector derive 16% of their revenues from Latin America compared to the European average of just 7%. Household & Personal Products is also highly geared to the region with close to 11% exposure.

Pharma is the most US-exposed sector in Europe. The US is an important source of revenues for Health Care companies in Europe, with the two industries in the sector, Pharmaceuticals and Health Care Equipment, each deriving \~40% of their revenues from the country. Media and Entertainment and Consumer Services are also highly exposed to the region at 34% and 32%, respectively.

Quantifying the revenue exposure of oil companies is not always a straightforward task

For all companies, we have attempted to provide the most accurate representation of the geographical split of the underlying demand that drives their top line. While this measure is a wholly intuitive concept for most companies, it is not always the case. This is especially true for many oil companies. For example, while an integrated oil firm may have a refinery in Angola, which would show up as African exposure in its annual report, this belies the underlying economic sensitivity of the company.

We assume the revenue exposure of oil companies is proportional to the share of global oil consumption. We assume that the revenue exposure for the oil sector is proportional to the region's share of global oil consumption. While this does not help explain the sensitivity of companies to any supply disruptions, we consider this the most representative measure of sensitivity to changes in demand. As shown in Exhibit 21 , where we compare the regional share of global oil consumption versus the proportion of revenues for European oil companies’ E&P divisions, the latter does not give an accurate picture of the sector’s economic exposure.

In recent years, we have had a lot of interest in ways to position for the regional investment themes through major equity indices. • DAX 40 – Germany’s DAX derives 20% of its revenues domestically and 25%? from the rest of Developed European markets. The market's relatively high EU exposure is mainly down to a large weighting in Defensives and Financials stocks. EM revenue exposure is 31%, compared to the pan-European average of 33%.

• CAC 40 – France is less domestically exposed compared to many of its Eurozone peers with 21%? of revenues coming from within the country. Developed Europe revenue exposure is in-line with the European average at 43%?, while EM accounts for 31% of exposure.

• IBEX 35 – Companies in Spain’s IBEX index derive \~70% of their revenues from outside Spain. Of all major equity indices in Europe, IBEX has the highest exposure to Latin America, contributing 20% of total revenues, driven mainly by Banks. Developed Europe in aggregate contributes 48% to IBEX revenues.

• MIB 40 – Italy’s MIB 40 index is one of the most domestic stock markets in Europe deriving 36% of its revenues from within Italy. Developed Europe contributes a relatively high percentage 51% to total revenues too.

• FTSE 100 – The UK's large cap FTSE 100 index has a relatively high exposure to Emerging Markets at 36%, driven primarily by commodity sectors. At 40%, FTSE100 exposure to Developed Europe is low compared to many other regional indices. Domestic exposure is also relatively low at under 30%.
