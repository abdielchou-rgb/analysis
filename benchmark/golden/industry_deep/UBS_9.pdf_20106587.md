# "China+1" expansion: How can Chinese corporates benefit from "China+ASEAN"?

## Capacity expansion and collaboration makes a win-win for China and ASEAN

China and ASEAN have been each other's largest trading partners since 2020, laying a solid foundation for mutual cooperation. ASEAN's population, as a whole, is half of China's with high market demand potential. Chinese corporates' capacity expansion into ASEAN is also promoted by geographic proximity and similar cultural backgrounds. China's supply chain expansion has in turn resulted in industry upgrades in ASEAN, as ASEAN-China trade is increasingly focused on developing high value-added supply chains. ASEAN has increasingly added value domestically by embedding itself in China's final demand chain, while simultaneously China has been growing its importance as a value-added contributor to the rest of Asia.

## Why do Chinese companies go to ASEAN?

We attribute ASEAN's attractiveness to: 1) potential for higher demand locally; 2) ASEAN as a whole is more attractive and competitive when compared with other emerging markets; 3) lower labour costs and a young sizeable working population; 4) increasing share of global exports; 5) policy support (eg, ASEAN's duty-free trade vs US/ EU's trade restrictions on China); and 6) abundant natural resources which are highly complementary to China's imports in many aspects.

## In ASEAN, which countries and industries do Chinese enterprises invest in?

China has been actively investing into ASEAN 10 countries over the past 10+ years. Top destinations for China's greenfield overseas direct investment (ODI) between 2019 and H123 are Indonesia, Cambodia and the Philippines. According to listed Chinese corporates' investment plans announced in 2022 and 2023 to set up subsidiaries and factories in ASEAN, the number and value of projects increased by 214%/8% YoY in 2023; among which Vietnam, Indonesia and Malaysia dominate in terms of investment value. Investment plans became more diversified in 2023 and the three industries with the highest investment project numbers and investment amounts are technology hardware and equipment, materials, and auto and auto parts.

## What did we observe at a macro level?

For the past 14 years, China has remained ASEAN's largest trading partner, and since 2020 the two regions have been each other's largest trading partners. More than 90% of the goods traded between China and ASEAN have enjoyed zero tariffs over the past decade. ASEAN-China trade promotes industry upgrades and is increasingly focused on developing high value-added supply chains. ASEAN has increasingly added value domestically by embedding itself in China's final demand chain. Moreover, China has been growing its importance as a value-added contributor to the rest of Asia, with value added originated from China representing c8% of gross exports for ASEAN and c2% for India.

## 2. ASEAN as a whole is competitive

ASEAN is a front-runner in East Asia’s economic integration. Instead of each country trying to control the whole supply chain, ASEAN, as a whole, is more attractive and competitive when compared with other emerging markets attracting industry relocation. Indonesia with its natural resources can focus on upstream metal supply and add value through processing, while Vietnam with its lower labour costs can provide a base for labour-intensive businesses, and lastly Thailand with its strong infrastructure and existing supply chain can serve as the final assembly and transportation hub. In the long run, compared with other emerging market destinations, ASEAN as a whole may enhance its competitiveness as a manufacturing destination.

## 5. Policy support

## Trade policy: duty-free trade within ASEAN and neighbouring countries

In the 1990s, ASEAN countries established the ASEAN Free Trade Area (AFTA) with a view to raising ASEAN’s competitive edge as a production base through the reduction or elimination of tariffs and non-tariff barriers on intra-ASEAN trade, in addition to attracting FDI. The AFTA achieved its original goal of reducing tariffs to 0-5% in 2002, and ASEAN-6 realized tariff elimination in 2010. AFTA has achieved a high rate of trade liberalisation (the percentage of tariff lines subject to elimination) of 98.6%.

On the heels of AFTA and as part of ASEAN’s Vision 2020, ASEAN embarked on a more ambitious strategy of regional economic integration by launching the AEC (ASEAN Economic Community) on 31 December 2015. In addition to free movement of goods, AEC aimed at deepening integration through free movement of services, investment, capital, and skilled workers. AEC covers a wide range of areas, including mutual recognition of standards, intellectual property rights, competition policies, infrastructure development, and narrowing the development gap. The AEC furthers integration into the global economy by participating in global supply chains and actively promotes FTAs with countries outside the ASEAN region.

## ASEAN actively integrates into global trade

Building upon separate free trade agreements (FTAs) secured with trading partners, ASEAN has adopted a proactive stance towards extra-bloc free trade and regional economic integration. ASEAN signed the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), which entered into force on 1 January 2022, with China, Japan, South Korea, Australia and New Zealand. RCEP has been billed as the world’s largest FTA, accounting for about 30% of the world’s GDP and over a quarter of global trade.

## Why do Chinese corporates choose ASEAN?

ASEAN is the closest Belt & Road region to China, and the Belt & Road Initiative (BRI) promotes cooperation between China and ASEAN. The BRI was first promoted in Indonesia. In H123, the proportion of trade between China and ASEAN accounted for 45% of China's trade with BRI countries.

RCEP promotes trade cooperation between China and ASEAN. The RCEP freetrade agreement encompasses the largest trade bloc in the world, with a diversified membership structure, implying significant economic and trade scale benefits. It has created new opportunities for broader regional cooperation and provides a powerful engine for regional prosperity. The RCEP is widely expected to bring tariffs lower and result in more favourable trade policies, creating a better business environment for ASEAN. After the agreement comes into effect, more than 90% of the goods traded in the region will eventually achieve zero tariffs.

US/EU‘s trade restrictions on China have prompted Chinese enterprises to expand their production capacity in ASEAN. Trade restrictions have brought great uncertainties and risks to the exports of several of China's manufacturing industries, which has pushed enterprises to either relocate their original factories in China to Southeast Asia, or expand their production capacity in Southeast Asia.

Similar cultures: China and ASEAN have deep historical and cultural ties. Similar cultural backgrounds and living habits lay the foundation for Chinese brands to gain access to the ASEAN market.

## Thailand

Thailand is the earliest developed country in ASEAN with a relatively complete industrial and infrastructure system. Such infrastructure, accompanied by a robust automotive market, with roughly 1m units of domestic vehicles sales per year, drew investment from the Japanese automotive industry in the late 2000s-2010s. Forward to the 2020s, the growth of Thailand's automotive cluster, which currently produces close to 2m vehicles per year, and the Thai government's push for zero emission vehicle production has drawn in a new round of FDI from global manufacturers, including Chinese manufacturers with manufacturers such as BYD, Chang An, SAIC and Great Wall Motors establishing factories in Thailand's Eastern Economic Corridor.

Outside of infrastructure, Thailand has been credited by global manufacturers as an investment destination due to the ease of doing business and its attractive tax incentives for manufacturers, with up to 13 years of corporate tax exemption and further incentives for its new economy industries; including EV, biotechnology, and smart electronics from its 10 highlighted industries.

As of 9M23, China emerged as the largest source of FDI applications, contributing 24% of Thailand's FDI value, followed by Singapore and Japan. The electrical appliances and electronics (E&E) industry secured the top position among sectors as an FDI target, increasing 84% YoY.

## Vietnam

Vietnam has been one of the most popular destinations in ASEAN and globally for the "China+1" supply chain relocation. In 2023, Vietnam stood out as one of the few economies in the world still registering significant positive FDI growth amid higher global interest rates and elevated global growth uncertainties. FDI (measured by registered capital) grew by 32% in 2023, with manufacturing FDI rising by 40% (measured by implemented capital, FDI also grew by 3.5%). The most significant FDI growth in Vietnam originated from 'Greater China' in 2023. FDI from Taiwan grew by 113% YoY in 2023. FDI from mainland China grew by 78% YoY. The outperformance of Greater China's FDI into Vietnam can be partly attributed to corporates’ pursuit of their "China+1" supply chain reconfiguration strategy. This strong FDI growth momentum has continued in 2024; FDI grew by 40%YoY and 9.6% YoY in January 2024, measured by registered and implemented capital, respectively.

In our view, Vietnam’s growth as a destination for global supply chain relocation is attributable to a combination of factors, such as its relatively low labour costs compared to regional peers, its improving business environment (as measured by the World Bank’s ease of doing business ranking), resilient macroeconomic environment with steady growth, moderating inflation trend, as well as a steady exchange rate environment. Furthermore, the government has been focusing on developing a supportive environment for foreign investment and Vietnam is among a group of economies with the biggest number of FTAs (free-trade agreements) in Asia. Early expansion by large international companies helped Vietnam to make a head-start in developing related supply chains and offered experience for other companies to follow, giving Vietnam an early-mover advantage in attracting FDI in recent years, especially after the US-China trade war. For example, Korea’s Samsung, one of the biggest foreign investors in Vietnam, started production in the country in 2009. Moreover, China’s Shenzhou, a leader in the textile industry, also started production in Vietnam in 2013.

Vietnam’s geographic proximity and connectivity to mainland China is another factor behind its attractiveness as a “China+1” destination for corporates. Within ASEAN, Vietnam is closest geographically to mainland China, especially China’s Greater Bay Area (GBA). Such geographic proximity could help corporates to connect production bases in Vietnam to China, gaining extended efficiencies from the comprehensive supply chain network in China. China’s GBA is one of Asia's biggest production bases for information technology goods, and Samsung Vietnam’s production base is located in the north of Vietnam. As such, north Vietnam has been an attractive location for supply chain expansion in the information technology production areas. Large international corporates in the sector, such as Luxshare and Goertek from China, have been expanding production capacity in the area in recent quarters.

Vietnam has also been further developing international collaborations. Vietnam and the US upgraded their diplomatic relationship to that of a 'Strategic Comprehensive Partnership' during US President Biden’s visit in September, a two-step upgrade from the previous 'Comprehensive Partnership' and enhancing Vietnam-US cooperation in several areas such as economics, trade, investment, science, technology and innovation. Importantly, Vietnam and the US also established a new partnership to explore semiconductor supply chain opportunities. Given Vietnam is already one of the biggest consumer electronic products assembly hubs in Asia, exploring opportunities in the semiconductors space should help it move up the global value chain. In November 2023, Vietnam and Japan also upgraded their diplomatic status to 'Strategic Comprehensive Partnership'. Furthermore, when China’s President Xi visited Hanoi in December 2023, Vietnam and China signed 36 cooperative documents. We believe collaborative relations with other economies would create a supportive environment for future FDI inflows to Vietnam. (Please see our recent report: Macro Keys – Economics "Vietnam: Ready for an upswing?"for further details.)

## Indonesia

Between 2014 and 2023, Indonesian exports to China increased from 10% to c25% of Indonesia's total exports. In the same period, China moved from beyond the top 10 to the second-largest source of FDI into Indonesia.

Apart from push factors from China (eg, environmental concerns), we believe the pull factors in Indonesia's favour include: 1) the growth opportunities within the Indonesian market; 2) the higher margin or ROE of the domestic businesses; 3) strategic access to key raw materials; 4) navigation of international trade barriers; and 5) the reduction of transportation costs.

In March 2023, China's Hailiang Copper decided to build a copper tubing factory in East Java, in front of the newly-built Freeport copper smelter. The company's reasons for investing in Indonesia included: 1) becoming overseas clients' go-to supplier by expanding production capacity overseas; and 2) helping to navigate international trade barriers, reduce tariffs and transportation costs as well as quickly increase global market share.

However, in some cases the arrival of Chinese competition resulted in oversupply and lower profitability overall. UBS Indonesia team saw this in the cement sector. At one point five years ago, there was also the potential for Chinese property companies expanding into Indonesia. However, in the case of nickel or in the above copper supply chain example, we believe Chinese expansion will help Indonesia move up the value chain, exporting more processed or intermediate goods, thus generating value-added export revenue, structurally improving Indonesia's trade balance.

## Key challenges for ASEAN

ASEAN's competitiveness compared to other EM countries: the limited resources of each individual country and ease of doing business remain key hurdles for relocation. ## Challenges for OneASEAN

According to the ASEAN Integration Report, ASEAN integration faces significant institutional, economic and political challenges. Institutional challenges are reflected in: 1) the ASEAN Secretariat is constrained in its ability to drive implementation by design; 2) the mechanisms to manage implementation at a national level vary in effectiveness; and 3) the consensus-based approach to decision making in ASEAN inevitably results in slower progress.

Challenges for OneASEAN include institutional, economic and political challenges

For economic challenges, different levels of economic development present challenges for ASEAN integration and this diversity can also motivate protectionist policies in some member states.

In terms of political challenges, progress with ASEAN integration depends on the willing participation of all member states and is therefore subject to political factors in each member state. Also, the importance of ASEAN varies across member states. UBS 2023 OneASEAN Conference takeaways mentioned that investment incentive is a major deciding factor for manufacturers when considering moving to ASEAN countries. Therefore, all factors that lead to the risk of policy continuation, such as the change of government, will bring risk to capacity expansion in ASEAN. Besides, ASEAN's bilateral relationship with its two largest trading partners, China and the US, might become more complicated.

## China containers, shipping and ports

Increased trade between China and ASEAN, in terms of either finished goods or intermediate goods, brings incremental demand for regional shipping. Chinese enterprises' capacity expansion in ASEAN is conducive to increasing intra-Asia shipping demand and benefits those ports with greater exposure to ASEAN.
