## United States:

Provided close proximity to July non-farm payrolls, macro markets exhibited acute sensitivity to a series of cautionary signals with respect to the labor market. Developed market bonds rallied and curves bull-steepened as market-implied central bank policy rate paths fell. Safe-haven currencies exhibited stark outperformance within the G10, while risk-sensitive currencies languished. Equities traded in a clear risk-off manner as the more economically sensitive Russell 2000 (- 3.0%) had its largest loss since February. Both investment grade and high yield credit spreads widened along with the broader risk-off macro tone.

The NY session began with a larger-than-expected rise in US initial jobless claims to 249k in the week ended July 27 (C: 236k; P: 235k). The highest number of initial claims since August 2023 proved catalyst enough to turn US rates richer for the session.

Treasuries sustained gains into the 10:00am ET release of the US ISM Manufacturing PMI for July. The headline index registered an unexpected decline to 46.8 (C: 48.8; P: 48.5), driven by the employment. Indeed, the employment subindex at 43.4 (C: 49.2; P: 49.3) reached its lowest level since June 2020. According to ISM Chair Fiore, “Respondents’ companies are continuing to reduce head counts through layoffs, attrition and hiring freezes. Panelists’ comments in July indicated a notable increase in staff reductions compared to June, supported by the approximately 1-to-1.8 ratio of hiring versus head-count reduction comments.”

Following the release, the Treasury curve began to distinctly bull-steepen as frontend yields pushed lower. As more rate cuts were priced into 2025, the 10y Treasury yield fell below 4.00% for the first time since February. While the reds pack led the rally in STIR, markets implied higher odds for rate cuts at near-dated FOMC meetings. In particular, markets now priced \~25% chance for a 50bp rate cut at the September FOMC meeting. In addition, the September/November FOMC OIS flattened by 7.5bp as the likelihood that the Fed would deliver consecutive rate cuts increased.

The NY afternoon brought a real-time reminder of the non-linear perils that lurk after a large-cap semiconductor company announced it would lay off over 15% of its workforce. This took US duration in the front end to its richest levels of the session as 2y Treasury yields closed 10.9bp lower at 4.15%, while the 2s20s curve finished 7bp steeper at \~20bp.

On Friday, at 8:30am ET, our economists expect US total non-farm payrolls rose by 195k in July (C: 175k; P: 206k) but flag downside risks from Hurricane Beryl, seasonals, and teacher payrolls. A market-based survey measure is largely in with consensus economist expectations.

## Japan:

JGBs rallied on Thursday, led by the long end of the curve following the rally in USTs on Wednesday. While the BoJ delivered a 15bp rate hike and a “detailed QT plan” on Wednesday, the pace of QT seemed to be more gradual than expected, and the purchase amount in tenors longer than 10y was unchanged. Accordingly, the twistflattening of 2s20s on the JGB curve accelerated into the 3:00pm Tokyo close. By

Tokyo close, 2y JGBs sold off by 1.0bp d/d to 0.460%, 10y JGBs rallied by 2.5bp d/d to 1.030%, while 20y JGBs rallied by 4.0bp d/d to 1.790% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS also twist-flattened and underperformed JGBs in the 10-20y tenor. XCCY basis tightened across the curve, especially in the long end.

During Tokyo trading hours, USD/JPY traded in a range between 148.51 and 150.34. Initially, the pair moved lower from the 150.30 level, to the 148.50 level, driven by constant squaring of the JPY carry trade. However, the pair gradually recovered toward the 150.30 level into the Tokyo close. During the London morning, the pair traded in a range \~150.00 while European currencies underperformed ahead of the BoE meeting. The combination of a dovish Fed and hawkish BoJ suggested squaring of the JPY carry trade may continue further. Our model suggests fair value should be \~143.00.

## Europe:

European duration opened stronger on Thursday following the Fed meeting on Wednesday, and the rally accelerated during the second half of the trading session. Weak US data once again fueled narratives of a softening in the labor market. Specifically, both ISM employment and unit labor costs were weaker than expected. Overall, 10y Bunds closed the London session \~6bp richer at 2.24% and outperformed against USTs. The Bund curve closed the London session steeper, led by the front end, although during the London morning it initially struggled to steepen as the 10y tenor led the move higher.

European front-end rates pricing followed the US and led the rally on the curve, as the market priced an 80% probability for 75bp of cuts by December; reds continued to drive the rally in the front end. The risk-off tone, evident via lower equity prices and wider credit spreads, pushed the peripheral complex wider. BTPs ended the London session 5bp wider against Bunds and also underperformed both Spain and France.

GBP (-0.9%) sold off ahead of the BoE rate cut, although began to reverse, until the soft US manufacturing ISM PMI put a damper on risk appetite and pushed GBP even weaker. Despite initial support from Norwegian PMIs, NOK (-0.4%) ended the London session weaker alongside Scandi peer, SEK (-0.3%). CHF (+0.5%) was boosted by safe-haven demand amid a local holiday. USD/JPY (-0.2%) fell alongside the rally in USTs. EUR (-0.3%) dropped, in line with rate differential as market pricing of the ECB policy path was recalibrated after the FOMC meeting on Wednesday.

## Dollar Bloc:

Price action in G10 currencies was choppy as market participants digested various data releases and central bank actions/comments. Ultimately, the DXY USD Index (+0.3%) gained. Both AUD (-0.9%) and CAD (-0.5%) weakened while NZD ended the session flat.

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies strengthened, buoyed by a dovish FOMC meeting on

Wednesday. CNH and SGD were the outliers. USD/CNH gained 0.4%, to \~7.2530, aided by a downside surprise in the July Caixin Manufacturing PMI, and ended the AxJ session higher than where it traded before the FOMC meeting. SGD was 0.1% weaker, while the other low yielders, KRW and TWD, gained 0.4% and 0.2%, respectively, with KRW aided by its high beta and TWD aided by the 2% rally in TAIEX and US\$499mn of foreign equity inflows. MYR continued to outperform, 0.5% stronger to 4.57, the strongest level since August 2023. INR, THB, and PHP were broadly stable.

AxJ rates rallied broadly and curves bull-flattened following the dovish FOMC meeting. China and Indonesia rates relatively underperformed. 5y CNY NDIRS yields fell 2bp and the 1s5s curve bull-flattened 1bp, in line with the weak Caixin Manufacturing PMI data. 10y IndoGBs rallied just 2bp, to 6.87%. In Korea, the 1y NDIRS yield fell just 1bp despite the meaningful rally in USTs, possibly reflective of already elevated market pricing for BoK rate cuts. Similarly, 1y swap yields in Thailand, India, and Malaysia had modest declines of 1-2bp. Yields further out, which are driven more by US rates, had a more meaningful rally, led by Hong Kong (5y: -7bp), Korea (5y: -4bp), and Singapore (5y: -4bp). Hong Kong and Singapore rates outperformed as their 1y swap yields fell more meaningfully, by 4-5bp.

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
