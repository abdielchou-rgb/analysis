## The BOC and ECB begin a game of BOCCE-Ball, likely without the Fed for now

Bocce ball is a game where the objective is to throw balls in such a manner that it stops as close as possible to a desired target, not unlike monetary policy across developed markets as a whole. This week, both the BoC and the ECB threw their first balls, cutting rates by 25bp each, as they seek to bring the economy as close as possible to a soft landing. Sovereign bond yields across major DM currencies, which have been reasonably well correlated in recent months, rallied in synchronized fashion. US yields were no exception, with the front end rallying by 17bp by Thursday's close (Figure 1). But this rally proved short lived in the US, as a strong employment report on Friday helped push yields back higher.

Last week, we had noted that too little Fed cuts were being priced into forwards, causing the Fronts/Greens curve to reach considerably steep levels. As a result, we had recommend Fronts/Greens flatteners in three different expressions - outright flatteners as well as versus shorts in 5s and/or versus long volatility positions (see The planets, if not the stars, are aligning). After the rally earlier this week, this was of course no longer the case, and we recommended unwinding such trades (see Reduce long duration exposure ahead of payrolls, J. Barry et al., 6/6/2024). After the strong NFP report on Friday, the Fronts/Greens curve resteepened somewhat, but it is still only modestly above its YTD average.

Thus, the risk reward in outright curve positions is not attractive, and we do not recommend outright curve exposure. Not only is the Fronts/Greens curve (for instance) near the middle of its YTD range, but it is also the case that policy uncertainty is back to being considerably elevated and extending further into the future. As we have often done in previous publications, one way to measure policy uncertainty is to extract the implied probability distribution from the premia of options on SFRZ4 and SFRM5 (for instance), and then further resolving those probability distributions into a weighted combination of single-scenario distributions (where each scenario corresponds to a certain number of Fed cuts or hikes, by YE24 or June 2025). Such an approach is a useful way to extract information from the tails of the distribution and re-express them in the form of weights (not to be confused with probabilities) on each scenario, which is much more intuitive. Currently, such an analysis of implied probability distributions tells us two things. First, Fed expectations are converging towards erasing almost all expectations of easing by year end. In that sense, the high-for-long expectation is becoming more entrenched in the near term. This is consistent with our economists' revised Fed call, which now looks for only one cut this year in November (see Strong jobs overshadow rising unemployment, M. Feroli, 6/7/2024). Second, policy uncertainty is migrating further forward, as seen from the weights associated with the June 2025 3M SOFR futures implied distribution, which can be thought of as blending hiking (or unchanged) scenarios with rate cut scenarios in equal measure (Figure 2).

Amidst such a backdrop of elevated policy uncertainty beyond the near term, and given the upcoming CPI data and FOMC meeting next week, we do not see outright curve exposure as compelling in either direction. Instead, we recommend taking advantage of relative value opportunities that typically arise when markets swing in both directions like they did this week. We also continue to recommend well hedged carry trades, with little outright rate or curve exposure to the front end, given that the Fed appears likely to remain on hold for the foreseeable future. One trade that combines carry as well as relative value is to initiate 3M forward 10s/30s back end steepeners (1:1.5 risk weighted) with M5/Z5 3M SOFR futures flatteners (1:1 risk weighted). As seen in Figure 3, the weighted yield spread corresponding to this box trade has been fairly mean reverting but is now over 5bp too low relative to its historical average year-to-date, largely because of this week's moves. Moreover, the carry/slide on this trade is fairly attractive at \~8bp over a 3 month horizon. Therefore, we see this trade as attractive to initiate currently (see Trade recommendations).

## Options

In what was a week of big moves in the Rates markets, swaption implied volatility itself was on the move. Implieds in the upper left increased into the rally earlier in the week, but reversed course and declined into the selloff on Friday after a stronger-than-expected Payrolls print. All in all, at the end of the week, implieds are slightly higher in shorter expiry sectors and slight lower in longer expiry sectors (Figure 4).

Looking ahead to next week, which brings the May CPI data as well as the FOMC meeting, we remain neutral on outright volatility positions. On the one hand, markets are likely to remain in a state of heightened data dependency and policy uncertainty until more time passes and more data emerges that makes the policy path more clear, which could keep delivered volatility elevated on a day-to-day basis. However, yields are also likely to remain rangebound until policy clarity eventually emerges, which is beneficial for carry-seeking short volatility positions that are delta hedged infrequently. Given these offsetting influences, implied volatility appears unlikely to either rise or fall in a sustained manner.

Moreover, next week's CPI data also represents 2-sided risk to implieds. As Figure 5 shows, over the past 9 months, implieds have declined in the week following softer CPI prints, while rising in the week following strong prints (although the latter has been somewhat less consistent). Additionally, Chair Powell's post-meeting comments next Wednesday as well as the new SEP "dots" will be closely watched by market participants, and any surprises could also have an impact on implieds. Therefore, with 2-sided event-risk looming large next week, we remain neutral on outright volatility positions.

That said, we continue to recommend pairing front end curve flatteners with long volatility positions. As we noted last week (The planets, if not the stars, are aligning), rolling 1M returns from selling swaption straddles have been well correlated to the rolling 1M P/L front end flatteners. Even though front end curves have flattened somewhat since last week, the reasons for the correlation between front end curves and volatility remain in force. Therefore, combining flatteners with long volatility positions remains attractive as a strategy to create carry-efficient positive convexity positions.

Our framework for constructing such trades is detailed in Figure 6 (see also The planets, if not the stars, are aligning). We start by estimating two betas - the beta between implied vols and the ATMF yield for each swaption structure, and a second beta between the ATMF yield and the reference front end curve (we use the 6Mx1Y/18Mx1Y curve for this purpose this week, which we simply refer to as the Fronts/Reds curve in this discussion for convenience). The product of these two betas gives us the sensitivity of each swaption's implied volatility with respect to the front end curve. Then, using the vega of the swaption, we can size up the notional of the swaption that can suitably be paired with a given amount of front end curve flattening risk. As can be seen, 2Y expiries appear to be a sweet spot, allowing for the construction of positive carry packages that combine long volatility positions and front end curve flatteners. Given this, and given the cheapness of 5- and 10-year tails relative to our empirical estimates of fair value, we recommend buying 2Yx5Y swaption straddles on a delta hedged basis, versus 6Mx1Y / 18Mx1Y flatteners (see Trade recommendations)

## Swap spreads

Swap spreads have been remarkably stable this week even as swap yields have traded in a 20-25 bp range, finally ending the week 1-2bp narrower across the curve, with the exception of 30-year spreads which are mostly flat (Figure 7).

Looking ahead, our views on swap spreads remain unchanged. Little has changed with respect to the fundamental drivers of swap spreads across the curve, and valuations remain similar to what they were last week - as Figure 8 shows, maturity matched swap spreads remain narrow to fair value in the front end as well as the 10-year sector, while 7-year and 20-year sectors remain a touch wide. As such, we maintain our widening bias in the front end, and our steepening bias on the 7s/10s and 20s/30s swap spread curves.

We also recommend initiating exposure to 2s/7s invoice spread curve flatteners as an efficient way to position for the above mentioned mispricings along the swap spread curve. Additionally, given that spread curves are naturally exposed to moves in term funding premium (see Term Funding Premium and the Term Structure of SOFR Swap Spreads for more information) , declines in which can steepen spread curves, we recommend weighting this spread curve by choosing a smaller risk weight (of 35%) on the 7-year leg (see Trade recommendations).
