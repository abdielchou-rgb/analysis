# Don't fight the Fed, don't short Cyclicals

It seems clear now that the Fed has shifted to growth preservation mode. Time will tell, but with incoming data holding up, a soft landing stays on track. The rebound in activity surprises and positive year end seasonality may give legs to the short squeeze in Cyclicals. Now focus turns to Sep PMIs and upcoming Q3 earnings.

Cyclicals short squeeze may have legs. In our EMR last week (Cyclicals undershoot, 13 Sep), we flagged that Cyclicals may be due a bounce, given their oversold levels and recent undershooting of fundamentals, having lagged Defensives since early Q2. Although headwinds from weakening China growth and US election uncertainty aren't going away, the recent pick-up in US/EU economic surprises and bear steepening of the yield curve could provide more fundamental support to Cyclicals. Furthermore, we note that seasonally Q4 is the strongest quarter for equities performance, and favours Cyclicals over Defensives (based on avg. perf since 1996). And finally, given how defensive equity positioning had become since summer (as discussed in our Who Owns What Dip buying and rotation, 28 Aug), more of a rebound in Cyclicals would likely be a pain trade for most investors.

Back to fundamentals - PMIs and earnings. With the long awaited first Fed cut in the rear view mirror, markets should focus back on fundamentals. September flash PMIs next week should give us an indication of the growth dynamics going into the year end. We note that EPS revisions have turned negative again most recently, but somewhat more than what PMIs suggest. From our recent conversations with clients ahead of the Fed meeting, it appears that most were not in a rush to raise exposure to Cyclicals, with the upcoming Q3 earnings season seen as a potential source of downside surprises. But if PMIs stabilise and broader activity surprises continue to rebound, investors may decide to look through near term earnings risks. This is particularly the case as consensus estimates for Q3 earnings have been tempered down since summer, lowering the hurdle to clear.

## Weekly Flows update

## Equities and Credit ride the rate cut wave with large inflows

Equities saw most inflows since July with \$39bn this week vs. small outflows previously. This was mainly driven by US inflows (\$34bn), followed by small inflows in Global funds, EM and Japan. Europe-ex UK also saw inflows this week after three weeks of outflows, while the UK continues to witness redemptions.

Globally, all sectors saw inflows. Tech led inflows at \$12bn, with Disc (\$5.4bn) being a distant second. Healthcare, Financials and Industrials also saw \~\$5bn inflows this week. Utilities and Telecom were relative laggards. In Europe, Disc, Utilities and Financials saw healthy inflows, while the remaining sectors saw redemptions this week.

Overall Fixed income flows remain healthy at \$16bn. In the US, treasuries saw minor outflows this week as expected, while IG and HY saw strong inflows. In Europe, Bonds witnessed redemptions, as IG saw strong inflows, along with minor inflows in HY. EM corp (\$) funds saw the most inflows since June this week, reversing some of its recent outflows. MM funds saw outflows of \$8bn this week, after six week of inflows >\$260bn.

On the year, equity inflows are up strongly to \$426bn, with the US capturing more than half with \$262bn. Fixed income flows inch higher to \$466bn, on consistent inflows. MM funds flows comfortably at the top with \$592bn.
