# Economics Asia Week Ahead

# What you need to know: 29 Apr-2 May

## Week ahead

We expect Hong Kong to report GDP growth of 1.5% yoy in Q1 vs 4% growth in the past two quarters, with the slowdown mainly due to two factors. First, an unfavourable base effect is in play. Hong Kong reopened its borders and gained an increase of 6ppt in GDP growth from Q4-22 to Q1-23. Second, the delay in the Fed's easing cycle and concerns relating to investor confidence in China’s economy have led to headwinds in Hong Kong’s financial sector. Considering the challenges in the financial sector and their negative spillover to tourism and other sectors, we trim our 2024 GDP growth forecast by 0.5ppt to 2.5%. Growth in retail sales volume and value likely moderated to -4.7% yoy and 0.9% yoy, respectively, in March.

Taiwan's GDP likely flattened out in Q1, after the 2.1% QoQ sa surge in Q4, although base effects likely left the yoy growth rate higher at 6% in Q1, up from 4.9% in Q4. This would be led by strong net trade contribution, while consumption likely remained resilient, justifying rate hike by the CBC.

South Korea's inflation is likely to ease, albeit only slightly, to 3%yoy in April from 3.1% in March. This is not sufficient to ease the BoK's concerns relating to inflation. After a surge in headline growth to 3.4% yoy in Q1, against a more modest gain in detailed domestic demand, upcoming high-frequency data will be of a particular interest, to better understand domestic demand pressure on inflation, while export growth likely remains robust through Q2, at 14.4% in April, led by the semi sector.

Vietnam is also likely to report another month of strong export growth, at 14.5%yoy in April, although not sufficient to ease the pressure on the dong, without a pullback in US rates. Meanwhile, domestic demand growth is unlikely to accelerate, on a real basis, although higher inflation (4.6% in April) will likely see retail sales growth higher (9.6%) on a value basis.

Inflation is likely to rise in Indonesia to 3.3% yoy in April, from 3.1% in March, justifying its rate hike.

## Financial condition indicators (FCIs)

Advanced Asian economies' financial conditions have continued to ease. Hong Kong, Singapore and South Korea FCIs have improved due to both domestic and external markets, while Taiwan FCI remains largely unchanged.

## Week in review

After BI delivered a 25bps rate hike – and left the door open for more – BSP remained reluctant to follow its example. The timing of the BoT rate cut gets pushed back to Q3, due to the Senate election now scheduled in mid-June, while the digital wallet programme remains a source of additional uncertainty. Both its trade and manufacturing production data (due out) are likely to underperform its peers in March, with the former also depressed by high base effects. The timing of Singapore's own general election is yet to be set, adding uncertainty to our forecast of easing by the MAS in October.

Hong Kong March CPI slowed slightly to 2.0% yoy from 2.1% in the previous month, in line with our expectation. Trade data came in stronger than expected, with exports growing by 4.7% yoy and imports increasing by 5.3% yoy.

The longer the maturity of a certain cash flow and the higher the move in the discount factor, the higher will be the loss. Upside surprises in inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most common adverse macroeconomic shocks to receivers.
