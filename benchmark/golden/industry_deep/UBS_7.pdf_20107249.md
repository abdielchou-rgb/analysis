# European Economic Comment Eurozone consumer: Slowly improving sentiment

## Consumer confidence recovers to highest level since start of Ukraine war

European sentiment improved slightly in March. The results of the latest European Commission (EC) Business and Consumer survey published today (survey period 1-20 March) show an improvement in both consumer and business confidence. The consumer confidence indicator was confirmed at the flash estimate of -14.9 (+0.6 points), still below its long-term average of -11.6 but at its highest level since February 2022 (Figure 1). Consumer confidence in the Eurozone had been depressed amid a very significant household real income squeeze, but real wage growth has now turned positive as inflation falls and nominal wage growth remains high. This supports the financial situation of households (Figure 6) and helps consumer confidence to gradually edge higher. The actual improvement in consumer spending up until now was more hesitant than we had expected – hard data such as retail sales, for example, remained below their Q4 level in January – but the trend is gradually pointing into the right direction. Signs of slowly improving consumer momentum are also visible in other economies.

## Improving financial situation supports consumer sentiment

Three of the four components of the consumer confidence index rose in February (Figure 2). The assessment of households' own financial situation over the past and next 12 months saw the biggest increases of 0.7 and 1.0 points to -13.1 and -4.3, respectively, supporting our interpretation that real wage gains help to lift sentiment. Households confidence in the economy also seems to improve, given that their outlook for the general economic situation over the coming 12 months has increased by 0.7 points to -24.2. Meanwhile, expectations to conduct major purchases over the coming 12 months is the only component that stayed unchanged, at -17.9. At a country level, consumer confidence improved notably in Spain (+1.3), France (+1.1) and Germany (+0.9) but fell in Italy (-0.7). The sentiment level remains most depressed in Germany by far (Figure 5).

## Business confidence up in all sectors except construction

Improving consumer confidence coincides with a broad-based uptick in business confidence. Retail confidence saw the largest increase of 0.9 points to -5.7, as consumers' recovering purchasing power supports demand (Figure 11). The heavily challenged industrial sector experienced improvement (+0.6 points), notably also in Germany, but remains the most depressed sector at -8.8 points (Figure 7). Services confidence saw a slight uptick of 0.3 points to 6.6 ( Figure 9). In contrast, construction was the only sector without improvement in March as it is still weighted down by tight monetary policy. The construction indicator stayed broadly unchanged (-0.1 points) at - 10.6 as the volume of order books keeps shrinking and demand shortages persist. Germany continues to be the main drag for construction confidence (Figure 13). Business employment expectations improved across the board (Figure 15), which coincides with lower unemployment expectations by households. An encouraging signal is also that selling price expectations have fallen quite substantially in services, retail and construction. The small uptick in industry price pressures is likely explained by the shipping disruptions (Figure 16).

## Overall sentiment indicates trough, but the recovery is likely to be slow

The survey results align with the message from the Eurozone composite PMI, which almost reached the neutral mark with 49.9 points in March. After the Eurozone economy stagnated in Q4, the data indicate that activity may have troughed. However, the recovery is likely to be slow. We expect a growth acceleration to 0.2% in Q1-24, but risks are to the downside. For further detail, see charts and tables in the Annex, including our UBS European Consumer Heatmap on page 8/9.

## EC Business and consumer survey – consumer confidence

## Methodology:

The Consumer Confidence Indicator (CCI) is the average of the balance series (i.e. the percentage of positive replies minus the percentage of negative replies) to the following four survey questions: 1. How has the financial situation of your household changed over the last 12 months? 2. How do you expect the financial position of your household to change over the next 12 months? 3. How do you expect the general economic situation in this country to develop over the next 12 months? 4. Compared to the past 12 months, do you expect to spend more or less money on major purchases (furniture, electrical/electronic devices, etc.) over the next 12 months?

## EC Business and consumer survey – business confidence

## Methodology:

The Business Confidence Indicators are produced for the industry, retail, services and construction sector. They are calculated as the average of the balance series to specific questions in each individual survey. The questions for industry are: volume of order books, stocks of finished products, production expectations. For retail: past sales, volume of stocks, sales outlook. For services: past business situation, past demand, demand outlook. For construction: order book, employment outlook.

## EC Business and Consumer Survey – overall sentiment indicator

## Methodology:

The Economic Sentiment Indicator (ESI) is a composite indicator of the Consumer Confidence Indicator and the Business Confidence Indicator. It combines the results through an aggregation of standardized input series with the following weights: 40% industrial confidence, 30% services confidence, 20% consumer confidence, 5% construction confidence, 5% retail trade confidence.
