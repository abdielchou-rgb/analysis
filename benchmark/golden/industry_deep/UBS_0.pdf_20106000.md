# APAC Economic Perspectives BI: Patiently holding before the easing cycle begins

## The policy rate was unchanged at 6% p.a., as widely expected

Bank Indonesia kept the BI Rate (7-day reverse repo rate) unchanged at 6.0% p.a. at its first policy meeting of the year, in line with consensus and UBS expectations. Two elements of today's analyst briefing suggest that BI has broadly checked the boxes for an easing cycle this year, with only the timing and magnitude of rate cuts left uncertain. First, BI has turned more confident on rupiah stability as it expects a Fed pivot. Second, BI reiterated its confidence that the path of inflation would be well managed within the target range. On growth, BI showed some optimism on domestic demand, reiterating its forecast range for 2024 GDP growth (4.7-5.5%), that is higher than last year's (4.5- 5.3%), despite expecting weaker global growth (2023: 3.0% vs. 2024: 2.8%) and a lower forecast range for the current account (-0.1 to -0.9% of GDP in 2024, compared to -0.4 to 0.4% of GDP in 2023).

## BI expects a Fed pivot to support rupiah appreciation

Governor Warjiyo shared that BI expects three Fed rate cuts this year (same as the median FOMC forecast), all in H2 2024. This is more than the 50bp of cuts expected at BI's December meeting. As a result, as explicitly noted in its policy statement (see here), BI believes the rupiah will strengthen moving forward, with less global uncertainty, lower bond yields in advanced economies, and a milder US dollar appreciation pressure. At the policy briefing, Governor Warjiyo hinted that BI may be countenancing a response to this anticipated Fed pivot. It reiterated that even as monetary policy remains "prostability" (i.e. FX stability), BI is now "seeing the possibility", and "becoming more open" in "how to respond to that direction" (of US dollar depreciation and rupiah strengthening).

## BI turns more confident on inflation and growth

In December, inflation fell to 2.6% y/y (from 2.9% in November), largely reflecting lower food inflation, which slowed to 6.2% y/y (from 6.7% in November). The Governor noted that food inflation is less volatile now, and attributed this to the government's collaboration with local governments to ensure better food supply. BI expects core inflation to end 2024 at 2.1% and headline inflation at 2.8%, within the government's target corridor of 2.5±1%. BI also sounded sanguine on growth. It clarified that macroprudential measures (including liquidity incentives for banks to lend to priority sectors) are its main tool to support growth. Governor Warjiyo also said that BI can continue to use "more granular" targeted liquidity incentives, rather than a blanket RRR cut. BI expects bank lending growth to be around 10-12% y/y in 2024, compared to 10.4% in 2023.

## The three preconditions for a rate cut

In our view, today's meeting underscored BI's three preconditions for a rate cut. 1) A Fed pivot, which it expects in H2 but which UBS expects in H1. 2) Contained inflation within the target range, and 3) Rupiah stability, which will be supported by lower UST yields, capital inflows, and a manageable current account deficit. We believe that all three conditions will be met this year, but we differ in terms of our expectations of the timing and extent of Fed rate cuts. Should the US economy experience a soft landing (and the Fed cuts only 75bp), we had highlighted that BI would be one of the most responsive central banks in this region to a change in the Fed rates trajectory, and would only be able to lower its policy rate by 75bp in this alternative scenario. For now, our baseline is that BI starts cutting rates in June, and by a total of 150bp in 2024.

## Thesis Map: Bank Indonesia Q: What policy direction does inflation signal?

Neutral. Bank Indonesia forecasts core and headline inflation within the 2.5±1% target inflation range in 2024. Headline inflation has fallen to 2.6% y/y from 2.9% y/y in November, largely reflecting lower food inflation, which slowed to 6.2% y/y from 6.7% y/y in November. However, food inflation risks due to supply issues and weather disruptions (including from El Niño) remain. BI thus raised its headline inflation forecast from 2.8% to 3.2% in 2024. Core inflation remains subdued. On a m/m basis, core inflation (excluding volatile goods and administered prices) has only averaged 0.1% m/m since May.

## Q: What policy direction does resource utilization signal?

Neutral to looser. BI projected Indonesia's growth to reach 4.7-5.5% in 2024 (vs 4.5-5.3% in 2023) on the back of strong government consumption confidence, the positive impact of the elections, and the sustainability of National Strategic Project (PSN) development. However, we believe that growth headwinds will increase as global demand slows. BI forecasted global growth to reach 3% in 2023 and 2.8% in 2024 and recognized slow growth in China due to weak household consumption and investment.

## Q: What policy direction does the financial stability situation signal?

Neutral. The rupiah recently recovered vis-à-vis the USD. Nevertheless, should the US treasury credit spreads widen due to recession risks, EM currencies could come under pressure. BI projects a current account balance of -0.4% to +0.4% of GDP in 2023 and -0.1 to -0.9% of GDP in 2024 (UBSe -0.5% in 2023, and -1.3% in 2024). Interventions to encourage export earnings to be deposited onshore, as well as the new SRBI and SUVBI instrument, could help capital inflows but would not be sufficient to support IDR in times of rapid dollar strength.

Prior to today's meeting, the Bloomberg median forecast is for BI's key policy rate to be 5.25% by the end of the 2024, implying 75bp of cuts. The upper end of the forecast range is 6.50% for end-2024 and the lower end of the forecast range is 4.75%.

We project policy rates to be remain on an extended pause from now, and for 150bp of rate cuts in 2024, from June onwards. Our view on Fed rates and easing inflation suggests BI has some space to cut rates in 2024.

If inflation surprises on the upside, then we would expect the policy response to include fiscal policy and coordinated supply-side interventions, in addition to monetary policy. However, having preemptively increased rates in 2022 and 2023, BI also has space to adjust interest rates downwards should growth or inflation trend down significantly going forward, especially as the Fed pivots. BI governor said that there is room to review BI's interest rate policy given the low inflation environment, if not for the strong USD.
