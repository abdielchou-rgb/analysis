## Executive Summary

• JACI China wrapped up the month with a total return of +0.78% vs. +0.14% in February. Strong demand from Chinese accounts and limited supply continued to provide supports to China USD IG credit. Ample domestic foreign currency liquidity continued to find its way in the USD bond market; onshore foreign currency loan-to-deposit ratio remained low at 0.44x. Onshore bond market a much cheaper source of funding given China USD IG credit is still trading 277bp higher in yield over onshore AAA credit.

• Several positive economic data has also helped market sentiment. The March manufacturing and non-manufacturing PMIs from both NBS and Caixin improved further, rising to their highest level since the 1Q23 COVID-19 reopening, suggesting the solid pace of economic activity carried on well into the latter part of 1Q24. On the external front, China’s FX reserves surprise on the upside again in March, rising Usd19.8 billion to Usd3.25 trillion. Gold reserves rose for the 17th consecutive month, though at a much slower monthly pace amid a gold price rally.

• China onshore bonds have also delivered decent return. CNY onshore Dim Sum Bond Index generated a return of +0.43%, compared to +0.6% in February. (pg 3) This is largely driven by lower rates. However, not all things are rosy as some parts of the China credit are still showing signs of stress, judging from their trading levels. Nearly 30% of private sector bonds are still trading at sub-80c levels, mainly concentrated within real estate. In fact, property bonds trading sub-50c and sub-80c represent 16% and 21% of the total private-sector bonds. (pg 10)

• Onshore gross issuance volume rose to Rmb5.0 trillion in March from Rmb3.1 trillion in February due to supply pickup from the SOEs (+63%mom), which accounted for over 97% of the total gross issuance for the month. POE gross issuance for the month also saw a significant pickup to Rmb91.9 million from Rmb38.5 million in February (pg 17). Demand for POE credits remained weak as it was largely towards refinancing rather than funding capex needs. (pg 13-19). In contrast, China supply in the offshore bond market remained muted. We saw Usd2.09 billion of supply in March, bringing YTD number to Usd8.45 billion, yet still higher than Usd6.80 billion in 1Q23.

• Maturities for the LGFV sector hit another record high in March (Rmb371 billion), doubling from Rmb185 billion in the previous month. With the quota for special local government bonds set at Rmb 3.9 trillion (vs Rmb3.8 trillion last year) during NPC, we think such a higher quota for special LGBs will increase the room for tightening of LGFV borrowing. Besides, that an SOE raised funds to support regional LGFVs, a practice we saw in late February, could also marginally ease refinancing needs and lower pressure on local governments in our view. (pg 21)

• The bullish momentum in CNY rates since 4Q23 has run out of steam, as recent upside data surprises reduce hopes of an imminent rate cut from the PBoC. Yields have largely remained range bound, sandwiched in between technical headwinds and structural dovishness from the PBoC. (pg 35-36)

• With money market rates failing to break lower (DR007 broadly on par with OMO rate) and front-end payers being carry-positive, the curve has flattened notably since the start of the year. To some extent, the swap curve even looks overly flat relative to the carry profile, pointing to risks of re-steepening from here. The expected mean reversion, however, is more likely if/when repo rates drift lower, prospects around which remain uncertain, taking into account accelerating issuance in the coming months. (pg 35-36)

## Explanation of Credit Research Valuation Methodology, Ratings and Risk to Ratings:

J.P. Morgan uses a bond-level rating system that incorporates valuations (relative value) and our fundamental view on the security. Our fundamental credit view of an issuer is based on the company's underlying credit trends, overall creditworthiness and our opinion on whether the issuer will be able to service its debt obligations when they become due and payable. We analyze, amon g other things, the company's cash flow capacity and trends and standard credit ratios, such as gross and net leverage, interest coverage and liquidity ratios. We also analyze profitability, capitalization and asset quality, among other variables, when a ssessing financials. Analysts also rate the issuer, based on the rating of the benchmark or representative security. Unless we specify a different recommendation for the company’s individual securities, an issuer recommendation applies to all of the bonds at the same level of the issuer’s capital structure. We may also rate certain loans and preferred securities, as applicable. This repo rt also sets out within it the material underlying assumptions used.

We use the following ratings for bonds (issues), issuers, loans, and preferred securities: Overweight (over the next three mo nths, the recommended risk position is expected to outperform the relevant index, sector, or benchmark); Neutral (over the next three months, the recommended risk position is expected to perform in line with the relevant index, sector, or benchmark); and Unde rweight (over the next three months, the recommended risk position is expected to underperform the relevant index, sector, or benchmark). J.P. Morgan Emerging Markets Sovereign Research uses Marketweight, which is equivalent to Neutral. NR is Not Rate d. In this case, J.P. Morgan has removed the rating for this particular security or issuer because of either a lack of a suffici ent fundamental basis or for legal, regulatory or policy reasons. The previous rating no longer should be relied upon. An NR desi gnation is not a recommendation or a rating. NC is Not Covered. An NC designation is not a rating or a recommendation.

For CDS, we use the following rating system: Long Risk (over the next three months, the credit return on the recommended posi tion is expected to exceed the relevant index, sector or benchmark); Neutral (over the next three months, the credit return on the recommended position is expected to match the relevant index, sector or benchmark); and Short Risk (over the next three month s, the credit return on the recommended position is expected to underperform the relevant index, sector or benchmark).
