# US Economic Perspectives July CPI recap: Ongoing progress

## Headline CPI +0.15%: 12-month inflation edges down to 2.9%

The headline CPI increased 15bp (seasonally-adjusted) in July with 12-month CPI inflation edging down from 3.0% in June to 2.9% in July — its lowest level since May 2021. Energy prices were essentially unchanged, and both food prices (+16bp) and core prices (+17bp) increased at moderate paces that would not be out of line with their pre-pandemic trends. The 0.15bp headline CPI change in July was a little below both our expectations and those of consensus (UBS Econ projection +0.19%, UBS Nowcast +0.17%, Bloomberg consensus average +0.20%).

The CPI for food away from home (i.e. restaurants) increased 21bp in July — close to their pre-pandemic pace (23bp). The size of these increases has trended down over the past couple of years, but they had picked up over the few months prior to today´s report. Along with rents (discussed below), food away from home tends to be one of the more persistent components and one of the better measures of the current inflation trend. Therefore the slowing in this component is a very good sign for slowing inflation.

## Core CPI +0.17%: 12-month core inflation edges down to 3.2%

Core CPI prices rose 17bp seasonally-adjusted in July — an increase that would not be out of place pre-pandemic. This core CPI increase was just a touch below both our expectations and those of consensus (UBS Econ projection 0.19%, UBS Nowcast 0.17%, consensus average 0.19%).

 July was continued progress: 12-month core CPI inflation rounded down from 3.3% (3.27%) last month to 3.2% (3.17%) in today's report. Core CPI inflation is down 3.4pp from its peak in September 2022 and for the 20th time in the 22 months from October 2022 the not-seasonally-adjusted core CPI one-month change (+7bp) was below the change 12 months prior (+16bp).

 While the remarkable downward progress over the past 20 months is showing little sign of stalling, core CPI inflation remains roughly 90bp higher than the level consistent with the FOMC's PCE inflation target.

## OER picked up as did some volatile components

Owners' equivalent rent (OER) picked up to a 36bp increase in July after a surprisingly weak 28bp increase in June. The 36bp increase in July is still notably below the 42bp to 47bp range that it had generally occupied in the 10 months before last month´s report; so today's data was still a good number, even if it was a little above our expectations (29bp). As the strength in rents account for most of the reason that core inflation remains elevated, OER will bear watching over the next few months to see if those elevated reading continue to ease as new tenant rent increases suggest. The path is unlikely to be smooth.

 Core goods prices declined for the 13th time in 14 months (falling 32bp in July) with used vehicle prices once again being the biggest downside contributor. Used vehicle prices fell 2.3%, a little larger decline than our expectations for a 1.9% decline. Our initial read of the JD Power Used Car Guide data for August suggests a smaller decline in next month's CPI release.

 Core non-rent services rose 21bp — a solid pick up from the declines of the past two months, but a little less than our expectation (27bp) and the pre-pandemic pace. Compared to last month the volatile components of lodging away from home and transportation services account for all of the pick up in core non-rent services, and, in fact, can account of all of the pickup in overall core inflation.

## July core PCE prices +0.17%

 Based on today’s CPI, and yesterday's PPI we project headline PCE prices rose 16bp, and core PCE prices rose 17bp, in July.

Today’s estimate of PCE prices, combined with our projection for slight downward revisions to prior months, would see 12-month headline PCE price inflation of 2.5% (2.52%) through July. For core PCE, today’s estimates place the 12-month change at 2.6% — though barely so at 2.645%. If the rounding stays at 2.6% this would represent no change from the current level, however either 2.6% or 2.7% is almost equally likely. Tomorrow's import price release could alter these PCE price estimates by a few basis points.

## Strengthening monthly increases likely ahead

 We expect a moderate strengthening in the CPI monthly changes to come through the October CPI (released November 13) as seasonal factors are less of a restraining force and used car prices are no longer pushing the core CPI down. That said, we still expect a moderate core increase (19bp) in next month´s release.

 12-month core CPI inflation is projected to remain within a couple of tenths of its current level until the end of this year as base effects make further downward progress difficult.

 We will review these forecasts as we go through the details of today's release and additional relevant data come in.
