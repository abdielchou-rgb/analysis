# Venture Vision: Allocation Tailwinds

US Endowments increased their alternatives allocation from 5% to 50% since 1990. US Public Pension funds lag this allocation by half but control nearly 7x the AUM. If they matched endowments' allocation, an incremental \$1.3trn would shift to alternatives, likely at the expense of public equities.

Yale's endowment fund led the way in alternative asset allocation. This has been re-enforced by a long-term trend of portfolio decisions from allocators accounting for \~\$850bn of AUM. Since 1990, US endowments increased their allocation to alternatives from \~5% to \~50% by the end of 2019. Public pensions managers have lagged in their allocation, moving from \~5% in 1990 to \~28% by 2019. However, this group accounts for nearly 7x the assets. Nonetheless, both have shown remarkable durability in driving the alternative asset theme frequently highlighted by our global financials teams.

The major difference between the two groups is their relative preferences for public equities in the balance of their allocations. Endowments have seen public equity allocations fall 18% points to 36% of their books. By contrast, the rise in alternatives for public pension plans has come at the cost of fixed income. For public pensions, the portion of public equities is at a similar level now to 1990 at around 48%.

More recently, as endowments slowed their pace of alternatives deployment, public pensions picked up the slack. Since 2010, US pension plans have been closing the gap on alternative asset allocations. 2009 was the peak in allocation discrepancy between endowments and pension plans; endowments had a 33ppt higher allocation vs their public pension counterparts. This difference has been shrinking since then as public pensions have increased alternatives allocations by roughly \~1.5ppt per year since 2006.

Public pension plans, relative to their endowment counterparts, are underallocated to alternatives and also have deeper pockets. Since 2000, public pension plans have grown their assets by 120%, now making up \$5.3 trillion. To illustrate the hypothetical impact, if this pension plan group copied the allocation strategy of endowments, an incremental \$1.3 trillion would become available to alternatives managers. Given also that fixed income's allocation in pension portfolios has fallen by half over the past 30 years, this hypothetical incremental increase in alternatives allocation would now be more likely to lead to a reduced proportional allocation in public equities; albeit not necessarily in absolute dollar terms of course.

Digging deeper into public pensions' allocations we find that recent momentum of deployment has favoured private equity and fixed income. Although, most long-term allocation patterns hold fairly steadily, compared to their long-term trend (since 2001) private equity and fixed income has seen the most positive momentum in the shorter term (since 2015). Fixed income, which has decreased \~11ppt in allocation since 2001 only lost \~2ppt since 2015. As allocations have stabilised in recent years, private equity continued to be the faster growing asset allocation since 2015, increasing at 6ppt vs 11% since 2001.

## FINTECH RADAR - ADAM WOOD & MARK HYATT

## THE FINTECH RADAR (8 SEP 2024)

• #1 3forge secures investment from Morgan Stanley: This week (4th September) 3forge, a high-performance code platform used by tier-one banks and buy-side firms, announced that it has closed on an investment by Morgan Stanley. This marks the first time that 3forge has raised external capital since its launch in 2011. Robert Cooke, founder of 3forge was quoted saying: “We are thrilled to close on an investment by Morgan Stanley, a longstanding partner who truly understands the value and performance of 3forge technology. This is an exciting milestone as we continually expand our capabilities to help enhance client workflows and productivity.” The firm plans to use the investment to accelerate its global go-tomarket strategy and expand its development community.

• #2 Ziina raises \$22m in Series A: This week (2nd September), UAE-based payments start-up Ziina has secured \$22m in a Series A funding round led by Altos Ventures, with participation from Fintech Collective, FJ Labs, Avenir Growth, Y Combinator, and Jabbar Internet Group. Ziina intends to use the funds to broaden its product offering and move towards its ambition to become an end-to-end financial services provider. This latest round brings total funding to >\$30m. Launched in 2021, Ziina originated as a peer-to-peer payments app for splitting bills, but over the years has broadened its offering to allow businesses to use their digital wallet to send and received payments as well. Ziina now runs its platform in two segments: Ziina Personal (P2P consumer app for splitting bills) and Ziina Business (which is used by businesses to collect payments). The business offering now includes features like a payment gateway, POS solutions, integrations with eCommerce platforms, and CRM functions. The firm reportedly serves 50,000 business and retail customers.

Unless otherwise indicated, the news reports referenced above have not been confirmed.

## Real-time valuation monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
