## Global Inflation Strategy Global Inflation Monday

## The wrong uncertainty

Long breakevens. Last week was plagued by the wrong kind of uncertainty for linkers. Markets questioned momentum in risk appetite and if the Fed's price for disinflation might be higher in the end after all, but uncertainty wasn't enough to keep energy prices higher. Breakevens suffered. But it was not a pivotal week for new information on inflation and we stick with breakevens. Our fundamental and tactical assessments still align on being long. We have no new trade recommendations or themes this week.

Value in linker asset swaps? Asset swaps have been cheapening globally (except US <5y). Inflation 'iotas' have been cheapening. Does that make linker asset swaps good value? In some cases it certainly looks like it because linker asset swaps are a leveraged version of nominal spreads. We see value in TIPS and Euro spreads compared to nominal - the incremental carry is marginal, but high enough quality. For buy-and-hold investors, significant proceeds spreads in UK are worth a close look.

## Linker asw and cheap iotas

Asset swaps always seem a struggle for strategists, perhaps because it's harder to appeal to economic fundamentals for help. But they look cheap historically (except shorter UST perhaps), linkers can be a way to a further pick up, and that further pick-up, the 'iota', has been increasing recently (Figures 4 and 5 below.)

We'll get the main conclusions up front because the discussion also quickly gets involved. Today's whip through is already lengthy but needs further development.

 TIPS iota looks cheap. Preferring TIPS to nominal asset swaps or TIPS breakeven

'iota' refers to the difference in valuation between the price of inflation in bond format and it's price in swap format. It is often estimated as the difference between the zspread for an inflation-linked bond and a nominal comparator bond. When we say 'iota' is cheap, we mean that bonds breakeven inflation to swaps should improve the carry-volatility tradeoff and there may be upside in a tightening.

## Value in linker asset swaps means 'iota

Iota is to swap spread as breakeven is to yield. The key question for spread investors, when confronted with linkers, is the risk-reward of any incremental spread.

## Iota more than pays for illiquidity

Linkers generally imply a step down in liquidity. But this clearly fails to justify the additional iota pick-up available in TIPS and most non-French European linkers. In almost no stress events, is it likely that bid-offer on an inflation-linked bond would exceed prevailing iotas in most cases (see Figures 22 and 28).

Regulatory liquidity rules could be distortive in principle, but official rules don't penalise inflation linked bonds with respect to comparable nominal bonds. Inflation linked are 'fixed rate' bonds for the same credit for HQLA and collateral eligibility with central banks. Neither are collateral haircuts differentiated (Fed, ECB, BoE). Banks are supposed to consider the expected cost of unwinding market risk hedges (FX, rates, inflation) when valuing HQLA, but that is not material compared with prevailing iota spreads.

## Iota also pays for incremental volatility

Incremental carry due to iota is also attractive compared to incremental volatility for higher-iota issues in the US, Spain and Italy. Inflation asset swaps are more volatile on a daily basis than nominal asset swaps, but higher daily vol is mitigated over time because iotas seem historically better bounded than outright asset swap levels. 30y Treasury asset swaps, for example, have traded a range of around 100bp to -40bp since 2015 while 30y iota has traded \~40bp to 10bp, with a tight distribution around 20bp-25bp. (Figures 6.) With this in mind, the carry/volatility ratio is attractive.

Figure 7 shows that, for TIPS, the incremental carry earned by the iota nearly covered annualised volatility over the past year for issues up to the 10y area. Even at the long end, where the price volatility of spread risk is much higher, the incremental carry due to the iota covered nearly 40% of the P&L volatility in an iota trade over the past year. There is room here to carry and to try to exploit mean reverting levels for entry-exit.

## Mean reverting, but hard to predict - need a better title

Besides a reasonably strong apparent historical anchor, what does drive iotas? Overall demand for inflation linked bonds in other formats, overall demand for spread vs liquidity, volatility, the base level of nominal asset swap spreads are all obvious candidates and all appear to have some predictive power, especially when they themselves are volatile. But these possible drivers are all correlated too. Regressions suggest that only the general level of breakevens and volatility really matter. As expected, high breakevens imply tighter iotas - presumably because they reflect underlying demand for the cash bonds. Higher volatility appears to scare liquidity providers toward greater liquidity.

Right now we have both reasonably high breakevens and inflation pressure and we'd expect that to continue. Volatility, meanwhile, is low. With the Fed likely on hold until after the summer, we don't expect it to pick up from here. TIPS iotas, however, are wide. It looks like there is value here.

## Euro iota are also high-quality pick-up for asset swap investors

Most of the observations above centred on TIPS also apply to Euro linkers. European iotas are also historically very stable but the relationship between breakeven level and iota may be different: linkers breakevens may outperform swaps when breakevens fall. That could be related to liquidity, because if selling of inflation is skewed toward more liquid swaps, less liquid cash (including SPGBei for example) may outperform.

## UK iotas are more complicated

The usual short-cut to iota is a spread of z-spreads, but that might get us into trouble. The implicit assumption in z-spreads that something parallel to the the nominal swap curve is a good way to judge the term structure of the sovereign curve. That's not a good assumption anywhere now, especially in the US and UK where swaps are so much flatter than government curves. We report 'true' iotas that discount on a fitted issuer curve in the charts at the end of this report to correct for deficiency in the usual z-spread estimation. Doing this in the UK, breakevens appear expensive compared to nominal bonds.

## Value for some is because linkers leverage long-end gilt/sonia value.

All this is abstraction though. Transactable asset swap levels (i.e. proceeds asw) are very attractive. Figure 10 shows the levels for Thursday 18th. High long-end spreads are available because the heavily back-loaded linker cash flow schedule is much more valuable when priced as a swap against sonia than as a gilt, because the sonia curve is much flatter. An investor able to generate sonia+10bp for 30 years (say) can turn that into 5.3% in GBP, 5.55% in USD, or around 4% in EUR. (Cross currency basis pays USD and EUR investors to lend their cash against GBP.) All these numbers are highly orientative, but 4% has attracted the attention of European insurance through structures that package it all for them.

## As a buy-and-hold investment, management is what matters

As buy and hold investment this may stack up, as long as you are confident of being able to fund the swap competitively over time. (The swap will progressively move against the buyer, who earns positive carry, but who has sold the bond cash flows in the swap which will increase with RPI.)

## Risk factors for shorter-term trade include the level of nominal asset swaps and breakevens

As a speculative trade, though, a high spread does mean more attractive carry. But that needs to be considered against other risk factors. Linker asset swaps will tend to widen when nominal asset swaps widen and breakevens rise. Linker asset swaps are a

leveraged play on the former, especially, which motivates the obvious concluding question: what might turn around the global cheapening in long-end asset swaps?

## Key themes in nominal asset swaps may continue

We won't go back over views on nominal asset swaps in detail here though. You have a view on the US here and on Euros here. Relative abundance of bonds will continue - there will be no collateral scarcity or shortage of acceptable paper in flight to quality for now.

What could turn the widening tide? The answer depends on currency and sector. Limiting the discussion to the long end where spreads have been most volatile, we see only two factors: a pick up in volatility to boost appetite for bonds, or a change in behaviour from pensions to put cash toward duration instead of other assets. (Lifecycle hedging in the Netherlands is further down the line, but could be significant.)
