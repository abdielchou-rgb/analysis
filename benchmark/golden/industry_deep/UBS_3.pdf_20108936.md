# European Economic Perspectives European Central Bank Watch

## Three more cuts from Riksbank this year, two from ECB & BoE, one from SNB

We expect the Risksbank (which turned a bit more dovish in last week's meeting) to deliver another three cuts this year, the ECB and BoE two cuts, and the SNB one - in fact the final one. In line with the consensus, we firmly expect the ECB to pause on 18 July, at 3.75%. On 1 August, we expect the BoE to deliver its first cut (-25bps to 5%) and the Riksbank to follow on 19 August with a 25bps cut to 3.5%. According to our forecast, the Riksbank would move again in September (-25bps to 3.25%), as would the ECB (- 25bps to 3.5%) and the SNB (-25bps to a terminal rate of 1.0%). While the SNB would already be "done" by September, all other central banks would deliver more cuts in Q4- 24 and over the course of 2025 - but in a highly data-dependent fashion (see Fig. 5).

## ECB: Next cut in September; rates to fall to 3.25% by end-24, 2.25% by end-25

We think the likelihood of a rate cut on 18 July is extremely low and expect the next 25bps cut on 12 September, followed by subsequent cuts on a quarterly basis (Dec, March, June, Sept, Dec). This would imply another 50bps of cuts this year and 100bps in 2025, bringing the depo to 3.25% by end-2024 and 2.25% by end-2025. Yet, we regard the risk to our 2025 ECB forecast as skewed to the upside, i.e. towards less than 100bps of cuts. The markets currently price -18.7bps by September and 43bps by end-2024 (cumulative).

## BoE: First rate cut on 1 August; 50bps of cuts in 2024 and 175bp in 2025

We expect the first BoE rate cut on 1 August 2024 (-25bps), followed by another 25bp cut in November, implying cumulative 50bp of cuts this year and bringing Bank Rate to 4.75% by end-24. For 2025, we expect cumulative cuts of 175bp to 3% by end-25. However, with risks to the inflation outlook skewed to the upside and given the uncertainty about the degree of easing by the other major central banks (i.e. Fed and ECB), we acknowledge that our projection of the speed and extent of cuts in 2025 might be too dovish.

## SNB: Next - and final - rate cut in September, to 1%

Following the second rate cut in June, we expect one last 25bps rate cut in September, to a terminal rate of 1%. The SNB views this as the broadly neutral rate according to a recent speech by Chairman Jordan (who will chair his last meeting in September, and will be succeeded by Martin Schlegel). However, we acknowledge that the timing of the next easing step is uncertain and also depends on exchange rate developments and policy decisions by other central banks; hence we see a risk of a delay. The market looks to be currently pricing -17.7bp for September and a cumulative -27.2bp by the end of this year.

## Riksbank: Next cuts in August, September, December; 100bps cuts in 2025

After staying on hold at 3.75% last week, the Riksbank opened the door for more easing this year by adjusting its previous guidance for two rate cuts to "two or three" in the second half of 2024. This reassures us in our view for three more 25bps cuts this year, with the next one expected for August, followed by September and December, and a total of 100bps cuts at a quarterly pace in 2025. This would bring the Swedish policy rate to 3.0% by end-24 and 2.0% by end-25. Markets are currently pricing a cumulative 20.8bps of cuts by August, 34.2bps by September, and 70.3bps by December.

While the June cut was well advertised by the ECB, the pace of subsequent rate cuts is much less clear. The ECB has stressed that it will "not pre-commit to a particular rate path". Instead, the pace of future easing will be highly data-dependent, with a focus on (a) the inflation outlook, (b) the dynamics of underlying inflation, and (c) the strength of monetary policy transmission - but with the relative importance of the first pillar likely increasing. This suggests that the ECB's quarterly macro projections - particularly for inflation - will be crucial. That said, data on wage growth, profit margins, productivity, unit labour costs will be also be very important as early indicators for services inflation, core inflation, and eventually, headline inflation - see Figures 6 and 9.

## Strategy implications

European Rates: We are once again looking to receive the Dec'25 meeting (currently: 2.69%) as we see a limited risk/reward of receiving the Dec'24 meeting compared to end May, when we pivoted into a more bullish stance on core bonds. ECB cuts should support valuations as will the slowdown in EGB supply in summer, but we think levels will also depend on the outcome of the French elections and news on 2025 budgets. In a scenario where a French government with an absolute majority explicitly rejects the Stability and Growth Pact, we would expect 10y France vs Germany spreads to test a level of c. 130bps with 10y bund yields around 2.30%. We maintain a long bias on German ASWs as we expect uncertainty to persist. German ASWs are significantly cheaper compared to our valuation framework based on the free float of German bunds and credit risk measures such as the iTraxx. German 10y ASWs have widened by nearly 10bps to c. 35bps in June. EUR 2s10 steepened in June, but we remain cautious on whether or not the time is right for structural steepeners in the front-end of the curve.

Once again looking to receive the Dec'25 meeting but eyes on French politics

FX: Election-related uncertainty has lowered the euro-optimism, with investors chasing USD, GBP or CHF as long alternatives for the euro. We think the US dollar could continue to benefit from its dual appeal of being high-yielding and defensive, and keep our EURUSD target unchanged at 1.05 for Q3 (here). See page 11 for all our FX forecasts.

## BoE

Next meeting: 1 August 2024; Bank Rate currently at 5.25%

Market view: The markets appear to be pricing 14.8bps of cuts by August, 21bp by September and cumulatively 43bp by the end-2024.

UBS view: First 25bp rate cut in August 2024, followed by another 25bp cut in November. Deeper cuts in 2025.

At its last meeting on 20 June, the BoE left the policy rate unchanged at 5.25%, a decision supported by seven MPC members, while two members voted for a 25bp rate cut, all in line with expectations and unchanged from the May meeting.

Despite the unchanged split of MPC votes, the minutes of the meeting noted that, within the group of seven members supporting no change, for some "the policy decision at this meeting was finely balanced”. For those members, the recent strength in services inflation did not have a significant impact on the overall assessment of the disinflationary progress since the upside surprises were largely coming from regulated and volatile components which should not have an impact on the medium-term inflation outlook. As regards the forward guidance, the MPC reiterated that "[it] will keep under review for how long Bank Rate should be maintained at its current level", while specifically referring to the next meeting on 1 August, which will also feature new macro projections.

Going into the June meeting our baseline already foresaw the first 25bp rate cut in August and the Committee's more "finely balanced" decision supports our expectations. Looking beyond August, we expect one more cut of 25bp this year - in November, to 4.75%. Given our expectations of further easing in labour markets and wage growth leading to a decline in inflation, we expect the BoE to continue cutting rates in 2025. With our baseline foreseeing inflation hovering around 2% from early 2025, we expect the Bank to accelerate the pace of cuts to every meeting from Q2 2025, i.e. cumulative cuts of 175bp to 3% by end-25. However, with risks to the inflation outlook skewed to the upside and given the uncertainty about the degree of easing by the other major central banks (i.e. Fed and ECB), we acknowledge that our projection of the speed and extent of cuts in 2025 might be too dovish. The markets look to currently price -14.8bps for August, a cumulative 43bp of cuts by end-2024 and 70bp of rate cuts in 2025.

What to watch: May GDP (11 July), June inflation (17 July), labour market data (18 July).

## Strategy implications

Rates: With our expectation of the BoE cut in August, we see potential for some steepening in the SONIA 2s5s curve vs EUR. The SONIA curve remains more inverted than other markets, while the SONIA 2s5s vs EUR lags other closely correlated parts such as SONIA 2s10s vs EUR box. We expect some convergence as the BoE eases its policy stance. Fiscal uncertainty surrounding the UK election could lead to an increase in Gilt term premia, thereby providing some steepening impetus. In the front-end, we took profit from the receive August MPC trade following the June BoE meeting, and we initiated an August/November flattener. The August meeting is currently priced at - 15bps, which appears fair amid a more dovish MPC tone. However, services inflation remains high and, with Clare Lombardelli replacing Ben Broadbent on 1 July, the market is questioning her voting intentions. Depending on the next inflation and employment prints, the August MPC cuts could be pushed further out, resulting in a flatter August/ November curve. The market is pricing c.35bps of cuts cumulatively by November. If the data continue to ease, we see room for more cuts being priced in the September and November meetings. Thus, the August/November curve could flatten further.

FX: We remain moderately bullish on GBP, with ongoing support from elevated BoE interest rates; we do not anticipate the UK elections to be a significant driver for FX. With the outcome for EUR being quite binary in the near term due to political concerns related to France, we have lowered our end-Q3 target on EURGBP to 84c (here), in line with our existing end-2024 target. ## SNB

Next meeting: 26 September 2024; SNB policy rate currently at 1.25%

Market view: Markets look to be pricing -17.7bp for Sept. and a cumulative -27.2p by end-24.

UBS view: Next and final cut on 26 September 2024 (-25bp to 1%)

As we had expected, the SNB cut its policy rate again in June, by 25bps to 1.25%. The inflation forecast was lowered further and the statement noted that the SNB "will adjust its monetary policy again if necessary to ensure inflation remains within the range consistent with price stability over the medium term". The SNB also seemed concerned about the strength of the CHF, noting that "political uncertainties in Europe" were behind the recent appreciation and that this is "contributing to uncertainty about the development of inflation remaining elevated". It reiterated that it is"willing to be active in the foreign exchange market as necessary".

In our view, the communication in June was dovish and left the door open for further easing. In particular, the SNB gave the impression that the June rate cut was not just in response to the marked appreciation of the CHF in the wake of political uncertainty stemming from the French election. First, Chairman Jordan said that monetary policy currently was not expansionary and that the real neutral interest rate was in the ballpark of 0% (reiterating the point from his recent speech). While he admitted uncertainty around the point estimate, he said this was an important guiding post. Adding 1% inflation (the mid-point of the inflation target range) would suggest a 1% nominal neutral rate. Second, he noted that the new inflation projection (the SNB's main guidance tool) was downward sloping, implying that further policy easing might be needed to keep inflation stable at target. He also suggested that the lower SNB inflation forecast reflected lower second round effects (from rents, wages etc.). Third, he mentioned that the output gap was closed, but noted that growth was moderate and below trend, and stressed growth risks notably from abroad. As such, he said that the June rate cut was also a response to the moderate growth outlook.

We maintain our call for one more rate cut in September to a terminal rate of 1%, i.e. to "neutral". Chairman Jordan will chair his last meeting in September, and will be succeeded by Martin Schlegel. We acknowledge that the timing of the next easing step is uncertain and also depends on FX developments and policy decisions by other central banks; the risks are tilted towards a delay. The market looks to be pricing -17.7bp for September and a cumulative -27.2bp by the end of this year. We will closely watch SNB balance sheet data for signs that the SNB intervened to counter CHF strength.

What to watch: June CPI (4 Jul); June balance sheet data (31 Jul); ECB Meeting (12 Sep)

## Strategy implications

Rates: Following the June SNB meeting, we entered into a long SARON 5y5y outright trade recommendation (entry: 1.25%, target: 1%, stop: 1.4%, current: 1.12%). We are holding onto this trade because we anticipate the SARON 5y5y rate to approach 1%, which aligns with the SNB's nominal neutral rate estimate. Flow-wise, we are seeing strong receiving interest from CTA accounts, particularly in the 2y and 10y sectors. This trend is likely to continue in the near term, and we think that there might be room for CHF rates to rally. That said, the SARON 1y1y rate, currently at 0.83%, is close to the lowest level seen since last December. We would look to pay tactically below the 0.75% level. UBS expects a terminal rate of 1%, with SARON 1y1y trading around 20bps below this level. Also, the ESTR vs SARON 1y1y is currently at the highest level of this cycle. We expect some accounts to fade this widening in cross-market spread. In the very front end, the September SNB meeting is currently priced at -15bps. We would consider receiving the September SNB meeting if pricing falls below -10bps.

FX: The rise in political uncertainty in Europe presents a binary set of possible EURCHF outcomes, with the upside being quite limited for now (end-Q3 target at 97c, here). However, we still like shorting CHF against currencies such as USD, AUD or GBP where we anticipate the ‘higher for longer’ narrative to persist.

## Riksbank

Next meeting: 20 August 2024; policy rate currently at 3.75%

Market view: -20.8bps of cuts priced by Aug, -34.2bps by Sep, -70.3bps by Dec (cumulative)

UBS view: Next 25bps cuts in August and September, subsequent cuts on a quarterly basis, to 3% by end-24 and 2% by end-25

Amid an easing inflation and a weak domestic economy, the Riksbank had started the easing cycle in May by cutting its policy rate by 25bps to 3.75%, but then stayed on hold at last week's meeting. This was in line with our and consensus expectations, given that the Riksbank had explicitly guided for the next rate cut to come only in H2 2024 and the flow of data since the rate cut in May had been mixed.

After the first rate cut, inflation came in lower than forecast by the Riksbank for April but surprised to the upside (on the back of volatile travel-related items) in May. Economic sentiment indicators improved, but hard data remained sluggish; the exchange rate appreciated by 3.2% in trade-weighted terms. Accordingly, the Riksbank in its June meeting assessed recent inflation developments to be in line with expectations, while economic activity was seen somewhat weaker and the krona slightly stronger than anticipated. The Riksbank therefore lowered its 2024 rate path projections, stating that: "If the prospects for inflation and economic activity remain the same, the policy rate may be cut two or three times during the second half of the year". This is a dovish change from the previous guidance of two more rate cuts in 2024.

The monetary policy report reveals that the Riksbank pays close attention to corporate pricing behaviour (as reported in surveys such as the Economic Tendency Survey), which is now assessed to be in line with the inflation target. Moreover, inflation expectations have improved further, "signalling strong confidence in the target". In addition, we have argued for some time now that - compared to the ECB - the better visibility and moderation of wage growth gives the Riksbank more confidence in its inflation forecast. This allows the Bank to commit to further policy easing more explicitly than the ECB. Nevertheless, the Riksbank still stresses the need for gradual easing in light of remaining uncertainties, implying that the latest shift in the guidance should not be interpreted as a sign for subsequent cuts at every meeting for the remainder of the easing cycle. Risks to the inflation outlook remain, stemming from geopolitics, krona developments as well as the extent and speed of the economic recovery in Sweden.

The Riksbank's updated guidance for "two or three" more rate cuts this year reassures us in our call for the next 25bp rate cut on 20 August. We then expect another 25bp cut at the September meeting, followed by a pause in November and another 25bps cut in December. This would bring the policy rate to 3.0% by end-2024. The timing of the cuts in our profile mirrors the fact that we forecast CPIF inflation to start undershooting the 2% target in June and to keep falling until September. This would allow the Riksbank to deliver two subsequent cuts in August and September before shifting back to a quarterly profile of cuts. We expect the quarterly pace to be maintained throughout 2025, with four 25bps cuts to a terminal rate of 2%. The current market pricing is for 20.8bps of cuts by August, 34.2bps (cum.) by September and a total of 70.3bp cuts this year.

What to watch: June Manufacturing PMI (1 July), June inflation (12 July), June unemployment (26 July), July Economic Tendency Survey (30 July)

The relatively dovish guidance from the Riksbank, combined with a resilient US dollar, should continue to add upside pressure on EURSEK, in our view. We maintain a moderately bearish outlook on SEK, with an end-Q3 target at 11.40 on EURSEK. In addition, we like to engage in tactical NOKSEK longs, aiming to buy dips sub-98.50c, with Norges constrained to keep rates higher for longer likely to act as short-term support for NOK.
