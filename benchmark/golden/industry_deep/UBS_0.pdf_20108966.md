# EM Equity Strategy Mid-year review: Can the narrow rally sustain?

## Five stocks contribute \~80% of EM returns YTD

The 7.7% return in MSCI EM YTD is the best start to the year in five years, but is also the narrowest - with just five stocks accounting for 78% of the index returns (primarily North Asia AI/internet themes, Figure 3). While EM has underperformed DM by 4.5% YTD, this has to be seen in context of EM EPS seeing 0-1.5% upgrades for 2024/2025 over this period as against DM seeing 3-4% upgrades. EM valuation discounts to DM are highest in history, but need to be seen alongside degradation in EM ROEs relative to DM (Figure 712m fwdROE: Misngthe avupl byD ockr ). In absolute terms, EM valuations are the highest in 15 years (barring the COVID disrupted period, Figure 4 & Figure 5). We see slower returns performance in H224 with election- and policy-related risks in EM, with further risks from delayed Fed rate cuts and USD strength. Despite the unfavourable macro backdrop, the region is poised to deliver stronger earnings growth vs DM (Korea/Taiwan tech account for 30%+ of our projected 13% EPS CAGR over the next couple of years). We roll forward our index targets, with mid-year 2025 MSCI EM/AxJ at 1140/745 respectively - implying 2- 4% upside.

## We remain overweight China amid the recent market pullback

We turned overweight China in April 2024 but since our upgrade, MSCI China has declined 9% from its May peak on mixed macro data, particularly lingering concerns on the turnaround of the property sector. Fundamentally, the recovery in corporate earnings for Chinese stocks is not different from that of other markets post pandemic, the ROE gap has closed over the past 18 months (Figure 32), and belies macro worries. While we keep an eye on slower-than-expected domestic consumption recovery that may weigh on domestic consumption stocks, overall earnings momentum is stabilising following 1Q24 results, particularly in large stocks in the internet sector (39% of MSCI China). We believe the market still has scope to outperform with continued government policy support and increasing focus on capital returns through dividends and buybacks. Geopolitics need to be monitored, especially in the run-up to the US elections in November 2024.

## Macro-political factors and rates drive changes to our market preferences

We upgrade South Africa to Overweight (from Neutral), with political uncertainty reducing following formation of cabinet post elections. We anticipate market-friendly policies, including SOE reforms. After multiple years of underperformance, South Africa is among the cheapest markets in our universe. We upgrade Singapore to Neutral (from Underweight) on less stretched valuations, solid earnings momentum in index heavyweights while higher-for-longer rates should support banks' earnings. Within LatAm, we see rising policy uncertainty in both Brazil and Mexico. While both markets have underperformed, EM portfolios are already 5y high O/W in these markets. We believe equities may struggle to perform until we get clarity on fiscal discipline in Brazil and the extent of constitutional reforms in Mexico. We downgrade Brazil to Neutral, and Mexico to Underweight. Mexico faces headwinds from slowing US growth, FDIs and remittances, while the upcoming US elections could bring further uncertainty, in our view. Valuations, premium to Brazil is the highest in nearly a decade; the political situation (and the corresponding impact on fiscal position) could weigh on investor sentiment in the near term. ASEAN Fx in focus: Fx and related policy actions have probably been the more influential factors on ASEAN equities YTD. Our Asia Fx strategy team helps decode the pushes and pulls across IDR, THB, MYR and PHP. Of these, the team believes PHP is closest to bottoming out (we rate Philippines equities OW), while there could be near-term headwinds for THB and IDR (and also MYR to a smaller extent).

Brazil and Singapore are the most favourable based on these factors, while India and Thailand are the least favourable.

## ASEAN Fx in focus - UBS outlook

PHP: The Philippines peso has been one of region's underperforming currencies YTD, especially over the past three months. Notwithstanding the pressure from building expectations of US-PH policy divergence, we think the peso's peak underperformance is behind us with 60 likely to be a strong resistance in the next couple of months. By end '24, we expect USDPHP to pare back some of the underperformance and ease towards 57. Three reasons: 1) The twin deficits have been consolidating since '22 with both expected to compress by 0.6ppt in 2024 (UBSe) to -2% of GDP (Current account) and -5.6% of GDP (Fiscal balance) respectively. 2) Countercyclical peso is finding some support from imports compressing domestic demand/investment growth slowdown. 3) Notwithstanding the known-unknowns, our models find the peso as 3% cheap with fair value closer to 57, a level of weakness not seen since late 2022. We also expect it to outperform ASEAN peers such as the THB and IDR.

IDR: Recent weakness of the rupiah (IDR) has been driven by market concerns that the incoming President's future fiscal policy could potentially be expansionary. This concern, alongside broader dollar strength, has sent IDR/USD to its weakest level since early 2020. Note: On the fiscal policy, UBS expects no major departure from authorities' 3% fiscal deficit cap, and expects only a gradual widening of the deficit from -2.3% in 2024 and -2.8% in 2025 (vs. -1.7% in '23). A widening, nonetheless. Meanwhile, Indonesia's '24 current account is also expected to deteriorate by 1ppt of GDP in '23 - a transition from a position of surplus/balance in '22/23 into a deficit in '24. This deterioration in Indonesia's twin deficits profile is why we remain underweight both the rupiah and IndoGBs. In fact, narrow ID-US yields' gap also adds to our defensive stance on the rupiah, via two channels of slow non-resident bond portfolio flows and via the channels of corporates' external debt deleveraging or/and slower conversion of exports' proceeds. Lastly, declining FX reserves adequacy could also potentially ease intensity of BI's FX intervention policy.

THB: THB has been one of the worst performing EM currencies this year, despite a notable improvement in tourist arrivals (to 86% of pre-pandemic levels). This underperformance has been, and could potentially continue to be, driven by: 1) wide US vs Thailand rates gap - at about 20+y extremes - continues to weigh on the financial account (foreign bond flows and residents' dollar deposits). This drag is further compounded by expected 4bn\$ outflows in coming ten months, to partially fund India's bonds' index inclusion. 2) The CA balance cushion is also quite depressed with past three months' surplus averaging only \~0.2% vis-à-vis 8% in '16-19. This is partly due to a slower recovery in tourists' per capital spending and drag from slower China growth, as visible in TH-CN goods balance deterioration by 4ppt since '19. Although the baht's underperformance reflects some of these risks, we see risk of an extended weakness amidst ongoing political uncertainty.

MYR: Although the MYR has continuously been dragged lower by a weak BoP flows position, a stronger intensity of authorities' pushback has shielded the ringgit vis-à-vis ASEAN peers. This policy stance, alongside signs of potential FDI help from China supply chain relocations and depressed REER valuations keeps us neutral on the ringgit into 2H. The risks however still lean towards potential FX weakness for two reasons: 1) the basic balance has deteriorated to the bottom decile of 10y history (\~1% of GDP), while resident outflows (PI assets + errors and omissions) are quite elevated at c.4% of GDP ('21-23 average). 2) Malaysia's net FX reserves (forward book included) screen vulnerable. After having fallen for two straights years, their import cover ratio at \~4 months (hovering at 10y lows) screens quite weak on our FX reserves adequacy metrics.
