## Developed Markets

## United States:

Over the weekend, President Biden announced that he will be stepping aside as the Democratic candidate and endorsed Kamala Harris to lead the Democratic ticket. Delegates are not bound to vote for any particular candidate at the convention in late August. In this unprecedented move, the US presidential election has now been pushed into uncharted territory. However, the announcement appeared to have a muted impact on global markets thus far. USTs initially modestly bull-flattened, and the USD was relatively stable, down just -0.1% on Monday.

Overnight, the PBoC lowered its policy rates by 10bp, in its first cut since August 2023. Our economists note that the easing is likely a result of policymakers' view of worsening growth in China in the recent months and clearer prospects of an upcoming Fed cut. Despite the fact that the move was somewhat unexpected, there has been more limited impact on markets.

It was a light volume NY session given the absence of first-tier economic data releases across the globe. Additionally, the Fed is currently in its blackout period ahead of the July FOMC meeting, which meant there was also no Fedspeak as a catalyst for market movements. USTs bull-flattened overnight, though traded in a narrow range. Mid-morning, the UST curve began to bear-steepen and reversed the earlier flattening of the curve without any clear driver behind the move. It was likely exacerbated by low liquidity. By NY close, USTs moderately bear-steepened, with 2y yields up 1bp and 30y yields up 3bp.

US equities rebounded after Friday’s sell-off in response to the global cyber outage. The S&P 500 rose 1.1%, the largest daily gain in over a month, led by gains in technology and industrials. Oil extended its recent decline, with Brent crude oil futures down -0.4% amid heightened political uncertainty.

## Japan:

In the Tokyo morning, JGBs twist-steepened ahead of the 1-5y TAP auction. There also appeared to be some concession efforts in the super long-end ahead of the 40y JGB auction on Wednesday. Although the TAP auction was weaker than expected, the impact on short- and medium-term JGBs was limited since the supply size was minimal. Market implied pricing for the July BoJ meeting receded and fell to 11.5bp, which is indicative of only a 25% probability of a 15bp hike. Long-end JGBs continued to sell off into the afternoon, and by Tokyo close the curve twist-steepened; 2y JGB rallied 0.5bp to 0.335%, 10y JGB sold off 1.0bp to 1.050%, and 40y JGB sold off 4.0bp to 2.415% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve bear-steepened and outperformed JGBs across the curve, especially on the long end. XCCY basis widened across the curve driven by the long end. In the Tokyo evening session, futures quickly sold off by 20c following reports that an LDP official had called for the BoJ to send a clear message toward normalization. The BoJ will conduct scheduled JGB purchases for 1-3y, 3-5y, and 5-10y sectors on Tuesday.

UST yields and the US dollar slid and traded choppily during Tokyo trading hours following President Biden’s announcement that he was stepping aside as the Democratic candidate. USD/JPY initially moved lower toward 157.16, but it gradually bounced back toward 157.61 into the Tokyo afternoon. Into the London morning, USD/JPY gradually declined toward 156.29, which may have been aided by unwinds of USD/JPY carry trades on the back of higher volatility of the pair as well as some market participants’ lower expectations for carry. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY.

## Europe:

European FX performance was mixed on Monday with limited catalysts. EUR and GBP gained modestly and rose 0.1% against the USD. Brent crude prices extended their decline from Friday and fell -0.4% to \~\$82/bbl in the NY afternoon, which weighed on NOK. EUR/NOK and USD/NOK rose 0.5% and 0.4%, respectively.

European duration traded heavy on Monday without any clear catalyst, and the 10y Bund closed up 3bp to 2.49% but outperformed on a cross-market basis. The curve bear-flattened, led by the weakness in reds on the strip. On the curve, the 5y point led the underperformance, and curvature expressions such as 2s5s10s cheapened by \~1.4bp during the day. The cash curve continued to underperform compared to swaps, and the overall ASW German complex headed tighter, led by the Schatz (- 1.4bp). Country spreads continued to tighten, led by the BTP which outperformed peers; the BTP/Bund spread closed 3bp tighter at 127.8bp.

In the UK, our economists note that Chancellor Reeves said she will consider giving wage increases for almost 2 million government employees later this month, as two independent pay review bodies have recommended a 5.5% y/y wage rise for teachers and NHS workers. The estimated cost of this measure would be of about £3bn, according to the IFS, which also added that if approved, this should have to be funded through increased taxes, borrowing, or a cut government spending in other areas.

## Dollar Bloc:

In the Dollar Bloc, the antipodeans underperformed. AUD (-0.6%) saw its largest decline since early June, as industrial metal prices came under pressure. Concerns around growth in China resurfaced as well, after the PBoC somewhat unexpectedly cut the main policy rate by 10bp to 1.7%, which also weighed on AUD. NZD fell 0.6%, which limited the AUD/NZD decline to -0.1%. The DXY USD Index was fairly stable and fell just -0.1%.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• ECB Governing Council Member Kazimir stated that the eurozone’s economic situation remains fragile. He added that the “door remains open” for additional cuts and that current market pricing of 50bp of cuts through December 24 is not “entirely misplaced,” but is not a baseline scenario

• BoE Executive Director for Markets Saporta delivered a speech on the BoE’s balance sheet. Our economists note that the BoE remains on the path of recalibrating its balance sheet in such a way that assets backing reserves are largely provided via repo operations. The Bank welcomed the recent increase in use of STR facility. The steady state of the balance sheet is yet to be determined, though our economists highlight that the Bank's preference seems to be to complement STR with ILTR, as opposed to hold a meaningful stock of gilts

## Emerging Markets

• The PBoC unexpectedly lowered its 1y Prime Rate to 3.35% (C: 3.45%; P: 3.45%) and its 5y Prime Rate to 3.85% (C: 3.95%; P: 3.95%), and cut rates for the first time in nearly a year. The easing was likely driven by policymakers' view of worsening growth in recent months, as well as clearer prospects of an upcoming Fed cut

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
