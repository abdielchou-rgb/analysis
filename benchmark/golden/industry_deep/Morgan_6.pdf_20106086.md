## Our current stance on Asia FX and rates

The developments in the past two weeks are in line with our view change two weeks ago, when we closed all our risk-on trades and recommend long USD and paying front-end rates trades. There are a few points to make:

1) It makes sense to see the Fed pushing back against the aggressive rate cut that is in the price in 2024: Again, we want our readers to focus on the financial conditions index, which has been easing since last November. In other words, the market has done the easing on behalf of the Fed, which might still be concerned about decent job data and a resilient inflation print. We will watch out for the annual revision to the seasonal factors for US CPI on February 9.

2) China data remain quite weak, evidenced by PMI, credit growth, CPI and retail sales. The downside surprise and recent government focus on regulating the financial sector have weighed on investor sentiment. Meanwhile, there doesn't seem any urgency from policymakers – GDP growth in 2023 is 5.2%Y, growth is weakening but past growth relies on an old model and the government is targeting high-quality growth. The gap between what the market would like to see and what the government is willing to do remains wide.

3) Recent data in Asia have been a bit weak: This is seen in most countries' PMIs (see Asia Economics: PMI Update: A Broad-based Decline, January 3, 2024). The weakness has been driven by new orders and production. While we don’t believe that the data themselves could be the driver for the market, recent data disappointment could add to the price action.

4) Investors are in the mode of profit-taking: When we turned more constructive on EM Asia in early November, we made the point that most macro hedge funds made low singledigit returns before October, and the FOMO among hedge funds into year-end could drive the market should there be a trend in USTs and USD. This is exactly what we have seen with lower USTs and a weaker USD. Most macro hedge funds generated 3-5% in the last two months of 2023. Hence, these investors have the incentive to take profit should last year-end's trend reverse.

With all these points in mind, we are comfortable with our current stance, which is to be cautious on Asia FX and rates via selectively long USD and paying rates exposure. To be clear, we believe that the current market trend should be faded at some point when the Fed starts to cut rates, but the timing is not now.

In FX, we like long USD/THB, short CNH/INR and short TWD/INR. We would like to increase our long USD exposure by adding long USD/PHP 3m NDF. The trade deficit is deteriorating on the margin, PHP has passed the favourable remittances seasonality and the BSP is probably one of the most dovish central banks in Asia now. We like long USD/ PHP 3m NDF, targeting 57.6 with a stop at 55.1.

We have done a deep dive into Korea's pension fund's outbound investment behaviour, and the conclusion is that the usage of the BoK FX swap line is limited. This indicates that the BoK still has plenty of room to remove the NPS' USD demand if needed. We believe that recent KRW underperformance creates an opportunity to be long KRW at a certain point. We are watching levels between 1,350 and 1,360.

In rates, we stick to both receiving 1-year CNY NDIRS and long onshore CNY-hedged 1- year CGBs as the growth momentum remains weak and the Chinese stock market is under pressure. We like paying 1-year INR NDOIS. Despite inflation being a bit lower, we believe that the RBI will keep the policy rate stable and liquidity tight to protect INR. Lastly, we stick to receiving 2-year KRW NDIRS versus paying 2-year USD. The BoK's shift to a neutral stance is meaningful, and with just \~50bp of rate cuts priced for this year and 7bp priced for next year, we see more room for BoK cuts to be priced in.

## Has Korea's NPS used up its FX swap line with the BoK?

The context: The BoK introduced a US\$10 billion FX swap line with Korea's National Pension Service (NPS) in late 2022 and increased its size to US\$35 billion in April 2023. As the NPS needs to buy foreign assets every month to increase its strategic allocation to foreign assets, the monthly USD buying/KRW selling activity puts downward pressure on KRW. The purpose of the FX swap line is to reduce the pressure on KRW.

Quite often, investors ask us the following questions: 1) How much foreign assets does the NPS need to buy every year? 2) Will such purchases increase annually? 3) How does the FX swap line affect USD/KRW demand? 4) How much of the swap line has been used? and 5) What does it mean for USD/KRW? We address all the questions below.

## The NPS's strategic and tactical asset allocation

Korea's balance of payments: Korea's current account surplus has declined sharply in recent years, mainly due to Korea's increasing energy imports and, to a lesser extent, a smaller transport services trade surplus ( Exhibit 3 ). The good news is that Korea's primary income surplus has been increasing thanks to Korean corporates' repatriation of foreign dividends, providing an offset.

Despite the deterioration, Korea's current account remains in a surplus. The excess savings over investment has allowed Korea to invest those savings abroad, leading to decent-sized outflows in the financial account ( Exhibit 4 ).

A good part of such outflows is from Korea's National Pension Service (NPS), which is a public pension fund created by the government in 1987. As of October 2023, it has KRW 968 trillion or US\$717 billion in assets under management.

Strategic allocation: Many domestic pension funds in other countries have increased their allocation to equities and foreign assets, and it is no different for the NPS. For example, its strategic medium-term target equity allocation has increased from 30% to 45% between 2018 and 2023 while its bond allocation has decreased from 60% to 40%. Meanwhile, its targeted allocation to alternative assets has increased to 15% from 10%.

Allocation to foreign assets: Within the strategic allocation, the NPS also set up an annual allocation target for each asset class – domestic equity, foreign equity, domestic bonds, foreign bonds and alternatives. What is important for the market is that the target allocation to foreign assets has been increasing. For example, the foreign equity allocation is up from 22% in 2020 to 30% in 2023, and the foreign bonds allocation is also up from 6% to 8% during the same period.

## How much foreign assets has the NPS bought in recent years?

We estimate that the NPS has bought \~US\$25 billion of foreign assets per year: To calculate the number, we take into account the annual AUM change, the fluctuation of USD/KRW, the annual return of each asset class and the NPS' allocation to foreign assets. The conclusion is that the NPS bought US\$16 billion, US\$24 billion, US\$24 billion and US \$25 billion between 2019 and 2022 ( Exhibit 11 ).

This is broadly consistent with the amount shown in Korea’s balance of payments. In 2019- 22, the gross portfolio outflows from the government sector were US\$15 billion, US\$28 billion, US\$24 billion and US\$18 billion, respectively. In 2023, as of November, the balance of payments data suggest that the NPS outflows were US\$28 billion.

## How much does the NPS need to buy annually in the future?

There are two sources of buying foreign assets: First, the NPS would continue to increase its allocation to foreign assets at a steady pace. As discussed above, the annual increase could be around 1.6% of AUM assuming there is no valuation effect of foreign assets versus domestic assets. This could be evenly split between foreign bonds and equities. This indicates US\$717 billion (current AUM) \* 1.6% = US\$11.5 billon purchase of additional foreign assets every year.

Second, the NPS also receives new contributions from pensioners every year. Our calculation suggests that its overall assets have been increasing by about 10% every year, which is a reasonable assumption for future growth. Meanwhile, the NPS' long-term return target is 5.2%. This implies around a 5% annual increase in AUM due to new contributions. Given that the NPS targets at least a 50% allocation to foreign assets, this suggests US\$717 billion \* 5% \* 50% = US\$18 billion.

Overall, our calculation suggests that the NPS needs to buy around US\$30 billion of foreign assets in the next few years in order to achieve its target allocation. This amount is consistent with the amount of foreign purchases seen between 2019 and 2022.

## Why did the BoK introduce the FX swap line with the NPS, and how does it work?

The BoK-NPS FX swap line was initiated in September 2022, when broad USD strength pushed USD/KRW towards 1,400, a level not seen since March 2009. To alleviate the KRW depreciation pressure, the authorities rolled out a slew of measures, which included a US\$10 billion FX swap deal between the BoK and NPS that ran until end-2022.

In April 2023, the swap deal was renewed and expanded to US\$35 billion. In December 2023, when it was due to expire, it was renewed again and is currently scheduled to run until the end of 2024.

How does the FX swap work? The FX swap deal allows the NPS to obtain the USD it needs for its foreign investments from the BoK's FX reserves instead of the FX spot market, in exchange for KRW. The FX swaps have a maturity of six months or one year, so after the stipulated period of time, the NPS would need to return the USD to the BoK's FX reserves.

## How much of the FX swap line has been used?

This suggests that we may be able to get an indication of how much of the BoK-NPS FX swap line has been utilised by looking at the FX hedge amount disclosed by the NPS.

The NPS started implementing a tactical FX overlay on its portfolio between May and July 2022, consistent with media reports suggesting June ( Exhibit 14 ). The FX overlay amount ramped up in 4Q22, coinciding with the signing of the BoK-NPS FX swap deal.

Interestingly, the FX overlay at the end of 2022 was US\$11.7 billion, more than the US\$10 billion BoK-NPS FX swap deal, and the size of the swap deal was expanded in April 2023 when it was renewed.

As of September 2023, the NPS had a tactical FX overlay of US\$13.2 billion, or 3.5% of its total foreign currency assets.

This leaves at least US\$21.8 billion of the swap line still available for the NPS to tap on.

## What's the implication for KRW?

The NPS is a significant investor in foreign assets: From 2018 to 2022, the NPS' investment in foreign equities accounted for nearly 40% of Korea's total equity outflows on average ( Exhibit 16 ). Last year, that ratio jumped to as high as 86% (as of November). On the bonds side, around one-third of Korea's total bond outflows have been driven by the NPS in the past two years ( Exhibit 17 ). Our analysis above suggests that the size of the NPS' foreign investment should increase by about US\$5 billion per year in the next few years.

NPS FX-hedging policy: As the NPS invests on an FX-unhedged basis, its flows can have an impact on KRW. The NPS is usually only allowed to have a ±5% tactical FX overlay, of which it has used -3.5% as discussed above. Until the end of this year, it is also allowed to temporarily increase its FX hedge ratio by up to 10% in the event of unusual FX moves, but this has not been utilised so far.

NPS flows may have a larger impact when KRW is already under pressure: This shows that the NPS' foreign investment is just one of the many flows that drive KRW, and can be outweighed by other factors. The NPS outflows may have a larger impact on the currency when KRW is already under depreciation pressure from other factors.

BoK-NPS FX swap could help to ease depreciation pressure: That said, for this year at least, with a minimum of US\$22 billion of the BoK-NPS FX swap line still available, the NPS could tap on it when KRW is under pressure to avoid exacerbating the move. Therefore, we think that the broader USD and risk environment will be more important in determining the KRW direction.

USD is key for the USD/KRW trend: Given that we still see room for USD upside tactically, we remain neutral on KRW for now. However, USD/KRW is approaching attractive levels to sell for the medium term, with the top end of the recent range at \~1,360. When we start to see catalysts for USD weakness, we'd expect KRW to outperform despite the NPS outflows.

Watch for maturity of the BoK-NPS FX swap deal: For next year, though, if the BoK does not renew the FX swap deal with the NPS, then KRW could face more downward pressure as the NPS would need to buy USD not just for its new foreign investment, but also to repay the BoK when the swaps mature. For this reason, the BoK-NPS FX swap deal may be terminated only when KRW is on an appreciating trend.

## Stay neutral on SGD amid MAS status quo

MAS to keep policy unchanged: After moving to a quarterly meeting schedule, the MAS will announce its next decision on January 29. Policy is likely to remain unchanged. Since the last meeting, Singapore's inflation has continued to decelerate, imported inflation is still declining by 1%Y, oil prices have fallen by 13% and S\$NEER has remained at \~1.4-1.6% above the midpoint of the MAS' policy band. At the same time, core inflation remains relatively elevated at 3.2%Y, limiting the scope for easing.

S\$NEER valuation looks rich: Based on our estimates, S\$NEER is currently trading \~1.5% above the midpoint of the MAS’ policy band. Considering that the MAS is likely to keep policy unchanged, the current S\$NEER valuation looks rich to us.

However, this rich valuation has been sticky. S\$NEER has been staying \~1.4-1.6% above the midpoint of the band since late October. This came as the significant USD weakness propped up S\$NEER, largely offsetting downward pressure on the basket from the SGD depreciation against the risk currencies ( Exhibit 23 ).

USD strength, preferably with equities rallying, is most conducive for S\$NEER weakness: This suggests that the best environment for S\$NEER to weaken is one where USD and equities rally simultaneously. Indeed, this is what historical moves suggest when we look at the weekly changes in S\$NEER versus moves in equities (S&P 500) and USD over the past five years ( Exhibit 24 ).

While USD and equities rallying simultaneously may sound rare, it has happened in 52 out of the past 260 weeks (five years), or 20% of the time. One environment in which this could happen is where the US economy outperforms the rest of the world, driving flows into US assets.

In rates, we are biased to look for higher SGD yields: The MAS stepped up its bill issuance significantly in 4Q23 to \~S\$5-6 billion per month, the highs seen in 2021/22, as US rates rallied aggressively ( Exhibit 25 ).
