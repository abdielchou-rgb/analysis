# European Economic Comment

# SNB: A surprise cut with a more uncertain outlook

SNB lowers policy rate by 25bp to 1.5%

As the first G10 central bank, the SNB lowered its policy rate by 25bp to 1.5% in today's meeting. Our expectation had been for no policy rate change, but we had acknowledged that it was a close call. The policy statement notes that "The easing of monetary policy has been made possible because the fight against inflation over the past two and a half years has been effective. For some months now, inflation has been back below 2% and thus in the range the SNB equates with price stability." It also notes that the SNB will "adjust its monetary policy again if necessary to ensure inflation remains within the range consistent with price stability over the medium term." Regarding FX interventions and as expected, the statement retained the sentence that "The SNB also remains willing to be active in the foreign exchange market as necessary." In the press conference, Chairman Jordan stressed that the SNB would not hesitate to purchase FX if necessary and reiterated earlier statements that the size of the SNB balance sheet was not a concern in this regard. As expected, no changes were made to the minimum reserve requirement and tiering framework.

## Rationale for the decision

The decision to cut policy rates already in March seemed to have been driven by at least three factors: First, the inflation forecast path has been shifted lower by around 50bp across the board - with the end-point now showing 1.1% in 2026 (down from 1.6% in December). According to the SNB, the revision reflected a quicker decline of goods inflation since December and weaker second-round effects. Regarding the latter, the SNB no longer expects further increases in the mortgage reference rate and hence forecasts lower rent inflation pressure. Also, Chairman Jordan noted that wage growth did not present risks to price stability. Second, the SNB apparently became much more concerned about the appreciation of the real exchange rate over the past year (Figure 12). In this regard, the policy statement notes that the policy rate cut also supports economic activity. This was a more surprising consideration, given that the Swiss franc depreciated since the start of the year and is lower than at the time of the December meeting in nominal and real terms. Also, the concerns had not featured prominently in past meetings, and the growth momentum has held up well in our view. Third, Chairman Jordan outlined in the press conference that risk management considerations supported today’s rate cut: The SNB sees less risks in lowering rates earlier than in keeping rates restrictive for longer.

## Conditional inflation forecast lowered markedly

The new conditional inflation forecast shows 1.4% for 2024 (-50bp), 1.2% in 2025 (- 40bp) and 1.1% in 2026 with a new end-point of 1.1% in Q4 2026 (50bp lower than the end-point of the previous forecast). The forecast still foresees an increase of inflation over the coming months with 1.4% and 1.5% in Q2/Q3 2024, respectively. This likely reflects the delayed impact of rents following the December mortgage reference rate increase which should impact notably May inflation. As today’s interest rate cut should be positive for growth, the SNB also revised up its growth forecast for 2024 to 1% (from between 0.5 to 1% at the December meeting). This is broadly in line with our forecast of 1.3%, given that our forecast includes the impact of major sports events while the SNB's forecast does not.

## More uncertainty and open questions

Today's decision arguably makes the SNB policy outlook more uncertain and we see at least two risks. First, the focus is now on other central banks. While the SNB takes independent decisions - and underlined this again with today's decision - , the actions of other central banks matter not least for the exchange rate. The policy statement acknowledged the risk of tighter monetary policy than expected "in some countries". Accordingly, the SNB did not commit to further hikes at today’s meeting, stating that this dependent on the updated inflation forecast. Second, the current conditional inflation forecast now shows inflation essentially at target with an end-point of 1.1%. This opens up the key question how much more policy easing the SNB actually needs to deliver, given that monetary conditions now seem appropriate. Chairman Jordan noted in the press conference that some academic studies (whose results he did not officially endorse) suggest a neutral real interest rate of 0-0.5% for Switzerland. Using a mid-point of the inflation target range of 1%, this would point to a nominal neutral rate of 1-1.5%. However, if indeed the SNB expected overall policy easing to remain limited, it would open up the question why it decided to cut the policy rate at such an early stage.

## We still expect two cuts, but risks are for fewer

Following today's decision, we expect two more rate cuts this year in June and September to a terminal rate of 1%. However, given the uncertainties noted above, we stress the risk that the SNB may lower policy rates by less (given Jordan's reference to neutral rates) and may also decide to not cut every meeting (in case other central banks do not cut in June or in case inflation surprises to the upside in coming months). At the time of writing, the market was pricing c.19bp for June 2024, c.18bp for September 2024 and a cumulative c.72bp by the end of this year (including today's cut).

## Strategy: Entering into a 1y1y ESTR vs SARON tightener

Against our expectations of a pause, the SNB decided today to cut its policy rate by 25bps, stating that "the fight against inflation over the past and a half years has been effective." According to the SNB's new inflation forecast, inflation is expected to remain below its 2% target throughout the entire forecasting period from 2024 to 2026. Before the meeting, we had warned that the risk of the SNB cutting its policy rate in March could keep the ESTR vs SARON spread at wide levels. Following the SNB's decision today, we recommend Receiving 1y1y ESTR vs SARON (open: 156bps, target: 140bps, close: 166bps) as the spread looks attractive from a valuation standpoint. We anticipate further convergence over time as the ECB begins to ease its policy stance.

Our economists maintain the call for two more cuts from the SNB in June and September, ultimately reaching a terminal rate of 1%. The market is currently pricing c.72bps of cuts (including March) by year-end. We think that the SNB's capacity to further reduce interest rates, beyond what the market is currently pricing, is now more limited. Further, as discussed above, Jordan’s reference to neutral rate, could lead to less cuts or a more gradual cutting cycle from the SNB.

While the ECB's focus on domestic inflation pressures could maintain pressure on the EUR front end, our baseline assumes 75bps of cuts from the ECB in 2024 and an additional 100bps of cuts in 2025. The market is currently pricing in c.62bps of cuts for 2025, which does not seem excessive. We anticipate that the ECB will embark on a gradual easing cycle, indicating that further cuts will be priced into 2025 as inflation continues to ease, leading to a further decline in the ESTR 1y1y. Moreover, comments from several ECB officials have indicated that June is the most likely date for the first ECB cut. Additionally, the FOMC's decision yesterday to maintain the three cuts in its 2024 projections could provide some reassurance that the global easing cycle is underway.
