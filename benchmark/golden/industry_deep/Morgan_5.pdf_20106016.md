# Chinese Growth/Tech – Active Fund Flows and Short Interest

Outflows from active managers on Chinese equities continued in Jan-24. Chinese Growth/Tech segment was under particular pressure due to lower trading liquidity and higher short interest. Passive funds also joined the selling, leading Hang Seng Tech index to lag MSCI China by 7% MTD.

## Long-Only Fund Flows:

• According to EPFR data, US and EU active managers' weekly net outflows from China/HK equities were estimated to be US\$0.56bn in January MTD vs. US\$0.92bn in December.

• Although the pace of outflows has slowed by \~40% post the peak of yearend redemptions, selling is ongoing. As the trading liquidity of China/HK equities was 25% lower in January MTD vs. December, the selling pressure persists.

• Passive funds have turned to be net sellers on China/HK equities in Jan-24 MTD with net outflows of US\$0.3bn. In 2H23, they were one of the rare net buyers on China with marginal net inflows of US\$0.7bn; their recent selling did amplify the downside pressure from market microstructure perspective. ( Exhibit 7 )

• For Chinese Growth/Tech segment, we estimate that active managers have net sold US\$2.3bn MTD, with large-cap names, like Tencent, Alibaba, Baidu and Meituan leading. ( Exhibit 1 & Exhibit 3 ).

• On the flip side, inflows were recorded on NetEase and Xiaomi ( Exhibit 3 ).

## Short Interest:

• According to IHS Markit data, outstanding short positions in the broader China/HK equities have decreased in January MTD ( Exhibit 2 ), but hedge funds were specifically adding new shorts in the Growth/Tech segment, which also helps explain the recent underperformance of Growth/Tech segment.

• Per our estimations, among the large-cap Growth/Tech stocks, there were US \$1.7bn of new short positions were added, in which Alibaba, BYD, NIO and JD dominated. ( Exhibit 5 )

• Short covering activities were mostly recorded in Staples, Info Tech, Utilities and Real Estate. We note that it is the third consecutive month of short covering in China's Info Tech sector, which we believe is a result of hedge fund de-grossing. ( Exhibit 2 )

• Regarding return implications of short interest fund flows, the most-shorted names continued to underperform the least-shorted names. i.e., the overall price action in stock loan market is still momentum driven. ( Exhibit 8 )
