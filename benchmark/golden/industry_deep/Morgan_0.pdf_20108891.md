# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 27

Riksbank on hold alongside lower rate forecasts; USTs rally following weaker-than-expected 1Q24 consumption; PHP gains despite dovish BSP meeting; JGBs bear-steepen; Banxico on hold, noting recent volatility; Fed's Bostic sees one cut in 4Q24; DXY at 105.92 (-0.1%); US 10y at 4.286% (-4.3bp).

• Riksbank maintains policy rate as expected, but forward guidance points toward easing, with forecasts signaling that the policy rate could be cut two or three times in 2024; SEK underperforms, falling 0.4% against the USD.

• USTs rally as US data show some weakening in economic growth with softer-than-expected 1Q24 GDP revisions, including a half a percentage point downward revision to personal consumption alongside increases in continuing jobless claims and a softening in durable goods ex-transportation .

• PHP (0.2%) gains despite a dovish tone from the BSP; rates are left unchanged, but inflation forecasts are revised down, and BSP Governor Remolona adds that an August rate cut is “somewhat more likely than before.”

• Growing concerns of further BoJ hawkishness amid the continued rise in USD/JPY send JGB yields higher, with the market-implied probability of a hike in July up to \~70%.

Banxico maintains its policy rate at 11.0%, with the statement highlighting the “noticeable” depreciation of MXN alongside the “idiosyncratic factors [that] generated high volatility,” though it also adds that the balance of risks for economic growth is “biased to the downside”; MXN falls 0.6% against the USD.

• Atlanta Fed President Bostic reiterates his view for one interest rate cut this year, in 4Q24, adding that he penciled in four rate cuts in 2025 in the June SEP.

It was a fairly quiet day across global duration, even alongside five central bank meetings. Riksbank, CBT, BSP, and Banxico all maintained their current policy rates, in line with expectations, while the CNB opted for a larger-than-expected 50bp rate cut. In terms of forward guidance, our economists interpreted the Riksbank and BSP as leaning dovish, with forecasts and commentary paving the way for future cuts. CBT retained a tightening bias, and CNB highlighted that "no further major rate cuts should be expected" despite the larger-than-expected cut. Bancixo added to its forward guidance the notion of weaker activity, and that the balance of risks for economic growth is biased to the downside, which our economists interpret as dovish.

USTs traded in a narrow range overnight while the USD weakened alongside the plethora of central bank meetings. In the NY morning, economic data were mixed but largely pointed to a potential slowdown in economic growth. 1Q24 GDP was revised upward to 1.4% q/q (P: 1.3%), as expected, but it was driven by slower imports rather than faster demand growth. Additionally, personal consumption was unexpectedly revised half a percentage point lower to 1.5%, driven by less international travel and less spending on gasoline than was earlier assumed. On the other hand, core PCE for 1Q24 was revised upward to 3.7% (P: 3.6%), but this did little to move markets, as market participants are likely more focused on Friday’s PCE data as opposed to data that can feel outdated by this point.

In addition to the GDP revisions, other economic data also hinted at some weakness, with an unexpected buildup in retail and wholesale inventories, a rise in continuing jobless claims, a fall in new home sales, and sizable downward revisions in durable goods orders. USTs fell \~4-5bp following the data and traded in a narrow range leading into the 7y auction.

Even after the rally in the NY morning, the 7y auction stopped through by 0.3bp as UST auctions maintain solid demand. This week the 2y, 5y, and 7y auctions all either came on the screws or slightly through the when-issued yield. The 7y auction had the lowest primary dealer allotment since October, with dealers left with just 11.9% given that allotment grew for both direct and indirect participants. Allotment to direct participants grew to 18.5% (P: 16.1%), above the six-auction average of 17.6%, alongside a rise in indirect allotment to 69.7% (P: 66.9%), also slightly above the sixauction average of 67.3%.

USTs largely maintained their gains following the auction and ended the day \~3-4bp richer across the curve ahead of the first US presidential debate and the PCE data release on Friday. USD weakened further following the weak economic data in the NY morning, but pared its losses, and the DXY USD Index ended the day down just 0.1%.

The S&P 500 rose for the third consecutive day, albeit a modest gain of just +0.1%. Real estate outperformed as US economic data reinforced speculation around future Fed rate cuts. Gold (+1.4%) rose ahead of the US PCE data and as politics come to the forefront, with the US presidential debate and upcoming French and UK elections.

Japan:

In the Tokyo morning, JGBs sold off aggressively across the curve on the back of the overnight sell-off in USTs. Concern among market participants of further BoJ hawkishness may have grown given that USD/JPY broke above 160. Despite the strong sell-off in the Tokyo morning, the 2y JGB auction was slightly stronger than expected, with a cut-off price at 100.04 (C: 100.03). Although the relatively strong outcome of the 2y auction pushed back against the aggressive sell-off pressure, JGB yields continued to rise into the Tokyo 3pm close. By Tokyo close, 5y JGB sold off 4.5bp to 0.595%, 10y JGB sold off 5.0bp to 1.070%, and 30y JGB sold off 4.0bp to 2.275% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS also sold off, led by the belly of the curve. XCCY basis tightened across the curve.

With fiscal concerns related to the upcoming first US presidential debate, UST yields rose across the curve, and accordingly USD/JPY finally broke above 160. During Tokyo trading hours, USD/JPY had moved lower from 160.8 to 160.3 as market participants became increasingly cautious about the risk of MoF intervention. As USD/JPY approached the lowest level since 1986, Japanese Finance Minister Suzuki became vocal about the concern for the recent FX move, stating that the MoF is “strongly concerned about the impact on the economy” and “will analyze the background to this move with a high sense of urgency, and take necessary action as needed.” Other CCY/JPY generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY except AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, and SEK/JPY. AUD/JPY continuously moved higher toward 107 into the London morning on the back of steady inflation data in Australia and a hawkish RBA stance. SEK/JPY traded choppily and moved lower instantaneously, right after Riksbank maintained its policy rate at 3.75%, but stated the policy rate can be cut two or three times in the second half of the year.

## Europe:

It was a relatively quiet day in G10 FX as investors awaited the US presidential debate Thursday night and US core PCE data on Friday. One of the main events was the Riksbank meeting where, as expected, rates were left unchanged; however, forward guidance and forecasts tilted toward the direction of easing. As a result, SEK underperformed and fell -0.4%, whereas its Scandinavian neighbor NOK gained +0.1%, driven partly by US dollar weakness and partly by strength in oil and gas. NOK/SEK posted gains of +0.5% and rose back above 0.9950. The Swiss franc fell - 0.1% against the USD as investors turned back to funding with low yielders following the SNB’s rate cut last week. USD/JPY remained above the 160 mark with no signs of intervention from the MoF, and JPY gained +0.1%. GBP (+0.2%) and EUR (+0.3%) gained on the back of USD weakness.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar was broadly on offer after US data was poorer than expected, with a 0.5% downward revision to 1Q24 personal consumption, which sent UST yields down and dragged the US dollar along with it. The DXY USD Index fell -0.2% during the London session. On the back of the weakness in the USD, AUD, CAD, and NZD all strengthened by 0.1%.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies recovered modestly after the breakthrough in key levels in USD/CNH and USD/JPY on Wednesday. As the USD/CNY fixing was set higher for the seventh straight session to 7.1270, USD/CNY rose slightly by 0.03% to 7.2689, not far from the top end of the allowed trading range. USD/CNH rallied over the time of the fixing release, but eventually retraced and finished the session \~0.04% lower at just under 7.30. CNH was a relative underperformer in the region along with MYR, which weakened 0.1%. KRW, THB, and PHP outperformed. After opening higher at 1,395, USD/KRW gradually retraced over the course of the session to finish 0.2% lower, aided by the rebound in JPY. THB was also up 0.2% as gold prices advanced 0.4%. PHP gained 0.2% despite the more dovish tone from the BSP, possibly aided by the outperformance of the local equity market (PSEi: +1.2%). Most other AxJ currencies (INR, IDR, SGD, TWD) were up by 0.1%, in line with the magnitude of the USD weakness.

Most AxJ rates continued to sell off. China was an exception as it extended its outperformance alongside the underperformance of Chinese equities (CSI 300: - 0.8%; Hang Seng Index: -2.1%). The China rates curve bull-flattened; 10y and 30y CGBs rallied 2bp, which brought 30y CGB yields just 1bp away from the April low of 2.41%. Hong Kong, Singapore, and Korea were the main underperformers following the Wednesday overnight sell-off in USTs; curves bear-steepened, and 10y swap yields rose 7bp, 4bp, and 3bp, respectively. The sell-off in other AxJ rates was more modest. In India, 10y G-sec yield rose slightly by 1bp, to 7%, ahead of Friday’s index inclusion. In Thailand, 10y ThaiGB yield rose 1bp to 2.7% while NDOIS yields were stable, with limited impact from the Deputy Finance Minister’s comments that the BoT’s inflation target is too low and the government wants monetary policy to help boost the economy. In Indonesia, 10y IndoGB yield also rose just 1bp.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The Riksbank maintained its policy rate at 3.75% at its June meeting (C: 3.75%). The statement leaned dovish and signaled that the policy rate could be cut two or three times during the second half of 2024. Risks appeared balanced as the Board cited that "the conditions for an inflation rate in line with the target also in the slightly longer term are good." However, the latest CPIF ex-energy inflation data was acknowledged to be higher than expected, showing that there can be setbacks. The baseline policy rate path forecasts \~3 cuts for 2H24, including in August, with a relatively high probability. In terms of forecasts, CPIF was revised down across the horizon to 2.0% y/y in 2024 (P: 2.3%), 1.8% y/y in 2025 (P: 1.9%), and 1.9% y/y in 2026 (P: 2.0%). CPIF ex-energy was not revised so markedly and stands at 2.6% y/y in 2024 and 2.0% y/y in 2025 and 2026. GDP was revised upwards to 1.1% y/y in 2024 (P: 0.3%) and taken down to 1.7% y/y in 2025 (P: 1.9%).

• RBA Deputy Governor Hauser said it would be a mistake to set policy based on one number, but conceded that CPI “picked up a little bit,” pointing to strong jobs as a factor supporting the strong services prices. He also pointed out that stubborn services inflation is not unique to Australia

• Atlanta Fed President Bostic reiterated that he expects one rate cut this year in 4Q. He went on to say that he penciled in four rate cuts in 2025 in the June SEP

## Emerging Markets

• The BSP kept its policy rate unchanged at 6.50% at its June monetary policy meeting (C: 6.50%; P: 6.50%) while striking a dovish tone. The central bank cut its risk-adjusted inflation projections to 3.1% y/y for 2024 (P: 3.8%) and 3.1% y/y for 2025 (P: 3.7%), and now sees the risks to the inflation outlook shifting to the downside instead of the upside due to lower import tariffs on rice. The BSP noted inflation expectations remain well anchored, and if inflation eases sustainably in 2H24, there could be more scope to consider a less restrictive monetary policy stance

• BSP Governor Remolona said the central bank is still on track to cut rates in 3Q24 and sees an August rate cut as being “somewhat more likely than before,” adding that the monetary policy stance is “somewhat more dovish” now. He noted that rate cuts would likely be done in 25bp increments, with a total of 50bp cumulative cuts on the table for this year. Regarding FX, Remolona said the BSP does not want PHP to depreciate sharply, and steps into the market when there is some stress or the market becomes dysfunctional. He confirmed the central bank intervened on Thursday’s session. Remolona also flagged that every 1% fall in PHP only has a 0.036% effect on inflation, which he said is “not very large”

• The CBT maintained its policy rate at 50% at its June meeting (MSe: 50%; C: 50%) and retained a tightening bias. Forward guidance remained hawkish and repeated a signal to keep rates higher for longer while leaving the door open for further tightening if required by the inflation outlook. The statement was largely unchanged except for a reference to a temporary pause in the decline in monthly inflation trend in May. The CBT pointed to continued slowdown in domestic demand, but added that it is still at inflationary levels, which our economists interpret as hawkish. There was no new signal on liquidity or macroprudential instruments, but the MPC stated it could enrich the liquidity management tools if required. Our economists expect the policy rate to be kept on hold until 1Q25

• The CNB lowered its policy rate to 4.75% at its June meeting (C: 5.00%; P: 5.25%). Out of the seven MPC members, two voted for a 25bp rate cute. During the press conference, Governor Michl said that the next move is likely a 25bp cut or a pause. He also warned that the CNB can interrupt the easing cycle if headline or core inflation deviates from its forecast. Our economists removed one 25bp rate cut from their 3Q24 rates forecast, but still see the policy rate at 4.25% at year-end

• Banxico maintained its policy rate at 11.00%, in line with expectations at its June meeting. The decision came from a majority of four members, with one member voting for a 25bp cut. The “noticeable” depreciation of the MXN post-elections was highlighted in the statement. Authorities mentioned that “idiosyncratic factors led to volatility,” with rates market repricing and MXN weaker. There were no significant changes to their inflation projections, as the effects of the weaker currency are compensated by weaker-thananticipated activity going forward. Indeed, although policymakers hinted a data-dependent approach to upcoming decisions, they added the notion of weaker activity in their forward guidance comments, which our economists

interpret as dovish

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
