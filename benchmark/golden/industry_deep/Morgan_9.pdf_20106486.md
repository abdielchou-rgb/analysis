## Key Takeaways

CRE is back in the spotlight: CRE issues should be scrutinized through the lenses of lenders and property types.
We see meaningful challenges for both regional banks and office.

CRE exposure is more heavily concentrated at the regional banks, where we expect CRE loan losses to rise further before plateauing at a higher level, and we expect losses to take several years to work through. Banks with a higher exposure to CRE loans tend to have lower CRE loan loss reserves, a risk to earnings at regional banks in the coming quarters.

CRE prices to decline in 2024: We expect CRE property prices to be down 20% peak to trough (versus -27% prior) due to lower rates and lower distressed sales in a soft-landing scenario – office -30%, multifamily -21%, retail -12%, and industrial -10%.

Fundamentals within CMBS are deteriorating, but with significant divergence across collateral types. A significant majority of loans in conduit CMBS pools that matured in 2023 were able to pay in full.

Corporate credit markets see recent headlines as idiosyncratic: Spread widening has been confined to smaller regional banks. Regional banks and REITs are a small part of the IG market. We expect limited spillover.

CRE challenges likely to persist but unlikely to be systemic along the lines of the global financial crisis (GFC) of 2008. The strong linkages between the regional banks and CRE may impair these banks’ ability to lend, leading to lower credit formation, with the potential to weigh on economic growth over the longer term.

## Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)

Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received, Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information,. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any premium paid
