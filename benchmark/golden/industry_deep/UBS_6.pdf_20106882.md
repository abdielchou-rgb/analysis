## Will ongoing recovery in China equities lift CNH?

## China equities showing signs of a bottom. Will CNH follow?

Policy support has helped China equities (A-Shares) stage their steepest rebound since early '23 re-opening optimism, while rolling one month foreign equity flows are also testing similar heights. With sentiment on China still quite beaten up, equities valuations screening cheap on most metrics and foreign funds positioning lowest in almost ten years, many investors are asking if this renewed equity markets' optimism could also inflect CNH. We think not.

## Five reasons why we think the bar for a CNH rebound is high

Our rationale, outlined below, is detailed on Page 2 and 20 self-explanatory charts.

 Very different starting points of policy support, valuations for equities and CNH

 Structural concerns emanating from the property sector have not yet subsided

 Deflationary pressure could keep interest in CNH funded carry trades elevated...

 ... especially amidst close to multi-year wides in US-CN growth gap

 CNH's vol-adjusted carry at multi-year lows is a near-term headwind

 Tariffs risks are unlikely to subside in coming months

## Prefer KRW, INR over CNH. Cheapening of tail hedges' premiums an opportunity

Firstly, amidst the above mentioned macro headwinds and CNH screening 4% expensive on our metrics, we find KRW, TWD as superior currencies in North Asia aided by the ongoing uplift in tech exports. Secondly, we'd expect markets to start building in a larger risk premium related to tariffs risks/US elections in a not too distant future. In fact, we find the recent suppression of FX volatility as an opportunity to add to cheap tail hedges (e.g. 5% OTMS 1y USDCNH digi calls cost only \~9%, close to lowest in 10 years). Lastly, with the recent normalization in CNH vs. TWD cross (and rate differentials), we find INR/ CNH as a better carry trade vis-à-vis the crowded INR/TWD longs. Over and above the technicals, this cross also carries a fundamental support from wider India vs. China growth differentials and rising bond inflows in India.

# Five reasons why we think the bar for a CNH rebound is high

1. Very different starting points for equities and CNH. Policy support that is reviving equities today, has already transpired in the exchange rate market since 3Q23. USD/CNY daily fixings indicate that authorities' grip on the RMB tightened to its strongest level since Aug-15 FX reforms. Indeed, despite Shanghai A-Shares' 16% underperformance vs. MSCI World since early Q3, the CNY TWI is 2% 'stronger'. Likewise, our analysis of USDCNH vs. equities and rates differentials suggests that CNH's test of 7.00 would need not only another 15% surge in A-Shares, but also a sizable upward GDP growth expectations' revision to the order of c.1-2ppt. A high bar in our view. More here==>

2. Structural concerns emanating from the property sector have not yet subsided. Having already unwound previous decade's growth in the past three years, the property sales have yet to show convincing signs of bottom. Per UBS's high frequency activity tracker, these sales contracted further YTD. Likewise, the property prices' diffusion index - of 70 cities' monthly home prices - continues to show that this sector is still stuck in a negative feedback loop. Amidst property sector's outsized macroeconomic significance, not only is there a risk of extended weakness in business/consumer confidence, but we also find resident outflows risk elevated - a phenomenon that historically materialised during property sector downturns even less deep than that in 2022-23. Note: The overall BoP position is already weak with the basic balance hovering close to multi-year lows. More here==>

3. Lingering deflationary pressure. Our economists note that despite a robust rebound in CNY holiday travelling, the economy faces growth headwinds from continued property downturn, risk of softer household consumption recovery as well as lingering deflation pressure (LINK). That raises the possibility of more PBC cuts, potentially keeping CNH carry depressed/negative, especially in a scenario of Fed's insurance cuts being slow and steady. Notably, despite 100+bps decline since 2020, 2y yields on a real metric (deflated by average of core CPI, y/y GDP deflator) are about 150bps above their 10y average - let alone accommodative. Admittedly, if US economy goes through a recession - as our US economists forecast - these headwinds on CNH/USD would be temporarily offset. More here==>

4. CNH's vol-adjusted carry a near-term headwind. The combination of a delayed Fed easing cycle, resilient equity market sentiment and low levels of realized vol has created conditions conducive for the carry trades, leaving low yielding Asian currencies on the back foot. This drag is unavoidable for CNH, with CNH/USD's vol adjusted carry attempting its all time lows. Meanwhile, UBS expectations of China vs. US GDP y/y growth gap hovering close to 20y lows (ex-pandemic, GFC) in Q1 also does not bode well for RMB. Admittedly, this could change when US consumption slows. But, because of the idiosyncratic headwinds on China growth, we'd expect Fed rate cuts to catalyze a step shift lower in the CNY TWI. We target 94 on the CNY TWI (CFETS weighted by end '24 from 98.2 currently. More here ==>

5. Markets could soon start pricing in US elections related risk premium in CNH. US elections are still eight months away, but market odds of Trump reclaiming the White House are not as low as they were going into '16 elections. If these polls do not materially shift in next couple of months, and the recent mentions of higher tariffs on China's exports to US are reiterated by the Presidential candidate, we'd expect CNH derivatives to price in a larger risk premium. As a reminder, CNY TWI depreciated by c. 7% during 18-19 tariffs when average tariff rate on China's exports to US was lifted from c.3% to c.19%. This year's discussions are alluding to a reset to about a 60% tariff rate. In this context, we find the decline in \$CNH 1y risk-reversals to \~10th%ile of 10y history as a dislocation/opportunity. More here ==>

That said, authorities’ intolerance for CNY weakness seems high, as visible in daily fixings' asymmetrical sensitivity to the broad dollar. For instance, realized volatility of daily USDCNY fixings has fallen to 1% - a low unseen since the 2015 FX liberalization. This is despite the consistent deviation of spot USDCNH from the daily fixings by about 1.2% (on average since early 3Q23). If this policy intent remains persistent, then it's possible that the CNY fixings could remain hinged for longer. Equally so, however, this de-facto pegging of the fixings is compressing FX vols, making conditions conducive for USDCNH or INRCNH carry trades. A deviation from the recent fixings peg could either break over episodes of dollar weakness, which we expect to catalyze a step-shift lower in the CNY TWI closer to our estimated FV of about 4-5% lower; or, after the US presidential elections if the tariffs threat does materialize. In that context, we find the recent vols/riskies compression as an opportunity to add to tail hedges (e.g. 5% OTMS 1y USDCNH digi calls cost only \~9%). Having taken profits on USD/TWD ratio call spreads initiated in January in this note, we would now look to opportunistically sell CNH/TWD closer to 2023 highs around low/mid 4.40s (only a % away from current levels).

NPC unveiled modest macro support and structural polices, with guidance mostly in line with UBS and the street expectations – an “around 5%” growth target was set and a headline fiscal deficit of 3% of GDP. While there was a modest upside from an additional RMB 1trn special treasury bond to fund long-term strategic projects (UBSe over 1% of GDP expansion to augmented fiscal deficit), lack of details on the actual execution of the government work report could weigh on business confidence and expectations. Amidst a persistent drag from the property sector, UBS economists see the 5% growth target as ambitious and maintain their baseline forecast of 4.6% in 2024. Our equity analysts note that the report conveyed a relatively supportive tone for select industries such as healthcare, consumer (autos and white goods in particular) and manufacturing, although the equity market is likely wanting to see details on the actual emphasis and the extent of execution before acting. In light of the recent equity markets performance, and weakness in Jan-Feb property sales data, we find the overall event as a modest disappointment for the RMB. Having already unwound previous decade's growth in the past three years, property sales have yet to show convincing signs of a bottom. Per UBS's high frequency activity tracker, these sales fell further YTD, as also confirmed by our property sector analysts' first read on 100 developers' contract sales (-49%y/y in Jan/Feb). Amidst property sector's outsized macroeconomic significance, not only is there a risk of extended weakness in business/consumer confidence, but we also find resident outflows risk elevated.

The property prices' diffusion index - of 70 cities' month-on-month home price changes - shows that this sector is still stuck in a negative feedback loop. Its convincing inflection, in our view, is a necessary condition for the deflationary concerns to subside. For now, it has not yet bottomed.

In light of the recent mentions of higher tariffs on China's exports to US as highlighted by the former US President Trump, we revisit how the 16% increase in average tariff over 2018-2019 weighed on the renminbi. Notably, the TWI fell by 7% over that period. This year's discussions are alluding to a much larger reset in such tariffs. Back then, USDCNH 1y 25 delta risk-reversal was trading at \~1.15, (currently 0.7).

US elections are still months away, but market odds of Trump reclaiming the White House are not as low as they were going into '16 elections. Markets could start pricing in US elections related risk premium in CNH sooner rather than later.
