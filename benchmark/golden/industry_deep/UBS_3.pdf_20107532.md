# Japan Macro Watch How is households circumstance?

## Consumer sentiment is improving

The consumer sentiment in March released this Tuesday confirmed another recovery. It added 0.5pt to 39.5, 6 consecutive months of improvement. The level is now highest since May 2019. Elevated inflation is steadily softening, and actual annualized three months inflation for core-CPI is now at 1.1%. The survey also implied consumers are getting optimistic on their employment and income growth broadly which is already consistent with hard data. Worth to note that households' perception on income growth is recovering broadly across the type of households including pensioners (Figure 17). Thanks to surging equities, perception of value of assets also reached highest level since 2013 (Figure 18).

## February wage growth remained decent at 2%

Wage growth in February released this Monday showed neither acceleration nor deceleration but steadily hovers at around 2%. The core-wage, scheduled earnings for full-time employees, rose 2.1%y/y, unchanged from past two months in terms of common establishment basis. Meanwhile, headline wage growth which is volatile and fluctuates frequently slowed 1.9% from 2.2% due to weaker special payment. We don’t expect much change in March print too. More attention should be on April-June print when wage will reflect the recent strong Shunto outcome. 83% of firms had reflected new wage by June according to the survey last year (link).

## Saving rate is low but real wage growth likely to support consumption eventually

Meanwhile, the saving rate for Q4 2023 which is estimated by the Cabinet Office and released this Wednesday was -0.3%, down from -0.1% (revised up from -0.2% in previous estimate). This is lowest level since Q3 2015, and implied consumption spending was more than disposable income (Figure 19-20). The disposable income growth was sluggish at -0.2%q/q after -0.2% in Q3 2023, which is concern for consumption outlook. However, we maintain to see the increase of real income will boost consumption along with income tax cuts and subsidies in June or July.

## Next week: nationwide CPI, trade statistics and inbound tourism

Key statistics next week is nationwide CPI in March due on 19 April. We envisage further steady disinflation in both core and core-core CPI, expecting 2.7% (from 2.8% in February) and 3.0% (from 3.2%) respectively. See our inflation tracker note. Both nominal exports and imports in March due on 17 April are likely to slow to 6.0%y/ y (from 7.8%) and -8.8% (from 0.5%). Also it will be important to check inbound tourism statistic in March due on 17 April.
