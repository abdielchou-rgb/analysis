# Global Oil: Monthly Agency Data Snapshot A wide range for supply growth too

## Mixed revisions to balances

The IEA and OPEC again made bearish changes for 2024, incorporating higher non-OPEC supply. The IEA now sees a larger surplus of 0.8Mb/d in 2024, vs. 0.5Mb/d previously, assuming OPEC returns 1Mb/d in 2Q and a further 0.4Mb/d in 3Q. While OPEC similarly cut the implied call on OPEC by 0.1Mb/d to 28.4Mb/d, its forecast suggests 0.5Mb/d of incremental call on OPEC crude in 2Q that is in contrast to the IEA's forecasts calling for flat OPEC output. The EIA's market balance was largely unchanged at -0.1Mb/d for the year, as it forecasts OPEC returning only 0.7Mb/d in 2Q. Our own forecasts indicate a marginally tighter market balance for 2024, largely driven by US supply disruptions in 1Q. We continue to expect a similar market balance over 2Q-3Q of a 0.5Mb/d deficit on average, which supports our \$84/bbl Brent forecast for 2Q-3Q.

## Demand forecasts for 2024 largely unchanged

Agencies' demand growth forecasts for 2024 were little changed this month. The IEA and OPEC left forecasts virtually unchanged at 1.2Mb/d and 2.2Mb/d while the EIA marginally raised its forecast to 1.4Mb/d. We revised up our own forecast by 0.1Mb/ d to 1.3Mb/d, on higher global GDP growth (+2.8% from +2.6%). For 2025, the EIA raised demand growth by 80kb/d to 1.3Mb/d (US +0.1Mb/d to 0.1Mb/d), while OPEC left it unchanged at 1.9Mb/d. Our demand forecast next year is more bearish at +1.1Mb/ d, as we see lower non-OECD growth (OPEC 1.7Mb/d; EIA 1.3Mb/d vs, UBSe 1.1Mb/d).

## A wider range for non-OPEC supply, driven by the US

Revisions to non-OPEC supply were mixed: upgrades from the IEA and OPEC and a downgrade from the EIA. As with demand growth, expectations for 2024 supply growth are now in a wide range: EIA +0.8Mb/d, OPEC +1.3Mb/d and the IEA +1.4Mb/d. Diverging views on US supply are the key driver: the EIA is the most bearish on +0.2Mb/ d, while the IEA has +0.8Mb/d and OPEC +0.6Mb/d. The EIA's DPR shows that January US crude output fell by 0.6Mb/d m/m, with both wells drilled and wells completed down m/m. We expect non-OPEC supply growth of 0.9Mb/d (unchanged), including US liquids growth of 0.5Mb/d (+0.3Mb/d exit-to-exit).

## OPEC+ compliance issues in January: output down only 0.2Mb/d m/m

Unsurprisingly, compliance with the new OPEC+ cuts was uneven in January: the UAE, Iraq and Kazakhstan produced a combined 0.7Mb/d more than their commitments made in early December. The combined output from OPEC-9 and partners was down only 0.2Mb/d m/m in January to 34.9Mb/d, 0.5Mb/d above the targeted level but in line with the drop we expect in 1Q24. We still expect the cut to be extended beyond the end of March, till at least the end of June 2024.

## UBS Scenario

In the near term, we model Saudi Arabia and other OPEC+ members sticking to their voluntary production cuts through year-end and 1H24, keeping the market in deficit, until bringing production back as demand requires over 2H23-2025. We do expect a negative impact on oil demand from slower GDP growth and higher prices, but we continue to expect demand to grow at least until the late 2020s. Rising efficiency and EVs' increasing impact should see demand growth slow sharply though, to \~0.5Mb/d within 3-4 years. We expect global spare capacity to remain stable at an average level by historical standards. Our base case for US shale is that it bottomed in 1H21 and is now recovering, but we do not expect a reaction akin to that in 2016-18 because we see greater capital discipline across the sector – responding to pressure from investors/capital markets and consolidation in the space. We see US liquids growth in 2024 of +0.4Mb/d. This is important supply because investment activity in the industry remains low. Even with a pickup in spending in 2023, we do not see the effects of 2014-16 and 2020-21 reversing; hence, we expect prices to remain elevated, and we see slightly more upside than downside risk.

## Upside price scenarios

In the near term, we see the main upside risk coming from a faster demand recovery than we expect, i.e. demand growth of >1.5M/bd for 2024, through higher global GDP. Supply disruptions have been limited over the past year (Iraq, Libya briefly) and such an event and/or further OPEC+ cuts, combined with robust demand could bring prices back above \$90/bbl in the near term, in our view. We would expect to see OPEC+ bring back production faster if prices get closer to \$100/bbl. Fuel subsidies and other tax cuts could further reduce elasticity of demand to higher prices in the near term. OPEC+ could stick to its production cuts for longer than we expect while underlying demand remains robust and non-OPEC does not respond to price signals while US supply growth slows. In this case and without an early and meaningful peaking in demand, we could see sustained \$90/bbl+ prices.

## Downside price scenario

In the near term, our downside scenario assumes a greater negative impact on oil demand from a global economic slowdown to the tune of \~1.0Mb/d vs. our forecasts, i.e. nearly the equivalent of our 2024 growth forecast. This could see Brent prices drop to a range of \$65-70/bbl. Recent decisions taken by OPEC+ and Saudi Arabia point to strong support around \$80/ bbl, in our view, and a further price drop increases the probability of further cuts. We think a reversal of OPEC+ policy, i.e. production increases to protect market share, is a lowprobability scenario but would see prices drop even lower. Supply increases, either within OPEC+ production – a further recovery in Venezuelan or Iranian production or a rebound in Russian production – or through a greater capex response to recent higher prices from non-OPEC could see supply rise faster, by a roughly similar magnitude of \~1.0Mb/d.
