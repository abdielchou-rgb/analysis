## April 4

Brent crude surges past \$90/bbl, geopolitics in focus; Fed's Kashkari floats idea of no cuts this year; FTQ drives UST bullflattener; Risk-off environment benefits JPY; Peripheral spreads tighten against Bunds; CLP leads, COP is supported by oil; DXY at 104.22 (-0.0%); US 10y at 4.309% (-3.8bp).

• Brent crude oil surges above \$90/bbl alongside a flare-up of geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

• Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari states he projected two rate cuts this year in the March SEP (unchanged from December) but cautions "If we continue to see inflation moving sideways, then that would make me question whether we needed to do those rate cuts at all."

• Front-end Treasuries grind to session lows following Kashkari's remarks but swiftly join the rally across the rest of the curve as geopolitics waver risk appetite; the curve bull-flattens.

• Peripheral spreads stage tightening against Bunds; 10y BTP-Bund spread tightens over 7bp.

• CLP (+1.3%) leads peers in a continuation of the prior session’s rally, with COP (+1.2%) not far behind, helped by stronger oil prices.

Even though continuing claims fell to 1791k in the week ended March 23 (C: 1811k; P: 1810k), Treasury yields pushed lower.

A heavy schedule of Fedspeak played a key role in shaping NY afternoon price action across macro markets. Richmond Fed President Barkin kicked off with prepared remarks, which noted data in early 2024 has been “a little less encouraging” and raises the question of whether “we are seeing a real shift in the economic outlook, or merely a bump along the way.” He is of the belief that “all this tightening will eventually slow the economy further” but is “still looking for the slowing in reported inflation to sustain and broaden.” He is open to a target range for the inflation target, but noted the Fed cannot back off 2% right now.

Chicago Fed President Goolsbee then discussed the impact of supply-side improvements from “robust immigration” that has raised the population growth rate faster-than-expected. Because of this, he stated “aggregate measures like payroll job creation or gross domestic product (GDP) growth can be larger without overheating the economy.” These remarks were in-line with his view that strong growth should not be an impediment to rate cuts this year.

However, his remarks on the biggest danger to inflation (continued high inflation in housing services) contained a more cautionary tone. Goolsbee noted market data for rents on new leases had not come down as quickly as he has been expecting. He thus admitted “if it does not come down, we will have a very difficult time getting overall inflation back to the 2% target.”

As geopolitical tensions in the Middle East once again flared, Brent crude oil futures (+1.45%) soared above \$90/bbl shortly before 2:00pm ET. Additional Fedspeak followed from Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari as unease now permeated macro markets. Kashkari stated he projected two rate cuts this year in the March SEP (unchanged from December) but cautioned "If we continue to see inflation moving sideways, then that would make me question whether we needed to do those rate cuts at all." He believes it is a question of why rate cuts are needed if the economy remains strong.

As both macro developments were negative for risk, equities and credit spreads were the first movers. The S&P 500 (-1.23%) rolled over and continued to make a series of lower lows and lower highs in the NY afternoon before closing at session lows. At the same time, both investment grade (+1.7bp) and high yield (+7.5bp) credit spreads made sharp moves wider before closing at session wides.

Front-end Treasuries extended to session lows as the recent narrowing of the distribution about Fed rate cuts now came under question. Although, in short order the main risk to being short the front-end and volatility (geopolitics) soon took precedent as front-end rates joined the rally across the rest of the curve. In the flight-to-quality rotation, the curve bull-flattened which perhaps weighed on the US dollar’s appeal as a haven currency. USD/JPY (-0.2%) fell sharply as JPY became the preferred safe-haven currency in the risk-off move.

For March nonfarm payrolls, our economists expect total payrolls rose by 245k (C: 215k; P: 275k), slightly above a market-based survey number but below “breakeven”

payroll growth estimates of \~265k per month. We have flagged risks of higher unemployment rates, on account of increasingly weak employment trends in the household survey. Even if the household survey rebounds, the impact of greater monthly population growth (based on CBO’s net immigration projections for 2024) would still apply considerable upward pressure on the unemployment rate (for more, read here).

## Japan:

JGBs traded relatively resiliently ahead of the 30y JGB auction, supported by demand to square short positions across the curve. The auction was slightly weaker at 1.815% (C: 1.812%), but the sell-off post auction was muted in the long-end. Since market participants broadly had a cautious view about long-end JGB issuance, given heavy supply, this outcome was seemingly in line with what was expected. There also seemed to be a decent direct bid from market participants. Yet, tenors less than 30y began to sell-off after the auction, as market participants seemingly focused on the 5y and 20y JGB auctions next week; 5y JGBs sold off by 1.0bp d/d to 0.370% on a simple yield basis, while 30y JGBs ended the Tokyo session unchanged. TONA OIS bear-steepened, with the belly of the curve better paid, while XCCY basis twiststeepened

Weak US ISM Services data overnight on Wednesday reversed the momentum for a stronger US dollar, alongside a recovery in risk markets. This trend continued during Tokyo hours on Thursday, as funding currencies (JPY, CHF, and USD) broadly underperformed. Although USD/JPY essentially traded rangebound \~151.60, JPY underperformed against risk-sensitive currencies; AUD/JPY ground higher above 100.00 while EUR/JPY approached 165.00. Market participants will now focus on the US nonfarm payrolls report on Friday and the subsequent repricing of the Fed policy rate path.

## Europe:

CHF weakness overnight followed a downside inflation surprise, but the risk-off move in macro markets benefited CHF – as well as JPY – in the NY trading session. A slightly more cautious tone toward rate cuts in minutes from the March Riksbank meeting benefited SEK. The central bank indicated currency depreciation and decisions of other central banks could warrant a cautious and gradual cutting cycle. However, as Brent crude oil rose above \$90/bbl for the first time since June, NOK/ SEK (-0.2%) erased some of its losses.

European duration rallied in the London afternoon after higher-than-expected US initial jobless claims and followed a quiet London session. 10y Bunds closed over 3bp lower at 2.36% and curves flattened. Most of the bear-steepening from Tuesday has retraced over the past two sessions, especially EUR 2s10s swaps which twist flattened by 3bp to -52bp while 10s30s swaps flattened by 1bp. In money markets, greens (-3bp) led the rally, which flattened reds/greens \~2.5bp. Peripheral spreads made a roundtrip; 10y BTPs outperformed 10y Bunds by 7bp while 10y OAT-Bund spreads and 10y Bono-Bund spreads tightened by \~1bp and 3bp, respectively. Core ASW tightened back, once again led by Schatz (-1.5bp).

Dollar Bloc:

A turn in risk sentiment in the NY afternoon reversed some of the initial USD weakness (DXY USD Index: -0.1%), as safe-haven currencies benefitted from a mover lower in equities and US yields. AUD/NZD briefly broke above 1.0945 – its November 2023 high – before a close at \~1.0940 (+0.1%).

## Economic Release Notes

## Developed Markets

• In Japan, Weekly Ministry of Finance International Transactions in Securities data for the week of March 24-30 suggested Japanese market participants were large net sellers of foreign long-term debt securities (-JPY 1.66tn). We believe Japanese market participants tried to liquidate underwater positions ahead of fiscal year-end disclosure. Japanese market participants may have sold equity exposure to offset realized losses from bond portfolios. Indeed, Japanese market participants were also net sellers of foreign equities (-JPY 233.6bn). Overseas market participants were decent net buyers of Japan long-term debt securities (+JPY 842.2bn). Overseas market participants also were large net sellers during the week of March 17- 23, but it was a reflection of redemptions from their bond portfolios. The 20th of each quarter is typically the maturity date for JGBs, and these data essentially count redemptions as “selling.” Overseas market participants continued to be net sellers of Japan equities (-JPY 441.3bn) over the past three consecutive weeks, likely squaring positions after BoJ normalization

• In the UK, the Decision Maker Panel was released. The single-month series showed perceived pricing power cooled to the levels from summer 2021, with expected wage growth for the 12 months ahead down to 4.7%. The key metric of labor market slack – recruitment difficulties – showed the most balanced labor market conditions since the survey began. Our economist's base case is still for a May start to the cutting cycle and 100bp of cuts to be delivered this year

• In Switzerland, Headline CPI rose 1.0% y/y in March (C: 1.3%; P: 1.2%), in-line with the 1Q24 SNB conditional forecast for a 1.2% y/y average. Services inflation ticked up to 2.0% y/y. Imported goods and services CPI fell further, to -1.3% y/y, while domestic goods and services came in at 1.8% y/y

• In the US, Initial Jobless Claims rose to 221k in the week ended March 30 (C: 214k; P: 212k), while Continuing Claims fell to 1791k in the week ended March 23 (C: 1811k; P: 1810k). The data do not suggest much change in the strong recent pace of employment gains

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
