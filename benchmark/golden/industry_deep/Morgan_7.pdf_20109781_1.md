Intervention and Fed policy: Recent reporting has suggested that public figures associated with the Republican presidential campaign have advocated for a weaker USD. And a Republican White House may act to weaken the US dollar through a range of policy avenues.

However, we do not expect the (USD-negative) market expectation of these policy options to fully offset the (USD-positive) effect of other potential Republican policies, including on trade and fiscal policy.

The White House may intervene against USD by directing the US Treasury to purchase foreign assets through the Exchange Stabilization Fund (ESF) – purchases which would likely be matched by parallel purchases by the New York Fed. Seeking Congressional authority to issue additional debt may introduce a political hurdle to augmenting the ESF, although there are policy avenues that would likely not require Congressional approval.

A Republican administration may seek to weaken USD by appointing a Fed Chair inclined to set interest rates at low levels, although former President Trump has said he would not seek to shorten current Fed Chair Jerome Powell's current term as Fed Chair which ends in May 2026.

A variety of unconventional policy options aimed at reducing the US trade deficit have been discussed by figures associated with the Republican campaign, including imposing a border adjustment tax, taxing imports to the US at a rate equivalent to the tariff charged by the exporting country on US goods, and taxing capital flows into the US via a "market access charge."

There are significant areas of uncertainty associated with all these policy outcomes, including the degree to which one or more of these proposals might require additional US legislation, whether they would be consistent with US obligations under USMCA and/or WTO agreements, their impact on US growth and inflation, and the degree to which former President Trump supports these proposals.

# Interest Rate Strategy

## United States

We discuss the continuation of the Treasury curve steepening trend. We think the 20y new issue and 30y TIPS reopening, coming just before the Jackson Hole Economic Symposium and Chair Powell's opening remarks, could help the trend continue. We continue to suggest UST 2s20s yield curve steepeners.

Given Treasury's recent guidance that current coupon sizes would be maintained for "at least the next several quarters" and continued large deficits, we don't expect comments from former Treasury Secretary Mnuchin about the viability of the 20y bond and the upcoming election to affect its performance or the yield curve shape.

With labor market data having showed some "unexpected" weakening since the July FOMC meeting, we think investors will contemplate more dovish remarks from Fed Chair Powell than what he delivered at the July 31 press conference. This idea should keep investors discussing a 50bp rate cut on September 18.

Even if Powell downplays a 50bp rate cut at Jackson Hole, important data remain on the calendar which could change his mind. The data include the second release of 2Q24 GDP and July PCE inflation (August 29-30), JOLTS (September 4), August payrolls (September 6), August CPI (September 11), and August retail sales (September 17). We continue to suggest Sep/Nov FOMC OIS flatteners.

## Euro area

We reviewed the recent market moves following this past volatile week. We note that as the market has been unable so far, despite the sell-off in risk-sensitive assets to price in a depo rate below neutral, the Bund has traded at maximum, at its fair value implied level, struggling to richen meaningfully despite the long positioning outstanding on European duration.

Given the uncertainties lingering around the economy, and as cuts have been frontloaded in this last wave of rally, we stress-tested front-end rates according to various assumptions on terminal rates and cuts distributions. We conclude that outright expressions currently are appealing given the low amount of convexity offered.

Conversely, the exercise conducted points to a flattening in white/reds under the majority of scenarios tested, while a flattening of reds/greens should be expected only on the premise of pricing a lower terminal rate with a cuts distribution continuing to be front-loaded toward earlier cuts. However, we note that the current steepening in reds/greens looks to be running out of steam.

The repercussion on the curve would be that EUR 2s5s could struggle to outsteepen the forwards over the next \~3m without seeing the ECB's terminal rate drifting materially lower, while EUR 2s5s10s should richen, with our 2s5s10s model pointing to cheap valuations in the sector. Tactically, we enter EUR 2s5s flatteners vs. ESTR.

## Japan

After the roller-coaster session this week, speculators are now likely to have largely completed their risk reduction efforts, which leaves flatteners the more attractive option from a risk/reward perspective, in our view. Given that markets are only pricing in less than one 25bp hike over the year, we see value in paying the short-end from a risk reward perspective.

On the other hand, we believe that the recent cheapness in the long-end, despite continuing concerns about risks of a US hard-landing, likely help to ensure strong demand for the long-end, especially from lifers. The near-term supply/demand balance should also be supportive, with no super-long auctions scheduled for next week. We therefore continue to recommend sticking with 20y JGB long vs pay 2y OIS positions.

We also expect 10y JGB ASW to keep richening back towards fair value as domestic investors gradually rebuild their exposure to duration in anticipation of BoJ normalization proceeding only comparatively slowly, and accordingly suggest sticking with 5s10s JGB ASW box flattener trades.

Regarding Jackson Hole, labor market data showed some "unexpected" weakening since the July FOMC meeting. As a result, we think investors will contemplate more dovish remarks from Fed Chair Powell than what he delivered at the July 31 press conference.

We still see value in positioning for a 50bp cut in November, even if the Fed only delivers a 25bp cut in September.

• If the Fed delivers the 25bp rate cut in September that our economists expect, markets could still price in non-zero probabilities of a 50bp rate cut in November, contingent on the September Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) showing the median participant penciling in at least 75bp of rate cuts in 2024.

Unforeseen surprises are liable to cause pain for many if not most market participants. With USD/JPY remaining elevated, markets had been positioning themselves for a higher BoJ terminal rate out of a perceived need to support the currency. The July rate hike decision and Governor Kazuo Ueda’s unexpectedly hawkish tone in his post-meeting press conference are thus likely to have come as pleasant surprises for many investors.

But any joy would have proved short-lived, with the subsequent weakness of the US manufacturing ISM print and Employment Situation report serving to fuel concerns about US hard-landing risk. The net upshot was a rush of short-covering at the short end of the curve as USD/JPY corrected sharply and expectations for further BoJ rate hikes were dialed back accordingly (see Exhibit 40 ).

## BoJ put?

As we discussed in "Japan | Hawkish BoJ + Global Risk-Off = Policy Mistake?", the July 31 monetary policy statement was viewed in a hawkish light for its guidance to the effect that “if the outlook for economic activity and prices presented in the July Outlook Report will be realized, the Bank will accordingly continue to raise the policy interest rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation”.

The “Summary of Opinions” for the July 30–31 meeting released on Thursday also indicated that many members had called for gradual further normalization given that monetary conditions would still be adequately accommodative even after the hike to 0.25%.

Uchida’s basic position was similar, but his emphasis ended up being quite different.

(…) the Bank explained its thinking in the July 2024 Outlook for Economic Activity and Prices (Outlook Report) that "as for the conduct of monetary policy, while it will depend on developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions going forward, . . . if the aforementioned outlook for economic activity and prices will be realized, the Bank will accordingly continue to raise the policy interest rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation." It should be noted that this approach is conditioned by the phrase "if the outlook for economic activity and prices will be realized." On this point, the significant movements in stock prices and foreign exchange rates since last week would be relevant.

## Moreover:

Japan's economy is not in a situation where the Bank may fall behind the curve if it does not raise the policy interest rate at a certain pace. Therefore, the Bank will not raise its policy interest rate when financial and capital markets are unstable.

As has been pointed out by our economists, the flipside is that the BoJ should still be in a position to hike its policy rate provided that markets settle back down and downside risks to the central bank’s envisaged outlook dissipate. In other words, a more hawkish rate hike trajectory might very well start to be priced in once again if stock prices rebound and the yen reverts to its previous downtrend.

We thus expect pricing at the short end of the curve to become sensitive to movements in stock prices and exchange rates. Moreover, we see only limited scope for greater dovishness given that not even a single rate hike is fully priced in for the next year (see Exhibit 41 ), and accordingly view paying positions in the short-term sector as the more attractive option from a risk/reward perspective.

## USD | The White House and the dollar

Policies proposed by the Republican presidential campaign would likely boost USD. A potential Republican administration may attempt to offset USD strength, but in the near term we expect USD and the likelihood of a Republican White House to be positively correlated.

Higher US tariffs on the rest of the world could lead foreign authorities to allow depreciation of their domestic currency. We address common arguments for why a Republican administration might weaken the US dollar (including by intervening in FX markets or through Fed Board appointments) and argue that the likelihood of these policies does not outweigh the USD-positive effects of a Republican administration's fiscal and trade policy agenda.

## Lessons from the recent turmoil

Recent volatility serves to underscore the importance of applying a framework to analyze markets. We forecasted that USD/JPY would decline as market participants started to factor in a commencement of Fed rate cuts and eventual convergence of US-Japan monetary policy stances (see JPY | All About The Fed, Not The BoJ, May 31, 2024).

## What's the relationship between JPY and Japan equities?

The yen's recent rebound appears to reflect both an unwinding of speculative positions and JPY-buying flows triggered by the dramatic fall in Japanese stock prices.

Why has the correlation between the Japanese stock market and the FX market been so strong of late? Correlation tends to be positive on average ( Exhibit 86 ), which is often explained from a "fundamentals" perspective in terms of the FX market impacting the stock market, with a weaker JPY serving to inflate the earnings of exporters while exposing import-dependent (domestic demand-oriented) firms to higher costs.

Exhibit 85: The current USD/JPY level remains rich versus implied fair value
![](images/aabd90d5981519c12b8fbc6bcde496e75cc7cc9105e40758b44d299bf7f3820e.jpg)
Source: Bloomberg, Morgan Stanley Research estimates

Exhibit 86: USD/JPY has typically traded in line with TOPIX movement
![](images/1789f066c33fa3021ccb4b367cecea1cdcfca6de2164328a35dbaa18c969511f.jpg)
Source: Bloomberg, Morgan Stanley Research

A weaker JPY is typically viewed as positive for (manufacturing-heavy) Japanese equities owing to a perception that the benefits to exporters will tend to outweigh negatives for domestic demand-reliant firms ( Exhibit 87 ).

But what about causation in the other direction? As we discussed in JPY | Contemplating JPY Flows: A Better Explanation Than “New NISA”? March 2, 2024, some overseas hedge funds appear to use FX swaps (and the like) to fund their Japanese equity investments in JPY. This approach makes obvious sense with US-Japan interest rate differentials close to 500bp.

But procurement of JPY funds in such a fashion is not enough to be perfectly hedged against FX risk. Allowance also needs to be made for the impact of unrealized gains (or losses). A foreign investor who is sitting on a big unrealized gain on Japanese equities may see an incentive to hedge away associated exchange rate fluctuation risk by buying USD/ JPY.

Given the positive correlation between Japanese stocks and USD/JPY, investors may very well see an incentive to "lock in" unrealized profits by buying USD/JPY rather than risk seeing them eroded if the yen starts to weaken.

Hedge levels are unlikely to be adjusted all that frequently in response to comparatively minor stock market movements, but a sharp rally such as that seen in 1Q24 might very well have triggered a surge of USD/JPY buying given that the market value of foreign investors' Japan equity holdings had surged from JPY 267 trillion at end-December 2023 to JPY 314 trillion as of end-March ( Exhibit 88 ).

Detailed data on FX hedge ratios for foreign investors are unfortunately lacking, but around 15-20% of Japanese foreign-denominated ETFs are hedged back into JPY. Even if we assume a hedge ratio of just 20%, this would still point to around JPY 9.4 trillion in USD/ JPY-buying flows for 1Q24. We thus appear to have witnessed a positive spiral in which JPY’s weakness buoyed equities and led to rising stock prices while helping to drive JPY yet lower.

What about when equity positions are deep in the red? Foreign investors then need to raise additional JPY funds to cover the initial JPY principal on their FX swaps and accordingly have an incentive to short USD/JPY. The net upshot is that big corrections in Japanese stock prices may end up causing hedge funds to sell dollars for JPY.

The recent roughly 20% stock market correction from the recent top is likely to have reduced the value of foreigners’ Japanese equity holdings by around JPY 70 trillion. Assuming a 20% FX-hedge ratio as above, the equities sell-off may therefore have ended up triggering around JPY 14 trillion worth of JPY-buying, dollar-selling flows within a very short space of time.

## How might exchange rate levels impact foreign investment from Japan?

We would not expect FX levels themselves to have more than a limited impact on the investment behavior of most investors in Japan. They would focus on the factors that have been causing JPY to strengthen rather than FX levels themselves.

NISA (Nippon Individual Savings Account): Most individual investors use investment trusts (mutual funds) to gain exposure to foreign equities (see JPY | Revisiting The Impact Of The “New NISA” Tax-Free Investment Scheme, January 27, 2024). Net purchases of such products have been running at around JPY 1 trillion per month so far in 2024, with the Tsumitate (regular investment) part of the program likely to have been accounting for much of these flows ( Exhibit 89 ).

We expect such investment to continue for the most part given that long-term wealth accumulation is the ostensible goal. It is of course possible that some individuals facing losses will stop making regular contributions, but our basic expectation is that net outflows will persist.

Life insurers: Lifers have until now tended to limit their activity in overseas bond markets and are accordingly likely to have had only a minimal impact on exchange rates. That said, major lifers have been lowering their FX-hedge ratios as hedging costs have remained so elevated ( Exhibit 90 ), and will thus have seen principal value eroded (in JPY terms) by the recent correction in USD/JPY (albeit with the rally in bond prices of course serving to partially offset this).

Exporters/importers: Exporters may now become bigger sellers of USD/JPY after having seemingly held off in the hope that the rally would continue, with an increasing likelihood of a sharper Fed rate-cutting cycle perhaps serving to fuel concerns about JPY appreciation risk. The June BoJ Tankan showed exporters forecasting a USD/JPY exchange rate of 142.68 (for FY2024), a level that is now close to being reached.

Meanwhile, importers are likely to be doing comparatively little, having until now relied upon long-term FX flat forward agreements and other structures to buy USD/JPY at constant price (typically lower than the spot FX level), taking advantage of large negative long-term FX forwards. Such instruments typically envelop selling USD/JPY knockout calls or similar so as to lower the level of constant price.

The rally in USD/JPY would have seen knockouts triggered and contracts ended accordingly, thereby leaving an incentive to build new positions for hedging purposes. Valuations: The BEI curve seemingly continues to be quite heavily influenced by outstanding issuance amounts ( Exhibit 106 ). JBI29 had been trading a little rich to its immediate predecessor by virtue of its comparative scarcity.

However, that valuation gap could narrow (or even disappear) as a result of the additional supply next week. The BEI curve has shifted so sharply downwards that even JBI29 now looks cheap relative to the expected inflation rates reported in the QUICK Monthly Market Survey (Bonds).

However, it does need to be noted that the latest survey was conducted on July 23–25, and that expected inflation rates might well have fallen since then due to the BoJ’s July 31 policy announcements as well as the aforementioned recent weakness of US economic data (and ensuing market volatility).

We would therefore caution against placing too much weight on the July survey results, and in any case see little reason to snap up modest cheapness from a risk/reward perspective given the obvious potential for markets to swing wildly once again amid persistent US recession concerns.

## JGBi outlook ahead - much focus on the US

Looking ahead, JGB linkers do indeed appear to be facing headwinds from real yields and BEIs, both of which which are dependent on the US economy.

The potential for further BoJ rate hikes will perhaps be the biggest focus when it comes to real yields. As discussed in our BoJ call note (BoJ Watch: Our Latest Thoughts on the BoJ Outlook (6 Aug 2024)), our economists are forecasting an additional hike in January 2025.

BoJ Governor Kazuo Ueda shifted onto a more hawkish footing at his July 31 post-meeting press conference, declaring that the policy rate was still some distance below “neutral” even after the hike to 0.25% and also insisting that he saw no particular reason to stop at 0.5%.

That said, Deputy Governor Shinichi Uchida subsequently dialed back the hawkishness to some degree in an August 7 speech (delivered two days after the significant market selloff, on Monday, August 5), stating unequivocally that “the Bank will not raise its policy interest rate when financial and capital markets are unstable” and also that “the Bank needs to maintain monetary easing with the current policy interest rate for the time being”.

The key message appeared to be that any further rate hike will be conditional on economic indicators and market reactions continuing to point to the US “soft landing” that has been factored into the BoJ’s "outlook".

We ourselves are envisaging a “soft landing” scenario, and forecasting a BoJ January 2025 rate hike. The bond market appears not to be fully pricing in a further rate hike, we thus see potential for real yields to underperform if things do end up panning out as anticipated by our economics team.

We expect BEIs to hold up relatively well given the aforementioned prospect of positive inflation carry. The uncertain outlook for USD/JPY could however prove problematic. The yen has declined sharply against the greenback over the past month or so in reaction to Japan-side intervention as well as weaker US economic data.

While the yen has indeed bounced back to some degree following Uchida’s August 7 speech, it remains difficult to predict where it might be headed next. Previous rises in BEIs had owed much to yen weakness, and it is eminently possible that things could continue to move in the opposite direction if economic data points to US recession risk and/or pricing of the Fed’s short-term r\* shifts downwards.

Furthermore, volatility in monthly indicators may make it difficult to assess in real time whether a “soft” or “hard” landing is more likely, which is also liable to weigh on BEIs. Given that BEIs can sometimes be quite significantly impacted by short-term risk asset movements, rather than just "inflation expectations", we believe that circumspection is probably warranted in the current (highly uncertain) climate from a risk/reward perspective.

To summarize, Japanese linkers are liable to remain quite sensitive to the perceived prospects of the US economy for at least the time being. In particular, we expect JGBi BEIs to face significant downward pressure in the event of data pointing to a higher risk of recession and thereby causing USD/JPY to keep declining.

On the other hand, a “soft landing” in line with the BoJ’s expectations would serve to increase the likelihood of a further rate hike. Given that headwinds are liable to be strongest under a hard landing scenario, we suggest a wait-and-see approach until a soft landing starts to look sufficiently probable.

In an environment with persistent and meaningful ON RRP balances, money market rates would tend to fall well below IORB, meaning that liquidity would be more than ample. While some observers have argued that maintaining significant ON RRP balances in the long run could provide a buffer against money market volatility, that buffer would come at the expense of supplying more than the efficient level of reserves and creating an unlevel liquidity playing field between banks and nonbanks.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Definition of terms

Buy/Long: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be positive over the relevant time period.

Sell/Short: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be negative over the relevant time period.

Selling protection or Buying Risk: The analyst expects that the price of protection against the event occurring will decrease over the relevant time period.

Buying protection or Selling Risk: The analyst expects the price of protection against the event occurring will increase over the relevant time period.

Pay: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will increase.

Receive: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will decrease.

Unless otherwise specified, the time frame for recommendations included in the Morgan Stanley Fixed Income Research reports is 1 - 3 months and the price of financial instruments mentioned in the recommendation is as at the date and time of publication of the recommendation.

When more than one issuer or instrument is included in a recommendation, analyst expects one part of the trade to outperform the other trade or combination of other trades included in the recommendation on a relative basis.
