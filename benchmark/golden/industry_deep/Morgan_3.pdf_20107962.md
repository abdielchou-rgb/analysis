## May 1

Fed on hold, announces June QT taper; Tsys bull-steepen, Powell rebukes hike prospects; JPY gains give rise to MoF intervention talk, USD/JPY falls \~3%; S&P 500 round trips post-Fed; AUD/ NZD above 1.10; Tsy holds coupons, announces buybacks; DXY at 105.73 (-0.5%); US 10y at 4.628% (-5.1bp).

• FOMC remains on hold at 5.25-5.50% and announces June start to QT taper; the statement notes "a lack of further progress" toward the inflation goal but retains reference to eventual rate cuts.

• Treasuries bull-steepen after Fed Chair Powell says "it's unlikely that the next policy rate move will be a hike," since the signal from 1Q24 inflation data the policymakers are taking is "that it's likely to take longer for us to gain confidence that we are on a sustainable path down to two percent inflation."

• USD fades in the press conference; downside momentum accelerates just after 4:00pm ET amid broad JPY strength; intervention concern rises as USD/JPY drops \~3% to 153 on heavy volume.

• An initial position-squaring-driven US equity rally fades as a reversal into the close sends the S&P 500 (-0.34%) back to pre-FOMC levels.

• The Antipodeans finish stronger, but slight AUD outperformance allows AUD/NZD (+0.04%) to close above 1.10 for the first time since June 2023.

• Treasury leaves nominal coupon auction sizes unchanged at May refunding, with guidance for stable coupons "for at least the next several quarters"; liquidity support buybacks start May 29.

Treasury continued to guide that it does not anticipate increases to nominal coupons or FRN auctions “for at least the next several quarters.” The cumulative increase in auction sizes since August 2023 leave Treasury “well positioned” to address potential changes to the fiscal outlook and to the pace and duration of future SOMA redemptions.

Based on “current fiscal forecasts” in which the Monday borrowing estimates cited “lower cash receipts” Treasury will begin increases to 4-week, 6-week, and 8-week bill auction sizes. This is consistent with our view that bills would be adjusted to meet any unexpected variations in borrowing needs.

Treasury intends to make the 6-week CMB into a benchmark, given strong market participant reception. The first buyback operation for the May-July quarter will be May 29 with Liquidity Support buybacks up to \$2bn per operation in nominal coupons and \$500mn per operation in TIPS. There are no planned Cash Management buybacks for the May-July quarter.

After a signal from the March meeting the decision to slow the pace of balance sheet run-off would be made "fairly soon," the May statement confirmed the FOMC would begin slowing the decline of its securities holdings in June.

Specifically, the monthly redemption cap on Treasury securities will be lowered from \$60bn to \$25bn but the monthly redemption cap on agency and agency mortgage-backed securities will remain at \$35bn. The FOMC will also reinvest any principal payments in excess of the MBS cap into Treasury securities.

Securities holdings in the SOMA portfolio have declined on average between \$75- 80bn per month since the run-off caps were raised to current levels in September 2022. MBS paydowns have remained well below the cap at \$15-20bn per month. The Fed lowered the monthly Treasury redemption cap by a bit more than our base case in an effort to reduce the overall pace of monthly run-off by "roughly half."

We note the QT taper decision reduces Treasury's additional financing needs in 2024 to \$475bn, from a potential \$720bn in the event QT would not have been tapered – a scenario noted in the New York Fed’s 2023 Open Market Operations report. After holding coupons unchanged at May refunding, we continue to think the Treasury will reduce bill issuance to the private market to accommodate the impact of QT tapering.

## Japan:

After the acceleration in the 1Q24 Employment Cost Index and the UST sell-off on Tuesday, JGBs sold off across the curve on Wednesday, led by the long-end. Although 30y JGB yields approached 2.0%, there seemed to be less demand to buy the dip from the real money community. 30y JGBs sold off by 3.5bp d/d to 1.965% on a simple yield basis, while 5y JGBs sold off by 1.5bp d/d to 0.485%. OIS bearsteepened alongside JGBs but outperformed JGBs across the curve. Despite no change in BoJ JGB purchases, there appeared to be little appetite to accumulate duration exposure in JGBs. There appeared to be decent demand to square short positions in OIS to take advantage of the UST-led weakness.

USD/JPY rose near the 157.70 level overnight on Tuesday amid renewed conviction in the “higher-for-longer” narrative in the US after the strong 1Q24 Employment Cost Index. USD/JPY cautiously climbed up toward 157.99 into the London morning, given risk of another possible intervention by the Japanese Ministry of Finance. Market participants estimated MoF intervention on Monday to be \~JPY 5.5tr compared to the total intervention size from 2022 of \~JPY 9tr. Other CCY/JPY pairs essentially traded sideways during the Tokyo session but began to climb from the London morning as USD sold off on a broad basis.

## Europe:

The US dollar weakened as FOMC Chair Powell assured market participants that the Fed’s base case remains to keep rates higher for longer, as opposed to additional rate hikes. During the press conference, Powell argued gaining confidence to cut rates “will take longer,” and indicated it would be “unlikely that the next policy rate move will be a hike.” Gains across the G10 were broad-based; the Antipodeans and Scandinavian currencies outperformed as US yields moved lower and equity markets responded positively to the Fed meeting. Strength in the Scandinavians and Antipodeans was most pronounced against USD but also visible against EUR. The DXY USD Index was 0.3% lower in the NY afternoon.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Changes to the May FOMC statement were minimal. There was recognition of a lack of further progress in recent inflation data, but the statement still acknowledged inflation has eased over the past year. During the press conference, Powell kept an asymmetric easing bias and strongly resisted saying the word "hike.” He stated that reaching the confidence to cut is taking longer than previously expected, but he still expects inflation to have moved back down over the course of the past year and rates to be lower by the end of the year

• BoC Governor Macklem noted the Bank is “getting closer” to rate cuts in his opening statement before the Standing Senate Committee on Banking, Commerce and the Economy. He said data since January have increased the Bank’s confidence inflation will continue to come down gradually, even with a pickup in economic activity. Nonetheless, Macklem erred on the side of caution, saying although the Bank continues to expect inflation to get back to 2%, it wants to be confident it will be sustained. He added the Bank will continue to closely monitor the evolution of core CPI, and reiterated the BoC does not have to do what the Fed does and that it can run its own monetary policy

## Economic Release Notes

## Developed Markets

• In Canada, the S&P Global Manufacturing PMI fell to 49.4 in April (P: 49.8), the new orders index fell to 48.4 (P: 49.4) and output ticked down to 49.1 (P: 49.7). The input price index rose to 54.7 (P: 54.2), as supply-side delays were a factor. Manufacturers’ pricing power remains limited by tepid demand and increased market competition. Employment remained in expansion at 50.5 (P: 50.7) as firms were still optimistic about the next 12 months

• In the US, JOLTS Job Openings fell to 8488k in March (C: 8680k; P: 8813k), with the job openings per unemployed person ratio at 1.32. The private sector hires rate fell to 3.8%, below its pre-pandemic averages of 4.3%. Layoff rates are even more unusually low and allow for rapid reemployment growth despite relatively low hiring

• In the US, the ISM Manufacturing PMI fell to 49.2 in April (C: 50.0; P: 50.3), driven by a decline in production to 51.3 (P: 54.6) coupled with weakness in new orders to 49.1 (P: 51.4). The employment index rose but still indicated falling employment. The prices paid index rose to 60.9 (C: 55.4; P: 55.8), likely due to higher energy prices; the index tends to be most sensitive to gasoline prices (except during the supply chain crisis)

• In the US, Construction Spending fell by 0.2% m/m in March (C: 0.3%; P: 0.0%), with the third consecutive month of contraction in private residential spending. Single-family home construction spending inched down after months of gains; multi-family and home improvement fell more sharply and extended earlier weakness. Business spending fell for the third consecutive month

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.

## Analyst Stock Ratings

Overweight (O or Over) - The stock's total return is expected to exceed the total return of the relevant country MSCI Index or the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis over the next 12-18 months.

Equal-weight (E or Equal) - The stock's total return is expected to be in line with the total return of the relevant country MSCI Index or the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis over the next 12-18 months.

Not-Rated (NR) - Currently the analyst does not have adequate conviction about the stock's total return relative to the relevant country MSCI Index or the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis, over the next 12-18 months.

Underweight (U or Under) - The stock's total return is expected to be below the total return of the relevant country MSCI Index or the average total return of the analyst's industry (or industry team's) coverage universe, on a risk-adjusted basis, over the next 12-18 months.

Unless otherwise specified, the time frame for price targets included in Morgan Stanley Research is 12 to 18 months
