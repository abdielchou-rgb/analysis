# India Fixed Income Strategy | Asia Pacific

# Bond Flows Tracker

FPI debt investments saw a notable surge, with FAR flows rebounding last week. Liquidity continues to be in surplus mode.

India's sovereign debt market has seen a steady influx of funds from FPIs since the inclusion of FAR bonds in JPMorgan's GBI-EM index. At present, India's weighting in the index is 3%, expected to increase to 10% – mirroring China's weight – by the end of March 2025.

This report covers the flows data for the week ended September 13, 2024.

• Debt flows: 2024 continues to stand for significant purchases, with FPIs parking more than US\$16 billion in Indian debt markets ( Exhibit 8 ).

Debt investments surged last week, with inflows exceeding US\$1.8 billion, compared to US\$922 million the previous week. The bulk of the inflow was directed through the FAR route, but the VRR (Voluntary Retention Route) and hybrid funds route also received substantial investment. This shift indicates that FPIs are broadening their investment focus, seeking higher returns and diversifying their portfolios, which bodes well for India's duration outlook ( Exhibit 1 ).

<sub>°</sub> September inflows have climbed to US\$2.8 billion, surpassing August's post-election peak of US\$2.1 billion ( Exhibit 4 ).

• FAR G-Sec flows continue to be on the uptrend, with FPIs adding nearly US \$13 billion in the FAR suite of bonds this year. As a result, FPI ownership ticked up by 16bp to 5.72% last week ( Exhibit 2 ).

Last week FAR bonds witnessed an inflow of US\$873 million versus US \$145 million in the week prior ( Exhibit 1 ). Consequently, the 5-day moving average of FAR flows exceeded the 20-day moving average. Overall, FPIs have added US\$1 billion in FAR G-Secs so far in September versus US\$2.8 billion in August and US\$2.3 billion in July ( Exhibit 10 ).

Yields on the benchmark 10-year G-Sec eased to 6.79% last week amid rising expectations of a Fed rate cut at the September 18-19 FOMC meeting. Consequently the average spread of 10y G-Sec to UST widened by 10bp to 315bp ( Exhibit 7 ). Our economists think the Fed will decide to cut 25bp and the decision "will likely be unanimous". Should a 50bp rate cut take place, we expect some FAR outflows ahead of eventual inflows as it would suggest that the US economy is slower than projected earlier and needed a deeper cut.

Although a 50bp rate cut could result in an FPI sell-off initially, domestic demand may mitigate this effect amid rising pressure on the RBI to cut the repo rate or at least change its stance at the October policy meeting. Yields on the longest tenure 50y G-Sec eased by 3bp and fell below 7% for the first time with strong demand from insurance companies and pension funds, which hold nearly 28% and 5% of the total G-Sec supply, respectively ( Exhibit 15 ).

## RBI Liquidity Management

The interbank liquidity surplus was recorded at INR 1.96 trillion as of September 12 versus INR 1.34 trillion on September 9. The weighted average overnight call rate tracked very close (just 2bp over) to the repo rate at 6.52% during the week. The RBI reduced the frequency of VRRR auctions and conducted only two auctions in the week ended September 13 versus seven auctions conducted in the week prior to absorb excess liquidity from the system. Banks parked 56% of the notified amount with the RBI during these VRRR auctions.

RBI secondary market operations: In the week ending September 6, the RBI sold bonds worth net INR 6.9 billion in the secondary market (the ninth consecutive week of selling). The RBI mopped up net INR 65.5 billion in August versus a net sale of INR 116.75 billion in July. The RBI has been actively conducting open market operations in 2H24, though the quantum of net G-Sec sales is decreasing with each week. We expect the RBI to continue with VRRR auctions and screen-based OMOs to manage liquidity in the system.

With the Government of India's surplus cash balances increasing due to less spending and robust GST revenue collections, the RBI has decided to cancel the T-bill auctions for remaining fortnight of this quarter as a part of the central bank's better cash-management strategy. As of August 6, the government's excess cash balance with the RBI tracked at INR 2.43 trillion versus INR 1.53 trillion in the prior year ( Exhibit 30 ).

Credit growth remains above deposit growth, although the divergence has narrowed: As of August 23, credit growth tracked at 13.6%Y versus deposit growth at 10.8%Y ( Exhibit 25 ). With credit growth outpacing deposit growth, banks have raised their term deposit rates in addition to mobilizing funds through higher issuances of CDs. The creditdeposit ratio rose to 77.48% as of August 23, after softening to 77.14% as of August 9 ( Exhibit 23 ). The incremental credit-deposit ratio rose to 63.57% as of August 23, 2024 ( Exhibit 26 ).

Monetary aggregates: Currency in circulation started to pick up pace in May 2024 with increased government expenditure. It grew from 2.75%Y in May 2024 to 5.58%Y as of September 6, 2024. Currency in circulation typically tends to pick up in 2H amid the festive and wedding season in India. Reserve money (M0) growth picked up to 4.58%Y as of September 6 ( Exhibit 27 ).
