# Global Macro Commentary | Global

# September 5

Mixed data in the US; strong Japan real wages data; European duration rallies and OAT/Bund spread narrows intraday; oilsensitive currencies underperform; THB outperforms regional peers; RBI Governor Das offers hawkish remarks; DXY at 101.06 (-0.3%); US 10y at 3.727% (-2.8bp).

• Roundabout moves in the UST market, with USTs ultimately bullsteepening after a miss in ADP Job Growth sends yields lower, only to be offset by stronger-than-expected ISM Services with a beat in the New Orders and Prices Paid indices.

• JGBs partially retrace overnight strength, and USD/JPY falls sharply in the Tokyo morning after a strong July Japan labor release with an unexpected monthly increase in real wages.

• US data continue to dominate market sentiment with European duration rallying following the soft US jobs data; French OATs outperform slightly, with the OAT/Bund spread narrowing intraday amid the appointment of Barnier as French Prime Minister.

• With Brent crude oil futures remaining near YTD lows (\~\$73/bbl), oilsensitive currencies underperform in the G10 complex, with CAD and NOK becoming the only two G10 currencies that did not strengthen against the USD.

• THB outperforms regional peers (USD/THB: -1.1%) amid in-line August inflation data, easing of political uncertainty, gains in local equities (SET: + 2.8%), and a rally in gold prices (XAU: +0.8%).

• RBI Governor Das offers hawkish remarks, arguing that the RBI’s growth estimate is “not out of place” despite slower-than-expected 2Q24 GDP growth, adding that the RBI must keep its focus on the “last mile of disinflation.”

Price action was choppy on Thursday within global duration, FX, and equities as market participants await the August US employment report that will be released on Friday. After the July report showed broad-based weakness, market participants are looking to Friday’s release for future clarity on further cooling of the labor market. Some believe that a soft report will increase the likelihood of a 50bp Fed cut in September.

Mixed data in the US also drove some of the whipsaw price action. Weaker-thanexpected ADP employment growth in August, coupled with downward revisions to the July data, initially sent UST yields sharply lower with fears around a soft nonfarm payrolls report gaining traction. However, a decline in jobless claims alongside stronger-than-expected ISM services and final services PMI sparked a reversal of the move in USTs. The ISM services data included an unexpected increase in the new orders and prices paid index. The employment index, however, fell by more than consensus expectations, though remained in expansionary territory. USTs bear-flattened following the release and retraced earlier gains.

Into the NY afternoon, USTs quietly rallied with the long-end leading the gains. The curve bull-steepened with 2y yields down 1bp and 30y yields down 4bp. STIR pricing was relatively unchanged ahead of the crucial labor data on Friday.

US equities also were volatile amid the mixed economic data, but ultimately the S&P 500 (-0.3%) ended in the red. Industrials, financials, and healthcare sectors underperformed the broader market. Oil prices were relatively stable even as OPEC + agreed to postpone its oil supply hike by two months.

## Japan:

JGBs bull-flattened ahead of the 30y JGB auction following the move lower in UST yields overnight. Strong Japan wage growth resulted in a partial retracement of the overnight strength in JGBs. The 30y JGB auction was solid with a cut-off price of 102.70 (C:102.70) due to the strong demand from the real money community. Although the JGB market remained almost unchanged during the Tokyo afternoon session, 30y JGB yields gradually moved lower into the Tokyo close. The JGB curve ultimately bull-flattened; 2y yields were unchanged at 0.37%, 10y yields rallied 1.5bp to 0.87%, and 30y yields rallied 2.5bp to 2.04% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve also bull-flattened and underperformed JGBs in the long-end. XCCY basis was unchanged across the curve. On Friday, the BoJ will conduct JGB purchase operations in the 5-10y, 10-25y, and over 25y sectors.

In the Tokyo morning, in response to the strong Japan real wages data, USD/JPY dropped sharply from 143.70 to 143.20. However, USD/JPY gradually bounced back and moved higher toward 143.90 alongside the steady Japanese equity performance. Importers may have contributed the move higher heading into the setting of the TTM rate at 10am JST. Although BoJ Board Member Takata reiterated the possibility of further policy adjustment, the impact to the currency market was limited. In the Tokyo afternoon, USD/JPY traded choppily in a range of 143.05-143.90. Other CCY/ JPY pairs generally traded in tandem with USD/JPY, while dollar bloc currencies particularly underperformed.

Europe:

In Europe, FX movements were relatively contained on Thursday as incoming US data (i.e., ADP, claims, and ISM Services Index) provided a mixed picture of the state of the US economy. Outperformers were seen both in safe havens, as USD/CHF fell - 0.2%, and in risk-sensitive currencies, with SEK up 0.2%. NOK, an oil-sensitive currency, underperformed in the G10 complex and became one of only two currencies in G10 that did not strengthen against the USD. Brent crudes prices did not decline further Thursday, but remained close to YTD lows (\~\$73/bbl).

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar traded weaker ahead of the release of the widely anticipated US jobs report on Friday (DXY USD Index: -0.3%). NZD outperformed and gained 0.3% against the USD. CAD weakened against the USD, likely weighed down by oil prices, with Brent crude futures remaining near YTD lows.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies strengthened broadly on the back of USD weakness after the soft US JOLTS data. USD/CNH weakened 0.2% to move back below 7.10, aided in part by a lower USD/CNY fixing of 7.0989 (P: 7.1148). THB emerged as the regional outperformer; USD/THB fell 1.1%, aided by the 0.8% rally in gold prices, a reduction in domestic policy uncertainty, and a 2.8% rally in the Thai equity market as the state equity fund said it will start investing in Thai stocks beginning on October 1. USD/MYR weakened 0.4%, in line with the moves seen in most of its regional peers, as the BNM kept rates on hold as widely expected. Amid the weak USD environment, INR and KRW underperformed and held stable. The underperformance of KRW came alongside its equity market underperformance and \$454mn of foreign equity outflows. USD/INR touched an all-time high of 83.9875, just short of breaking the 84 level.

AxJ rates rallied broadly following the Wednesday overnight move in USTs though moves were mostly modest. China rates rallied across most points of the CGB curve, but 10y CGBs sold off modestly by 1bp. 1y CNY NDIRS yield fell by 1bp, and the 1s5s curve bull-flattened 1bp amid comments from the PBoC. Malaysia rates saw limited volatility amid a widely expected on-hold decision from the BNM; 1y NDIRS yield rose 1bp, while 5y yield fell 1bp. Unlike FX and equities, Thailand rates also saw just a modest rally of \~1bp across the NDOIS curve, following an in-line August inflation release. In India, 1y NDOIS yields fell 2bp despite hawkish comments from the RBI. Hong Kong, Singapore, and Indonesia rates outperformed. Yields fell 7-8bp in the front-end of the Hong Kong swaps curve, and the curve bullsteepened. In Indonesia, 10y IndoGBs rallied 3bp.

## CEEMEA:

Rates continued to rally in the CEEMEA region. Czech rates moved in line with core rates and were lower by \~5bp. In Poland, NBP Governor Glapinski’s comments spurred an intraday rally, but ultimately the curve closed flat on the day. NBP Governor Glapinski emphasized that rate cuts could happen only after mid-2025. In Hungary, the curve bull-flattened with front-end yields lower by \~2bp and 10y

yields down \~11bp. In South Africa, rates continued to rally, and the whole curve was lower by \~6bp.

In FX, ZAR moved in line with the US dollar and appreciated 0.8%. EUR/PLN was higher by 0.2% after the NBP governor’s comments. EUR/CZK and EUR/HUF were flat for the day.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• BoJ Board Member Takata spoke at the regional leader meeting in Ishikawa prefecture. His remarks reinforced the BoJ’s intention (as delivered recently by BoJ Governor Ueda) to raise the policy rate once the central bank is sufficiently confident that economic activity and prices are following the BoJ’s baseline scenario. At the same time, he signaled there is no rush to move immediately, citing the need to monitor financial markets after global markets saw turmoil early in August. He stressed the importance of further progress on achieving the virtuous cycle in which rising wages spurs spending, which in turn will support demand-driven price gains

## Emerging Markets

• PBoC Head of Monetary Policy department Zou said there is still space for reserve requirement ratio (RRR) cuts considering the average RRR for financial institutions is \~7%, adding that the effects of the RRR cut in January are still showing. However, he highlighted constraints on reducing lending and deposit rates further due to narrowing bank profit margins and money flowing into asset management products. He also confirmed the 7-day reverse repo rate is now the official policy rate

• RBI Governor Das said the RBI’s growth estimate is “not out of place” despite slower-than-expected 2Q24 GDP growth, as private consumption and investment have recovered and bank credit to agricultural and manufacturing businesses has improved. He sees the fundamental growth drivers gaining momentum, underpinning his continued confidence in the growth story. He also highlighted that “the best contribution that monetary policy can make for sustainable growth is to maintain price stability,” adding that RBI must keep its focus on the “last mile of disinflation.” With the terms of three external members of the MPC ending on October 4, he expects the new MPC members to be appointed in time for the October 9 meeting

• The BNM maintained its policy rate at 3.00% at its September meeting, in line with consensus expectations. The central bank continues to see the current monetary policy stance as being supportive of the economy and compatible with its present assessment of the inflation and growth

prospects. Despite the diesel price adjustment, the BNM sees the spillovers as being contained, and maintained its headline and core inflation forecasts in the earlier projected ranges, unlikely to exceed 3%. The BNM also sees continued support for MYR going forward given Malaysia’s positive economic prospects, structural reforms, and ongoing initiatives to encourage flows

• NBP Governor Glapinski held a press conference after the MPC decision on Wednesday. He said that rate cuts are possible after mid-2025 if inflation stayed under control. He added that his previous remarks in which he stated that 2026 was the earliest that MPC could reduce rates were “misunderstood”

• The CBE maintained its policy rate at 27.25 at its September meeting, in line with consensus expectations

• In Turkey, Vice President Yilmaz presented the government's Medium-Term Program (MTP) which includes the government's macroeconomic projections for the next three years and outlines the main set of policies and reforms that underpin these forecasts. The MTP presentation reiterated disinflation as the main policy priority and signaled policy continuity. While the downward revisions in growth were more limited than our economists had expected, a tighter macro policy mix remains their base case (for more, read the preview here and an update on downside risks here)

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
