# China Economic Perspectives China by the Numbers (August 2024)

## Our guide to Chinese monthly data

What the numbers are, what they mean, and the outlook going forward.

## Growth momentum remained weak in July and early August

Retail sales growth edged up YoY, while manufacturing and infrastructure FAI remained resilient in July, all mainly due to a lower base last year. Property activity remained weak, as seasonally adjusted new start volume stabilized in July vs. June but remained lower than the Q2 average and property sales edged down further. Meanwhile, export growth cooled YoY but real export volume remained solid. On a five-year CAGR basis, most activity decelerated from June, suggesting growth momentum remained weak. Loans to the real economy contracted, while TSF growth edged up on a low base. See more in our July data comment.

## Lowering our 2024 full-year property activity forecast

The progress of property easing measures since May, especially inventory destocking, has been slow, and the impact has been limited. Property activity has yet to stabilize, while high-frequency data suggest sales in 30 major cities stayed in double-digit contraction in July (-16%y/y) and month-to-date in August (-19%y/y). We still assume property activity will stabilize at a low level in upcoming months, but given the YTD weakness, we lower our 2024 full-year forecasts for property sales, new starts and investment, expecting them to decline 15-20%, 20-25% and 8-10%, respectively.

## More policy support is likely needed in H2

The July Politburo meeting called for faster and better implementation of existing policy support measures, but with no apparent new stimulus. The government rolled out more easing measure in July, including policy rate cut, using special CGB funds to support consumption and equipment upgrade (see our comment). Given the disappointing growth in recent months and the persistent property downturn, we think the government may need to ramp up policy support for the rest of 2024, including enhancing policy support for the property sector, especially inventory destocking, accelerating government bond issuance and easing local finances to achieve meaningful fiscal expansion after overall tightening in H1, while further cutting policy rates and RRR. We revise our 10-year CGB yield forecast to around 2.3% at end-2024, considering continued deflationary pressure, underlying market sentiment, the PBC’s intention of managing the yield curve and an expected uptick in government bond issuance.

## GDP growth to recover QoQ but slow YoY in H2, with more downside risk

A continued property downturn may further weigh on local government spending and household consumption. In particular, a further housing price decline is likely to have more negative wealth effects and suppress consumer sentiment in upcoming months, while the labour market remains relatively weak. On the other hand, China’s exports have been robust this year and may end 2024 better than we expected. Overall, we think there is notable downside risk to our GDP forecast of 4.9% due to a bigger drag from the property sector and a tighter-than-expected fiscal policy stance. While we expect GDP growth to improve sequentially in H2 from Q2, we foresee lower YoY growth.

## Business indicators

July NBS manufacturing PMI edged down, while Caixin PMI weakened notably. NBS manufacturing PMI edged down 0.1ppt to 49.4 in July, with new orders and production both turning softer by 0.2ppt and 0.5ppt to 49.3 and 50.1. New export orders edged up 0.2ppt to 48.5. Raw material inventory and quantity of purchases improved 0.2ppt and 0.7ppt to 47.8 and 48.8. Finished goods inventory slid 0.5ppt to 47.8. Input and output prices dropped 1.8ppt and 1.6ppt to 49.9 and 46.3, indicating slightly cooler price momentum. Employment edged up 0.2ppt to 48.3, reflecting lingering labour market pressure. Meanwhile, Caixin PMI weakened a notable 1.9ppt to 49.8, the first time below 50 since Oct 2023, with a weaker production index (-4.4ppt) and weaker new orders (-2.6ppt).

NBS non-manufacturing PMI softened 0.3ppt to 50.2, although it has been above 50 consecutively since Jan 2023. Services PMI edged down 0.2ppt to 50, while construction PMI softened 1.1ppt to 51.2, partly due to extreme weather and a heavy rain/flood shock last month. By sector, PMIs of railways/airlines, postal, cultural/sports/entertainment services stayed above 55, while those of retail sales, capital markets and real estate remained subdued.

## Property

Property sales continued to decline, by -15.4% YoY (vs. -14.5% in June). New starts contracted by another -19.7% YoY (vs. -22% in June). After seasonal adjustment, new start volume largely stabilized in July (albeit lower than the Q2 average), while property sales edged down further from June and Q2. Property investment stayed weak, at -10.8% YoY (vs. -10.1% in June), while property completions contracted -22% (vs. - 30% in June). Developers’ sources of funds had a narrower YoY decline in July, with funds sourced from domestic loans and self-raised funds improving to positive YoY growth, partly reflecting regulators’ efforts to expand “white list” support. Meanwhile, secondary property prices in 70 major cities continued to decline sequentially, by 0.8% MoM in July, leading to a larger YoY decline of 8%.

Property sales volume may stabilise in upcoming months; downside risks remain. A combination of weakening fundamentals, previous policy tightening and subdued home purchase intentions has driven the current sharp property downturn. The government started to ease policies in Q422 and more notably after the July 2023 Politburo meeting. The April 2024 Politburo meeting called for ensuring housing delivery and destocking. In May, the government relaxed property policies further, and all four tier 1 cities followed through with local easing measures. The PBC rolled out a new re-lending facility or Rmb300bn in May to support property destocking, but progress has been slow, with only Rmb12bn utilized by Q2. We believe the government needs to provide more low-cost funding and roll out top-down guidance to clarify implementation details. Highfrequency data suggest sales in 30 major cities stayed in double-digit YoY contraction in July (-16%y/y) and August (-19%y/y in the first 20 days). We continue to assume property activity will stabilize at a low level in upcoming months, but given the YTD weakness, we lower our 2024 full-year forecasts of property sales, new starts and investment, expecting them to decline 15-20%, 20- 25% and 8-10%, respectively (vs. -8.5%/-20.4%/-9.6% in 2023).

## Fixed Asset investment

Overall FAI growth slowed more than we expected to 1.9% YoY (vs. 3.6% in June). Infrastructure FAI growth stayed robust at, 10.8% YoY, from 10.2% YoY previously, helped by a much lower base in July 2023. The strength may also be due to the recent uptick of government bond issuance, while funding from local land sales revenue and LGFV financing remained very weak. Manufacturing FAI growth moderated to 8.3% YoY (vs. 9.3% in June) due to a low base last year. Overall FAI in equipment purchases has grown a strong 17% YoY YTD. Meanwhile, FAI in other tertiary sectors (excluding infrastructure) likely weakened and weighed on overall FAI growth. With the continued decline in property investment, overall FAI growth slowed to 1.9% YoY, with a softer five-year CAGR.

We expect infrastructure and manufacturing investment growth to slow in 2024. The central government plans to provide more explicit support to infrastructure FAI, with Rmb1trn of special CGBs and Rmb3.9trn of special LG bonds in 2024. However, LGFV financing remains restricted, while local land sales are still under pressure YTD (-29% YoY in Q2). We expect full-year infrastructure FAI　growth to slow to 5-6% in 2024 (vs. 8.2% in 2023), with downside risk. In the manufacturing sector, we believe FAI in sectors along the property supply chain are likely to be held back by weak demand and profit, although FAI related to the green transition could remain robust, while FAI along the tech supply chain could improve. Overall, we expect 5-6% growth in manufacturing investment in 2024 (6.5% in 2023). In July, the government announced it will use Rmb300bn in special CGB funds from the existing plan of Rmb1trn special CGBs to support trade-ins of consumption goods and upgrading corporate equipment, which may support corporate capex but also means less funding for infrastructure FAI.

## Industrial production

Industrial production growth edged down to 5.1% YoY despite a low base, from 5.3% in June. The official seasonally adjusted IP level increased 0.35% MoM in July following robust growth of 0.4% MoM in June. We estimate the five-year CAGR of IP slowed to 4.8% in July (vs. 5.3% in June). By sector, value-added (VA) growth in the auto sector softened to 4.4% and auto production slid to -2.4% YoY, both despite a lower base. VA growth in communication appliances picked up to 14.3% YoY (vs. 11.3% in June) due to the continued global tech upcycle, as reflected in strong imports of IT products. Production of electronic ICs strengthened notably to 26.9% YoY, and that of mobile phones remained resilient, at 6.5% YoY. Meanwhile, YoY growth in ferrous metal VA and steel production weakened, partly due to a high base effect, while VA growth of nonmetal minerals and cement production declined more, echoing overall subdued construction activity, especially property investment.

China's inventory growth edged up in nominal terms in June (to 4.7% vs. 3.6% in May) and in real terms (to 4.8% from 4.1% in May). The industrial inventory-to-sales ratio (seasonally adjusted, 6mma) has risen notably since 2022 and is still well above the historical average, which means destocking will take more time.

## Consumption

Retail sales growth edged up, helped by a low base. July nominal retail sales growth edged up to 2.7% YoY from unexpected weakness in June (2% YoY), while the five-year CAGR moderated. We estimate seasonally adjusted retail sales (in nominal and real terms) dropped slightly in July after sliding in June, while the official estimate (in nominal terms) rose 0.35% MoM in July after declining 0.1% MoM in June. Heavy rain and hot weather may have weighed on consumer mobility and consumption activity in July, while soft income growth and the negative wealth effect of the housing price decline were lingering headwinds. By key category, goods retail sales picked up to 2.7% YoY (vs. 1.5% in June) on a low base, while catering sales growth decelerated. YTD service sales moderated slightly, still outpacing headline retail sales. See more details in our here.

Consumption recovery may continue at a slightly slower pace in 2024. The official unemployment rate appears to have stabilised, while our labour market survey indicated continued pressure on the labour market and income growth. Household income growth slowed YoY in Q2 on a nominal and real basis. The official consumer confidence index weakened again in recent months after a modest recovery in Q423-Q124. Echoing that, the household savings rate stayed the above pre-COVID 2019 level by 1.9ppt in Q224, indicating continued cautious consumer behaviour and further accumulation of excess savings. In July, the government announced Rmb150bn in special CGB funds from the existing plan of Rmb1trn special CGBs to support the trade-in of consumption goods (equivalent to 0.3% of 2023 annual retail sales), which could boost durable consumer goods. If property activity stabilizes and prices stop falling, household confidence may not slide further. Overall, we expect annual real consumption (GDP basis) to slow to 5.1% in 2024 from 6.4% in 2023, with downside risk.

## Inflation

CPI edged up to 0.5% YoY in July from 0.2% YoY previously on a lower base (0.5% MoM vs. -0.2% MoM before), a bit higher than the market expected. Food inflation led the improvement (1.2% MoM and 0% YoY vs. -0.6% MoM and -2.1% YoY before), of which pork and vegetable prices picked up to 20.4% YoY (2% MoM) and 3.3% YoY (9.3% MoM due to the heavy rain supply shock). Nonfood inflation edged down 0.1ppt to 0.7% YoY (0.4% MoM vs. -0.2% MoM before), of which touring outgoing price softened to 3.1% YoY (albeit a strong 9.4% MoM due to rising summer holiday demand) and fuel prices cooled to 5.1% YoY (1.5% MoM), respectively.

PPI was unchanged, at -0.8% YoY, in July with a steady sequential pace of 0.2% MoM. Investment goods edged up 0.1ppt to -0.7% YoY (-0.3% MoM), of which the mining quarrying price picked up to 3.5% YoY (0.7% MoM), raw materials improved to 1.8% YoY (-0.2% MoM) and the manufacturing price edged down to -2.1% YoY (-0.4% MoM). PPI consumer goods cooled 0.2ppt to -1% YoY (0% MoM).

We expect positive but soft CPI inflation in 2024. We still expect non-food consumer goods prices to increase slightly and pork prices to bottom out gradually for the rest of 2024. We still expect CPI inflation to rebound gradually to around 1% YoY in Q4, while full-year CPI may only print 0.4% YoY in 2024 (vs. 0.2% in 2023), given YTD softness, robust production activity and a slow domestic demand recovery. Manufacturing and consumer goods under PPI could remain soft in upcoming months, given the relatively low capacity-utilisation rate. Raw material prices may stabilise gradually after a sharp decline since the end of 2023. Therefore, we expect PPI to continue contracting YoY for most of 2024 and to turn positive YoY only in early 2025, printing full-year contraction of 1-1.5% (from -3% YoY in 2023).

## Money and credit

Loans to the real economy contracted, while TSF growth edged up. Headline RMB loans were Rmb260bn in July, Rmb86bn lower than a year ago (on a low base). However, new loans to NBFIs picked up to Rmb206bn, while loans to the real economy (under TSF) declined 77bn (-113bn YoY). M&L corporate loans softened to Rmb130bn (-Rmb141bn YoY), short-term corporate loans contracted by Rmb550bn (-Rmb172bn YoY), while household loans contracted by Rmb216bn, mainly due to short-term loan contraction, together more than offsetting small improvement in corporate bill financing. Overall, weak bank loans point to subdued credit demand from corporates and households. In addition, net issuance of government bonds came to Rmb691bn (+Rmb280bn YoY), while that of corporate bonds was Rmb203bn and contraction in shadow credit narrowed to -Rmb76bn, all largely in line with our expectations. As such, official TSF growth edged up 0.1ppt to 8.2% YoY due to a low base, while the credit impulse declined by less.

 Expect only a modest credit growth recovery in 2024. We expect a slight rebound in overall credit growth in H224, as property activity stabilizes sequentially (in volume terms) and corporate activity improves. That said, soft credit demand, statistical rule changes and tighter financial regulations are likely to limit the upside. Given the weak credit growth in H1, we expect the TSF credit growth to reach only 8.3-8.4% YoY at end-2024.

## Base money and liquidity operations

Base money growth stabilised at 3.8% YoY in July (3mma), after adjusting for RRR cuts (eg, Jan 2020, July/Dec 2021, April/Dec 2022, March/Sept 2023, Feb 2024 and various targeted RRR cuts) and changes from other liquidity facilities.

The PBC injected liquidity in the interbank market in July. The PBC injected Rmb715bn of liquidity in July, with Rmb613bn of liquidity injections via open market operations and Rmb197bn through MLF, together more than offsetting the withdrawal of Rmb93bn through pledged supplementary lending. The PBC cut RRR by 50bp on 5 Feb, which brought down the overall weighted average RRR for financial institutions to 7.0% from 7.4% and released base money liquidity of around Rmb1trn, helping keep overall liquidity ample (see our RRR cut comment).

 We expect continued accommodative monetary and credit policy but no aggressive easing. We expect the PBC to ensure ample liquidity by possibly cutting RRR once more and the use of other liquidity facilities in 2024 to accommodate the expected large bond issuance.

## Interest rates and bond market

Repo rate cut by 10bps in July. The PBC cut the reverse repo rate by 10bps in July, after a 20bp cut in 2023. The PBC also announced that it would conduct overnight repos/reverse repos when needed, allowing the central bank to narrow the range of interest rate fluctuation. We believe the repo rate may become the new policy rate. Interbank rates typically move up toward month-ends on seasonal liquidity demand and edge down at the start of the following month. DR007 averaged 1.94% in 2023 and hovered around 1.7-1.8% recently.

Yields have stayed relatively low recently. Since late 2023, the 10-year CGB yield has trended down on expected policy easing and investors’ preference for safe assets. The PBC warned against long-term yields being too low since April 2024, yet the 10-year and 30-year CGB yields continued to trend down and touched lows of 2.13% and 2.33% in mid-Aug, before PBC managed to push up rates lately (albeit still <2.2%). The credit spread remains low.

We foresee another 10bp cut in the 7-day reverse repo and LPRs in 2024. In 2023, the PBC cut the MLF rate by 25bps, the 1-year LPR by 20bp and the 5-year LPR by 10bp. Commercial banks were asked to lower rates of outstanding mortgages and deposit rates. In 2024, the PBC cut the 5-year LPR by 25bps in February, cut the 1-year/5-year LPRs each by 10bps and the MLF rate by 20bps in July. Given the exchange rate, we foresee another 10bp cut in the 7-day reverse repo and LPR in 2024, while the PBC may cut more notably in 2025 in case of a 60% US tariff hike. We revise our 10-year CGB yield forecast to around 2.3% at end-2024 (from 2.4%).

## Trade

Headline export growth moderated to 7.0% YoY in August from 8.6% in July. Imports bounced to 7.2% YoY growth from 2.3% YoY contraction. The monthly trade balance narrowed to US\$84.6bn in July from US\$99.0bn in June. Taking 2019 as the base, exports and imports grew 6.3% and 4.0% in July vs. the previous 7.7% and 5.1%, respectively. Full-year exports contracted 4.6% YoY and imports shrank by 5.5% YoY in 2023, after growing 5.6% and 0.7%, respectively, in 2022. Real exports and imports expanded 4.6% and 2.9% in 2023.

Shipment growth to the G3 improved again; IT export growth accelerated further. Shipments to the G3 (the US, the EU and Japan) registered 5.3% YoY growth in July vs. a prior 4.5% YoY, as shipment growth to the US moderated slightly and that to the EU rose. Export growth to ASEAN economies softened despite a lower base. Export growth of the IT basket accelerated further to 17.4% YoY, as shipment growth of computers jumped further. Shipments of consumer goods recorded wider contraction again, whereas the shipment growth of household appliance remained strong.

A slight trade recovery in 2024. The latest survey data showed a mixed picture, with weakened ISM PMI new orders in the US, although survey data from neighbouring economies seemed robust. Sequential export momentum moderated in China and its neighbouring economies, although the run rate remained consistent with the gradual recovery. In light of potential US tariff hikes in 2025, there could be some front-running of exports from China in Q3-Q4. With the support of the tech cycle, we believe export growth is likely to fluctuate around the mid-single digits in upcoming months. We expect full-year exports to record 3.5% YoY in 2024 (vs. -5% in 2023). There is potential upside risk to our forecast.

## FX reserves and capital flow

 Headline FX reserves expanded US\$34bn to US\$3.26trn in July vs. June. China’s FX reserves increased US\$110bn in 2023, declined US\$123bn in 2022 and increased US\$34bn in 2021, following gains in 2019-20 and a loss in 2018.

The valuation effect offered strong support amid a weaker USD. We estimate valuation support from major reserve currency movement in July was likely around US\$26bn due to a significantly softer USD at end-July vs. end-June. Global financial market fluctuation may have added valuation gains, with falling bond yields externally. Overall, capital outflow pressure likely continued in July, following net inflow in Q124. Meanwhile, the balance of total foreign holdings of China's onshore bonds registered further expansion of Rmb88bn in June. The holdings expanded Rmb311bn in Q224 after gaining Rmb328bn/Rmb474bn in Q124/Q423, respectively The foreign holding balance gained Rmb282bn in 2023 (contracting in most of the early months but turning positive since September), after a Rmb616bn loss in 2022.

We expect the new normal to continue, with larger two-way capital flow. The rise in capital outflow will mainly reflect non-government entities’ accumulation of overseas assets, especially as control on capital outflow gradually eases over the longer term. Continued supply chain shifts overseas will also drive outward direct investment. The recent sharp drop in foreign direct investment (FDI) into China is partly due to a decline in retained profit and high USD costs, but it also reflects decoupling pressure. We expect FDI to recover in 2024-25 but not back to the prepandemic level. Meanwhile, we believe the continued opening of China's financial markets and solid economic growth potential could continue to attract more inflow (see our deep dive).

## Exchange rate

RMB appreciated 0.5% against the USD in comparing end-July to end-June, and by another 1.6% into Aug vs. end-July amid further USD weakness. The RMB depreciated by 0.6% against the CFET basket in July. In 2023, full-year RMB weakened 2% vs. the USD and strengthened 1% against the basket. That followed 9% depreciation vs. the USD and 2.4% weakness vs. the basket in 2022.

The RMB exchange rate has been driven by various factors. A resilient trade balance is a supporting factor, while negative China-US rate differentials and concerns about China's growth momentum slowdown are the main headwinds. The PBC lowered RRR for FX deposits 200bp/100bp in Sept/April 2022. The SAFE raised the macroprudential adjustment parameter for cross-border financing from 1 to 1.25 in Oct 2022 and further to 1.5 in July 2023.

CNY may hover around 7.1-7.2 by end 2024E. Similar to recent depreciation episodes, the dynamics of interest rate differentials, the USD index, as well as China's growth data and property market weakness may still bring depreciation pressure to RMB. That said, the PBC is likely to use various tools (including the counter-cyclical factor in daily fixing) to prevent further RMB weakness much beyond 7.3, amid rapid episodes of depreciation and significant volatility increases. Given our expectation of stabilisation in China’s growth momentum, as well as an expected US growth slowdown and Fed interest rate cuts, we still expect RMB to hover around 7.1-7.2 by end-2024.

## Fiscal

Fiscal revenue and expenditures weakened in June. Headline fiscal revenue declined -2.6% YoY in June from -3.2% YoY in May (-3.2% YoY in Q2 vs. -2.3% YoY in Q1). On a comparable basis (after controlling for a high base due to delayed SME tax collection and carryover of tax cuts since mid-2023), underlying fiscal revenue grew 1.5% YoY in H1 (vs. headline of -2.8% YoY). Fiscal expenditures slowed to -3% YoY in June from 2.6% YoY in May (1.1% YoY in Q2 vs. 2.9% YoY in Q1). Corporate income tax revenue slid to -27% YoY in June (-10% YoY in Q2), while personal income tax cooled to -4% YoY (-7% YoY in Q2). As such, June had a headline fiscal deficit of Rmb921bn, slightly smaller than a year ago. Local land revenue declined more, by -35% YoY from -27% YoY in May (-29% YoY in Q2).

Progress in addressing LGFV debt issue; adjustment of central-local fiscal relations. Local governments issued Rmb1.39trn in special refinancing LG bonds to swap for qualified LGFV debt and/or pay arrears to corporate suppliers in H223. In 2024, we expect additional issuance of special refinancing LG bonds and for banks to increase restructuring of LGFV bank loans. To adjust central-local fiscal relations, the Third Plenum vowed to increase local governments' revenue by expanding more local tax revenue resources (eg, consumption tax) and the central government's increasing spending responsibilities and share in overall fiscal expenditures (see Plenum takeaways).

Modest and explicit fiscal support ahead. China set a headline general fiscal deficit of 3% of GDP, an additional Rmb1trn in special CGBs and Rmb3.9trn in special LG bonds for 2024. We estimate the combined budget deficit (general fiscal plus government funds) on a cash basis to be 8.2% of GDP (+1.2ppt larger than 2023 out-turn, see NPC takeaways). Amid disappointing growth recently, we see faster government bond issuance and easing local finances to achieve meaningful fiscal expansion after overall tightening in H1 2024. The overall augmented fiscal deficit may expand 1ppt in 2024 after no increase in 2023, with stronger on-budget spending. We expect local government land sales revenue may decline another 10% or more in 2024, while off-budget LGFV financing is likely to be muted, given tight control of implicit debt risk.
