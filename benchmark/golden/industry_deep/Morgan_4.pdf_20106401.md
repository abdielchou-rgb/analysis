# MLCC Updates: Good, Not Great

It’s not like we ever have complete certainty on the MLCC cycle, but today we have a lot less uncertainty than we did 12 months ago. We do not expect meaningful upgrades at this stage but the shares offer some value on top of structural growth from vehicle content upgrade and on-device AI.

What's changed? Huaqiangbei Mart (the largest electronic component market in China) has returned to busy before Lunar New Year holidays, despite distributors' bearish sentiment. Overall we see gradual recovery into 2H24 and do not see much downside from here.

• Inventory has remained at healthy levels for more than 2 quarters and there is no sign of it picking up again as the supply chain has become more disciplined.

• Overall spot price remained flat in 1H January and we see sporadic price hikes on certain specs due to temporary shortages.

• Contract prices reached trough level as well with only 1-2% negotiation room on big orders. But distributors have noticed more severe competition among Chinese local players, which could potentially cap the upside on price hikes if the cycle start to recover

• Utilization rate is on track to improve, although levels differ across different players.

Edge AI impact on MLCC demand. AI is about to remake PCs and smartphones and gives another boost to increased MLCC TAM from this year. We estimate higher MLCC content in AI PCs and the next iteration of AI phones in the form of higher capacitance but a marginal rise in unit content per device; along with replacement demand, this could drive 2-9ppt higher demand by 2025. It is still very early days in adoption but Samsung first AI phones are reaching new highs in terms of weekly pre-orders in Korea.

Conclusion. Things will normalize and it isn’t different this time. Demand is rising, inventory levels are normalizing, and suppliers are managing their capacity utilization rates effectively. Demand for passive components is expected to pick up as the economy recovers and then as demand increases, manufacturers will be able to raise prices again without fear of losing customers. We prefer high-end MLCC exposure (smartphone/auto) over commodity and Korea/Japan over Greater China due to relative exposures.

## Channel Checks

Huaqiangbei Market remained busy with downstream customers re-stocking before Lunar New Year holiday, but sentiment among distributors remained low.

• Distributors and customers' inventory levels have returned to healthy levels since 2H23. Distributors now hold 1-2 months of inventory vs. customers holding less than one month of inventory. Distributors anticipate the dynamics to be maintained as the industry is now more disciplined to avoid severe inventory buildup, which happened during Covid.

• Spot prices see sporadic hikes of 10-15% for specific specs (such as 0603 106M6.3V) that are in a temporary shortage caused by production vs. a demand mismatch, but the overall MLCC spot market blended ASP remained flat in 1H of January.

• Contract prices (suppliers to distributors) remained flat in 1Q24 and distributors do not see aggressive price hike coming in 1H24 given demand will likely remain tepid due to a combination of low seasonality and a lack of catalysts for recovery in the first half.

• Contract prices (distributors to customers) remained flat with 1-2% discounts negotiated according to orders. Distributors remained bearish on price recovery in 2024. Many Chinese local customers started a new online bidding scheme that makes MLCC suppliers directly compete with distributors on prices, which intensified competition and allowed less room on price hikes.

• Utilization rate is increased gradually. Chinese suppliers are now at 50-80%, depending on their end demand revenue contribution mix. Yageo commodity MLCC UTR remained low at around 35-45%. Both Murata and Taiyo Yuden are at around 75% entering into CY1Q24e. SEMCO is flat QoQ at 75% in 1Q24 but likely to gradually improve to 85-90% by year-end on demand recovery and limited inventory.

## How Will Edge AI Impact MLCC?

We have answered the Edge AI question. On January 26 Samsung received preorders for a record 1.21mn units of the Galaxy S24, S24 Plus and Ultra models during the 7-day preorder period that began on January 19 in Korea. The number represents an 11% increase from 1.09mn preorders for its previous Galaxy S23 series released last year. Its 173K Galaxy S24 smartphones sold a day during the period, surpassed the previous record daily sales of 125K units for the Galaxy Note 10 in 2019. Demand is largely on the most expensive Galaxy S24 Ultra which accounted for 60% of its total preorder volumes, while the basic model and the S24 Plus accounted for 19% and 21%, respectively. Samsung aims to expand AI beyond smartphones to other devices such as TVs, home appliances and cars.

Quantifying the incremental opportunity from Edge AI. The global smartphone and PC replacement cycle is now at 3.2 years and 4.6 years, respectively. However, if we are correct and new LLM-enabled software features drive an upgrade cycle, then we see the potential for up to 23% and 15% upside to our 2024 mobile and PC shipment forecast ( Exhibit 7 ), which will translate into potentially 2-9% upside for global MLCC market shipments.

This is based on a hypothetical sensitivity analysis benchmarking historical 5G penetration rate of adoption for smartphones. We note that 5G smartphone penetration, which began in 2020, drove double digit unit growth for global MLCC unit shipments in 2020 and 2021 (+14 and +19%, respectively) before US-China tensions and COVID adversely impacted demand from 2022. For PCs, we use the historical rate of smartphone penetration.

Both are conservative benchmarks if we assume AI applications can radically change the way consumers interact with AI portable devices. Furthermore, MLCC content growth is likely to increase especially for higher capacitance products given additional NPU

requirements on the SOC to drive on device computations.

## Market Outlook

We anticipate MLCC demand recovery as the inventory cycle turns up. In 2024, we look for the inventory adjustment to be completed during the year, and for a shift to production in-line with demand and later followed by an inventory accumulation phase, with rising production and sales. As shown in Exhibit 8 Exhibit 8 , global MLCC shipments declined from US\$17.5bn in 2021 to US\$14.3bn (-18% YoY) in 2022. While 2023 revenues also fell – we estimate US\$12.8bn (-11% YoY), 2024 should be a recovery year and forecast US\$14.1bn in 2024 (+10% YoY). In our view, a second consecutive year of YoY decline in global MLCC shipment value in 2023 was probably unavoidable. However, in 2024, we expect growth to turn positive due to 1) ongoing recovery off the 1Q23 bottom, 2) inventory no longer a headwind and 3) anticipated increases in shipments of mainstay products that use MLCCs, such as automobiles, smartphones, PCs, and servers.

## Stock Implications

Staying constructive. The MLCC group underperformed our Asia tech coverage in 2023 (MLCC +18% vs. Asia Tech +60% on average) and YTD ( MLCC -3 % vs. Asia Tech +0% on average). Concerns on traditional demand weakness and a lack of MLCC content lift in AIrelated themes have kept investors skeptical on this traditional cyclical segment. However, with limited downside on both spot and contract pricing, inventory returned to healthy level and UTRs are firmly increasing, we believe it is time to turn more constructive on this segment. A potential catalyst would be the acceleration of edge AI growth contributing to MLCC shipment volume.

Prefer high-end over low-end demand, Japan, Korea over Taiwan, China. MLCC group average valuation is now at 1.7x Fwd PB at historical average, but earnings revision breadth is already climbing out of the trough and just turned positive this week. We prefer Japanese and Korean MLCC suppliers (Murata, Taiyo Yuden, SEMCO) and stay relatively more cautious on China/Taiwan names given intensified competition from China domestic suppliers on mid-low end products.
