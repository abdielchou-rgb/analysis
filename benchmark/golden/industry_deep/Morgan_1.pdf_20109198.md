## Developed Markets

## United States:

The bull-steepening in the aftermath of soft US CPI continued, and the 2s30s UST curve closed another 4bp steeper. The slowing in US inflation provided additional confidence the Fed needs in order to cut rates, and hence market participants' expectations for imminent rate cuts have increased. The bull-steepening momentum spilled over into Japan rates overnight, and a similar trend was seen in Sweden after local inflation also surprised to the downside. Meanwhile in FX, JPY remained in focus during NY hours amid elevated intervention risk and outperformed the G10 complex; USD/JPY (-0.6%) fell below 158. GBP (+0.6%) also outperformed given favorable rate differentials amid relative optimism around the political environment compared to peers and healthy growth data.

In the NY morning, PPI data was stronger than expected, with beats to headline PPI and core PPI (ex-food and energy) which rose 0.2% m/m (C: 0.1%) and 0.4% m/m (C: 0.2%), respectively, coupled with upward revisions to the May values. Core PPI (exfood, energy, and trade) was flat over the month. While UST yields briefly spiked following the seemingly strong values, the underlying details painted a different picture. Components that feed into PCE such as airfares and financial services were noticeably soft. Our economists lowered their forecast for June core PCE to 0.16% m/m (P: 0.21%; May: 0.08%) once the PPI components were incorporated. USTs

quickly retraced the initial sell-off after it became evident that consensus expectations for June PCE are another soft value.

Preliminary July University of Michigan consumer sentiment data was also weaker than expected, with sentiment falling to 66.0 (P: 68.2) despite consensus expectations of a moderate increase. Both the assessments of current conditions and expectations fell. While both 1y and 5-10y inflation expectations slowed to 2.9%, consumers continued to note frustrations regarding high prices, as it deteriorates their financial situation. The University of Michigan's Surveys of Consumers Director noted that nearly “half of consumers still object to the impact of high prices, even as they expect inflation to continue moderating in the years ahead.” The university also released a report on interpreting recent trends in long-run inflation expectations; it found the recent trend of divergence between average and median long-run expectations was not unusual. The report noted that it is driven by a small number of consumers and is “unlikely to reflect a fundamental deterioration in consumers’ inflation expectations.”

In the NY afternoon, USTs continued to bull-steepen as some market participants became increasingly confident in more imminent rate cuts. Over the course of the week, 2s20s steepened an additional \~7bp, with 2s down 15bp and 20s down 8bp. The USD extended its sell-off and is down 0.7% over the week.

The S&P 500 increased 0.6% with gains across sectors and over 80% of constituents up on the day. Small-caps continued to outperform, and the Russel 2000 index gained another 1.2% and ended the week up 6.1%, its largest weekly gain of the year. Bank earnings releases on Friday showed mixed results as higher financing costs continue to weigh on consumers and businesses. The KBW Bank Index was down 0.3% on the day.

## Japan:

Following the overnight rally in USTs on the back of soft US CPI data and the subsequent drop in USD/JPY, JGBs, particularly the long end, rallied in the Tokyo morning. The combination of lower USD/JPY and lower UST yields may have pushed the term premium lower and accelerated the bull-flattening trend into Tokyo close. It may have also been driven by demand from real money market participants to rebalance from equities to bonds on the back of strong equity performance. By Tokyo close, 2y JGB rallied 1.0bp to 0.325%, 10y JGB rallied 3.5bp to 1.045%, and 30y JGB rallied 5.5bp to 2.170% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve also bullflattened and underperformed JGBs in sectors greater than 20y. XCCY basis tightened across the curve. Next week, there will be the 5-15.5y TAP auction on Tuesday and the 5y climate transition JGB auction on Thursday. We expect steady demand to square positions in the tap auction, but whether the demand continues may depend on the rebalancing needs of real money market participants.

Following the soft US CPI data and sharp decline in USD/JPY on Thursday, USD/JPY continued to trade choppily during the Tokyo morning. Some market participants think that there might have been FX intervention given the relatively sharp decline in CCY/JPY within a short period of time. Vice Minister of Finance Kanda said he was not in a position to say whether the decline was related to an intervention. He followed up with comments in the early Friday Tokyo morning, stating that given

the yield gap between the US and Japan, speculation was probably behind the moves. Additionally, media reports that the BoJ conducted a “rate check on JPY against EUR” weighed on CCY/JPY pairs. After USD/JPY moved lower from 159.45 to 157.76 with significant volatility, the pair gradually bounced back and traded in a narrow range \~159.1 during the Tokyo afternoon. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY. EUR/JPY gradually moved higher toward 173.3 into the London morning. In the Tokyo evening, it was reported that “Japan likely spent \$22bn on yen intervention Thursday” given that the expected change in BoJ’s current account balance seemed to diverge from money market broker forecasts.

## Europe:

Sentiment on the Scandinavian currencies remained depressed as NOK and SEK underperformed compared to the USD and EUR. SEK posted the largest declines (EUR/SEK: +0.7%; USD/SEK: + 0.2%) as Swedish inflation surprised to the downside relative to both consensus and the Riksbank’s forecasts. Market-implied pricing is consistent with a high likelihood that the next rate cut from the Riksbank will be delivered in August, with 22bp of cuts currently priced. Meanwhile, NOK weakened 0.1% against the USD. EUR/GBP (-0.2%) reached its lowest level since August 2022. GBP (+0.6%) edged closer to 1.30 as robust risk sentiment and favorable rate differentials boosted the currency.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar sold off after the PPI inputs that feed into June PCE forecasts pointed to further evidence that inflationary pressures are subsiding. The DXY USD Index (- 0.3%) traded slightly above the 104 level in the NY afternoon. CAD was largely unchanged on Friday. AUD and NZD rose 0.4% alongside USD weakness.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies rallied overnight after the weaker-than-expected US June CPI, but exhibited mixed moves during the Tokyo session. CNH partially retraced its overnight gains and fell 0.1% to 7.2745 per USD despite better-than-expected Chinese exports in June. CNY held steady at 7.2580. KRW had the worst performance in the region as the local equities underperformed (KOSPI: -1.2%) following the slide in NASDAQ given declines in the technology sector; USD/KRW rose 0.3% to 1,377. TWD was also weighed down by the retreats in the technology sector; USD/TWD rallied 0.2% to 32.57 alongside the sell-off in local equities (TAIEX: -1.9%) and large foreign equity outflows of US\$1.3bn on Friday. MYR outperformed the most in the region and increased 0.4% to 4.67 per USD, its seventh day of consecutive gains. USD/THB rallied 0.2% to 36.19, while gold prices broke above US\$2,400 per ounce overnight.

AxJ rates rallied broadly amid a repricing of US rates toward more Fed cuts. In China, the 2s10s NDIRS curve shifted lower by 1bp, while 2y CGB yields fell 1bp to 1.62%, the lowest level since June 2020. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-steepened 2bp, given 2y NDIRS rates aggressively dropped 8bp to 3.15%, while the 3y KTB yields fell 7bp to 3.10%. Both Hong Kong and Singapore 2s10s swap curve bullsteepened 2bp given their high correlations to US rates. India rates were relatively stable after RBI Governor Das reiterated that it is premature to discuss rate cuts currently. The 2s5s INR NDOIS shifted lower by 1bp, while G-sec yields remained subdued. In Indonesia, the 2s10s INDOGB yield curve twist flattened 7bp; 10y INDOGB yields declined 5bp to 6.93% and 2y INDOGB yields rose 2bp to 6.68%.

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
