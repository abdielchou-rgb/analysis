## February 8

BoJ's Uchida says hard to imagine rapid hikes, USD/JPY returns above 149; 30y UST auction comes 2.0bp through; MXN weakens after Banxico opens door to cuts; Further China deflation weighs on Antipodeans; European rates show mild losses; DXY at 104.17 (+0.1%); US 10y at 4.154% (+3.3bp).

• BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida says it is hard to imagine a path in which the bank would keep raising interest rates rapidly, given their economic outlook.

• US Treasuries grind cheaper through a smaller-than-expected rise in initial jobless claims. Long-end underperformance ceases when the \$25bn 30y auction comes 2.0bp through.

• Broad JPY weakness coupled with mild USD strength, alongside a modest sell-off in US rates, push USD/JPY (+0.8%) back above the 149.00 level.

• MXN (-0.6%) finishes near session lows after Banxico keeps rates on hold at 11.25% (C: 11.25%) but opens the door to rate cuts in its forward guidance.

• Data which indicate further CPI and PPI deflation in China weighs on AUD (-0.4%) and NZD (-0.3%).

• Amid limited catalysts, European duration displays mild losses led by the belly.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

Sparse catalysts defined the Thursday session for macro markets, in which the S&P 500 (+0.06%) waited until just before the cash equity close to tick above 5,000 for the first time.

It was not overly optimistic risk appetite that weighed on JPY (-0.8%), but remarks from a widely anticipated speech by BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida. Uchida said it is hard to imagine a path in which the bank would keep raising interest rates rapidly, given their economic outlook. He also said the bank does not project inflation will significantly exceed 2%, even if the policy path materializes.

USTs ground cheaper for much of the NY session, through a smaller-than-expected rise in initial jobless claims to 218k in the week ended February 3 (C: 220k; P: 224k), with long-end underperformance ahead of the afternoon bond auction. Yet, supply concerns once again dissipated this week, after the new \$25bn 30y UST auction stopped-out at 4.360%, 2.0bp through.

In the Q&A session after he delivered prepared remarks, Richmond Fed President Barkin said he was “cautious” of the January nonfarm payrolls numbers, due to the size of seasonals. A prominent focus into the end of the week will be the annual US CPI revisions released on Friday.

Our economists expect a minor upward revision in core CPI 3-month annualized inflation for December 2023 (+5bp), but error bands are wide (-14bp to +24bp) – these revisions will play a key role in the Fed’s thinking about its progress on inflation (for more, read here).

## Japan:

JGBs opened lower as market participants broadly anticipated hawkish remarks from BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida. Yet, his remarks were broadly in line with what BoJ Governor Ueda mentioned at the January monetary policy meeting – although Uchida denied the possibility of rapid rate hikes, given the current economic and price environment. Market participants may have interpreted these comments dovishly and JGBs rallied across the curve, driven by demand to square short positions; 5y JGBs rallied by 1.0bp d/d to 0.3% on a simple yield basis. 30y JGBs lagged as market participants digested the 30y auction; 30y JGBs ended the Tokyo session unchanged at 1.765%. For the 5y JGB auction on Friday, we expect demand from the banking community now the rapid rate-hike cycle seems unlikely in the current economic and price environment. TONA OIS rallied, led by the 10y sector, as market participants covered paid positions after Uchida’s speech. XCCY basis tightened, led by the long end.

Although USD/JPY traded almost rangebound \~148 during the Tokyo morning, BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida’s speech led to a broad JPY sell-off. Market participants seemingly were disappointed with his remarks that it is hard to imagine a path in which the BoJ would keep raising interest rates rapidly, even if the Bank were to terminate NIRP. USD/JPY ground higher into the London morning and approached 149.00. European currencies broadly outperformed; EUR/JPY approached 160.50. With a dovish BoJ now essentially priced in, the upcoming annual US CPI revisions will be the key focus for USD/JPY.

## Europe:

NOK diverged from SEK, 0.3% weaker against USD and EUR ahead of CPI data on Friday. Movements in the rest of G10 were relatively muted.

European duration traded sideways in the London morning but sold off in the afternoon alongside USTs (10y UST-Bund spread: +1bp), sustained by hawkish comments from Richmond Fed President Barkin and lower than expected US initial jobless claims. The 5y and 10y sectors underperformed in the sell-off. In swaps, 2s5s10s and 5s10s30s butterflies cheapened by 1bp and 1.5bp, respectively while 2s10s bear-steepened by \~1bp and 10s30s bear-flattened by 1.5bp. In money

markets, greens led the sell-off (+6bp). The German ASW complex tightened in the sell-off (Schatz: -1bp) and Bund/Buxl ASW flattened further. Peripheral spreads widened slightly; the 10y BTP-Bund spread widened 0.5bp to 157bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

Signs of worsening deflationary developments in China weighed on the Antipodeans; AUD (-0.4%) and NZD (-0.3%). In a relatively quiet NY trading session with limited macro catalysts, the US dollar ended little changed (DXY USD Index: +0.1%). USD/JPY was the outlier; JPY underperformed following comments from BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida, as he tempered expectations of future policy normalization. USD/JPY (+0.8%) hovered \~149.40 in the NY afternoon.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies traded rangebound ahead of the Lunar New Year. USD/CNY opened at 7.1901 and rose to 7.1950, alongside deeper China consumer deflation. THB underperformed as USD/THB rose 0.6% to 35.79, after the BoT vote split showed two members voted to cut the policy rate by 25bp. USD/MYR rose 0.2% to 4.77, while USD/SGD rose 0.1%, to 1.3451. PHP outperformed, USD/PHP fell 0.2% to 55.87.

Most AxJ bonds traded in a narrow range. In China, the 10y yield rose 2bp, to 2.44%, while the China NDIRS curve remained steady. In Korea, the 10y yield ended at 3.39%, 1bp higher. In India, the yield curve twist flattened as the 3y yield rose by 2bp, while the 30y yield fell by 2bp. RBI kept its benchmark rate unchanged and maintained its hawkish stance. Thailand was the outlier as the 10y yield rallied 10bp as market participants increasingly positioned for BoT rate cuts.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mostly weaker. CLP (-1.2%) weakened substantially following release of Chilean January CPI, which included a revision to the calculation that implied inflation was closer to target than previously thought. MXN (-0.6%) and BRL (-0.4%) also ended the session weaker, while COP (+0.3%) outperformed peers as oil ended the session with gains of over 3%. TIIEs ended the session lower as markets reacted to Banxico's statement that opened the door to rate cuts in coming meetings. The rest of LatAm rates sold off; the CAM curve sold off with losses concentrated in the front end (2y: +13bp). The IBR curve ended \~8bp higher across the curve following hawkish comments from board governor Villar.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida spoke at a meeting with local leaders in the Nara prefecture. There seemingly was not any indication of policy normalization at the upcoming March meeting, but he explained the basic thesis for the normalization path. For the future rate hike path, he said it is hard to imagine a path in which the bank would keep raising interest rates rapidly, given their economic outlook. He noted accommodative financial conditions likely will be maintained, even if termination of negative rates were to take place. He also mentioned the bank refers to the market-implied path of policy rates when they prepare the outlook for economic activity and prices. He said the bank does not project inflation will significantly exceed 2%, even if the policy path materializes. He emphasized it is difficult to assess the situation in Japan through analogies with Europe and the US

• Riksbank Deputy Governor Jansson said a rate cut in 1H24 is a possibility, although a cut in May or June is more likely than March

• ECB Governing Council Member Wunsch explained, “I’m on the side of those that believe there’s some value to waiting” before deciding to start cutting rates, especially as wage growth is running at a level not compatible with the ECB’s 2% inflation target. On the other hand, he said “but again, we won’t get full comfort. So, at some point, we’ll have to look at all the information we have and take a bet,” echoing President Lagarde and emphasizing the ECB data dependency

• BoE External Member Mann said her decision to vote for a rate hike was “finely balanced” as she does not expect the “near-term deceleration in headline inflation” to continue

• Richmond Fed President Barkin reiterated the Fed can take its time before cutting rates, and he is very supportive of being patient to get inflation

## Emerging Markets

• RBI kept its benchmark repurchase at 6.50% (C: 6.50%) and the cash reserve ratio at 4.50%. The Monetary Policy Committee decided to remain focused on withdrawal of accommodation to ensure inflation progressively aligns to the target of 4% +/-2%. The central bank forecasts India FY25 GDP growth at 7% while inflation is projected to grow 5.4% in FY24 and 4.5% in FY25. RBI Governor Das said inflation is on a downward trajectory, although it remains above the 4% goal because of surging food costs. He also said it was premature to talk about rate cuts until there is evidence inflation will be kept firmly around the target level. He added the central bank will remain “nimble and flexible” on liquidity and an appropriate mix of instruments will be used to adjust banking-system cash. Das said the INR exchange rate is market determined and India’s current account deficit is manageable

• The CNB lowered its repurchase rate by 50bp, to 6.25% (C: 6.50%), at its February meeting. Six members voted in favour of this decision, and one member voted for lowering rates by 75bp. The statement noted, “Although the CNB started lowering rates gradually in December 2023, the fight against inflation is not over. Interest rates remain significantly positive in real terms and are dampening inflation”

• Banxico kept the policy rate unchanged at 11.25% (C: 11.25%) but opened the door to cuts in their forward guidance. While they noted they will remain data dependent, the change of tone is a dovish signal. Elsewhere in the statement, they revised up their CPI projections, but think these are only temporary adjustments and convergence to the target inflation will not be hindered. Rates rallied alongside the release as the March meeting repriced to -20bp (P: -15bp)

• BanRep Governor Villar stated further cuts must be cautious as inflation and inflation expectations remain far from their targets. He also mentioned possible El Nino risks and central bank’s credibility were further reasons to stay cautious. The timing of these comments adds to the hawkish tone, as Colombia CPI surprised to the downside on Wednesday. Markets have priced an acceleration to 50bp cuts as more likely for the March meeting (-40bp)

The new NISA scheme began at the start of the new year and there were decent net inflows into foreign equities/funds from investment trusts (+JPY1.21tn). The weekly data suggested most of the flow seemingly emerged in the second week of January, while activity had been muted over the rest of the weeks, which indicated one-off inflows related to the launch of the new NISA scheme. Pension funds (trust accounts) seemingly rebalanced from foreign equity to foreign bonds after outperformance in foreign equities. They were decent net sellers of foreign equities (- JPY805.1bn) and decent net buyers of foreign bonds (+JPY341.9bn). GPIF disclosure as of December 2023 suggested they were already slightly overweight foreign equities (25.14%) and underweight foreign bonds (24.44%). Foreign equity continued to outperform foreign bonds throughout January. Banking accounts for both banks and trust banks were decent net buyers of foreign bonds (+JPY1.3tn and +JPY548.1bn, respectively). As UST yields retraced some of the year-end rally in January, there may have been some demand for tactical dip buying. Activity in lifers was almost balanced (+JPY40.1bn). Since FX hedge costs remain high, they seem to be reluctant to invest in foreign bonds in earnest. The amount for gross purchases (+JPY943.5bn) seemingly increased, compared to the past could of months (+JPY650bn), this suggested some may consider non-FX hedged investments to take advantage of the dip in USD/JPY

In Europe, they were modest net buyers of German sovereign bonds (+JPY154.8bn) and UK sovereign bonds (+JPY261.2bn). MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
