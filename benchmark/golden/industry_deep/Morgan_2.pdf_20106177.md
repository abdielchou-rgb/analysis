# January 26

Mild UST twist flattener ensues, spending comes in strong;
ECB's Vujcic urges patience on rate cuts, EUR retraces losses;
USD/CNH remains isolated from equity vol; JGBs rally after weak Tokyo CPI; CLP weakens sharply after FinMin pauses USD sales;
DXY at 103.47 (-0.1%); US 10y at 4.137% (+1.9bp).

• A mild UST twist flattener partially reverses the prior session's bullsteepening after slighter weaker core PCE of 0.17% m/m (C: 0.2%) pares with strong personal spending, with upward revisions.

• ECB Governing Council Member Vujcic says “we need patience at the moment to get enough data to be confident that inflation is really firmly sustainably on the way to our medium-term target.”

• More explicit pushback against market pricing of rate cuts from ECB policymakers provides a slight boost to EUR (+0.1%).

• USD/CNH (+0.1%) is little changed despite further local equity market volatility (Hang Seng: -1.6%).

• The belly of the JGB curve outperforms after Tokyo CPI (ex-fresh food) slows to 1.6% y/y in January (P: 2.1%) as optimism for sharp BoJ normalization eases.

• CLP (-1.4%) displays substantially weakness after the FinMin pauses their dollar sales.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

Exhaustion and apprehension were perhaps the two best words to sum up the Friday session. After the concentrated volatility of the Thursday session, macro markets were seemingly all too eager to idle into the weekend – save for a brief bout of volatility around December PCE data – ahead of major risk events next week.

The string of positive days for the S&P 500 (-0.07%) stalled at six as artificial intelligence beneficiaries gave back some of their recent outperformance. More explicit pushback against market pricing of ECB rate cuts supported EUR (+0.1%) and weighed on the US dollar (DXY USD Index: -0.1%). JPY (-0.3%) exhibited mild weakness after preliminary January Tokyo CPI (ex-fresh food) showed a larger-thanexpected deceleration. A late afternoon surge in WTI crude oil futures (+0.8%) above \$78/bbl capped the strongest week for the commodity since September.

A closely-followed December core PCE reading at core PCE of 0.17% m/m (C: 0.2%; P: 0.1%) potentially underwhelmed expectations, which had formed after 8:30am ET on Thursday. Yet, it was strong personal spending, with upward revisions to prior month, which was the focal point of the session. This produced a mild UST bearflattener, which partially reversed the bull-steeper from the prior session and left the rates market debating the Fed reaction function to quicker-than-expected disinflation against less of a moderation in economic activity. The first release of the Atlanta Fed’s 1Q24 GDPNow estimate came in at 3.0% q/q.

Consider this a truly choppy week. Initial capitulation of China equities was met with a brief revival in sentiment after a surprise 50bp RRR cut, only for renewed uncertainty in the US-China regulatory sphere. A hawkish lean at the January BoJ

meeting weighed initially heavily on JGBs, given the BoJ’s gradual rise in “likelihood” of achieving their outlook, but a larger-than-expected deceleration in preliminary Tokyo CPI ultimately led to fervent squaring of short positions. Not to mention the dovish interpretation of the ECB press conference, which produced a notable EGB bull-steepening, only for concerted pushback from policymakers to end the week with EGBs mildly cheaper.

One concrete aspect from the week is that the Fed confirmed its Bank Term Funding Program (BTFP) will expire on March 11. In addition, going forward, the interest rate on new BTFP loans will be adjusted to be no lower than the interest rate on reserve balances (currently 5.40%) in effect on the day the loan is made (as opposed to 1y OIS + 10bp).

January 31 will be marked on many a market participants’ calendar. For the Treasury quarterly refunding announcement, we expect Treasury to continue with one final quarter of gradual increases to coupon auction sizes, but at a more moderate pace in longer-dated tenors – a replica of the increases in November. We do not expect Treasury to “opportunistically” target the long-end given the direct contrast with “regular and predictable issuance” (for more, read here).

For the January FOMC meeting, our economists expect the Fed to remain on hold at 5.25-5.50% and expect little change to the statement. Should the FOMC outcome be neutral for duration, we see potential for the recent rise in yields to fade amid a cooling inflation backdrop (for more, read here).

## Japan:

The rally in USTs overnight on Thursday and weak January Tokyo CPI data led to outperformance in the belly of the JGB curve, as market participants rushed to square short positions. With CPI (ex-fresh food) below 2.0% y/y, market participants seemingly repriced the future BoJ normalization path lower; 7y JGBs rallied by 4.0bp d/d to 0.44%. Long-end JGBs continued to lag, without demand from real money market participants; 30y JGBs only rallied by 1.5bp d/d to 1.815% on a simple yield basis. TONA OIS rallied, led by the 7-10y tenor alongside JGBs. XCCY basis tightened across the curve.

During Tokyo trading hours, there was limited price action in the FX market. While the weak Tokyo CPI data led to a massive rally in JGBs, the JPY reaction seemed limited. Throughout Tokyo trading hours, USD/JPY hovered \~147.60. Other CCY/JPY pairs also traded sideways, but they began to rally as risk sentiment recovered from the London morning; EUR/JPY rallied to 160.60.

## Europe:

The London morning opened with light activity across the G10 before EUR weakness started to trickle in after weaker-than-expected German GFK consumer confidence data. It was not long before the sell-off reversed, as ECB policymakers began to push back on the market’s dovish interpretation of the January ECB meeting. EUR strength continued amid larger-than-expected Euro Area M3 money supply growth, before progress stalled as markets awaited US PCE data. EUR ended the London session 0.2% stronger against the US dollar. Overall, it was relative quiet London session for price action in G10 FX.

Other than EUR, activity remained light across G10 currencies for the London session. CHF outperformed and closed the London trading session +0.4% stronger against the US dollar. NOK/SEK broke above 1.00, a level at which it tends to face considerable technical resistance, and closed the London session at 1.0019.

In the London morning, European duration opened richer after the overnight rally in USTs and after German consumer confidence was below consensus expectations. The rally was short-lived and yields then edged higher for most of the London session. US core PCE inflation rose by 0.17% m/m in December, which lowered the y/ y rate to 2.9% (P: 3.0%). Initially, European duration rallied but the move was faded quickly, likely as spending accelerated and was revised higher. The UST sell-off spilled over into Europe and 10y Bund yields closed the week at 2.30%, 4bp lower w/w (+1bp d/d). Despite some volatility on curves during the London session, the 2s10s swap curve (against 6m) was unchanged, 10s30s steepened by 0.5bp (7bp w/ w) and 5s10s30s richened less than 1bp (-6bp w/w).

ECB pricing was stable on Friday, as markets priced in \~137bp of cuts in 2024, including a 70% probability of a 25bp cut in April and 50bp of cuts in 2025. Core ASW widened opened richer but tightened during the session and closed 0.5bp tighter. The 10y BTP-Bund spreads tightened by 1bp to 152bp, to test new lows.

## Dollar Bloc:

Despite strong personal spending, headline and core PCE inflation data were in-line with consensus expectations, thus the US dollar reaction was quite muted. There were only marginal USD gains, despite a small rise in 10y UST yields. The DXY USD Index (-0.2%) was weighed down by EUR gains while JPY also closed the London session 0.2% lower, despite the broader US dollar weakness.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies were stable against USD, except for THB and PHP. THB outperformed, 0.5% stronger to 35.56, as gold prices rose. A surprise surplus in the December trade balance for Thailand could have also aided. PHP gained 0.4%, to 56.3, following media reports BSP is considering easing documentary requirements for FX transactions and the central bank’s reiteration it stands ready to adjust policy to mitigate second round effects of inflation. USD/CNH finished the session largely unchanged at \~7.18, despite renewed weakness in the local equity markets (CSI 300: -0.3%; Hang Seng Index: -1.6%), dragged down by a proposed US legislation that would block some Chinese biotech companies from government contracts. USD/ KRW was similarly unresponsive to the local equity market, stable at \~1,336, despite KOSPI outperformance (+0.3%) and US\$415mn of foreign equity inflows.

AxJ rates rallied broadly, most curves bull-steepened following the overnight trend in global rates. Hong Kong and Korea rates outperformed. In Korea, the 2y NDIRS yield fell the most, by 5bp, as BoK rate cut pricing increased following market participants’ dovish interpretation of Thursday’s ECB meeting. The 2s10s curve bullsteepened by 1bp. Hong Kong 2y swap yields fell by 4bp. In China, 1y CNY NDIRS yield fell 3bp and the 1s5s curve bull-steepened 2bp. India and Indonesia rates relative underperformed. India yields were largely stable across the NDOIS curve, while 10y IndoGB rallied just 1bp amid political uncertainty.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mixed. COP (+0.7%) outperformed peers and erased Thursday's losses. MXN (+0.3%) and BRL (+0.2%) continued to grind stronger. CLP (-1.4%) weakened substantially as the FinMin paused their dollar sales. In rates, LatAm curves ended mixed as well. 10y TIIEs and IBR sold off 4bp, while the very front end rallied \~2bp. CAM rallied, mostly in the front-end (1y: -6bp), amid few local developments. DIs ended \~4bp lower, the very front-end outperformed (January 2025s: -7bp) after the downside IPCA surprise of 0.31% m/m in January (C: 0.47%; P: 0.40%).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Minutes from the December BoJ monetary policy meeting suggested there were divided opinions about the distance toward normalization. Some members expressed a view that the determination of whether the achievement of their target had come into sight should not be based on specific numerical values, such as the rate of wage increases achieved in labor-management wage negotiations or other indicators. On the other hand, some members said the Bank was currently not in a situation where it would fall behind the curve. They added, it would not be too late to wait for developments of the spring wage negotiations. Members also discussed what means and which order of procedures would be appropriate in the event of a removal of the NIRP and YCC frameworks. A few members pointed out one option could be to keep a moderate framework to avoid instability in long-term interest rates, even after sustainable and stable achievement of the price stability target had come into sight. Members also discussed policy conduct from a somewhat long-term perspective. A few members expressed the view that the Bank was likely to continue with substantial monetary easing for the time being, even after termination of the NIRP and YCC frameworks

• Riksbank sold USD 420mn and no euros in its sixteenth week (January 8-12) of hedging FX reserves. So far, 89% and 78% of the targeted USD 8bn and EUR 2bn have been reported as hedged

• ECB Governing Council Member Kazaks argued “risks to inflation are balanced." He pushed back against early rate cuts and said "even if we revise our inflation forecast significantly lower...even if we slide below 2% in 2025 or 2026, as long as this difference from the target of 2% is not wide and persistent, in my view we should not be reacting aggressively with rate cuts” and “if you move too early, then the damage you do is much bigger than if you are a bit too late”

• ECB Governing Council Member Simkus explained “the further we go into the year of 2024, the likelihood of a cut increases.” He also said he was less optimistic than market pricing for April

• ECB Governing Council Member Vujcic refuted the ECB press conference on Thursday was dovish and said “lately I have the feeling that markets take whatever you say as being dovish.” He urged to be patient: “we need patience at the moment to get enough data to be confident that inflation is really firmly sustainably on the way to our medium-term target [and] that will come gradually, that will come over every month”

## Emerging Markets

• BoT Assistant Governor Piti said the MPC is not wedded to a fixed stance and is ready to adjust its neutral stance if the incoming data and outlook for growth, inflation and financial conditions warrant it. This comes after Deputy Finance Minister Julapun said on Wednesday the benchmark rate was too high, hurting the public’s standard of living and weighing the economy

• BSP is considering easing documentary requirements for FX transactions related to inward investments and non-trade current account transactions. The BSP is proposing to have free-of-charge processing for the processing of foreign currency loans, inward investments and other FX transactions, and the exemption of registration of foreign portfolio investments unless the market participant buys foreign currency from banks for conversion to PHP or earnings for remittances. It is also considering allowing banks and FX companies to sell foreign currencies to tourists or migrant workers returning to the Philippines, and requiring the registration of debt securities issued by private sector residents

• BSP Governor Remolona reiterated the central bank stands ready to adjust monetary policy settings to “mitigate second round effects and better anchor inflation expectations.” He pointed out there are “significant” upside risks to food and transport prices. Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
