# CBC charting its own path

## A dovish hike, but the door left slightly ajar for more hikes

While we were expecting it to strike a hawkish tone, as warned in our report, we thought it would conclude its study on "structural change in inflation" before resuming rate hikes.

Governor Yang underlined "limited upside for rates in future", rendering the CBC's latest rate hike as a dovish one. More importantly, he noted that the policy rates are "now at an appropriate level" and that the CBC "won't keep hiking rates whenever CPI is over 2%", especially as it expects inflation to "gradually fall". Although the CBC raised its 2024 headline and core inflation forecasts by 0.27ppts and 0.2ppts to 2.16% and 2.03%, respectively, as it assumed a 10% hike in electricity prices, the figures would fall below last year's headline and core inflation of 2.49% and 2.59%, respectively.

That said, the risk of further rate hikes remains non-negligible, given ongoing concerns about a "structural change in inflation". By February this year, Taiwan's five-year headline inflation had risen to 1.57% from 0.77% in February 2020. In fact, both headline and core inflation stood relatively high, at 2.24% yoy and 2.28% yoy, respectively, in the first two months of this year, with services inflation (2.6%) leading that of goods (2.2%) (Figure 19). Like in advanced economies, Taiwan's labour-market conditions have tightened, with the employment rate rising to its highest level since 2000, to 96.7%. Taiwan's working-age population peaked in November 2015, although the impact was mitigated by the rising participation rate (by 0.5ppts to 59.3%) and increasing foreign (migrant) workers (constituting 4.4% of the workforce now). Data (through January) pointed to a notable acceleration in wage pressure, which could result in more persistent, higher inflation. (Figure 4) The Yuan passed a minimum wage act that allows for more regular predictable increases, taking into account CPI, among other factors. This year, Taiwan's minimum wage was hiked by 4.1%. Together with electricity price hikes, the matter of removing the hospital registration fee cap has also added to inflation concerns.

The central bank also remains concerned about the recovery in housing-market activity, as "growth in housing loans and loans to the real estate sector has picked up...and all banks' ratios of real estate lending to total lending remain elevated" (Figure 23 and Figure 24). The January election results highlighted the public's call for, among others, quality jobs, higher wages and affordable housing. Affordable housing – pitted against rising housing prices – has certainly reinforced our expectation of no rate cuts by the CBC through 2025, despite our US team's forecast of 175bps of rate cuts by the Fed, starting in June.

On growth, the CBC's latest policy statement reflected the board's growing confidence in Taiwan's growth prospects – also raising its 2024 growth forecast by 0.1ppts to 3.22% – noting that private consumption "gained momentum on stronger consumer confidence amid an economic recovery" amid sustained gains in payrolls, while exports recorded "continuous growth, underpinned by stabilizing final demand and booming business for AI and other emerging technology applications" (Figure 9), although investment remained an area of weakness (Figure 3). Note that consumer sentiment has continued to recover this year, led by a sharp rebound in the consumer outlook for the stock market, to levels not seen after the pandemic-induced plunge (Figure 5).

Our baseline view has the CBC holding its rates steady for the foreseeable future, with average inflation likely moderating to slightly below 2% in 2025 as growth moderates. In connection, our US team's forecast of a total of 175bps rate cuts by the Fed (starting in June) point to a rather high bar for further rate hikes by the CBC. That said, the risk of further rate hikes by the CBC looks non-negligible. First, the CBC has yet to conclude on its assessment of "structural change in inflation", suggesting that its view on neutral rate is subject to change - ie higher than the current level. Second, our US team has raised the risk of an alternative scenario that could see the Fed funds rate bottoming well above 4%, with the Fed opting for a prolonged pause after mid-cycle (limited) rate cuts, leaving the TW dollar notably weaker than expected against the US dollar. Such scenario could prompt the CBC to hike further, albeit after the Fed's pause. If nothing else, the CBC's latest decision is a reminder that both cyclical and structural factors are in play. Moreover, its reaction function prior to the GFC is far more informative than the post-GFC period. Recall that the CBC hiked after a prolonged pause in early 1981 and it also continued to hike through June 2008, despite the Fed's rate cuts. (Figure 22).
