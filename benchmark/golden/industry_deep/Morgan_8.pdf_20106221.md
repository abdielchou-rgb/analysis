# Morgan Stanley

## PFR Primer / Data Description

## CFTC Non-Commercial Futures Positions

The CFTC publishes a weekly Commitments of Traders report (COT) that provides a breakdown of open interest in futures and options markets by trader category – commercial and non-commercial users. Together, these positions normally account for 70-90% of the market, with the remainder below reportable levels. Of these, we track the net non-commercial positions as a proxy for how active investors are positioned, including the delta-adjusted futures-equivalent option positions. We then convert all positions to 10y Treasury note equivalents to allow us to compare positions across contracts.

CME futures positions include Eurodollars (ED) and Treasury futures (TU, FV, TY, US and WN). We aggregate ED, TU and FV positions to come up with a ‘front-end’ positioning metric and aggregate TY, US and WN to come up with a ‘back-end positioning metric. The steepener/flattener positioning metric is then calculated as ‘front-end’ positions less ‘back-end positions, and this has typically been well correlated with the slope of the UST 2s10s curve.

Release time: Every Friday by 4:30PM Central Standard Time, for the week ending on Tuesday.

## Primary Dealer Positions (PDP)

The Federal Reserve releases primary dealer positions for Treasuries, TIPS, Agency debt, MBS and Corporate debt on a weekly basis. We track these primary dealer positions versus the respective price action of these assets (e.g., Treasury positions versus 10y Treasury yields).

Release time: Every Thursday at 4:15PM Eastern Standard Time, for the week ending the prior Wednesday.

## Large Commercial US Bank Positions

The Federal Reserve’s H.8 release provides a weekly update of the aggregate balance sheet for all commercial banks in the United States, and of this, we track non-seasonally adjusted positions for large domestically chartered commercial banks. Specifically, we look at bank holdings of Treasury/Agencies (break-up not available), MBS, C&I loans, and cash assets. All four series are graphed versus bank deposits to see where banks are putting their money. In addition, we graph Treasury/Agency holdings versus the 2s10s curve, with a flattening of the curve typically leading to lower NIM and lower bank purchases (and vice versa). MBS holdings are likewise graphed versus current coupon OAS; when MBS is cheap, banks will typically buy more.

Release time: Every Friday by 4:15PM Eastern Standard Time, for the week ending the prior Wednesday.

## Foreign Central Bank (FCB) Positions

The Federal Reserve’s H.4 release provides the face value of US Treasury and Agency debt / MBS held in custody for foreign official and international accounts. We report the holdings of each, the rolling 1-month Treasury versus Agency debt/MBS bias (calculated by subtracting the Agency/MBS flows from the Treasury flows, with a positive number signifying a preference for Treasuries), the rolling 1-month Treasury purchases versus the 2s5s curve and 5y yields outright, and the rolling 1-month Agency debt / MBS purchases versus Agency debt 3y Libor OAS. The idea is to see which assets the FCB’s buying and how these purchases are correlated to the respective price action of these assets.

Release time: Every Thursday by 4:30PM Eastern Standard Time, for the week ending on Wednesday.

## Mutual Fund Flows

ICI and Simfund both collect data on mutual fund flows, and we track the flows of various funds, including total fixed income funds, corporate funds, global bond funds, money market funds and equity funds. This gives us an idea of what retail investors are doing with their money. We also offer a breakdown of the total fixed income fund flows among the different asset class categories.

Release time: Usually by the third week of every month, for the previous month.

## TIC Data on Foreign Flows

The Treasury International Capital (TIC) reporting system collects data on transactions in long-term securities, for both public flows – i.e., foreign central banks (FCBs) – and private flows. We show the total foreign long-term TIC flows (versus 10y yields), foreign Agency debt/MBS flows (versus MBS CC OAS), foreign T-bill flows (versus 6-month T-bill rate), foreign corporate debt flows (versus IG credit index), foreign Treasury flows (versus the 10y) and foreign equity flows (versus S&P 500). The idea here is to track what foreign account are doing, as this is extremely important for certain asset classes such as Treasuries, where foreign accounts own >50% of the marketable debt.

We also report the five largest buyers/sellers across Treasuries, Agency debt/MBS, and Corporates over the most recent three months. This helps identify where the foreign flows are coming from. Historically, the largest five buyers/sellers have accounted for approximately 80-90% of the foreign flows.

Release time: Mid-month, with a lag of one and a half months.
