## Key highlights:

## The US has the highest domestic exposure within Developed Markets, at 73%

US companies derive 73% of their revenues domestically, 11% from Europe, 8% from Asia Pacific ex Japan (including 3% from China) and 4% from Latin America.

## Europe's domestic exposure, at 44%, is the lowest seen in 27 years of running this data

44% of European companies' revenues come from Developed Europe, followed by 31% from Emerging Markets and 22% from North America. China exposure is estimated to be 8%, more than double that of the US.

## Japan domestic exposure at 57% – remainder split equally between DM and EM

57% of Japanese companies' revenues are estimated to come from Japan, with the remaining exposure split fairly equally across other DM countries and EM (North America 17%, Europe 7%, EM \~20%).

## Just 14% of EM exposure comes from DM, 72% of revenues come from their home country

72% of EM companies' revenues come from their home country, according to our estimates. The remaining 28% is split fairly equally between EM and DM countries, with North America and Europe accounting for 8% and 5%, respectively, of EM sales exposure.

## Our database also shows end-demand revenue exposure at a global

level. In addition to geographical exposure, we have also compiled a database of revenue exposure to end-demand sources, i.e. government, corporates and consumers, for all global regions. US companies derive 49% of their revenues from consumers directly, while European corporates are the least exposed to consumers with 39% exposure.

## We have also compiled cost exposure data for European and US

companies. Based on our analysts' estimates, 54% of European cost bases are located inside Europe. Outside of Europe, the US is the most important region for European companies' costs, with 18% exposure. In the US, around 77% of companies our analysts surveyed reported that 50% or more of their cost bases were domestic. After the US, Asia is the largest cost centre of US companies, with 7% of companies incurring over 50% of their costs in the region.

Global stock screens to play global regional themes. Our database allows us to construct screens of global stocks with the highest exposure to different geographical regions and currencies. For example, we show those companies in the US, Europe, Japan and EM that are most exposed to China.

## Global Highlights

For almost three decades, we have been publishing a strategy report, known as the Global Exposure Guide, which quantifies the revenue exposure of European companies to different geographical regions around the world. This has become one of our most popular and requested products, given the lack of quality, granular data available from other sources and/or in company reports.

Today, we publish the 9th edition of the Global version of this product. This report quantifies the revenue exposure of companies in Europe, the US, Japan and Emerging Markets to different regions around the world. Our database covers over 3,000 companies globally, has revenue exposure data for 17 different regions and is compiled through an internal survey of over 250 Morgan Stanley Research analysts who leverage their experience and knowledge of their stocks to provide us with the most granular and comprehensive exposure estimates possible. Given it is difficult to compile the data in a consistent format using reported company information, we consider our database to be highly proprietary and valuable. Please contact us or your Morgan Stanley sales representative if you wish to receive anything from this database.

## Regional Exposure

We make four points about European companies' aggregate geographical revenue split:

1) European companies derive over half of their revenues from outside Europe. This number rises to over 60% on a free-float market cap weighted basis. – As we show in Exhibit 15 , 44% of European companies' revenues come from within Developed Europe. This number falls to 39% on a free float market cap weighted basis. Emerging Markets are the most important foreign region for European companies, contributing 31% of the market's aggregate revenues. North American revenue exposure stands at 22%.

2) In nearly three decades of running this data, Europe's domestic exposure has never been as low as it is today – Our data suggest that European domestic revenue exposure is expected to return to 2022 levels this year, after an uptick in 2023. This decline likely reflects the normalization in revenues from domestically-exposed utilities companies following outsized revenue growth from the sector in recent years.

3) EM revenue exposure is expected to tick higher in 2024 – Our analysts are expecting European corporates' revenue exposure to Emerging Markets to rise in 2024 and to account for 31% of European revenues in aggregate. The uptick in estimated EM exposure is fairly broad based, with exposure to China, EM Europe and Latin America all forecast to increase in 2024. Exposure to the Middle East and Africa is expected to fall to 4.6%.

We adjusted our revenue exposure methodology this year to improve the way we capture indirect exposures (e.g. through joint ventures and minority stakes). This change in methodology contributed 50bps to the rise in EM exposure ( Exhibit 19 ) and 100bps to the pick up in both Developing Asia and China exposures ( Exhibit 20 and Exhibit 21 ). Without these adjustments, European companies' revenue exposure to Emerging Markets, Developing Asia and China would be 31.3%, 14.5% and 7.4% respectively.

4) North America expected to contribute \~22% of European sales – The importance of North America to corporate Europe's revenues is expected to continue rising in 2024 ( Exhibit 18 ). Our analysts' estimates suggest that North America will account for \~22% of European revenue exposure in 2024; a historical high, on our data. The US and Canada are estimated to account for 20.3% and 1.5%, respectively.

Pulling all this together, the expected increase in EM exposure in 2024, coupled with North American exposure remaining high, means European domestic exposure is near an all-time low on the 27-year data history we have available to us. These companies are not manufacturing intermediate goods in Europe for use as inputs in products sold elsewhere; they are generating revenue from final goods sold in the region. Software & Services is driving this exposure, and Software companies are deriving 23% of their revenue from Europe.

Consumer Discretionary, Communication Services, and Industrials all have revenue exposure to Europe in the double digits. Although Health Care doesn't have much exposure to Europe at the sector level, Pharma, Biotech, and Life Sciences companies generate 21% of their revenue in the region. Consumer Staples just missed the top five sectors cutoff at 10% but the Food, Beverage & Tobacco and Household & Personal Products industry groups each generate more than 20% of their revenue in Europe.
