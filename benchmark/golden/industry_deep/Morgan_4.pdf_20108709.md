# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 14

Risk-reduction theme drives Euro area activity; BoJ keeps rates on hold and delays QT announcement; BoJ's Ueda keeps July hike on the table; USTs rally amid risk-off sentiment; Fed's Mester wants more months of "good data;" Indonesia underperforms; DXY at 105.52 (0.3%); US 10y at 4.221% (-2.3bp).

• Risk reduction theme in the Euro area continues to dominate and supports a flight to safety (USD/EUR: 0.3%; 10y Bund: -11bp; Euro Stoxx: - 2.0%).

• At the June MPM, the BoJ kept rates unchanged, as expected, but deferred the announcement of QT plan to the July MPM; USD/JPY surged toward \~158 immediately following the more dovish outcome.

• During the BoJ press conference, BoJ Governor Ueda says a July rate hike remains a possibility if macro conditions support it; market implied pricing indicate a little less than a 50% probability of a July hike.

• USTs rally led by the long-end, alongside risk-off sentiment in Europe coupled with weak economic data in the US with consumer sentiment falling to a seven-week low.

• Cleveland Fed President Mester says May inflation data is a “great gift” but needs to see additional data, adding that the median SEP projection is “pretty close” to her own outlook on the economy.

• Indonesia underperforms amid media reports that the new administration may increase the government’s debt ratio to its highest level in two decades (10y INDOGB: -19bp; USD/IDR: +0.8%).

## Developed Markets

## United States:

The overnight session was highlighted by the BoJ meeting where, as expected, rates were left unchanged. However, contrary to many market participants' expectations, there was no immediate reduction to JGB purchases. Instead, the BoJ deferred the decision on the detailed QT plan to the July meeting. The dovish outcome sent USD/ JPY higher to \~158 and JGB yields rallied across the curve. During the press conference, BoJ Governor Ueda maintained the optionality of a July hike depending on macro conditions. He also noted that the JGB reductions would be "substantial," but the BoJ will not use the reduction of JGB purchases as an active monetary policy tool.

Into the London morning, the risk-off sentiment in Europe began to dominate the market. There remained heightened political uncertainty regarding the French elections as news reports of the formation of left-wing and right-wing coalitions emerged, which supported a flight to safety from European market participants. European equities continued to fall and EGB spreads continued to widen with the 10y OAT/Bund spread widening 29bp w/w, a record move. Additionally, the USD continued to strengthen overnight as market participants turned to safe-haven currencies.

The risk reduction theme supported USTs, which opened the NY session better bid. In the NY morning, economic data also backed the UST rally. May import and export prices were softer than expected, further confirming the deceleration in inflation in

May. Later, the preliminary University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment survey for May showed that sentiment fell to 65.6 (P: 69.1), the lowest level in over seven months, with sentiment around both current conditions and expectations falling.

Now that we have exited the Fed blackout period, we heard from Cleveland Fed President Mester who acknowledged that the May CPI data was welcome news but noted that she needs to see a few more months of good inflation data as the risks to inflation are still on the upside. The June FOMC meeting was the last meeting where Mester served as a voting member, as she will retire on June 30 and will be succeeded by Beth Hammack.

Into the NY afternoon, USTs gradually ground higher and the front-end fully retraced the overnight richening. Ultimately, the prevailing driver of UST activity this week was softer-than-expected May inflation data combined with risk-off sentiment in Europe, which both supported a global duration rally. UST yields ended the week \~20bp lower across the curve and have fully reversed the move higher in yields following the upside surprise in the May headline nonfarm payrolls data last Friday.

Usage of the Standing Repo Facility returned to near zero on Friday, which confirmed the \$100mn pickup on Wednesday was likely related to a small value exercise.

Gold rallied +1.3% and ended the week with its first weekly gain in four weeks. The risk-off sentiment in Europe, coupled with weaker inflation data in the US signaling that the Fed may cut rates soon, supported gold prices.

The S&P 500 closed relatively unchanged. Divergence in the underlying sector components persisted with technology stocks continuing to gain with multiple companies reaching new highs, while nearly all other industries ended Friday in the red.

## Japan:

Following the rally in USTs on Thursday, JGBs broadly rallied across the curve. The curve bull-flattened during the Tokyo morning. We believe that the lower UST yields drove the lower term premium, particularly in the long end. The upward momentum in prices accelerated after the dovish BoJ outcome with no immediate reduction in JGB purchases. The BoJ announced it will decide on a detailed QT plan at its July MPM. The BoJ also announced it will set up a meeting with bond market participants to discuss the reduction plan.

The rally continued across tenors into the Tokyo close. The 2y JGB rallied 3.0bp to 0.295%, 10y rallied 3.5bp to 0.930%, and 30y rallied 2.5bp to 2.090% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS bull-flattened and outperformed JGBs on the long end of the curve on the back of demand to square positions. XCCY basis widened on a broad basis, particularly in the front end.

Although a downward surprise in the US PPI data pushed USD/JPY down to 156.6 instantaneously overnight, USD/JPY gradually retraced the initial loss into the Tokyo morning. USD/JPY continued to trade higher from 157.0 to 157.4 ahead of the BoJ MPM. Despite some weakening US inflation and labor market data, Fed officials seemed reluctant to pivot, and accordingly carry-focused trades continued to support USD/JPY.

As widely expected, the BoJ maintained the policy rate status quo. While market participants broadly anticipated an immediate JGB reduction at the June MPM, the BoJ carried over the decision for a detailed QT plan to the upcoming July MPM. In response to the BoJ’s announcement, USD/JPY jumped up toward 157.9 and traded \~158.0 ahead of BoJ Governor Ueda’s press conference. At the press conference, Ueda’s remarks were relatively hawkish, and USD/JPY failed to break meaningfully above 158.0.

After the press conference, USD/JPY steadily dropped toward 157.0 into the London morning alongside the rally in UST yields. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY.

## Europe:

In France, news of left-wing and right-wing coalitions forming that would effectively squeeze out President Macron’s middle drove a flight to safety. 10y Bunds rallied 11bp, and the EUR traded progressively lower and ultimately ended the London session down -0.4%. Elsewhere in Europe, Swedish inflation data surprised slightly to the upside, but it did little to affect SEK given the impact of one-off events in the May data, and the fact that CPIF remained well within the Riksbank’s accepted “variation band.” Instead, SEK continued to show its strongly negative US dollar beta and sold off -0.5%. In the rest of G10, the theme was firmly around risk-off or risk-reduction. Growth currencies were hit across the board. NOK, supported by oil steadiness, fell only -0.2%. Sterling underperformed and fell -0.6% ahead of the CPI-sensitive BoE meeting next week. The funder/safe-haven currencies fared better with CHF up +0.5% and JPY down -0.2% due to the BoJ leaving rates unchanged and delaying the announcement of QT. The BoJ drove a JPY sell-off early in the London session that the safe-haven bids were unable to fully reverse.

## Dollar Bloc:

Momentum in G10 FX price action spilled over from Thursday and the USD continued to climb. The antipodeans, AUD and NZD, were down -0.3% and -0.5%, respectively, as growth currencies were hit given a risk-off sentiment in Europe. CAD was relatively stable on Thursday. Ultimately, the DXY USD Index rallied +0.3% amid the risk-reduction theme.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly amid USD strength on the back of the BoJ meeting and weak risk sentiment in the euro area. CNH, INR, TWD, and THB were relative outperformers in the region, and remained largely stable against USD. USD/CNH gained just 0.05% to \~7.2740 even as the USD/CNY fixing made a new recent high of 7.1151 (P: 7.1122), the Hang Seng Index (-0.9%) underperformed and China’s May credit data came in softer than expected. The stability in USD/TWD was aided by

the outperformance of TAIEX (+0.9%), while KRW weakened 0.4% alongside the more lackluster performance of local equities. IDR underperformed the most in the region amid fiscal concerns. USD/IDR rallied 0.9% to 16,412 amid media reports that the new administration may raise Indonesia’s government debt-to-GDP by 10% over the next five years. Local equity markets fell 1.4% and Indonesia rates underperformed, with 10y INDOGB selling off 19bp to 7.17%, not far from the April 2024 and October 2023 high of 7.22%.

Other AxJ rates rallied broadly following the trend in US rates. Similar to FX, China, India, and Thailand rates were relatively stable. In China, 10y CGB yield was down 1bp, with the NDIRS curve similarly shifted down by 1bp. In India, 10y G-sec yield fell 1bp to 6.98% amid INR 340bn of bond auctions as planned, while the NDOIS curve was stable. In Thailand, 10y THAIGB and the NDOIS curve also shifted down by just 1bp. Hong Kong rates outperformed the most in the region; 5y IRS yields fell 10bp and the 1s5s curve bull-flattened 5bp. Korea and Singapore rates also rallied; 5y swap yields fell 5bp and 4bp, respectively.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• At the June BoJ MPM, the central bank maintained the policy rate status quo as widely expected. The BoJ announced that it will release a detailed plan for future reductions of JGB purchases at the July MPM. It maintained the current purchase stance until the July MPM. As for the reduction plan, the BoJ announced it will set up a meeting with bond market participants to discuss the specific plan for the next 1-2 years. At the press conference, BoJ Governor Ueda emphasized: 1) the reduction of JGB purchases would be a “substantial amount,” but BoJ will proceed with the plan carefully in a foreseeable manner; 2) the BoJ will not use QT as an active monetary policy tool; and 3) the BoJ does not rule out the possibility of a rate hike at the July MPM if macro conditions support one, despite the timing of QT plan announcement. As for underlying inflation, Ueda did not clearly mention how the recently weaker JPY might have impacted underlying inflation. That said, he mentioned the BoJ is “carefully watching the weakening yen’s effect toward domestic inflation”

• Cleveland Fed President Mester said this week’s inflation data are welcome news and she is happy to see inflation moving lower after stalling in 1Q24. Nonetheless, Mester said she wants to see a few more months of good inflation data before cutting rates. She also said the median SEP projection is close to hers for the economy

## Emerging Markets

• The PBoC pledged to increase funding for scientific and technological innovation and to offer strong financial support to hasten the development of new quality productive forces. The central bank has chosen \~7,000 firms for tech relending loans and will assess another 320,000 companies

• BI Governor Warjiyo said IDR’s current situation is better compared to the end of 2023, with IDR's depreciation being relatively low compared to KRW, PHP, THB, and JPY. He highlighted the BI’s triple intervention strategies have kept the currency stable despite global economic volatility. Despite headwinds, he sees inflation remaining within the BI’s 1.5-3.5% target for 2024 and 2025

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
