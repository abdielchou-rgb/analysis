# Global Economics & Strategy Remapping market structure in Japanese stocks

## What is this all about?

Our multidimensional correlation framework provides a comprehensive overview of how any relatively large universe of assets is evolving. Here we apply it to the TOPIX 100 index, constituting all Japan's listed companies with a market cap in excess of JPY1 trillion (about USD6.5 billion). The point is to describe how each institutionally relevant stock behaves normally and how that has been changing of late.

Tables one and two filter for the biggest outliers over the last two months relative to their long-term (post-COVID) and shorter term (prior two-month) states. The charts on pages three and four provide broader context by showing snapshots of the entire market structure in each period back to and including a pre-COVID baseline. Individual stocks are represented as dots and are coloured by industry group. Size is a reflection of connectedness and outlines illustrate vol-normalized returns (darker blues are strong positives, reds negative). Plot positioning is relative, distance indicating difference mapped back into 2D space. Pages five and six detail the top and bottom quartiles performance wise.

## Which names are diverging most from their post-COVID norm?

Today's top outlier relative to our long-term benchmark is M3, where caution lingers in their mainstay pharmaceutical marketing business; we have the stock rated as a Sell. Shimano (Buy), Keyence (not rated) and Nintendo (Sell) are also screening as behaving idiosyncratically relative to their peers, despite not moving much either. Only Omron (not rated) and Kyocera (Neutral) are diverging positively, while Sysmex (Neutral) as well as NIDEC (Buy) have been slipping.

## What is changing most on the margin?

The same exercise but measured against a mid-May through mid-July 2024 reference window (Figure 2) flags Shimano again plus FANUC (not rated) and Asahi Group (Buy). The former is steadily underperforming on sustained weakness in the EV and battery capex space. The latter is not doing much but warrants watching, with concerns having receded after the company posted better results from its domestic and European businesses.

## How can we categorize the situation today versus two months ago?

Stock groupings are less disparate than they were two months ago, and market performance overall has been somewhat better (Figures 3 & 4). Financials are still doing well, and auto companies plus rail transportation continued to lag. Toyota (Neutral) screens towards the bottom of our current vol-adjusted returns table, hit by its recent certification scandal and some ongoing production line halts, while Subaru (Sell) joins it and Honda (Sell) reappears there (Figures 6-9). General trading companies have finally flagged, with Mitsui & Co (Neutral) appearing third bottom in the performance table after having been second top when we last produced this report. Lasertec (Neutral) and Seven & i (Neutral) occupy the worst two spots this time around. Tech names like Tokyo Electron (Neutral), SMC (not rated), and Disco (Neutral) did quite poorly over the most recent period, too.

MHI has done best of late. Financials are still doing well, but general trading companies have flagged, and autos have done especially badly. Tech has splintered, with AI plays well supported but other famous representatives at the foot of the table. Pharma names are making a comeback.

At the other end of the spectrum, some tech stocks are in the top table now, including NEC Corp (Buy), Softbank (Buy) Murata (Buy), Hitachi (Buy) and Fujitsu (Buy), largely reflecting links to the AI theme / associated optimism. But the best of the bunch of late has actually been Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (not rated), riding a wave of the country's stepped up defence build-out. Healthcare is also broadly doing better, with Otsuka (Neutral) still doing well but now joined by Chugai Pharmaceutical (Buy), Shin-Etsu Chemical (Neutral) and Fujifilm (Neutral). Recruit (not covered) has built on recent gains, and financials remain well represented by Tokio Marine, Daiichi Life, MS&AD, SMFG and Mitsubishi UFJ—all of which we do not have coverage for at the present time.

## … and versus the start of 2024 when Japan briefly outperformed?

Clusters were clearer earlier in the year when most of the market rallied hard. Some of that was short covering by foreigners though expectations were also piqued by the expansion of tax-free allowances for individual investors and risk appetite was generally strong as rates markets rallied globally. Brokers were the standouts, autos surged, retail stocks did well, Nitori (Neutral) even diverged positively only to be subsequently hurt when yen weakness became pronounced.

## How did things look during prior steady states?

It's worth comparing recent market plots to the post- and pre-COVID regimes when stocks grouped much more tightly. In the time since 2020, stocks in the financial sector, consumer discretionary space, real estate and healthcare all grouped fairly tightly (Figure 5). Industrial cliques for infrastructure, precision and general machinery were clear and largely dominated by a central player. General trading companies were notoriously bolstered by Warren Buffet's growing interest, and financials did pretty well. Notable underperformers were cosmetics, pharma, medtech, M3 and LY (Buy).

Pre-COVID groupings were at least as tight but the dominance of particular players was less pronounced (Figure 6). Moreover, the semiconductor group to the right was was much less correlated/correlated, despite Lasertec topping the table. Oriental Land (not rated), Recruit, Hoya (not rated), Advantest (Buy), Daiichi Sankyo (Buy), Fujitsu, Bandai Namco (Buy), Sony (Buy) and Keyence (not rated) all stood out. Many auto makers did poorly, as did Japan Tobacco (Buy), Japan Post (not rated), Ajinomoto (not rated), Nippon Steel (Buy), Takeda Pharmaceutical (Buy) and MHI.
