## United States:

All signs pointed to a steepening on Wednesday as 2y fell 6bp to the lowest level since February while the long end sold off. The 2s10s curve steepened 9bp and reached the steepest level since July 2022, when the curve inversion began. 2s30s steepened \~12bp, the largest one-day steepening since December 2023.

There were a number of drivers behind the steepening of the curve. Overnight PMIs in the euro area were softer than expected, with manufacturing PMI surprising to the downside for France, Germany, and the eurozone. Services were also particularly weak in Germany and the eurozone. The disinflation narrative was supported as prices in the euro area services data were soft. Front-end EGB yields moved lower as market participants continued to price in additional and more imminent ECB cuts. The move spilled over into USTs and served as an early catalyst for the steepening in the curve.

Early in the NY morning, an article from former New York Fed President Dudley, who had previously self-proclaimed to be in the “’higher for longer’ camp,” argued that the Fed should cut rates as soon as July. For some market participants, this served as a green light for an acceleration of steepeners; 2y yields fell following the article's release, and it came alongside strength in the USD.

Soft economic data in the US also continued to push 2y UST yields lower while corporate issuance helped anchor the long end. Wholesale inventories rose less than expected, new home sales were particularly soft, and the manufacturing PMI dipped into contraction territory, although the services PMI reached its highest level in nearly a year.

Additionally, the BoC delivered its second consecutive 25bp rate cut. While the forward guidance was largely unchanged, the communication leaned dovish as it emphasized that the downside risks to inflation are becoming increasingly important, and the statement highlighted that “ongoing excess supply is lowering inflationary pressures.” Canadian bonds rallied following the meeting, which may have also supported the rally in front-end USTs.

Into the NY afternoon, the belly of the curve and the long end began to sell off ahead of the 5y auction. The 5y UST auction was not nearly as remarkable as Tuesday’s 2y UST auction, with a cut-off yield of 4.121%, a 1.1bp tail. Underlying details were mediocre; the bid-to-cover ratio increased to 2.40x (P: 2.35x), allotment to direct participants rose to 18.8% (P: 17.7%), but allotment to indirect fell to 67.2% (P: 68.9%). This left dealers with 14.0% (P: 13.4%), which was still below the oneyear average of 15.5%. The weak auction results were another catalyst to allow for further steepening.

Shortly following the auction was the scheduled Treasury buyback operation of securities maturing between 2031 and 2034. However, the Treasury accepted none of the \$3.7bn that was offered. It seemed these results added to the selling pressure of the long end of curve. Moreover, the overall movement in markets on Wednesday resulted in technical factors and market fundamentals that only further accelerated the steepening.

Long-end USTs were not the only instruments under pressure on Wednesday. Disappointing earnings in the technology sector, which in particular suggested that investments in artificial intelligence may take longer than initially expected to become profitable, led to a tumble in technology stocks. The S&P 500 fell -2.3% in its worst day since December 2022. The risk-off sentiment within the equity space may have also supported the outperformance in 2y USTs.

According to the weekly EIA Crude Oil Inventory Report, US crude oil inventories fell for the fourth consecutive week and were down by 3.7mn barrels in the week ending July 19. WTI crude oil futures rose 0.8% and closed higher for the first time in five sessions.

## Japan:

JGBs began to sell off across the curve ahead of the 40y JGB auction. The auction was weaker than expected amid concerns around supply and demand, with a cut-off yield of 2.420% (C: 2.385%). Although market pricing implied only a 30% probability of a 15bp rate hike for the July BoJ meeting and the super long-end of JGBs offer relatively attractive yields, there seemed to be little appetite from real money market participants to add super longs. Accordingly, super long-end JGBs accelerated their sell-off into the Tokyo afternoon. By Tokyo close, the JGB curve bear-steepened; 2y JGB sold off 1.5bp to 0.365%, 10y JGB sold off 1.0bp to 1.070%, and 40y JGB sold off 3.5bp to 2.460% on a simple yield basis. The TONA-OIS curve bear-flattened and outperformed JGBs across the curve, especially on the long end. XCCY basis tightened across the curve, particularly on the long end.

USD/JPY initially moved higher from 155.60 to 156.00, supported by demand to buy at the low levels, but it immediately retraced the rally and accelerated the sell-off without any clear catalyst. USD/JPY approached 154.40 into the London morning. Due to the increased volatility of the pair ahead of the July BoJ meeting, there seems to be an acceleration of carry trade unwinds. It seemed that the decline in USD/JPY was also supported by demand from the real money community to buy JPY from export companies, especially around the Tokyo 10am TTM rate-setting. Other CCY/ JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY, and AUD/JPY declined toward 101.67.

## Europe:

Safe-haven currencies gained in the G10 complex, while risk-sensitive currencies underperformed for another day. CHF strengthened on the risk-off move in FX by - 0.8% against EUR and 0.7% against USD. EUR fell to an intraday low of 1.0826 per USD after weak PMIs in Europe, but retraced the move and ended the day down - 0.1%. Sterling was relatively unchanged.

## Dollar Bloc:

The dollar bloc currencies weakened the most as risk sentiment deteriorated, and AUD (-0.5%) broke below 0.6600. Diverging performance in the G10 left the DXY USD Index roughly unchanged at -0.1%. USD/CAD (+0.2%) climbed above 1.3800 as the BoC delivered a second consecutive rate cut and emphasized the downside risks to its symmetric inflation target, as opposed to the upside risks. .

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies gained amid the continuous strength in JPY. Both USD/CNH and USD/CNY remained largely unchanged at 7.2880 and 7.2760, respectively, despite the weakest USD/CNY fixing of 7.1358 since November 2023. THB was the major outperformer. While Thailand revealed details of the digital wallet program, USD/ THB declined 0.2% to 36.14 alongside the rally in gold prices. USD/KRW fell 0.1% to 1,385, mainly aided by the strength of JPY. MYR was the outlier, down 0.1% to 4.68 per USD. INR and IDR remained stable at 83.71 and 16,215, respectively. Taiwan and Philippines markets were closed due to Typhoon Gaemi.

AxJ rates also outperformed broadly following the rally in USTs on Tuesday. In China, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-flattened 2bp; 10y swap rates fell 2bp to 1.95%, while the short-term rates held steady. In the bond market, the short-term 2y CGB yields fell 1bp to 1.52% given the persistent underperformance in local equities, while long-term CGB yields traded in a narrow range. In India, the lower-thanexpected borrowing plan in the interim budget continued to push the rally in G-secs; 2y G-sec yields dropped 4bp to 6.87%, while 10y G-sec yields fell 2bp to 6.96%. Meanwhile, the 5s10s INR NDOIS curve shifted 3bp lower. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-flattened 1bp while the 3s10s KTB yield curve bull-steepened 2bp given that 3y KTB yields slipped 6bp to 3.06%. Both Hong Kong and Singapore rates bullsteepened 2bp across their 2s10s swap curves. In Indonesia, 1y IndoGB yields fell 4bp to 6.61%, while long-term yields remained steady. Thailand bonds relatively underperformed; the ThaiGB curve was largely unchanged after the government detailed the digital wallet program.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The BoC lowered its policy rate by 25bp to 4.50% at the July meeting, in line with our economists’ and consensus expectations. The Bank highlighted that more slack in the labor market, continued progress on inflation toward the 2% target, and the economy continuing to operate in excess supply were the main drivers for the July cut. The statement highlighted that the balance of risks has changed, as the downside risks are becoming more important in policy decisions. The final paragraph of the rate announcement was updated to show the opposing forces from inflation of “ongoing excess supply” versus shelter and some components of services inflation. The rate cut guidance was kept unchanged. Our economists maintain their call for two more additional cuts this year to reach 4.00% by the end of 2024

• The BoC’s July Monetary Policy Report showed the Bank still pulling off a soft landing. Though growth expectations were lowered for 2024 and 2025, the Bank expects growth to pick up in 2H24 and into 2025 as interest rates decline and economic sentiment picks up. Moreover, it still expects inflation to reach the 2% target by the end of 2025

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
