# Biweekly Perspectives: Mind the Reversal Risk of Momentum Factor in Asia/EM

"Price Momentum" factor has dominated alpha opportunities in Asia/EM YTD. We note that its outperformance is approaching extreme scenarios in history, and our quant model suggests taking profits from the factor. We illustrate our model takeaways from macro and market dynamic perspectives.

QUANTWISE[]

## Key Takeaways

■ Cumulative return of "Price Momentum" factor over the last six months is now standing at 2-sigma above long-term average.

■ Our quant model estimates a high reversal risk of the trading pattern and the momentum factor volatility to rise in near term.

A bear steepening UST yield curve is also a macro headwind against Momentum factor to outperform.

To manage the reversal risk, we suggest neutralizing long exposure of Momentum factor and only stay long on Quality.

What is Momentum factor? The simplified idea is that top-performing stocks in the past tend to outperform in the future. It is a market-neutral factor, being driven by systematic underreaction to new information. Such trading patterns are evident across the majority of markets and sectors. In normal times, Momentum factor delivers steady alpha to stock pickers, but it also exhibits strong mean-reversal tendencies if overshot ( Exhibit 1 ).

Our latest observations and thoughts: Price Momentum factor has been outperforming in the last 12 months in Asia/EM, and it has accelerated YTD. The factor gained 16.1% and 9.4%, respectively, in APxJ/EM and Japan in the last six weeks, bringing its rolling returns to stand above 2-sigma level from the long-term average. As its factor volatility is on the rise concurrently, we believe the prudent strategy now would be to neutralize long exposure on Price Momentum factor.

Meanwhile, our model also recognizes that a bear steepening UST yield curve also tends to drag return of Momentum factor in Asia/EM, because a tightened financial condition might bring fund outflows from Asia/EM, resulting in crowded trades unwinding and also the Momentum stocks. From a quant perspective, we suggest to only stay long with Quality stocks, and reiterate a neutral stance between Value and Growth via preferring High Dividend stocks and Quality Growth stocks concurrently.

## Market Dynamics and Style Implications

## Latest Style Performance Overview In Asia/EM

Momentum factor led alpha in Asia/EM YTD: Our factor monitor shows that the bestperforming style YTD are all momentum strategy related. "Price Momentum" and "Consensus Earnings Revisions" are the top two factors that delivered positive alpha consistently in both January and February. Our QuantASIA model attributes such trading patterns to be a result of de-risking trend among asset owners in Asia/EM.

Value vs. Growth remained marginal: Per what we discussed in our Fed Pivot Playbook, the style rotation between Value and Growth tends to be muted when market cycle approaches a Fed Pivot. The same pattern has been repeating in the current cycle that Fed fund futures market is pricing out rate cuts expectation, but a tightened financial condition did not lead to a broad-based Value outperformance in APxJ/EM and Japan.

Our recommended strategy is to stay neutral between Value and Growth, and cut Momentum-related exposures: For investors with investment horizon beyond six months, we suggest looking for outperformers via High Dividend stocks and Quality Growth stocks in Asia/EM. Yet we note that the near-term risk is a potentially overshooting of Price Momentum factor, while a bear-steepening UST yield curve usually leads to underperformance of Price Momentum factor. As the Momentum factor volatility has been on the rise quickly YTD and the factor is highly correlated with Value stocks for now, we believe a prudent risk management process should be to neutralize Momentum exposure tactically over next one to two months.

## Macro and Market Dynamics That Matter

The below analysis is based on the latest takeaways from our QuantASIA Model that it evaluates the correlations of various factor performances against macro and market dynamics. The goal is to flag what is working and not working compared to history; as such, we highlight where we see the risk of decoupling and reversal.

In this report, our model highlights a high reversal risk of Momentum Factor in next one to two months. It is not yet an immediate risk, but we believe it would be prudent to start neutralize portfolio exposure on 12M Price Momentum. Here we elaborate what macro and market dynamics would require attention.

i) Moderation of Fed rate cuts expectation: The recent hawkish guidance from Fed officers led Fed fund futures market to price out rate cuts in near term, postponing the first rate cut to be in 2Q24 rather than in March ( Exhibit 5 ).

Exhibit 5: Investors have been pricing out Fed rate cut expectations in January and February MTD 2024; Fed Fund Futures curve has shifted higher

ii) UST 10Y yield rose ( Exhibit 8 ) and US Dollar (DXY Index) was higher: Such tightened financial conditions only led to a short-lived Value outperformance in January, and the outperformance was fading out into February. In Asia/EM, we note that Value vs. Growth rotation remains marginal in near term, as the market volatility driven by rate cut expectations are high, and the near-term trading pattern on duration exposure is mainly short-term reversal basis.

Our quant model estimates that such risk-off sentiment has been the dominant macro factor YTD, driving Price Momentum and Consensus Earnings Revisions factors to outperform significantly during the period.

Such trading pattern has not played out YTD as risk-off sentiment dominated market dynamics. However, it is also the top factor risk that our quant model flags for a potential near-term reversal.

## Factor Monitor – Biweekly Update February 12, 2024

## Methodology

Factor return calculation: To evaluate historical factor performance, we rank stocks as at month-end within defined universes, and form quintile portfolios based on the underlying factor values. We go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile (i.e., long the top 20th percentile of stocks within the defined universe and short those in the bottom 20th percentile), rebalanced monthly. We then calculate the performance of the time series of this L/S market-neutral portfolio over various time horizons.

Composite formation: To calculate the composite factors, we first z-score the individual factor values at each monthly observation within the defined universe. Individual factor zscores are then averaged into composite scores for each stock on an equal-weighted basis.
