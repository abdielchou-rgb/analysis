# The myth of income, spending and financial asset growth

Contrary to market concerns, our analysis shows still healthy growth in household financial assets, income and consumption amid de-risking efforts in China. However, some structural changes in allocation of income growth and over competition among goods and service providers could be concerns.

Household wealth growth remained healthy in 1Q24 with stable income growth amid the housing market correction, LGFV cleanup and China's economic structure shifting towards industrial development: HH financial assets increased 9.5% yoy in 1Q24, +5.1% YTD, led by still elevated deposit growth at \~12% yoy in 1Q24 despite some moderation in deposit growth towards 11% in April and May. In addition, disposable income seems to have gradually stabilized with 1Q24 disposable income per capita increasing 6.2% yoy, picking up from the lowest level in 2022. While both areas of growth are modestly lower than pre-COVID levels, we think they are healthy given China's de-risking efforts in recent years.

Payment and job applications data shows consumption downgrade in first-tier cities and upgrade in rural/lower-tier cities. Job application and employment data showed a notable shift from internet, property and financial services sectors to tech and industrials in recent years. We believe rebalancing is necessary given China's negative working age population growth and the faster productivity gains available in industrial sectors. Therefore given the greater concentration of internet and financial services employment in first-tier cities, and the higher industrial investment in second to fifth-tier cities in recent years, a notable shift in income growth towards lower-tier cities and rural areas is likely ahead, particularly with increased credit support being allocated to rural areas such as farm land upgrades. This shift is also reflected in the payments data we have discussed with major payment firms. Notably, growth in consumption payment for lower-ticket-size items has been much higher YTD in 2024. As growth in total consumption payment has remained at 5-6% CAGR over the past three years - largely in line with income growth - we believe the consumption upgrades in lower-tier cities have outweighed the downgrades in firsttier cities, although the shift may not necessarily be favorable to corporate margins.

Financial assets shifted towards insurance products; WMP should gradually stabilize: While deposits still dominate HH financial assets at 67% of new household financial assets, we have seen some mix shift towards insurance products. In fact, insurance assets recorded 9.7% YTD growth in 1Q24 with a strong policy sales trend, outpacing overall asset growth, which supports our positive view on the insurance sector. On the other hand, WMP AUM has recorded positive growth on a quarterly basis since 2Q23. The proportion of WMP in HH financial assets stabilized at \~9.5% in 1Q24. We think WMP and insurance products will continue to grow with a gradual shift in household preferences away from deposits.

## Our analysis in detail

# Household financial asset growth and household income growth still healthy despite some moderation

1Q24 household financial assets saw healthy growth of 9.5% yoy or 5.1% YTD in 1Q24, up from 4Q23. This was driven by strong household deposit growth at 12% yoy in 1Q24. We think the strong deposit growth results from: 1) still healthy income growth; and 2) less attractive returns from other investment products due to the low rate environment and subdued equity market returns.

However, with the suspension of manual interest payments by banks to compete for deposits since early April, HH deposit growth moderated to \~11% in April and May. Although deposits still make up the majority of HH wealth, we think a lower deposit rate will push deposits towards other products with better risk returns.

## Consumption downgrade in high-tier cities and upgrade in lower-tier cities evidenced in payment and job applications data

We have seen a clear trend in job applications shifting away from internet, property and financial sectors to manufacturing, while household income growth has been fairly stable over the past two years. Considering the higher concentration of internet and financial services employment in first-tier cities, and the greater industrial investment in second- to fifth-tier cities in recent years, we believe we could see a notable shift in income growth towards lower-tier cities and rural areas, particularly considering the increasing credit support being provided to rural areas such as farm land upgrades. This was also reflected in the payment data we discussed with the major payment firms. Notably, growth in consumption payment for lower-ticket-size items has been much higher YTD in 2024.

The payment data also shows a shift to offline services and higher spending growth in lower-tier cities reflected in smaller-ticket items but much higher payment volume in terms of number of transactions.

## Shift in financial assets towards insurance products while WMP should gradually stabilize

While 1Q24 household financial asset growth was mainly driven by deposit growth, we have observed some mix shift towards insurance. Specifically, insurance assets increased
