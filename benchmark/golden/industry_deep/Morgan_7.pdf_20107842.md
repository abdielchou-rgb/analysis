# Capital Policy & Shareholder Return in Focus at F3/24 Results

However, we expect a post-results shift to greater focus on stocks with improved cash-generating capacity.

## Key Takeaways

Consensus based full-year F3/24 results to deliver record profits, with aggregate growth both in sales and profits (sales: +4.3% YoY, NP: +16.4%)

Further consensus sales and profit growth in F3/25 (sales: +2.8%, NP: +7.6%), to be driven by a broader range of sectors

However, cautious guidance is unlikely to form upside catalysts. Spotlight to be on stocks with prospects for boosted shareholder returns and capital efficiency

We expect the focus on shareholder returns to fade after results and AGMs in June; may see greater interest in stocks with improved equity spreads

Japan's earnings season is now underway. Given overseas economic uncertainty, firms' FX assumptions may not fully reflect recent JPY depreciation against USD. Generally, cautious guidance releases are unlikely to form upside stock catalysts.

We expect strong interest in new mid-term plans, capital policies, and shareholder returns at full-year results. While 40% of TOPIX still trades below book value, there has been a steady decrease in sub-0.5x P/B stocks. TSE market reforms are changing Japan’s old corporate culture of neglecting capital efficiency (i.e., holding cash instead of allocating it to business investments or shareholder returns).

Investment strategies based on the low-P/B theme may broadly take the following approaches: (1) investing in stocks with prospects for improvement in shareholder returns and revised capital policy, aiming to capture subsequent returns once these changes materialize, and (2) a slightly longer-term approach of investing in firms that have announced enhanced shareholder returns and other improvements, with an eye to longer-term improvement in corporate value.

For (1), our short-term focus is names with upcoming announcements of new midterm plans and ample scope for balance sheet efficiencies, in addition to coverage names highlighted by our analysts for shareholder return potential. After the fullyear results and general shareholders’ meetings, however, the focus on shareholder returns is likely to fade until November's 1H results. In this period, (2) we expect greater interest in firms that have improved cash-generating capacity via efficient usage of assets, providing a source of sustainable and flexible shareholder returns.

That said, we do not necessarily recommend short positions in value stocks. Activist investors are likely to complete building their portfolios by October, with an eye to AGMs in June 2025, so we also keep in mind a scenario where value stocks with prospects for improved returns and changes in capital policies continue to find favor.

At full-year results, we expect investor focus to center on how companies can achieve earnings growth by passing on the higher costs, including personnel expenses, reflected in guidance releases. However, company projections tend to be more conservative than consensus estimates. Given the uncertainties over the overseas economy, we think companies’ forex assumptions will not fully reflect the yen’s recent depreciation against the dollar. Generally speaking, cautious guidance releases are unlikely to form an upside catalyst for share prices.

That said, TSE Prime Market constituents allocated a record ¥10trn to share buybacks in F23( Exhibit 3 ), exceeding the ¥9.3trn posted in full-year F22. The series of aggressive moves to bring returns more in alignment with shareholder interests, despite the record stock gains made since the start of the year, arguably owes to progress with initiatives to deepen corporate governance reforms initiated by policymakers.

P/B and ROE levels at Japanese firms have remained low despite the steady increases in both metrics at their US counterparts, with 40% of TOPIX constituents trading below book value ( Exhibit 5 ). It therefore seems clear that the improvement in capital efficiency at Japanese companies is still a work in progress.

That said, there has been a steady decrease in stocks with P/Bs of less than 0.5x, as well as an increase in stocks trading at 1.0-1.5x multiples ( Exhibit 6 ). The TSE’s market reforms are leading to steady change in Japan’s old corporate culture of placing little emphasis on capital efficiency (i.e., holding onto cash instead of allocating it to business investments or shareholder returns).

In a continuation from last year, we think investor interest is likely to center on improvement in shareholder returns and changes in capital policy at full-year results and through general shareholders’ meetings in June.

Expecting focus on shareholder returns to fade in the wake of full-year results, may see greater interest in stocks showing improved equity spreads (ability to generate earnings)

Why are Japanese firms’ P/Bs so low in the first place? One might cite companies’ high levels of retained earnings and cross-held shares, not to mention their measly shareholder returns ( Exhibit 7 , Exhibit 8 , Exhibit 9 ).

However, the greatest issue lies in a lack of effective capital utilization to raise profits. The flipside of the story is that shareholder returns not accompanied by earnings improvement do not lead to sustainable improvement in corporate value.

We therefore think that investment strategies based on the low-P/B theme will be broadly divided by the following approaches: (1) investing in stocks with prospects for improvement in shareholder returns and revisions in capital policy, aiming to capture subsequent returns once these changes materialize, and (2) a slightly longer-term approach of investing in stocks of companies that have announced enhanced shareholder returns and other improvements, with an eye to longer-term improvement in corporate value.

## activist investors in June.

In 2024, there could be an increase in motions related to business strategy (e.g., corporate acquisitions), as well as enhanced shareholder returns (dividend hikes, share buybacks, divestment of cross-held shares, etc.) and governance-related items (separate disclosure of director compensation, etc.).

As stated in Bracing for Potential TOB/MBO Wave (28 Feb 2024) we would advise caution on short positioning in Japanese stocks because such proposals could contribute to a rise in “acquisition without consent” TOBs by strategic buyers or MBOS in response to soaring listing costs.

We note that the deadline for submission of shareholder proposals is April, eight weeks before the wave of regular general shareholders’ meetings that are mostly held in June. In order to submit shareholder proposals, investors must own shares in the applicable company at least six months before the said deadline. Activist investors are therefore likely to complete the process of building their portfolios by October, with an eye to general shareholders’ meetings in June 2025. Accordingly, we should also keep in mind a scenario whereby value stocks with prospects for improved shareholder returns and changes in capital policies continue to find favor into the early autumn.
