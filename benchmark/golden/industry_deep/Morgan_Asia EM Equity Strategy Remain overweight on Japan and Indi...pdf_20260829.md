# Remain overweight on Japan and India; reduce targets for China further; downgrade Mexico to underweight

We stay overweight on Japanese equities – we see nominal reflation and corporate reform continuing, but trim our June 2025 TOPIX target to 3,000 (+14% from spot). The EM outlook remains tricky – we lower targets for China amid ongoing deflation; institutional risks lead us to downgrade Mexico.

A difficult juncture for Asia/EM equities: A US soft landing is back in the price, with MSCI EM trading at the 69th percentile of its 20-year range and the TOPIX rallying 20% from its 5 August lows (to the 40th valuation percentile). However, we see lingering risks and a still narrow set of aggregate drivers at the market and thematic level. We have strongest conviction in Japanese and Indian equities. In Japan, we see above-consensus earnings growth of 10-11%, supported by nominal reflation and corporate reform, which we don't think are disrupted by recent volatility. Our June 2025 TOPIX target is trimmed to 3,000 from 3,200 but stilll implies 15% returns in JPY and 19% in USD-unhedged terms.

China is posing ongoing structural concerns: Our equity strategy team for China lowers its targets further amid ongoing deflationary pressures on earnings. Our base case shows flat returns for the Hang Seng (-3%, to 17,000) and MSCI China (-3%, to 56), with the team preferring onshore exposure to the CSI 300 (4%, to 3,500). We are seeing spillover into earnings of China's weak operating conditions: a set of EM companies with most exposure to China has underperformed the MSCI EM index by 14ppt over the last two months.

Stay cautious on Semis; rotate to Defensives: We see still elevated valuations and crowded positioning, with ongoing debate about return on AI Investment and peak revenue growth meaning that Asia-Pac semis offer weaker risk-reward over the next 9-12 months. Instead, we recommend reducing beta and shifting to more domestic and defensive sectors, which are quietly assuming leadership (the top four EM sectors QTD being Healthcare, Utilities, Financials, and Staples).

Downgrade Mexico to underweight; upgrade Indonesia to equal-weight: Mexican policymakers look to be challenging the judicial and electoral system guardrails, posing risks to valuations and the structural near-shoring opportunity. We move to an underweight stance in our APxJ/EM market allocation framework, while upgrading Indonesia to equal-weight given a recovery in the IDR and its historical tendency to outperform following Fed rate cuts.

## Taking stock of the targets – continue to prefer Japan vs. EM

Three months on from our mid-year outlook and following a bout of market volatility and shift in sector leadership, we take stock of our outlook across Asia/EM equities. In short, the key pillars of our worldview are:

• Growth: DM economies remain on track for a soft landing, although the market continues to oscillate between ignoring the slowdown altogether and extrapolating it into recession. The EM growth outlook is highly divergent, but has been increasingly investment/supply-centric given the impacts of a multipolar world emphasis on economic security and defence, digitalisation, and decarbonisation. In India, this necessary investment is powering a secular boom, while in China an ongoing focus on manufacturing capacity and technology is exacerbating overcapacity and debt-deflationary dynamics.

• Policy: A global central bank easing cycle has started already (e.g., ECB, BoE, RBNZ, and BSP) and will broaden out once the Fed starts cutting – in our economists' view, by 25bp at every meeting through mid-2025. In Asia/EM ,the steepest cuts will come from Indonesia, the Philippines, and Thailand. We're not expecting much easing from India, Brazil, and Malaysia. The BoJ remains the outlier, but we retain our base case view for just one more hike in January 2025, to 50bp (leaving real rates below -1% and well below the real growth outlook).

• Rates/FX: Our macro strategy team sees the USD likely to stay relatively well supported into year-end despite the start of a Fed easing cycle. However, global yields are likely to fall further over the next 12 months, while narrowing policy/rate differentials should lead USD/JPY to fall to 146 by end-2024 and 140 by mid-2025.

## Japan remains preferred

As discussed below in the Japan Equity Strategy section, we trim our TOPIX target to 3,000 from 3,200, but we retain a bullish view and see 14% upside from spot levels. The market has already rebounded 20% from its abrupt drop, but our targets imply that the index will move beyond the 11 July peak of 2,929 and make fresh all-time highs.

Valuation marked at 14x: In our 3,000 target, we now assume a conservative 14.0x 12- month forward P/E multiple, which is in line with its 10-year average (13.9x) and below its 20-year average (14.8x). This would imply a slight re-rating from the spot 13.7x multiple, but we no longer assume the market will trade above its historical average in light of the recent volatility event, which may weigh on the equity risk premium given a likely slower pace of foreign and domestic inflows for a period. Our 14x target implies a 5x P/E point discount versus our US counterparts' S&P 500 target multiple of 19x (versus 10-year and 20-year average discounts of 4.2 and 1.2x, respectively).

## MSCI EM and APxJ

We make minor adjustments to our MSCI EM and APxJ earnings and valuation forecasts, taking into account an even more cautious outlook for China, as discussed by our colleagues in the China Equity Strategy section below. On the surface, the move into a global policy easing cycle could be positive for EM and APxJ – especially if we see a US soft landing. However, our colleagues do not expect a weaker USD outlook versus EM FX (which is a key earnings driver) just yet, given the risk of trade tensions and matching policy easing elsewhere.

# Market allocation – downgrade Mexico to underweight; upgrade Indonesia to equal-weight

Tricky Asia/EM outlook with wide divergence in conditions and valuations: At the recent trough, Japan, Korea, and Singapore were close to prior recession valuation levels; they have now moved back into cheap but not distressed levels. In contrast, Indian equities are near historical high valuations, buoyed by the strong secular growth momentum and domestic retail sentiment. Australia also looks expensive versus history, supported by its valuation correlation with the US, but perhaps at risk given its greater exposure to macro weakness in China.

We downgrade Mexican equities to an underweight stance in our APxJ/EM market allocation framework: This move is in conjunction with our Latin America equity strategy team, led by Nikolaj Lippmann. The market has moved down to #24 rank on the basis of a weaker score across Morgan Stanley's Macro and Strategy views, with the key concern being the outlook for constitutional reform of the judiciary and electoral system. These changes may also increase uncertainty about the capex outlook amid bottlenecks in nearshoring capacity. For more details, see our Latin America Equity Strategy sectio, and Mexico Economics & Strategy: Approaching a Crossroads (17 Jul 2024).

Indonesia moves up to the #15 ranking in our framework and we upgrade to equalweight: Valuations stand neutral to slightly cheap versus their five-year historical relative levels, although earnings estimate revisions remain negative. Our FX strategists have been generally positive on the IDR, which is now strengthening in the lead-up to the Fed's easing cycle.

## Retain preference for India, South Africa, Singapore, Greece, and Poland

India presents a compelling structural opportunity, in our view: We cite 11-12% nominal GDP growth, INR support from improved macro stability, and a rising profit share of GDP. Relative valuations stand 0.2 to 1.3 standard deviations expensive versus history, which is justified by superior profitability and net profit margins, and a positive bottom-up and country strategist outlook.

South Africa offers a key turnaround opportunity: The Government of National Unity is improving the prospects for SOE reform, while energy infrastructure investments coming online and a cyclical trough in the economy should support an earnings recovery.

Singapore represents an attractive base of profitability and yield: Its sector skew hedges exposure to higher rates (via Banks) and lower rates (via Real Estate). We add Capitaland Ascendas REIT to our APxJ Focus List for exposure to the latter, joining existing exposure to UOB Bank.

Poland and Greece offer exposure to stronger Eastern European growth profiles: The outlook is boosted by a structural reform agenda, ongoing distributions into the European recovery funds, and favourable political/economic climates.

## More caution on earnings growth is the major driver behind the cuts

Real GDP tracking lower vs. government target, with further downside risk of deflation: The new targets are the results of lowering both our earnings growth forecasts (for 2024 and 2025) and our valuation forecast, with the earnings growth forecast adjustment being the bigger driver.

Despite some promising signs of macro stabilization at the beginning of this year, and an upside surprise in the 1Q real GDP print, China's macro growth had started to trend below target on both real GDP and inflation/deflation recently, especially since July. Morgan Stanley's economics team for China expects China's nominal GDP growth to stay subdued at 4%Y in 3Q, same as in 2Q, and real GDP YoY growth is tracking at 4.5% (vs. 4.7% in 2Q) defying slight QoQ improvement.

Even with some additional policy easing pass-through, which could lead to a modest growth upturn in 4Q, our economics team still thinks full-year growth may still miss the 5% target. The team sees up to 20bps of potential downside to its real GDP growth estimate of 4.8%. GDP deflator is at -0.5% (Morgan Stanley's estimate) for 2024, implying that China's nominal growth may turn out to be as low as 4.1% for this year.

Housing market slowdown continuing to weigh on demand: Meanwhile, domestic deflationary pressure still persists as the housing market failed to show signs to stabilization – contracted sales of the 30 major developers Morgan Stanley Research tracks fell 38% y-y and 28% m-m in July, while secondary home prices fell 1.4% m-m (-11.3% y-y) in July in the \~50 sample cities tracked (vs. -0.9% in June and -1.3% in May), with 96% of cities recording m-m drops (vs. 70% in June and 83% in May).

As a result, we saw a broad-based credit demand growth slowdown in July – broad credit growth was weaker than expected at 8.6% YoY (consensus 8.7% YoY), as fiscal easing was unable to offset private demand weakness. New loans to the real economy turned negative for the first time in 19 years, with loan repayments exceeding new borrowing. Consumption also remains sluggish, with broad-based sequential decline across retail sales items (including auto).

Deflationary pressure persisting longer than expected: As a result, the process for China to completely get out of deflationary pressure could be more prolonged than originally expected. Our economics team for China believes that policy support for social welfare reforms and housing inventory digestion would remain modest, given policymakers' aversion to large stimulus and tendency toward path dependency, while the status quo of a supply-centric policy framework would keep deflationary pressures lingering. GDP deflator would only edge up from -0.5% this year to 0.2% in 2025, and stay <1% in 2026-27.

Consensus earnings numbers overly bullish: Our new earnings growth forecasts for 2024 and 2025 are 6% and 8%, respectively (down from 9% and 9%), compared to consensus at 14% for 2024 and 12% for 2025. China unlikely to be the safe haven despite recent spike of volatility in other global markets: Both the US and Japanese markets had just gone through the biggest market drawdown event YTD and the debate about further risks is still ongoing. Contrary to what had happened in April-May this year, we do not expect China to be a major diversification destination despite its low correlation vs. the other markets. The uncertainty on the macro front, as discussed earlier in this report, would be the main hurdle. ## Recommend staying defensive with a slight skew towards Ashares in the near term

Staying defensive in the near term with the right sector allocation: In our latest sector allocation report (China Equity Strategy: Sector Allocation and Focus List Changes: Be More Defensive), we advised adding more defensiveness to China-dedicated portfolios, at least in the near term, because we expect multiple macro, sentiment, and geopolitical concerns to persist:

1. The deflation and macro slowdown debate continues.

2. More disappointing earnings may come through during the 2Q results season.

3. Market expectations regarding a policy easing step-up come and go, each time weighing on sentiment and liquidity.

4. US election season is approaching.

We want to get more exposure to sectors/stocks with more defensiveness and stable cash returns. We upgraded Telecom from equal-weight to overweight and Banks from slight underweight to equal-weight.

Maintain a slight overweight in A-shares while monitoring the valuation dispersion vs. offshore: We continue to prefer A-shares over offshore in the near term given:

1. The liquidity support from the National Team when market volatility spikes;

2. Being more shielded from the currency FX fluctuation and/or geopolitical concerns.

That said, we are mindful of the valuation premium and when that may potentially peak. We note that the P/E premium of A-shares (using the CSI 300 as a proxy) vs. MSCI China has risen to 23%. Historically, we would be more alarmed about a reversal trade if that pricing premium starts to approach or exceed \~40%.
