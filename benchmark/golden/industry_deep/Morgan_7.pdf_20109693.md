## Move from UW to MW Asia FX in GBI-EM; lower USD/ CNY target

Rate differentials have dominated low yielding Asia FX. Short-term valuations have corrected in funders (i.e., JPY, CNH and THB), and even look tactically rich in MYR. INR remains a laggard on further USD weakness, while PHP, SGD and (eventually) TWD present better opportunities to be short USD. We close out UW THB and OW INR and turn MW Asia FX in GBI-EM.

• USD/CNH’s recent downdraft has been a pure reflection of relative rate momentum. While short squeeze on speculative CNH shorts could be taking a pause, further unwind of dollar longs from Chinese corporates is a near-term risk for further CNY strength. We estimate that Chinese corporates have saved up to \$300-600bn unsold dollars in the past two years.

The Fed is expected to cut at a faster pace and terminal rates are reset to lower levels. Sharper falls in US yields would mechanically lower USD/CNH’s fair value range in the next year. We move MW CNY FX in GBI-EM after closing out outright CNH shorts earlier, and also lower our year-end USD/CNY target to 7.15 (vs 7.3 previously) and 1y target to 7.05 (vs 7.2 previously).

Geopolitical risks remain an important swing factor for CNY FX and this leaves us with lower conviction on the far end of the forecast and reckon that risks still remain two-sided for USD/CNH. Hence, pay CNH points is a better play for narrowing US-CN yield gap and we wait for better levels to add payers.

Asian currencies tend to perform best in the early stage of the business cycle, requiring a further firmer current accounts – via trade or energy – and easier financial conditions before a sustained rally can take place. We remain constructive on domestically oriented high yielders INR and PHP for now, but await fairer valuations and cleaner positioning before re-engaging.

Malaysia is back, but psychology and reserves are lagging. Prudent fiscal reforms, stable politics and income repatriation are bringing investors back to the market, but significant FX hoarding remains. Reserve accumulation presents a risk to the rally, and repairing market psychology will be key to realigning the ringgit with its improving fundamentals.

## China: Back to square one

Core rates have done a great favour to the PBoC as the collapse of US yields amid rising recession fears triggered further unwind of bearish CNY trades (Figure 3). The positioning shakeout has been so drastic that PBoC’s prolonged headache around outsized fixing-spot gap has suddenly fully dissipated. USD/CNH is now more than 2% lower vs its July highs, with spot USD/CNH even breaking below onshore USD/CNY and the CNY fix at one point on Monday. While the pace of yuan appreciation is getting closer to recent year extremes, our valuation metric has yet to flag alarming signs of stretch. Of note, the downdraft in USD/CNH in recent sessions has been a pure reflection of relative rate momentums: USD/CNH’s fair value based off US-CN yield gap now stands at 7.16 vs 7.30 a month ago, and spot is tracking merely 0.2% below (Figure 4). The broadly fair valuation marks an interesting contrast vs the meltdown in USD/CNH in late 2022 and to a lesser extent in late 2023 as well when USD/CNH reached \~3% and \~1% below rate fundamentals, respectively. The absence of a meaningful undershoot in USD/CNH vs rate differentials this time suggests the latest decline in spot looks more of a passive appreciation fueled by unwind of dollar longs instead of active positioning for fundamentals-driven CNY strength.

Short squeeze on speculative CNH short looks likely done but mindful of further unwind of dollar longs from corporates. We argued previously that the yuan is different from the yen as bearish positioning especially from speculative accounts is much lighter to start with. Judging by the fact that the spot-fix gap has broadly closed while USD/CNH has also moved below USD/CNY, the short squeeze on speculative CNH shorts is probably done for now (Figure 5) but further positioning adjustment from real money investors and corporates is likely still far from complete. Chinese corporates in particular, head into the latest CNY appreciation rather under-hedged. They have been signing less short USD forwards in recent years and their overall hedge ratio has fallen to recent year lows (Figure 6). Additionally, corporates and households have been net long dollars: the latest net FX settlement and sales ratio in June recorded the lowest print in recent years (Figure 3), underlining strong outright dollar buying interest. Meanwhile, the gap between monthly goods trade surplus and the net FX conversion in China has been on a widening track, another indication of consistent dollar hoarding from the corporate community. Based on different assumptions on the conversion ratio, our estimate indicates that Chinese corporates could have saved up to \$300-600bn dollars in the past two years that are now due for conversion (Figure 4USD/CNH screnboadly fiv- t). Against this backdrop, panic USD selling can not be ruled out and even if a small share ends up being converted, it could be market moving over the short run accounting for the sheer size of the unsold dollar pool. This presents a non-negligible risk for further CNY strength in the near term.

Move MW CNY FX in GBI-EM and lower 1y USD/CNY target to 7.05 (from 7.20 previously). The sharp repricing in US terminal rates will also likely impact the risk calculation for corporates: lower core rates may motivate them to sell more dollars and at a faster pace. With the latest weakness in the US labor market, our economists are indeed now expecting an accelerated easing path for the Fed with 50bp cut in September and November, followed by 25bp cut for each meeting thereafter until the Fed settles at 3%. Against this backdrop, our US rate forecasts have also been revised aggressively lower and we now expect 10y UST to move closer to 3% by mid-2025. Sharper falls in US yields would mechanically lower USD/CNH’s fair value in a rate differential model. While the path (lower) is directionally unchanged vs before, the delta is really a much lower ending point which moves the needle on the risk calculations: the fair value range for USD/CNH is now lowered to 6.95-7.15 from 7.18-7.25 earlier for the next 12 months (Figure 6), and that preserves room for further downside in USD/CNH should the rate momentum in our forecast materialize. Against this backdrop, after closing out our outright CNH shorts earlier, we also move MW CNY FX in GBI-EM and lower our year-end USD/CNY target to 7.15 (vs 7.3 previously) and 1y target to 7.05 (vs 7.2 previously).

Geopolitical risks remain an important swing factor for CNY FX. This is why we have set our USD/CNY forecasts broadly above the rate-based fair value, a small and likely incomplete risk premium for potential tariff risks. With geopolitical headwinds lingering, the path for USD/CNY could also be less linearly sloping in reality than what’s indicated by the rate momentum. As a result, we have lower conviction on the far end of the forecast and reckon that risks remain two-sided for USD/CNH despite the trend lower in US rate forecast. Against this backdrop, instead of positioning for a trend lower in USD/CNH, paying points looks a better play for narrowing US-CN yield differentials in the medium term. In a simple interest parity framework, every 1bp change in US-CN rate differentials is associated with \~7pip move in 12m CNH forward points. The near-term caveat though is that increased hedging demand from corporates and unwind of dollar longs could pressure points on the lower side. 12m CNH points is only \~1 s.d. low vs rate differentials, still far from previous extremes (Figure 712m CNHforwad pintsckg \~.l ve bu  x), a setup that leaves us on the sidelines for now while waiting to add payers if/when levels become more conducive.

## Asia FX: More technicals than fundamentals right now

Asia FX continued its squeeze for the 3<sup>rd</sup> straight week: Regional currencies tracked JPY strength as poor seasonality – whether or not – weighed on high carry cyclical currencies, favouring funders as carry trades unwound. Breakevens and real rates collapsing – as proxies for the business cycle and monetary policy response – provide a useful (though imperfect) framework for relative value differentiation at turns in macroeconomic cycles. Historical sensitivities suggest these forces should pull Asia FX in opposing directions, with an easing monetary stance a tailwind, but a slowing cycle a headwind. This cycle, however, has been somewhat unique, with real rates dominating low yielding Asia FX.

Asian currencies have exhibited much lower beta to the business cycle. Late cycle dynamics have been at play in Asia, as wide rate differentials and elevated energy prices that have either raised the bar for the current account or seen it been recycled by outbound portfolio flows. In turn, the low yielding region has seen greater sensitivity to real rates and financial conditions, which pushed valuations in USDAsia to very rich levels where mean reversion is typically imminent . Despite the fundamental outlook for Asia remaining downbeat, rate differentials continuing to move in Asia’s favour could see a further positioning squeeze and re-alignment of valuations . Particularly for the likes of the JPY or THB, which have been key funders this cycle, the pain trade feels like a weaker USD vs low yielders. Additionally, seasonal firmness in the Thai current account may provide an additional tailwind, helping to offset risks from political uncertainty ahead. We turn MW on THB given these dynamics.

Defensive carry under pressure: We continue to maintain a constructive view on domestically oriented high yielders in the late cycle, with INR and PHP being our top picks at this time. Elevated risk premia relative and an improving external balance (respectively) should support carry in the two energy importers. Tactical headwinds remain, however, with India’s current account likely to see seasonal weakness into the festive season, and Philippines still facing a latent terms of trade shock from metals prices. Further carry unwinds also pose a risk - leaving us sidelined for now - so we await fairer valuations, and cleaner positioning (especially in low yielders) to re-engage with our defensively oriented EM Asia carry trades. We move to MW INR on the GBI-EM Portfolio as well.

Hold the follow through: With the cycle clearly for the Fed and USDAsia continuing to offer attractive entry levels to trade the USD cycle, the question of ‘which USD to sell’ has been a popular one of late. Short-term valuations versus rate differentials suggest differentiated correction thus far, with the JPY, CNH and THB having corrected meaningfully and the MYR now looking rich on a tactical basis. Significant misalignment of tactical valuations remains with the TWD,IDR, INR and PHP, with the INR a particular laggard in this move and TWD suffering from heavy portfolio outflows and the ongoing TWSE dividend season. As we argued previously, however, a shift in the narrative around US foreign policy from tariffs to FX manipulation could be a significant tailwind for TWD, with local real money likely to increase their hedge ratios as rate differentials ease.

Amongst the high yielders, the INR could continue to be a laggard due to the RBI’s preference for FX stability vs the USD. The PHP could thus be the ideal place to be short USD, given its more attractive carry and BSP’s less interventionist approach to FX policy. Finally, the SGD offers an ideal proxy to position for a weaker USDAsia, given MAS’ policy regime of managing it versus a (largely) Asian basket and sticky wage pressures likely to keep the SGD NEER supported on the policy bands. For now, however, we remain on the sidelines and await greater clarity on the cycle, given seasonal distortions to the data and poor liquidity in markets.

Sensitivity to the JPY remains another tactical consideration, having been the key catalyst for the Asia FX rally in recent weeks. Our prior analysis suggests the batting order is generally CNH, North Asia, ASEAN and India, in terms of sensitivities, but considerations could shift in the opposite directions. With bilateral exchange rates no longer pushing against the strong end vs the JPY, Asian central banks may be less motivated to shift their smoothing efforts lower. Particularly if USDAsia continues to go lower, export reliant economies may not necessarily need the same extent of adjustment that historical CNH or JPY sensitivity might imply.

Malaysia is back, but psychology and reserves are lagging: The MYR has been on a tear of late, rallying 6% against the USD in the past 10 sessions. FX fundamentals have been softening on the margin, with exports slowing but imports rising on a nascent investment cycle, leaving the trade surplus at half of its 2022 wides. Inbound FDI has generated excitement in equities, but Malaysia’s ability to capture significant value from data-centre related investment remains unclear, given the capex intensive nature of the sector. That said, prudent reforms on subsidies and a maturing democracy are bringing investors back to the market, though accessibility remains an issue for some.

The recent rally has been sparked by the repatriation of income on foreign investments, a measure that has been encouraged by policy makers onshore to shore up the MYR. Longer-term valuations remain significantly mis-aligned from fundamentals, owing to a shift in market psychology after mandatory conversion rules introduced in 2017. Corporates have seen a dollarization of their deposits since regulations were removed in 2021, with FX deposits onshore rising to \$54.7bn – an excess of \$10.1bn or 2.8% GDP above the pre-pandemic trend. Poor conversion rates have also resulted in Bank Negara having to lean on its forward book for FX intervention and liquidity injections, leaving net reserves 3% GDP below the IMF’s ARA metric. This metric may be somewhat overstated given the high levels of intra-organization debt with subsidiaries of Malaysian corporates, and even less relevant if the USD continues to go lower, but markets will be watching closely for potential reserve accumulation into the rally.

Longer term, the rally in the MYR can continue if fundamental reforms bring investors back, given significantly cheap valuations. Tactically, the MYR has overshot short-term valuations versus rate differentials given the one-sided nature of the spot market. The need to re-build buffers down the road may also serve as a headwind for the rally, as could a smaller and secondary dividend season for KLCI stocks in Sep. Fixing market psychology will be key to re-aligning the ringgit with its improving fundamentals, with free flow of capital and accessibility of FX markets critical to rebuilding the credibility of the MYR market.
