China’s July Caixin manufacturing PMI came in notably below expectations, falling 2.0-pt to 49.8 (J.P.Morgan: 51.7; consensus: 51.5). The notable decline in the July Caixin manufacturing PMI reversed five consecutive monthly gains through June, with the July reading registering at the lowest level since October last year. Regarding the major PMI components, the output component fell significantly by 4.4-pt to 50.2 in July, new orders fell 2.6-pt to 49.2, and export orders edged down 0.1-pt to 50.5. Besides, the employment component stayed unchanged at 49.9, the input price component fell 1.8-pt to 50.5 and the output price component fell 1.2-pt to 49.9.

## Combining NBS and Caixin PMI readings implies cautious outlook on nearterm economic activity

Following the downside surprise in 2Q GDP with June activity data highlighting a fragile consumption trend, the July NBS PMI report points to continued softness in consumption and service activities, along with moderate easing in industrial sector growth. In particular, services and consumption appear to remain the weak link in the economy.

Meanwhile, the notable weakening in the July Caixin manufacturing PMI, which is generally perceived to tilt towards the export sector, adds to the concern on the economy’s near-term momentum. Recall that the Caixin manufacturing PMI has generally outperformed the NBS manufacturing PMI since 2Q23, consistent with solid performance of the export sector (net exports accounted for 0.7%-pt of the 5.0%oya headline GDP growth in 1H24). In this regard, the 2.0-pt decline in the July Caixin manufacturing PMI to 49.8 seems to close the gap with the NBS manufacturing PMI (at 49.4 in July) to a large extent, while the 4.4-pt plunge in the Caixin output component (to 49.2) potentially hint at slowing in near-term exportrelated industrial production activity.

Overall, while the export sector has been an important driver for the Chinese economy’s near-term growth in recent quarters, amid ongoing weakness in the housing sector and sluggish consumption growth, potential slowing in the export engine would imply growing uncertainty on the industrial sector’s growth outlook going ahead. In this regard, concerns of trade tensions, including recent tariff increase by the Biden administration and the EU on selected Chinese goods, as well as the US election later this year and the trade policy implications, are a major uncertainty for the Chinese economy going forward.

In addition, regarding the pricing environment, it is worth noting that the Caixin manufacturing PMI input price component fell 1.8-pt to 50.5 in July, while the output price component fell 1.2-pt to 49.8. This seems to reflect the impact of insufficient demand conditions, sending cautious signals about producer pricing power, and may hint at lingering pressure for Chinese exporters’ price competition in the global market.

Putting together the combined signals from the July NBS PMIs and Caixin manufacturing PMI, it appears China’s economic momentum remains on the soft side in the near term, with lingering softness in service and consumption as the weak link for the economy, while the export sector, a crucial driver of growth in recent quarters, may have started to lose some momentum. This highlights the crucial role of macro policy to stabilize growth going into 2H.

## Boosting consumption is high-priority for 2H macro policy

On the policy side, as weaker-than-expected 2QGDP has put China’s full-year growth target (around 5%) at risk, the 3rd Plenum and the Politburo meeting both emphasized that this year’s economic and social development goals must be achieved, raising the question whether macro policy will be adjusted quickly.

The politburo meeting on 30 July emphasized that countercylical policy measures should be strengthened. Our interpretation is that macro policy will become growth-friendly, but the focus is on better utilization of the existing policy space rather than additional stimulus. On the fiscal side, the priority is to accelerate the issuance of special local government bonds, as the issuance in 1H was significantly lagging behind normal years. About 2 trillion yuan special local government bonds can be issued this quarter. Following the latest announcement that 300bn yuan out of 1 trillion special treasury bonds will be used to support equipment upgrade and consumer goods trade-ins, the government may also relax the rule and expand the scope of use of special local government bonds. On the monetary policy front, after last week’s rate cuts, we expect a 25bp RRR cut in the coming months, but credit growth will likely stay subdued and the PBOC may enter a wait-and-see mode again for next rate cut – our forecast timing for the next rate cut is 1Q25, though the possibility of 4Q24 cannot be ruled out if China’s economic activity continues to disappoint and the Fed starts rate cuts in September as widely expected.

The interesting change in policy guidance from this week’s Politburo meeting is that boosting consumption receives higher priority. The statement called for boosting consumption to expand domestic demand, shifting the focus of economic policies to social welfare and consumption, increasing household income via multiple channels, enhancing the consumption capacity and willingness of low- and middle-income groups. In particular, the Politburo meeting highlighted service consumption as an important area to expand and upgrade consumption (e.g. cultural tourism, elderly care, childcare and housekeeping). This raises the hope that, after the recent announcement of consumer goods trade-in subsidies (in autos, home appliances), the government may also roll out new measures to support service consumption. The shift in policy is clearly welcome, yet the fiscal difficulty of local governments is a main constraint (any central government support or expanded scope of use of special LGBs would be helpful).

Regarding further details in the two sets of July manufacturing PMI reports:

• The headline Caixin manufacturing PMI fell 2.0-pt to 49.8 in July, while the NBS manufacturing PMI fell 0.1-pt to 49.4.

Regarding industrial production activity, the output component of the Caixin manufacturing PMI plunged 4.4-pt to 50.2 in July, while the NBS manufacturing PMI output component fell 0.5-pts to 50.1.

Regarding forward-looking demand conditions, the new orders component in the Caixin manufacturing PMI fell 2.6-pt to 49.2, while the new orders component in the NBS manufacturing PMI fell 0.2-pt to 49.3. Regarding external demand conditions, the export orders component in the Caixin manufacturing PMI fell 0.1-pts to 50.5 in July, while that for the NBS manufacturing PMI rose modestly by 0.2-pts to 48.5 in July.

• Regarding labor market conditions, the employment component in the Caixin manufacturing PMI stayed unchanged at 49.9 in July, while the employment component in the NBS manufacturing PMI rose 0.2-pt to 48.3.

• Regarding input price pressure, the input price component in the Caixin manufacturing PMI fell 1.8-pt to 50.5 in July, while that in the NBS manufacturing PMI fell 1.8-pts to 49.9.

Regarding supply chain conditions, the suppliers’ delivery time component in the Caixin manufacturing PMI edged down 0.1-pt to 49.7 in July – recall that lower readings reflect longer delivery time. Besides, the suppliers’ delivery time component in the NBS manufacturing PMI declined 0.2-pt to 49.3 in July.
