## Japan Equity Strategy

Jan-Mar earnings wrap: Weak guidance indicates conservatism; acceleration of corporate governance reforms a positive for share prices

More than 90% of TSE Prime Market companies have announced their FY2023 financial results, for which overall profit grew in the double digits (%) and beat initial guidance (for a profit decline). The FY2024 guidance for net profit to decline 3% YoY looks conservative, and it looks likely to be revised upward during the year to profit growth, as the reasons for the better-than-expected earnings in FY2023, including US demand, demand from foreign visitors, yen depreciation, and pass-throughs of cost increases, are likely to continue to be at play to a certain extent, in our view. We expect that a likely 2H peak-out in pressure from the yen<sup>’</sup>s depreciation, which is currently weighing on stocks, should support business sentiment. More importantly, we have confirmed that companies have been accelerating their efforts to reform corporate governance and improve capital efficiency in the year since the TSE first put pressure on companies to do so. The amount of share buybacks announced grew 1.5x YoY, and dividends reached a new record high. In addition, a series of announcements of upward revisions of RoE targets and other actions would possibly support a structural uptrend in share prices, which have stalled in the current macro environment characterized by the weak yen. In this report, we list companies with FY2024 guidance that exceeded market expectations as well as companies that made positive announcements on governance reforms and capital efficiency improvements.

## Summary

FY2023 results show profit growth exceeded initial guidance for a profit decline (the weak yen contributed 30-40% of the upside). Profit margins have increased due to price pass-throughs.

C FY2024 guidance for a profit decline is conservative, and we expect upward revisions during the year.

Announcements to improve corporate capital efficiency, such as expanding shareholder returns, accelerating reductions of strategic shareholdings, raising profit targets, and generating cash flow, are continuing.

We show lists of companies with guidance that significantly exceeded market expectations, and information about companies that are positive on corporate governance.

The main reasons for the earnings overshoot were (1) yen depreciation +7-8ppts (initial corporate guidance of ¥130/\$, versus the average rate for the fiscal year of ¥145/\$), (2) stronger-than-expected US demand, (3) pass-throughs of cost increases, (4) stronger-than-expected demand from foreign visitors, (5) crude oil price declines, and (6) interest rates increases. The auto, banking & insurance, and electric power & gas sectors contributed to the better-than-expected results (Figures 3-6).

Weak yen contributed 30-40% of earnings overshoot; earnings sensitivity to exchange rates declining: The weak yen contributed 30-40% of the sales and operating profit initial guidance overshoots in FY2023, when the yen/dollar rate averaged ¥144.6/ \$, 11.2% above the initial guidance and representing yen depreciation of 6.7% YoY. Japanese companies<sup>’</sup> earnings have become less sensitive to exchange rates, as the companies have been accelerating the localization of overseas production and import costs have been rising (Figures 8-11).

FY2024 guidance weak but conservative; upward revisions likely during fiscal year: The FY2024 guidance was weak (YoY sales growth of 2.2% and a net profit decline of 2.9%; for companies announcing guidance; Figure 12). The exchange rate assumptions for initial guidance are conservative (an average rate of ¥143/\$), and ¥155/\$ for the year would lead to net profit growth of 6%, based on our calculation (Figures 13-14). We expect the weak initial guidance could be revised upward to profit growth during FY2024, as the factors that led to upward revisions of FY2023 guidance from profit declines to profit growth are likely to be at play some extent in FY2024 also. We estimate FY2024 TOPIX EPS growth of 10%. The I/B/E/S consensus estimate for TOPIX EPS growth, currently 8.6%, has continued to be revised upward (a YoY increase of 15.4% for FY2023; Figure 15). Figure 16 shows a list of companies with FY2024 guidance that is considerably above consensus estimates (guidance from Sumitomo Mitsui Trust Holdings (SMTH), Shionogi, NEC, Dai-ichi Life Holdings, Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group (SMFG), Mizuho Financial Group, and Suzuki Motor outperformed market expectations).

In first year of wage hikes, profit margins expanded partly owing to pass-throughs of cost increases: In FY2023, TOPIX companies<sup>’</sup> operating margins widened even though wages and material costs rose. There were concerns at the start of the fiscal year that margins would shrink owing to wage hikes, but pass-throughs of cost increases and other factors mentioned above ended up offsetting personnel cost increases (Figures 17- 18). Although the FY2024 base pay increase negotiated in the spring was 3.6%, more than the year-ago increase (2.1%), we do not expect margins to worsen based on companies efforts to pass on cost increases and improve productivity, as well as the global economy and the forex outlook. Figure 19 shows operating margin expansion/contraction over the past year by sector (vertical axis) and comparisons with the average since 2011 (horizontal axis). Operating margins improved in more than 50% of sectors, including domestic demand-driven sectors (e.g., retail, transportation, and food) as well as overseas demand-driven ones, which benefited from the weak yen. Pass-throughs of cost increases also contributed to the improvement in corporate earnings.

Series of announcements of measures on corporate governance reforms　through increases in shareholder returns and reductions of cross-shareholdings: The announcements of measures on corporate governance reforms and capital efficiency improvements this earnings season were positive, in our view. As of May 15, the authorized amount of share buybacks announced was ¥6.3 trillion, more than 60% of the ¥10.1 trillion announced in FY2023 and up 1.5x YoY (Figure 21). The announcements were particularly positive in such sectors with strong earnings as autos, electric appliances & precision instruments, information & communication, trading companies, and banks (Figure 22). Many companies announced dividend increases. TOPIX companies<sup>’</sup> total dividends in FY2023 look like they could be the highest in the past 10 years (Figure 23). For specific companies, there are series of announcements about share buybacks/dividend increases announced (Toyota Motor, Japan Post Holdings, Recruit Holdings, Komatsu, Mitsubishi Corporation, Mitsui & Co.; Figures 25-26), accelerating strategic holding reductions (Toyota group companies like Denso; Toray; Figure 27), revision up of ROE/profit target (Isetan Mitsukoshi Holdings, West Japan Railway), cash flow generation, asset/business restructuring (Sony, trading companies) (Figures 28-29).

Negative reaction by share prices: Share price reactions after the earnings announcements were biased toward the downside (Figure 30), with price changes in line with the historical averages for those that beat the Bloomberg consensus estimates and slightly greater than average for those that fell short of the consensus estimates (Figure 31). This trend is also evident in the earnings results of US and European companies, as risk asset prices continue to rise in global markets.
