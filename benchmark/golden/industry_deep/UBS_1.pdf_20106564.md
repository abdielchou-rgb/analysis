# Quant Models for Fundamental Investors: Opportunities & Risk, February Update

## Quant Navigator: Leveraging Quant Tools with Human Insights

Quantitative tools enable us to analyze large volumes of historical information: test observed historical relationships, build forecasting models, identify sources of risk and subsequently, rank stocks on favored characteristics. Where quant models are at a disadvantage, we can leverage human insights to make more informed investment decisions. By combining human insights with machine analytics, we're able to address the various sources of return variation (beta, macro, style, sector, and idiosyncratic). In our recent publication, Quant Navigator "Quant Models for Fundamental Investors: Opportunities & Risk", we introduced a suite of tools and models that help navigate both risk and opportunity (Figure 1). In this note, we look to see where our models are currently positioned.

## Where are Opportunities Today?

Over the last month, both cross-sectional volatility and pairwise correlations edged lower and are now at or near historical norms. Headwinds persist for investors. Our forecast volatility model is showing that volatility will continue to remain low for the first half of the year. Generally, low volatility coincides with periods of investor greed (riskon), while higher volatility points to investor fear (risk-off). Though we are in a period of risk-on, investors bucked historical trends, favoring quality, growth and momentum (normally favored during periods of fear) to start the new year. The level of outperformance was outside of historical ranges for most of the factors, while value's underperformance was within historical ranges. The current mix of our Sector Rotation is overweight Cyclicals (+39%) and underweight Defensive (-39%). Our ML Earnings Growth model has highest conviction on the upside for Real Estate Management, Banks and Transportation with high conviction for the downside on Energy, Teleco Services, Food Bev & Tobacco and Utilities.

## Macro

Do we expect macro conditions to improve for active managers? The VIX has fallen dramatically since the Fed cut interest rates by 50bps in November and signaled three cuts will be coming in 2024. In January, the VIX edged marginally higher from 13.2 at the start of the month to 14.35 at month end. Our model is currently showing the VIX to remain stable to low for the early part of 2024. (Figure 6). Based on the trend in the fed funds rate, we believe that volatility will rise in the coming year as companies refinance their debt. Higher volatility does signal an improved environment for active investors. The cost of capital started rising mid-2022, so it makes sense that we'll see the impact flow to the bottom line and bottom-line growth in 2024, with a rise in volatility.

## Factor Rotation

Generally, during a period of Greed, investors tend to favor Value, with a rotation into Quality and Growth as investors pivot to Fear. Based on where we think volatility will go, we would expect Value to outperform into Q1, with a rotation to Quality in midyear.

In Figure 7, we look at the long-short performance of select Growth, Momentum, Quality, Value and Risk factors over over the course of January. Using regimes based on lagged Fed Funds rates, we find ourselves currently in a period of Greed. Normally, in periods of Greed, we expect investors to favor Value, while in periods of Fear, Quality and Growth have been preferred. We started out the month with a return to Quality, Momentum and Growth where returns for many of these factors were outside of historical norms, so unusually strong outperformance. Value was out of favor for much of the month though within historical bounds.

## Sector Rotation

Using a combination of Delta Quality and Free Cash Flow Yield, we construct a sector rotation model. We have modified the universe this month to utilizing the Russell 1000, whereas previously we were constructing the sector rotation based on the broader 3000 universe. Currently our sector rotation model has the largest overweights to Industrials, Info Tech and Consumer Staples, and the largest underweights are to Energy, Materials and Health Care. Normally, a higher interest rate environment would signal investors to favor Defensive sectors with Cyclicals out of favor. The current mix of our rotation model is underweight Defensives (-39%) and overweight Cyclicals (+39%).

## Valuation Method and Risk Statement

Our quantitative models rely on reported financial statement information, consensus earnings forecasts and stock prices. Errors in these numbers are sometimes impossible to prevent (as when an item is mis-stated by a company). Also, the models employ historical data to estimate the efficacy of stock selection strategies and the relationships among strategies, which may change in the future. Additionally, unusual company-specific events could overwhelm the systematic influence of the strategies used to rank and score stocks.
