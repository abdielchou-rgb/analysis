# FOMC Preview: Annual Housekeeping

We expect little change to the statement and no signal of an imminent cut. The rotation of voters tilts a bit less favorable for an early cut. Discussion will center on inflation and the rates path ahead, and the balance sheet. Our strategists stay long UST 5y.

## Key expectations

• The FOMC remains on hold at 5.375%. We continue to expect 100bp of cuts this year, beginning in June. The Fed cuts by an additional 200bp in 2025, returning policy to a neutral setting.

• We expect little change in the FOMC statement as the Fed awaits direction from important revisions to employment and inflation in early February.

• In the Q&A, Chair Powell acknowledges discussion of the Fed's balance sheet and sounds pleased even while cautious on inflation progress as revisions loom. He is likely to repeat that rates will be lower this year, but avoid committing to any particular meeting.

• Our rates strategists stay biased long duration with 5y notes into the FOMC meeting, but even they do not see a major move happening on the back of the FOMC meeting itself. Even if the FOMC is neutral for duration, they see potential for the recent rise in yields to fade, amid a cooling inflation backdrop.

• Our FX strategists forecast that the USD will rise against G10 currencies by the end of 2024. Our economists are above consensus on US growth, and below consensus for growth abroad. USD has closely tracked investor sentiment on the China outlook, which may remain subdued. However, we do not currently recommend long USD positions due to our expectation that 5y Treasury yields will decline and the potential for further equity index gains between the last Fed hike and the first Fed cut. A high-frequency fair value FX valuation model also suggests that a number of G10 currencies are undervalued relative to the USD. The historical record suggests that FX volatility climbs ahead of the first Fed insurance cut and declines as cuts continue.

• On the agency MBS side, our strategists remain neutral on mortgage basis.

## Annual Procedural Meeting

A good deal of housekeeping occurs at the January meeting, including the rotation of voters, Committee assignments, and publishing refreshed/revised policy documents. At this meeting, we expect the Fed to keep the target range for the federal funds rate unchanged at 5.25% to 5.50% (5.375% mid-point). We expect little change in the FOMC statement and Chair Powell to sound pleased with inflation progress. He is also likely to acknowledge discussion of the balance sheet and that no decisions were taken at this meeting.

We expect little change to the FOMC policy statement, but focus on key areas of risk to look for.

Current conditions: We expect little change from the last FOMC statement. Regarding the labor market, we expect the statement to reflect the slowdown in job gains, e.g., job gains have slowed, but remain strong; unemployment has remained low. For inflation, we think the statement could add the word "further" to acknowledge softer intermeeting data, though the sentence still rings true as is; "Inflation has eased over the past year but remains elevated".

In last week's Friday Finish we also asked ourselves: Does the Fed still remain "highly" attentive to inflation?Fedspeak in the intermeeting period suggests that policymakers are pleased with the progress on inflation, but are not ready to declare victory. Removing the word "highly" might be a way to convey that upside risks to inflation have diminished – a tricky move ahead of CPI revisions on February 9.

Forward guidance: Does the Committee change how it characterizes its reaction function? We have argued for several meetings that the Fed should have already revised that language to provide more flexible guidance, in particular to match Chair Powell's view that the current stance of policy is balanced. Changing the language to "In determining the appropriate stance of policy to return inflation to 2 percent over time... " would allow for a greater degree of freedom as up, down, or sideways are all equal options. Perhaps this is the meeting in which our suggested change is finally made, but we don't know which forces were at play in the Committee's decision to maintain the less flexible guidance. To be sure, the Committee has been making incremental changes to the sentence, and the change we suggest could be seen as another step. Ultimately, we think that if the Committee chooses to maintain that inflation remains "elevated",

then the less flexible guidance likely stays in this statement as the two parts are tied together.

Press conference: We expect Chair Powell to reiterate that the current stance of policy is better balanced between the risk of over- and under-tightening. Like Governor Waller, Chair Powell is likely to sound pleased with the progress on inflation, optimistic that strong growth will slow ahead, and state that the Committee continues to discuss its outlook for cutting rates this year. The courage for the Fed to remain on hold for such a lengthy period is being challenged, but we think a reacceleration in trend core services inflation ahead holds off policy action until June.

Though we expect no change to balance sheet guidance in the statement, Fed communication has put QT in the spotlight. We look for Fed discussions at upcoming meetings – which Powell is likely to acknowledge when asked in the Q&A. We look for a June start to taper, earlier than our previous expectation. But the market narrative that the end of QT is coming soon has been overdone, in our view. An earlier start would be paired with a slower taper, and we continue to look for a February 2025 end, with risks skewed later.

## The Fed's Balance Sheet in the Spotlight – Our Key Expectations

The Fed will start discussing tapering QT soon but will not end QT soon. Recent Fed communication has stirred market discussion about the end of QT. That narrative is overdone. We see the Fed announcing the parameters of a taper to QT in May and initiating the taper in June. QT ends around February 2025, but could go on for longer. Key expectations:

• The Fed will cut the pace of QT in half for Treasuries, to \$30bn/month, but leave MBS to run off with prepayments.

• The SOMA portfolio falls about \$750bn from the current level. We see the RRP facility falling to roughly \$0, and QT ending in early 2025 when reserves are about \$3.2tn.

• Important parameters – If the SOFR - IORB spread goes to zero or turns positive, it will be important. If the standing repo facility (SRF) is used in size, it will also be a meaningful signal.

• The shift in supply of Treasuries relative to expectations will likely not be substantial, and the effects will be most noticeable in the front end and swap spreads, rather than longer-dated yields.

## Rate cuts on their mind?

Market context into the meeting: The January FOMC meeting comes at a notable juncture where on the one hand markets see the Fed gearing towards rate cuts, while on the other, the Fed communication itself has been more ambiguous about it. Our economists do not see a rate cut in March but note that there is a risk of a rate cut in May, with their base case in June.

Markets have also see-sawed on a March cut, currently pricing nearly a 40% chance of a cut by March, down from nearly 80% earlier in the month (see Exhibit 3 ). While the timing of the rate cut is going to matter, the pace of rate cuts will matter as well. To that end, it is notable that the timing of the first cut has recently been moving much less (see Exhibit 4 ), while duration moves are mostly coming from the pace of cuts – suggesting the pace is becoming the main variable for markets.

Is the Fed thinking about rate cuts yet? As there will be no dot plot at the upcoming meeting, markets will not get a new datapoint to understand how the Fed's view is evolving on the pace of cuts. The focus instead will be on understanding the Fed's view on the timing of rate cuts in 2024.

Our economists do not see the Fed coming across as being ready for a cut – in large part due to their inflation forecast, which sees a bumpy path for services inflation. Investors have seen this from a viewpoint that the holistic weakening of the economy would get the Fed into "insurance cuts" mode, and Powell's comments on the topic of insurance cut will be watched closely.

How much confidence on inflation, and supply side: The fact that the FOMC meeting comes ahead of the February 9 CPI revisions makes it less likely that the Fed will express confidence about the inflation decline or the move towards rate cuts. Our economists think the Fed could remove the word "highly" from the "highly attentive to inflation risks" phrase – but that remains tricky before the CPI revisions.

Another linked topic for Powell will be whether there if forward-looking confidence around inflation and the role and support from the supply side, namely, immigration and global supply chains (see Exhibit 5 ). Both at the November and December FOMC meetings, Powell highlighted the role of improving supply in cooling inflation, and ultimately sounded dovish to investors.

However, the FOMC minutes noted that the improvements from the supply side may have played out. It will be important to see whether the Fed continues to see a role of the supply side in moving inflation towards 2%.

March cut probability to stay around current levels: Ultimately, even if the Fed may not express much confidence about cutting soon, investors see a low bar to cut in March – with 2 payroll reports and 2 CPI reports between January and March FOMC meetings. As long as the Fed doesn't forcefully rule out rate cuts in March, investors will stay interested in the possibility, and we do not see much lower movement from the 40% probability of a cut priced for March.

Even if the Fed results in a neutral outcome for Treasury yields, we expect the cooling of inflation datapoints like the new tenant rent (see Exhibit 6 ), the low level of CPI fixings implied inflation, and no major changes at the CPI revisions - to all support being long duration at current levels. We suggest staying long 5y notes into the FOMC meeting.

Time to talk about QT: Recent communication from various Fed speakers, as well as the December FOMC minutes suggested the Fed was talking about talking about QT tapering in December. Which means they could actually talk about a QT taper at the January FOMC meeting. While it is unlikely the Fed furnishes concrete details at the press conference tomorrow (there might be some in the minutes 3 weeks later), the market will listen for any clues on timing, pace of tapering and eventual end of QT. Our view is that the Fed starts tapering QT in May, and runs QT through 1Q25.

## USD outlook

We continue to forecast that the USD will rise against G10 currencies by the end of 2024 for several reasons:

First, our economics colleagues expect US growth to be significantly stronger than the average sell-side economist. We expect the US economy to grow by 2.1% this year – faster than the pace at which it has growth since 2000 and well above the 1.3% median forecast among economists polled by Bloomberg.

Conversely, we expect the UK and China (and to a lesser degree the euro area) to grow slower than most other economists ( Exhibit 7 ).

Our expectation for stronger-than-expected US growth and slower-than-expected growth abroad means that data prints are likely to surprise to the upside in the US and to the downside abroad.

Second, our economics colleagues forecast that European inflation will remain subdued in the coming months relative to inflation in the US. While we expect core US inflation pressures to decelerate, we expect them to remain below a 2% annualized rate in the euro area for several months.

Furthermore, recent declines in European gas prices raise "an increased risk to undershooting the 2%, in particular in 2025" in the ECB's projections.

As a result, market pricing for ECB policy may continue to reflect the possibility of one or more 50bp cuts from the ECB later this year, with risks that the market-implied possibility of 50bp cuts increases. Such an outcome would likely move front-end yield differentials in the USD's favor.

Third, in recent months the USD has traded closely in line with the price of stocks of firms that derive a significant proportion of their revenues from China ( Exhibit 8 ). This "China exposure" equity basket is available on Bloomberg as MSXXCHNA Index.

As sentiment on the Chinese growth outlook (as measured by this equity basket) has declined, the US dollar has gained, and vice versa. Notably, the broad USD has recently traded more closely in line with the prices of the China-exposed stocks than with the broader S&P 500, outright Treasury yields, or yield differentials ( Exhibit 9 ).

Investor sentiment on the China growth outlook is likely to remain downbeat, given our expectation that policy easing so far has been reactive and insufficient, keeping the risks of a debt-deflation loop alive and that policymakers will maintain a modest pace of reactive easing.

Despite these factors, we are not currently recommending long USD positions for several reasons:

First, our rates strategy team expects that 5y Treasury yields will decline in the coming weeks (see section above).

Second, our equity research team notes that strong returns for the S&P market cap weighted index often tend to come between the last Fed hike and the first Fed cut. Potential further S&P 500 gains may reduce upside risks for the USD. That said, equity market gains so far in January (+2.7%) have not prevented the DXY from rising nearly 2% month to date.

Third, our high-frequency fair value model (see discussion here) suggests that a number of G10 currencies are undervalued relative to the USD.

For example, recent moves in 2y and 5y US-Japan yield differentials, copper prices, and a basket of stocks reflecting international vs. domestic exposure (MSZZILUS Index on Bloomberg) would suggest that USD/JPY should be roughly 3.8% below current levels.

And recent moves in the S&P 500, copper prices, and baskets of stocks reflecting metals and mining exposure and China revenue exposure (MSXXMET Index and MSXXCHNA Index) imply that AUD/USD should be roughly 3.5% higher – as discussed here.

Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)

Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received.
