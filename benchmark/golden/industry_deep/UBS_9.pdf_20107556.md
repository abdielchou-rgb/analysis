# Global Rates Strategy

# Rates Map: Core bond markets to diverge for longer

Global rates rose dramatically on the surprisingly strong US CPI before geopolitical concerns resurfaced. The US print challenged the "Fed must cut because the real Fed Funds policy rate is too high" Taylor rule mindset. 3m annualized core CPI is now 4.5%, which would put the real Fed Funds just below 1%, and is not particularly tight by historical standards.

 We expect the 10y US Treasury vs Bund spread to stay wide in line with our earlier work. The divergence is now also reflected in the cuts priced in 2024 for ECB versus Fed, with the BoE lagging.

UBS now expects just two cuts from the Fed in 2024 but we have high conviction that the ECB delivers 75 bps this year given economic growth differentials, relative inflation dynamics, and central bank communication. Unlike the US, Europe's supply of safe assets has been shrinking as % of global GDP (Figure 5).

 We recommend longs in the US front end every time implied easing for 2024 falls below 50 bps. US TIPS breakevens look attractive along the curve.

 We have been recommending investors to look for entry points to build longs in Europe and receive the Dec 25 ECB meeting on spikes. This week has certainly provided an entry point.

 The US 10y auction tailed sharply, bringing back supply fears. That prevented the curves from flattening too much on the strong CPI. TIC data to be released on 17 April could be critical, as any perceived weakening of overseas demand would put supply on the front burner.

 Fears about a shift to a new regime of higher long-term real rates persist, with the IMF releasing a blog ahead of its spring meetings.

 We have argued before that policy rates need to be lower if term premia are highe or financial conditions will be too tight. A decrease in the policy rate could also prompt investor to increase duration and lower term premia.

 We think US 10y yields will be meaningfully higher in a no-landing scenario, where we see a real risk of the Fed raising rates to 6.5% by mid next year.

 We see steepening as more of a theme for 2025, certainly compared to forwards, after incorporating our US rates forecasts with later and less aggressive Fed easing.

 We think that ECB comments on the Transmission Protection Instrument and the Executive Deficit Procedure support our constructive calls on country spreads (see our note on OAT spreads here).

 There remain several reasons to be long the UK curve.

 Receiving 1y1y ESTR vs SARON as the spread looks attractive. We expect just two more cuts from the SNB.

## Bund-UST spread to stay wide for longer as markets prices out Fed cuts

Arguments we made in Oct 2023 and Feb 2024 on why the10y US Treasury vs Bund spread was set to stay wide still hold, and we expect the spread to remain around 200 bps in 2024-25 (Figure 3). In our previous work, we pointed to the relative resilience of the US economy, inflation dynamics, and central bank outlook. We have received several questions of clients on the implications of a weaker euro and the recent increase in commodity prices on euro area inflation. According to UBS economics, a 10% depreciation in the nominal EUR/USD exchange rates leads to an 0.5 ppt increase in HIPC inflation 1y out, whereas a 10% increase in oil prices leads to an increase of 0.25pp increase in HIPC inflation 1 year later, so moves have been contained for now (see page 62 here).

## What about monetary policy and higher long-term rates ?

Global rates rose dramatically earlier in the week but we are holding on to our early March views that longs across the curve are starting to have value again and investors should look for entry points. Nevertheless, fears to a shift to a new regime of higher long-term real rates are rising, as indicated by a recent IMF blog We have argued in essay 2 of our 2024 outlook and previously that policy rates need to be lower if term premia are higher or financial conditions will be too tight. A decrease in the policy rate could also prompt investor to increase duration, lowering term premia as the curve steepens again (Figure 4). A big challenge for bonds has also been that they have not been a good hedge for equities following the recent inflation surge. But that correlation should change again as inflation settles lower. Europe's supply of safe assets has also been shrinking as % of global GDP (Figure 5). We do think 10y US yields will be meaningfully higher in a no-landing scenario.

Our US economics colleagues have also made the point that higher debt levels as a consequence of lower structural growth imply lower r-star. Debt-to-GDP during the decades pre-Covid was inversely, not positively, correlated with r-star. High and rising government debt levels, with already large fiscal deficits, does not automatically imply higher potential growth for an economy, nor even added short-run growth.

## UK curve attractive for multiple reasons

We continue to see reasons for UK outperformance. First, we expect UK inflation to further ease in the coming months with differentials turning negative (Figure 6). Second, there has been a shift in the MPC with 2 hawks no longer voting for hikes and the Committee acknowledging the recent decline in services inflation and moderation in wage growth. Governor Bailey also said that a June cut is in play. Third, the UK spring budget was relatively uneventful and we think that the fiscal risk is relatively higher in, for example, the US due to the upcoming elections. Fourth, the market is pricing a BoE cutting cycle not that differently from the Fed, despite the weaker growth outlook and policy rates at similar levels (Figure 7). Fifth, cross-country spreads are also wide. More details are here.
