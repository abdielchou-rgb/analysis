## 3QFY24 Earnings Dissector : Upgrade Hospitals / Downgrade Staples

Nifty 50<sup>’</sup>s ex-financials revenue/PAT came in at +5%/15% YoY, and EBITDA margin expansion was 78bps. Nifty 50<sup>’</sup>s Beat/Miss came at 24%/28% (using Bloomberg consensus estimates for the non-covered companies). MSCI India ex financials companies<sup>’</sup> revenue/PAT grew at 6%/31% YoY with EBITDA margins expanding by 171bps leading to an overall operating margin of 19%. MSCI India<sup>’</sup>s 3Q24 Beat/Miss came at 29%/33%. Softer commodity prices along with lower energy and freight costs benefited the volume drive for the companies. Within MSCI India universe, Real Estate, Consumer Discretionary and Materials had the most beats (>+5% of est.), whereas Financials and IT had the most misses (<-5% of est.). Overall, 3Q results were decent, with uptick in revenue growth and margin expansion. Going ahead, we expect that the input cost benefits will slowly taper; focus will shift towards business prospects and balance sheet strength. We are upgrading the hospital sector to OW and downgrading the Consumer Staples sector to neutral but continue to prefer premium consumption plays.

Over the last one month, MSCI India consensus earnings expectations for 2024/25 were marginally revised up by 0.4%/0.9% respectively. A higher number of companies in Energy, Auto, Healthcare, Industrials and Utilities had earnings revised upwards. For FY24, IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, Coal India, Zomato, Trent, Tata Motors, Lupin, Cummins and GAIL India witnessed double digit positive earnings revisions (Table 2). Overall for 2024 consensus earnings estimates, Auto, Healthcare, Energy, Communication Services and Diversified financial services had >1% upward revisions, while Materials, Consumer Staples, Industrials and Insurance had negative revisions of 1% (Table 1). MSCI India consensus earnings growth estimate for CY24/25 is 14%/15% respectively.

Key catalysts to watch in coming months: Fed meeting in March, General election in April-May, RBI meeting in April and a few state assembly elections. Our Nifty-50 Index target is 22,000. If BJP retains power in the 2024 elections and RBI starts easing amidst a potentially benign US/global macro outcome (“goldilocks<sup>”</sup>), then the Nifty may trade between our base and bull case (25000) target led by a further P/E re-rating. However, if the general election results are unexpected, along with a global recession, geopolitical tensions, higher oil prices, or higher domestic unemployment - that could lead to a period of uncertainty with the Nifty trading close to our bear case (16000).

Sector allocation: Overweight sectors are Financials (ICICI Bank, Bank of Baroda, Shriram Finance, ICICI Prudential, LIC, ICICI Lombard), Auto (Bajaj Auto, Tata Motor, Hero Motocorp, Exide, Samvardhana Motherson), Real Estate (DLF, Prestige Estates), Healthcare (Sun Pharma, Abbott India, Mankind, Apollo Hospital, Max Healthcare) and selective Energy names (BPCL, Reliance Industries). Underweight sectors are IT and Materials.

## 3QFY24 Earnings Review

Oil price continues to be the main earnings driver. The Energy sector has been the standout performer with the most contribution to the overall top-line in the MSCI Index (Figure 9). Along with Financials, it contributed 50% of the earnings (with a combined market-cap weight of only 35% in the index). Even with flat revenue growth YoY, earnings were supported by three factors: a) higher retail price, b) Russian crude discounts, and c) a combination of high diesel cracks and favorable taxation (helping margins). IOCL/BPCL/HPCL delivered 9M FY24 EBITDA of Rs592bn/Rs349bn/ Rs200bn (standalone), but consensus full year forecasts are still Rs700bn/Rs415bn/ Rs241bn (link).

Strong credit expansion and spending fueled Financials’ earnings, but NIMs disappointed. Private banks reported robust earnings growth YoY due to high credit demand. However, NIMs came under pressure due to deposit repricing and higher CD ratio, leading to heavy sell off in banks like HDFCB following the results (Figure 5). Whilst NBFC performance has been broad-based and strong in 3Q (ex SBIC), the regulator has made some adverse comments around regulatory arbitrage even for upper layer NBFCs and also flagged usurious behavior of micro finance companies via rate hikes. With system constraints on deposits, credit risk in unsecured retail and a potential uptick in mortgage/corporate capex, PSU banks continue to screen better. We remain selective on private banks. Within NBFCs, we like mortgage companies given RE upcycle leading to higher growth and lower NPLs (link). Real estate sector witnessed strong revenue/PAT growth of +38%/+25% YoY. Companies reaped the benefits of strong pre-sales and firm demand.

IT earnings delivery was not stellar, MSCI India IT aggregated revenue/PAT growth was +2%/-4% YoY. Price reaction was mostly positive for IT post earnings, with investors pricing that the worst is likely behind. Within our coverage, TCS, INFO, HCLT, and WIPRO beat, whilst TECHM was mixed and LTIM missed. We remain skeptical about sustained uptick in the order book of the large IT companies amidst a global growth slowdown outlook. The Health Care sector showed resilient earnings growth aided by improvement in EBITDA margins by 245bps. This is particularly due to: a) strong domestic formulation segment results, b) lower R&D spends and c) US demand for generic drugs and moderate price erosion. We are upgrading the hospital sector to OW driven by strong earnings visibility, increasing occupancy, margin accretion on existing hospital beds, rising insurance penetration, higher spend on healthcare and calibrated expansion that is unlikely to create a material drag on profitability.

Operating leverage benefits and lower raw material cost drove margin expansion for the Auto sector. Accelerated retail trend for two wheelers supported top-line growth for Autos. Soft commodities price cooled in recent months led to better margin for most consumer companies. Consumer Discretionary/Staples<sup>’</sup> aggregated Q3FY24 revenue rose by 23%/10% y/y with EBITDA margin of Discretionary stocks expanding by 256 bps. Price reaction to earnings were more visible in Discretionary stocks whilst Staples was more muted. Note, 60% of Staples stocks within the Nifty 50 missed expectations. Beats in consumer staples sector were largely led by the urban market and premium products. Lackluster growth in wages and high inflation have hurt blue collar/middle class, particularly in rural areas. Anticipated demand during the festive season lagged expectation. Consumer staples growth remains sub-par either due to the K-shaped recovery or shifting share of the consumer wallet. The government desisted from increases in social schemes in the interim budget. In our view, rural demand recovery will remain gradual and warrants close monitoring. We are downgrading the Consumer Staples sector to neutral but continue to prefer premium consumption plays.

Strong profits growth in capex-heavy sectors show that India’s large industrial companies are in good shape (revenue/PAT growth +15%/+41% YoY). Companies are optimistic about the near to medium term business outlook with the enquiry level strong across industries. Order inflows for the quarter were strong. This offers hope for private capex to regain momentum as and when capacity utilization peaks. However we expect a slowdown in the near term due to the upcoming model code of conduct. Metals & mining sector witnessed steady domestic demand, lower input commodity cost supporting margins and sequentially higher prices (steel). However lower exports amidst weaker global demand remain a key concern. Chemical companies reported a mixed set of numbers due to high cost inventory, lower absorption of fixed cost and competition from Chinese exporters.

In 3Q24, private listed life insurers’ margins declined by -0.4% YoY, -1% QoQ (on median basis). This was largely on account of changes in product mix i.e. whilst the taxation of higher ticket and higher margin non-par products continued to put pressure on sales volumes (APE growth 5% oya on average), it was broadly offset by higher sales of ULIP products (lower margin) amid buoyant equity markets. Going forward, we expect potential weakness in share price for private life insurers in the near-term majorly driven by market<sup>’</sup>s concerns on potential regulatory pressure from rising customer protection on savings policies (i.e. potentially higher surrender value for non-linked product), which could dent the product margin outlook. On the non-life side, the underwriting cycle is showing signs of improvement due to cooling off of competition from expense of management (EoM) guidelines, especially in the motor own damage (OD) segment. Also, potential premium hike in motor third party segment in the next one or two quarters could be a likely tailwind for the sector.
