## May 2

Relentless Tsy bull-steepener unfolds before NFPs; Bunds rally but lack the steepening in the US; USD/JPY reverses lower, ends at session lows; CHF gains after upside inflation surprise; ECB's Stournaras backs 3 cuts; USD weakness supports CLP, BRL; DXY at 105.30 (-0.4%); US 10y at 4.581% (-4.7bp).

• Relentless UST bull-steepener precedes April US nonfarm payrolls; 2s30s steepens over 6bp.

• 10y Bunds close \~4bp richer at 2.54% as the curve moves in a parallel fashion but lacks the steepening in the US.

• USD/JPY (-0.6%) weakness persists as the pair reverses down from the 156 level to close below 154.

• CHF strengthens due to an upside surprise in inflation, which drove a small repricing higher in front-end Swiss rates.

• ECB Governing Council Member Stournaras now considers "three rate cuts in 2024 as the most likely scenario," instead of four.

• Broad US dollar weakness supports LatAm currencies; CLP (+1.5%) and BRL (+1.6%) lead.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

There was little resistance to the prevailing moves in macro markets ahead of the April nonfarm payrolls report in the US. A relentless bull-steepening of the Treasury curved highlighted a rather sluggish session, with the residual seasonality in the preliminary 1Q24 unit labor costs perhaps now well understood. The US dollar progressively weakened over the course of the session, with remnants of Wednesday’s dovish press conference and (unconfirmed) MoF intervention still fresh. Supported by a return of positive micro narratives from earnings as well as macro tailwinds from lower yields and a weaker US dollar, the S&P 500 (+0.91%) rallied broadly.

JGBs rallied modestly in the Tokyo morning on Thursday after the rally in USTs overnight on Wednesday, following downside surprises in ISM Manufacturing and JOLTS data as well as a dovish FOMC outcome. Minutes from the March BoJ meeting were largely in line with the Summary of Opinions, and the liquidity tap auction was digested smoothly. However, in the Tokyo afternoon session, JGBs began to sell off across the curve, especially in the super long-end, and the curve bear-steepened. Market participants may have initiated short positions ahead of the long weekend due to: 1) possible concessions in the upcoming 10y and 30y JGB auctions; and 2) belief by some market participants that unconfirmed consecutive MoF interventions (with possibly short-lived effects) could make the BoJ more hawkish. Market participants may have also expected a reduction in the BoJ’s monthly JGB purchases on May 7; 5y JGBs were unchanged at 0.485% on a simple yield basis, while 30y JGB yields sold off by 1.5bp d/d to 1.98%. TONA OIS bear-steepened but outperformed JGBs in tenors beyond 5y as demand to square short positions remained strong around the 10y tenor. XCCY basis tightened, led by the belly of the curve.

USD/JPY fell sharply from 157.6 to below 154 overnight on Wednesday after Fed Chair Powell’s press conference, when liquidity was low, and data did not support the decline. Japanese Ministry of Finance officials stated they had “nothing to say on whether we intervened on yen,” but price action suggested another intervention had occurred. BoJ current account balance data suggested the MoF seemingly intervened in the market by \~JPY 3.5tr. The total estimated potential intervention amount is \~JPY 9tr this week, almost the same amount as the intervention in September-October 2022. In the Tokyo morning session on Thursday, USD/JPY recovered from the initial sell-off to 156.00, but JPY depreciation did not continue in the Tokyo afternoon session as UST yields ground lower. Concern for more intervention by the MoF also perhaps weighed on USD/JPY. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in line with USD/JPY.

## Europe:

The US dollar rallied as US yields fell after Fed Chair Powell’s press conference. JPY (+0.9%) outperformed and sent USD/JPY back below the 154 level to 153.30. This comes after potential intervention by the MoF on Wednesday but no indication of further intervention on Thursday. CHF strengthened due to an upside surprise in inflation, which drove a small repricing higher in front-end Swiss rates. EUR (+0.1%) staged a slight gain while most of the risk currencies fared significantly better; NOK and SEK outperformed. GBP lagged alongside a rally in gilts spurred by reports the BoE would end bond sales, this kept yield differentials flat.

European markets re-opened after the May 1 bank holiday, with duration well supported as 10y Bunds closed \~4bp richer at 2.54% as the curve moved in a parallel fashion and lacked the steepening in the US. Both 2s10s and 10s30s were relatively flatter than their US equivalents, with the move likely attributable to month-end flows. At the front-end, market participants added 5bp of cumulative rate cuts to December 2024, although struggled to price in a full 75bp of cuts. Peripheral spreads performed well, as BTPs outperformed; 10y BTPs tightened 1.3bp against 10y Bunds. OATs lagged the move, likely due to long-end issuance. Peripheral spreads performed well.

## Dollar Bloc:

AUD (+0.6%) and NZD (+0.4%) gained alongside CAD (+0.4%); the DXY USD Index fell by 0.4%.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies advanced against USD after Powell signaled rate hikes are highly unlikely, which outweighed weak equity sentiment. Initially, CNH strengthened as the Hang Sang (+2.50%) outperformed, but it eventually finished the AxJ session unchanged at \~7.2340. KRW outperformed with its high beta; USD/KRW fell 0.8% to 1,375.6 even amid KOSPI (-0.31%) losses. Foreign equity flows were largely stable as the second round of corporate value-up guidelines were released. PHP and IDR – currencies that have lagged since the US dollar rally in April – were relative outperformers, 0.5% stronger. USD/MYR fell 0.4% to 4.7545. USD/THB weakened 0.2% to \~36.96 even as gold prices fell 0.8%; local equity markets were largely unchanged. INR relatively underperformed in the soft US dollar environment; USD/ INR rose \~0.1% to 83.48.

AxJ rates rallied broadly with the overnight decline in US yields; China and Malaysia were the exceptions. 5y swap yields remained stable despite the Hang Seng Index outperformance. Hong Kong, Singapore, and Korea were not the main outperformers in the region despite their high beta to US rates; 2y swap yields fell just 2bp in Singapore and Korea and were flat in Hong Kong. Instead, Indonesia rates outperformed; yields fell across the curve, with 10y INDOGB yields falling 6bp. In Thailand, although 10y THAIGB was stable, 5y THB NDOIS yields fell 8bp; the 1s5s curve bull-flattened by 4bp amid media reports that the government planned to hike the minimum wage to THB400 from October. India rates rallied amid record GST collection data; 10y G-sec yields fell 2bp, while NDOIS yields fell more sharply by 6bp at the 5y tenor.

## Latin America:

After a local holiday on Wednesday, LatAm currencies caught up with the weaker US dollar. CLP (+1.5%) and BRL (+1.6%) led the EMFX complex, while COP (+0.4%) also strengthened; MXN finished flat. Rates in the region also rallied after opening following the holiday. TIIEs rallied substantially in the long-end and belly (5y: -26bp) and DIs performed similarly (January 2031s: -27bp). The IBR curve underperformed, but still rallied with gains concentrated in the front-end (2y: -7bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Minutes from the March BoJ meeting, when YCC and NIRP were removed, were released. The minutes were generally in line with the Summary of Opinions. Board members had more confidence in achieving the 2% inflation target with a well-established development of the positive feedback loop for wages and inflation. Board members agreed the degree of policy easing needs to be adjusted, with the main policy tool being the policy rate. An ongoing policy review for 20 years of monetary policy has taken into account the March decision and concluded that a smooth transition from nontraditional monetary policy is possible. For future policy normalization, a board member said it is appropriate to proceed with policy normalization “deliberately but steadily” in response to economic conditions. Some members were concerned with risk of non-linear changes in expectations of economic entities, but a member pointed out the policy changes in March made it easier for the Bank to make more flexible responses, even if the risk materialized. The policy for JGB purchases is to prevent a surge in Japanese rates, while a board member said “it was appropriate to conduct the purchases with an allowance of, for example, about plus and minus 1-2 trillion yen.” Some members also expressed the view “it was desirable for the Bank to reduce the amount of JGB purchases at some point in the future, and also reduce the amount outstanding of its JGB holdings through redemptions of JGBs”

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
