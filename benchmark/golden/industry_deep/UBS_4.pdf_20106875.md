# China Equity Strategy

# Broadly inline messages and targets from the 2024 NPC meetings

## More emphasis on policy coordination, lifting income and consumption

The objectives set out in the government work report at the NPC meetings were broadly in line with expectations with the GDP growth target set at around 5%, fiscal deficit target of 3% and a RMB1trn special government bond issuance and supportive monetary policies. Greater emphasis seemed to be placed on policy coordination, lifting private business confidence and in expanding domestic demand (consumption in particular) this year, though these topics have been discussed in previous government announcements. Overall the report conveyed a relatively supportive tone for equity markets and individual industries such as healthcare, consumer (autos and white goods in particular) and manufacturing though given the number of priorities, the equity market is likely wanting to see details on the actual emphasis and the extent of execution before acting.

## Notable differences in the NDRC report vs last year

We observed the following notable differences in this year's NDRC report vs last year's: 1) no mention of "housing is not for speculation" in this year's NDRC report, in line with recent government directives, 2) a slightly more supportive tone on income and consumption growth, with autos and white goods being singled out as areas for existing product renewals as well as improving the appeal of inbound travel though there is no mention of direct subsidy for autos and white goods, 3) for new growth engines, AI+ was introduced as a driver while other new areas mentioned included private spaceflights, new material, innovative drugs and the drone economy; 4) a much stronger focus on regulatory and legal support for private business with the word "private business" appearing close to 3 times as many in this year's report vs last year, 5) in healthcare this time, greater support for innovative drug supply chain and accelerate development for advanced medical equipment, 6) more emphasis on One Belt One Road in this year's report which outlined eight initiatives including transportation and green infrastructure building, cultural exchanges and technological innovation, and 6) a desire to promote a stable and healthy financial markets with investors being at the foundation of any rule setting.

## Implications for the broader equity market

We believe this year's government work report was broadly inline with investor expectations. While the message around the support for private businesses are certainly comforting, more details on the actual execution may be needed given it is not easy to quickly turn around business confidence and expectations. Nevertheless with a clear desire to stabilise equity markets from the current levels, broad policy support and undemanding valuation, we believe the upside for the equity market continues to outweigh the downside at the current juncture.

## Sector implications

It would seem that the key objectives set out in the NPC meeting should be positive for the manufacturing, consumer and AI related sectors while slightly negative for the banking sector which may need to lower profit margins and face more regulatory scrutiny. For healthcare, by singling out promoting innovative drugs and medical equipment advances, policy stance seemed to be slightly more supportive vs last year. ## Supportive tone overall

Consistent with the message from the Central Economic Work Conference (CEWC) held in Dec 2023, the government work report put key emphasis on stabilizing growth through boosting domestic consumption and investment with the GDP target set at c.5% and headline fiscal deficit at 3% respectively, which are both broadly in line with market expectations.

The Work Report indicated a more supportive policy setting in multiple areas including supporting employment, boosting household income and carrying out counter-cyclical measures, which should be positive for market sentiment in the near term. On fiscal policies, despite the headline budget deficit will be flat at 3%, the quota for special local government bond issuance in 2024 is slightly raised from Rmb 3.8trn to Rmb3.9trn, along with the issuance of long term special treasury bond every year starting with Rmb1trn in 2024.

## Key implications for equity market and sectors

We highlight the key implications for the equity market below:

Broadly positive message to market overall: While the key economic targets are only broadly in line with market expectations, the policy tone appears to be generally supportive. The Work Report called for better coordination in policy setting process as well as allowing more room for enterprises to adapt to changes. Greater emphasis seemed to be placed on boosting domestic demand, lifting business confidence in private sector and expanding effective investment into strategically important areas;

 Boosting domestic demand remains the top priority: As a continuation of recent government directives, boosting consumption and investment continues to be the top priority in 2024;

Ongoing effort on technological advancement and industry upgrade: The Work Report called for better cooperation between public and private sectors to strengthen the scientific innovation capabilities as well as an acceleration of industry upgrade and digitalization in manufacturing sector;

 More emphasis on private sector relative to SOEs: While SOE reform remains equally important, greater emphasis is put on improving the business environment for the private sector this time;

No major change in housing market policy: The Work Report continued to call for supporting the healthy development of property market and the transition of developers without significant stimulus measures;

Banks remain under pressure from lower interest rate and regulations: The Work Report called for lowering the borrowing cost which may present margin headwind for banks while and strengthening the regulations towards financial institutions, in particular the small and mid-sized ones which may result in some increase in compliance related costs.

## Valuation Method and Risk Statement

For various stocks across the industries we cover in the Hong Kong and mainland China stock markets, we use a variety of valuation approaches, including DCF models, Gordon growth model analysis and relative valuation analysis using various multiples such as PE, EV/EBITDA and P/BV.

We think the risks facing China's equities include a hard landing for the property market, a capital exodus associated with currency depreciation and slow structural reform progress. In our view, any government policies that do not adequately address these risks could result in a shock to the market. For example, an excess of stimulus policies could pose a risk to the transition from an investment-driven to a consumption-driven economy and increase the debt of government and state-owned enterprises.
