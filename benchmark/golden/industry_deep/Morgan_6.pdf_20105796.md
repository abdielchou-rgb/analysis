# 2024 Outlook: Improving Returns and Valuation Re-rating

Re-rating, driven by improving returns and peaking rates, is the overriding theme in 2024. Solid growth to be fueled mainly by improving/stable competition and 5G upgrade, on top of robust Industrial Internet (China), lower rates (HK), and merger efficiency gains (Taiwan). Key OWs: CT, CM, CU, HKT.

Reiterate our Attractive industry view in 2024 – improving returns and peaking policy rates to drive re-rating: The combined market cap of our Greater China Telecoms coverage gained 18% in 2023, outperforming MSCI China (-13%) by 31ppts. We remain constructive on 2024 growth outlook, underpinned by improving/stable competition, continuous 5G upgrade, robust Industrial Internet (particularly for China), and efficiency gain (particularly for Taiwan). In addition, peaking policy rates is a key valuation re-rating catalyst for telecoms globally. Our country order of preference is China > Hong Kong > Taiwan.

China: We expect Big Three operators' healthy growth to be sustained in 2024, with service revenue +6.3% and net profit +7.7%. The growth is fueled by a combination of stable competition, benign regulation, and robust market share gains in Industrial Internet (such as cloud). Although we see macro challenges arising from China's '3D' problems (deflation, demographics and debt), we believe the aforementioned factors should alleviate the pressures and sustain growth resiliency. In addition, as we are at the end of peak 5G investment cycle, improving cash flows should continue to enhance shareholder returns. Our order of preference is CT-H > CM-H > CU-H > CTC > CT-A > CM-A > CCS > CU-A.

Hong Kong: Competition is improving in this space. As the main broadband pricing disrupter shifts its focus from subscriber to ARPU, we expect the industry to enjoy a steadier ARPU growth up-cycle; while mobile pricing competition has also been stable. Despite improving fundamentals, HK telco stocks de-rated meaningfully in 2023, due to US rate hikes. At current valuations of 7-12% 2023e dividend yield (300-800bps spread), we think declining US rates should drive meaningful rerating. Our order of preference is HKT > PCCW > SMT > HTHKH > HKBN.

Taiwan: The industry consolidation's benefit is already being seen in Big Three's mobile service revenue and ARPU growth reacceleration in 2023, and we expect the momentum to be sustained in 2024, with a healthier competition landscape and 5G penetration upside (\~40% currently). FET and TWM should benefit from merger cost synergies, but these are not likely to be pivotal to make dividends more attractive in the near term. Despite strong fundamentals, we rank Taiwan least preferred within Greater China, due to rich valuations (\~4% 2023e dividend yield). Our order of preference is FET > TWM > CHT.

# China: Growth Outlook In A China 3D Framework

## Industry view

Analyzing telcos' growth in a China 3D framework: Our macro team launched the China's 3D Journey series in 2023, identifying '3D' – deflation, demographics and debt – as the three problems affecting the country's long-term growth. For telcos, we analyze each of the three factors' impacts from two perspective – the impacts on traditional telecom services (mobile, broadband, etc.) and Industrial Internet service (cloud, IDC, cybersecurity, big data, etc.).

## Deflation:

• For traditional telecom services, industry ARPU (mobile, broadband, fixed-line) has been more driven by factors such as competition, technology (e.g., 5G upgrade), and regulation (e.g., "speed upgrade, lower tariff") in China. While we acknowledge that deflation is indeed a macro headwind for telcos' pricing power, we believe improvement in the aforementioned three factors could help alleviate the pressure – competition has remained benign over the past years, 5G continues to drive usage growth, and the regulatory environment has also been stable.

• For Industrial Internet service, a deflationary macro backdrop results in enterprises' lower confidence on IT spending, as it is often discretionary long-term capex (e.g., cloud migration). From enterprise customers' perspective, this in turn suggests lower expected returns (ROI) and higher uncertainty in enterprises' investment.

## Demographics:

• For traditional telecom services, slower population and household growth will result in slower mobile and broadband subscriber growth. Thus, we have factored in a weaker population growth outlook. Mobile subscriber growth, for example, will decelerate to 1.5-2% p.a. in 2024e and onwards, vs. a 3% CAGR in 2022-23e.

• For Industrial Internet services, we do not see a direct impact from weaker demographics.

## Debt:

• For traditional telecom services, we do not see a direct impact from downstream's debt burden.

• For Industrial Internet services, we think downstream's debt concern, particularly that of local government, could lead to weaker overall IT spending, which was already seen in 2023 to some extent. Governments and other public sector areas (such as schools and hospitals) accounted for \~20% of total IT spending in China in the past few years, according to IDC.

Despite deflationary and debt macro pressures, we remain optimistic on telcos' Industrial Internet outlook. We believe telcos are better positioned than peers and will continue to gain market share in key Industrial Internet segments (such as public cloud, see Exhibit 12 ), thanks to their proprietary advantages: 1) comprehensive business

footprint, from network operations to full-suite solutions; 2) SOE status, which suggests easier access to government and SOE enterprise budgets (roughly one-third of enterprise IT spending and public cloud market, we estimate); 3) significant internal and external investments to drive long-term growth; and 4) large user base, with rich data.

All in, we forecast steady industry service revenue growth of 6.3% and net profit growth of 7.7% in 2024, slightly moderating from previous years' but remaining solid. Service revenue growth is fueled by steady traditional service revenue growth of \~3%, and still robust Industrial Internet revenue growth of 15-25%. Capex ratio (as % of service revenue) should continue to improve, as we are at the end of peak 5G investment cycle, albeit partially offset by investments into areas such as data center/cloud. These should drive improving FCF and shareholder returns.

## Stock implications

Stay OW on all three HK-listed operators; prefer CT-H: Our positive view reflects solid earnings growth even amid macro uncertainties, declining capex, and enhanced shareholder returns. The stocks trade at attractive 2023e P/Es of 7-10x and dividend yields of \~8%. We prefer CT-H, given the best combination of growth potential, visibility, and dividend yield. Stay EW on CT-A, CM-A; and UW on CU-A, given full/stretched valuation.

Stay EW on CTC and CCS, given near-term growth pressures and less attractive valuation compared with operators.

## Hong Kong: Improving Competition

## Industry view

Improving broadband competition is the major focus for 2024: The main pricing disrupter, HKBN, shifted its focus from subscriber market share gains to a steady ARPU recovery in 2H23. Both HKBN and HKT have raised prices of new contracts in certain segments, based on our checks. We think this shift is a major positive for the industry as a whole, after the fierce competition pressure that has weighed on this market for the past decade. We expect a steadier broadband ARPU growth cycle ahead ( Exhibit 16 ), yet we remain mindful of any disruption the smaller players might bring. For example, HGC has rolled out targeted promotions to attract existing HKBN customers, which could lead to higher churn rate at HKBN. For mobile, pricing competition saw sequential improvement in 1H23, and remained largely stable in 2H23. We expect competition to remain stable in the near term as operators focus more on upgrading users to 5G (penetration remains low at \~25-30% in HK), and based on our channel checks. However, we are cautious of any sign of heightened competition in this crowded market.

US policy rate cuts should drive a re-rating: HK telco stocks de-rated meaningfully in 2023, due to weak sentiment in a higher-for-longer interest rate environment, although key players' fundamental earnings and dividend growth remained healthy. At current valuation levels – 7-12% 2023e dividend yield (300-800bps spread vs. 10yr US Treasury yield) – we think interest rate concerns ares mostly priced in. Looking ahead, greater visibility on US interest rate cuts will be a key re-rating driver for the stocks, in our view.

## Stock implications

Prefer HKT amongst HK telcos (OW, price target HK\$10.5): HKT is our only OW amongst HK telcos. It offers the best combination of resilient earnings growth (amid competition and interest rate risks), earnings visibility, and attractive valuation (8-9% 2023e dividend yield, with \~4% 2022-25e DPS CAGR). (Read more – HKT Trust and HKT Ltd.: Re-rating Catalyst Has Arrived; OW.)

HKBN (EW, price target HK\$3.5): At an 11-12% F23 dividend yield, we find valuation insufficiently attractive, given its challenging and uncertain growth outlook, despite the strategy is pointing at a right direction. We also see a balanced risk-reward – while the high yield and maintained dividend offer downside support; but the chance of our bull case (stronger and speedier execution across business lines) playing out in the next 12 months is limited. (Read more – HKBN Ltd: Dividend Kept, What's Next?)

PCCW (EW, price target HK\$4.0): At \~12% NAV discount to HKT (vs. historical avg. of \~25%), we find the reward is not attractive enough to bear the risk of a <100% dividend pass-through, given its potential FCF pressures. (Read more – PCCW Ltd: Thoughts on its FCF Pressures.)

SMT (EW, price target HK\$4.7), HTHKH (EW, price target HK\$1.2): Both stocks have less resilient fundamentals compared with industry leader HKT, yet they are trading at a premium 2023e dividend yield of 7-8%, compared to market leader HKT.

# Taiwan: Solid Fundamentals Amid Consolidation, But Rich Valuation

## Industry view

Merger benefit is already apparent for the industry as a whole: Taiwan's five-player mobile market has been highly competitive in the past decade. However, with the ongoing 5-to-3 consolidation, we see meaningful improvement in the overall competition landscape, with more balanced market shares of \~40%/30%/30% for CHT/FET/TWM respectively after the merger. In 2023, the Big Three already delivered meaningful growth reacceleration in both mobile service revenue (4-7% yoy) and postpaid ARPU (3-5% yoy). We expect this healthy trend to continue in 2024. In addition, we see ample 5G penetration upside from \~40% currently, driven by more diversified 5G pricing plans (30- 50% 5G ARPU uplift currently), smartphone upgrade cycle, and more use cases.

We see cost synergy upside from the merger for FET and TWM, but not pivotal to make dividends more attractive: We forecast 5-10% total cost savings for the two combined entities, mostly from network consolidation. In 2024 (year 1), we estimate both mergers to be 15-20% EBITDA and \~10% net profit accretive, but with limited EPS/DPS impact, due to dilution; more synergies will be realized in future years.

## Stock implications

Despite strong fundamentals, we still rank Taiwan least preferred within Greater China, due to rich valuations: Taiwan's mobile market outperformed many other regional peers in 2023, and we expect the momentum to be sustained. However, rich valuations remain our key concern for Taiwan telcos – \~4% 2023e dividend yield vs \~8% for China and 7-12% for HK.

We prefer FET (EW)>TWM (EW)>CHT (UW): The stocks are trading at similar dividend yields. We rank CHT least preferred, given the lack of merger synergy upside. We prefer FET over TWM, despite recent outperformance, given FET's smoother network integration (FET and APT already co-share network), stronger earnings growth, greater enterprise solutions upside in the long term, and TWM's weak momo performance in the near term.
