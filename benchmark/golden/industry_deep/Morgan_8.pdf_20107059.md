# March 15

Bearish UST trend persists, curve twist flattens; USD/JPY reclaims 149, despite Rengo results; NZD lags on gloomy economic outlook; Bunds bear-flatten; Brazil DIs underperform peers; PBoC maintains policy rates, announces net liquidity withdrawal; DXY at 103.45 (+0.1%); US 10y at 4.306% (+1.6bp).

• USTs twist flatten as firm import price data allows for a continuation of the bearish trend; market pricing now implies less than 75bp of Fed rate cuts this year.

• USD/JPY (+0.5%) returns above 149 despite preliminary results of Rengo spring wage negotiations, which were stronger-than-expected, with a March BoJ hike already in the price.

• NZD (-0.8%) exhibits the sharpest weakness against the US dollar as New Zealand Finance Minister Willis claims official growth forecasts will paint a gloomy picture of the economy.

• European duration trends sideways, Bunds bear-flatten in-sympathy with front-end UST losses.

• January 2033 Brazil DIs sell-off 13bp after more strong data follows the retail sales beat on Thursday.

• PBoC maintains its key policy rates and announces a net withdrawal of liquidity from the financial system, this signals the benchmark lending rates may remain unchanged this month.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

The extent to which market participants had already positioned for strong preliminary Rengo spring wage negotiations results and subsequent action by the BoJ at next week’s policy meeting was readily apparent. USD/JPY (+0.5%) returned above 149 despite announced wage increases of more than 5% for the coming fiscal year. The US dollar remained supported in the NY session alongside a continued sell-off in equities (S&P 500: -0.65%) and a twist flattening of the Treasury curve. A continuation of this week’s bearish trend in Treasuries came alongside firm import price data, which proved to be a catalyst to further reduce implied Fed rate cuts this year to \~71bp.

The effect of this week’s stronger-than-expected inflation data on the calculus of the FOMC at next week’s meeting was a durable theme throughout the week. Discussion among market participants oriented around whether the Fed’s dot plot would still show three rate cuts this year, or perhaps one fewer (given a low bar). As the number of Fed rate cuts in 2024 receded to the lowest since October, 2y UST yields rose 25bp over the course of the week to a year-to-date high of 4.73%.

## Japan:

JGBs remained under steepening pressure, as media reports seemingly have shown increased conviction for a March BoJ lift-off. Front-end JGBs traded resiliently, as market participants almost fully priced in a March lift-off. Long-end JGBs continued to lag, in the absence of specific buyers, but this sector eventually found support into the 3:00pm Tokyo close. There may have been demand to restore duration exposure from domestic market participants now the March lift-off has seemingly become almost a done deal. 2y JGBs rallied by 0.5bp d/d to 0.185% while 30y JGBs sold off by 1.5bp d/d to 1.850%. Rengo released preliminary results of spring wage negotiations, which were stronger-than-expected. But JGB futures rallied further from the 3:00pm Tokyo close as market participants seemingly “bought the fact.”

TONA OIS twist-steepened alongside JGBs but continued to outperform JGBs. Strong receiving pressure in the front-end was likely demand to square short positions, as market participants seemingly do not believe there will be an aggressive rate hike cycle in the near future. XCCY basis tightened, led by the longend.

Strong US PPI data drove broad USD strength overnight on Thursday and USD/JPY climbed above 148. During Tokyo trading hours on Friday, USD/JPY had traded \~148.50. Market participants awaited the preliminary results of the Rengo spring wage negotiations, which were above 5% for the first time in 33 years. A media report suggested the BoJ will likely remove NIRP at its March meeting. Initially, JPY responded with broad strength, but this quickly retraced and turned into broad JPY weakness. Price action seemingly suggested “buy the fact” for USD/JPY, as consensus expects a March BoJ hike is almost a done deal. USD/JPY had ground higher toward 149.00 while CAD/JPY rose above 110.

## Europe:

NOK (-0.7%) and SEK (-0.4%) depreciated alongside equity weakness, which sent NOK/SEK (-0.3%) lower despite local backdrops that arguably favor NOK strength over SEK. GBP (-0.2%) weakened given a surprising front-end gilt sell-off, given a moderation in inflation expectations, that counterbalanced equity weakness. EUR and CHF were unchanged for the session.

European duration traded sideways on Friday after an open lower amidst higherthan-expected final February French CPI data. A sell-off in the US, led by the frontend, weighed on European duration. 10y Bunds sold off by 2bp d/d and finished the week at 2.44%; 2s10s flattened by 1.5bp in Germany while 10s30s edged lower by 1bp. Core ASW richened slightly, led by Bobl (+0.5bp), for the second consecutive session. This occurred alongside renewed weakness in equities. The 10y BTP-Bund spread tightened as much as 5bp in the London morning but later widened and closed 2bp tighter at 126bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

It was an uneventful session for the US dollar amid low volumes, but the DXY USD Index (+0.1%) inched higher as market-implied Fed rate cuts were reduced. USD/JPY (+0.5%) pushed past 149, despite strong results of wage negotiations in Japan.

NZD (-0.8%) exhibited the sharpest weakest against the US dollar as New Zealand Finance Minister Willis claimed official growth forecasts will paint a gloomy picture of the economy. AUD (-0.3%) was also weaker, weighed down by lower iron ore prices and a sell-off in equities. CAD (-0.1%) was largely unchanged, unable to fully shake-off the equity sell-off.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies slipped after US PPI data trimmed market-implied Fed rate cuts. KRW underperformed, given its high beta, and depreciated for the fourth consecutive session; USD/KRW rose 0.9% to 1,330. CNH remained stable in the AxJ morning session at 7.20, supported by a low fixing by the PBoC. THB underperformed alongside losses in local equity markets; USD/THB rose 0.4% to 35.78, despite the uptick in gold prices. IDR weakened as markets incorporated Indonesia trade balance data; USD/IDR rose 0.1% while a fall in local equities was accompanied by a rally in INDOGB yields. MYR weakened 0.5% to 4.71. TWD and PHP relatively underperformed, INR was little changed, and SGD as well as HKD remained relatively stable.

AxJ rates sold off for the third consecutive session after the rise in UST yields overnight on Thursday. South Korea bonds underperformed as rates sold off across the curve; 10y yields rose 6.3bp and 2s10s bear-steepened. In China, long-end bonds gained while 5y yields fell by 1bp; the NDIRS curve remained unchanged. In Singapore, rates continued to sell-off with 10y yields at 3.07% (+5.1bp). In Hong Kong, 2s5s shifted 2.2bp higher and 5y yields rose 5.6bp. Thailand and Malaysia rates underperformed while India G-secs also weaker, 10y yields rose 1.7bp.

## Latin America:

It was a quiet session for LatAm currencies. MXN (-0.03%) and BRL (-0.2%) were mostly unchanged while CLP (-0.3%) underperformed peers after initial gains of 0.5%. COP (+0.3%) gained with few local drivers. Rates ended largely higher as DIs sold off (January 2033s: +13bp) after more strong data, following Thursday's retail sales beat. TIIEs sold off in the belly (5y: +7bp) while the IBR curve rallied following lower-than-expected retail sales data and the front-end led the way (1y: -6bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Riksbank Deputy Governor Bunge claimed the risk of high inflation becoming entrenched had decreased and said inflation is moving in the right direction, which provides greater confidence in achieving price stability

• Riksbank appointed Anna Seim as new deputy governor, succeeding Martin Floden. The first monetary policy meeting in which she will take part in on June 26

• In the BoE’s Inflation Attitudes Survey near-and medium-term inflation expectations continued to moderate. The median expectation of inflation over the next 12 months fell to 3.0% in February (November: 3.3%). In the 12 months following, expectations were 2.8% (November: 2.8%), and longerterm expectations fell to 3.1% (November: 3.2%)

• ECB Governing Council Member Rehn echoed peers, who have suggested a possible first cut in the summer, and said the ECB “will be able to gradually start taking its foot off the monetary-policy brake when the summer nears” in a context where inflation is expected to near 2% in 2024 and 2025

• ECB Governing Council Member Makhlouf argued “the picture should be sufficiently clearer when the Governing Council meets in June (as we will have a lot more information – particularly on wage dynamics – available in our deliberations) to give us sufficient confidence to make monetary less restrictive.” However, he added, “I remain open-minded as to when any reduction in our policy rates should take place,” which underscored the ECB’s data-dependent approach

## Emerging Markets

• The PBoC maintained its key policy rates and announced a net withdrawal of liquidity from the financial system. The one-year MLF rate was maintained at 2.5% and the central bank injected 387bn yuan (\$53.80bn) of liquidity. This suggests the benchmark lending rates may remain unchanged this month after the reduction in the five-year loan prime rate last month

• The BSP stated the economic slowdown in the second half of the year is a result of the central bank’s most vigorous tightening to reduce inflation in the last 20 years. According to the central bank, despite global challenges and tighter financial conditions, the Philippines economy is expected to expand in 2025

• The Reserve Bank of India purchased 8.7 tons of gold in January, the largest in nearly two years. The plan is to diversify foreign exchange reserves and protect against currency risk, which includes the dollar’s standing as the world’s reserve currency for settlement, given geopolitical concerns

• BanRep Board Member Taboada said recent data shows the policy rate must be cut more aggressively than the current 25bp pace. She stressed the board is still very attentive to inflation pressures, but activity data, particularly private investment, has been weak. She continued to stress optionality, and noted they should be open to a pause in rate cuts if needed. This comes as markets have priced in an acceleration to a 50bp cut in their March meeting (-51bp)

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
