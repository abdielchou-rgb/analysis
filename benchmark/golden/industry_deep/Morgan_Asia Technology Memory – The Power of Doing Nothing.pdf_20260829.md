## The Power of Doing Nothing

Memory investing is simple… In a cycle upturn, margins go up and stocks rise in value, companies start making outsized profits and investors like the returns they get in theses stocks. What matters now is if we think in the middle of this year, memory earnings will be higher next year? The one factor that remains constant is that to derive these positive returns, you have to be invested. Unfortunately, that involves enduring discomfort or volatility that is part of memory and staying invested through periods when markets go up and down. Why? Because no one can accurately and consistently time the market. Although we know memory stocks have always outperformed in a cyclical upturn, the price action is never linear and constantly punctuated by turbulence, rallies and dips, as seen in Exhibit 1.

… but the human negativity bias gets in the way. The way the human brain is mentally wired is to think that reaching a 52-week high or historical peak in a share price equals some imminent end to the stock rally. So, we are making a new high now with memory stocks driven by AI, and investors may think it has to turn… but it does not have to turn. Human beings are always searching for patterns, and often we don’t even know that we are doing it… like chasing the laggard thinking it's an opportunity that the market and everyone else has missed and trying to time that trade. But those patterns don’t usually exist, and the 1-year historical performance after markets make new highs is up 14% on average (using the US S&P 500 as an example).

It's not whether you are right or wrong that is important, but how much money you make. We make some of our worst decisions by paying too much attention to opinions about the near-term direction of memory where many of the variables are either unimportant or unknowable when it comes to DRAM investments. If we look back on the memory investment journey to date, and the various hot tips/noise received – either via the media reporting the latest market craze (HBM qualification obsession, concerns on early utilization ramp up, weak demand, oversupply, etc.), or something as simple as

DRAM spot prices falling near-term – how many times did we act and sell into these hot tips? How many times did it pay off? With the benefit of hindsight, are we happy to have timed those events?

Time in the market vs. timing the market. As memory cycle trends always promise to be volatile and create uncertainty – as we have seen in the last year – opinions become louder, the pressure in the market mounts and the headlines start jumping out at us, encouraging us to try and time the market (switch, sell, buy that dip). When persistent narratives are so visible, it becomes difficult for investors to focus on anything else, and to ignore these narratives. We become gripped with the things we don’t know and it becomes increasingly tempting to take profit on our current investment portfolio. But avoid making big changes to it and remember that in a DRAM upturn, “To do nothing is often the best course of action...” (The Crown by Peter Morgan).

## Channel Checks

• 2Q DRAM price outlook may be revised up to double digits, according to initial negotiation feedback: Suppliers have been asking for another 15-20% QoQ price hike in 2Q24 vs. the current consensus view of 3-8%, indicating their firm stance to maintain the upward pricing trend through tight supply control. While limited visibility into demand recovery may hold buyers back from procuring large volumes, industry participants' expectations are moving higher and we estimate high single digit to double digit price hikes are likely now.

• 2Q NAND contract price hike significantly higher at +10-15%: NAND contract transaction prices are soaring +10-15% in 2Q24 following March's +15% MoM price resurgence in wafer prices, and an improving demand environment from a large Chinese customer and enterprise SSD.

• Utilization gradually increasing across most producers as industry digested inventories and shortages build into year-end: We estimate Samsung currently operates at around 80% production for DRAM and 60% for NAND, and aims to ramp up to 90% and 70%, respectively, into 2Q. Hynix operated at a slightly higher 90% for DRAM and 70% for NAND during 1Q, in our view. We expect its DRAM production to gradually ramp up while keeping NAND under-utilized until margin stabilizes and visibility into a demand recovery is better.

## Earnings

Don't take somebody’s estimates as gospel but trust market trends. In the last 12 months, the analysts who follow memory companies have had to raise their earnings projections for the year by nearly 98% on average. In essence, the “E” estimate for these companies has consistently been way too low and typical in a cycle upturn as analysts move from skepticism to optimism and later overshoot on euphoria. Hence, the P/Bs and P/Es have looked higher than reality. This is a reminder that stock prices embed future expectations, and if those expectations are raised, stocks tend to go up. If expectations are lowered, stocks usually go down. That is why memory stocks have surprised so many this year.

It's not magic. On the argument of whether or not there is some sort of artificial reason for the higher rise of Hynix vs. Samsung, it turns out earnings growth explains most of it. In the last 12 months, Hynix stock is up 103%. Guess what else is up about 100%? Hynix’s earnings estimates (up 108%). How about Samsung? Well, over the last 12 months its earnings are up just 36% and the stock price up 32%. Speaking of earnings, be cautious when someone declares a cycle could stall based on a singular methodology, such as quarterly contract price forecasts. The ”E” is an estimate of future earnings. Yet, whose estimate is it? Does that 'expert' have a track record of predicting the “E” accurately?

## Stock Preference

When you have a secular story like AI, one should not get overly caught up in valuation sensitivities and past peak multiples fears. If the amount of dollars and capex allocated to AI continues to increase, the share that memory will be able to capture will also likely continue to grow, leading P/B ratios well beyond past peaks. So in our view there is a multiple expansion story on top of an earnings story from a cycle recovery. We also have to be mindful that nothing goes straight up, so we can expect many tactical pull backs along the way – but we would certainly regard those as dips to buy instead of a top.

We counter the view held by some investors that memory stocks have risen so much that they have become unattractive – in fact, many are no higher than they were at the end of 2021. So although they certainly rose significantly in 2023, they were hit hard the year

before. In our view, this suggests that these stocks have more fuel in the tank for 2024 ( Exhibit 6 ).

## Least preferred (UW) – Nanya Tech, Macronix

We expect Hynix to re-rate further as growth and margins outperform peers and exceed consensus expectations. We value the commodity memory business at W120,000 per share and HBM W110,000 for W230,000 PT. It's not just the AI story either as there is also the cyclical argument of where the numbers will likely go up and P/B may fall meaningfully. Waiting for the right valuation leaves people behind in every cycle and the market does not usually give them the valuation they need to get back in. Valuation is where supply meets demand and not a moment pre-ordained by history. We have to re-price future cash flows to account for this newly created HBM TAM; it gives duration to the earnings and ultimately results in betterthan-expected growth. Stay OW. ## SK hynix: What's Changed?

Our channel checks indicated better-than-expected 2Q pricing given suppliers' firm stand on pricing hikes. Hence, we revise up QoQ pricing assumptions from 7% to 8% for DRAM and from 10% to 13% for NAND in 2Q24. Taking the 20% price premium of HBM3e into considerations, we also revise up 2H24 HBM ASP from US\$1.4 to US\$1.5 per Gb.

As a result, our 2024E-2025E EPS have been increased by 4% and 11% to W19,305 and W23,555, respectively. Our 2024e EPS is significantly higher than consensus of \~W13,000, as we are more bullish on the magnitude and duration of memory pricing hikes. Current consensus data implies DRAM pricing will only go up by 45% YoY and NAND 17% YoY vs. DRAM 60% YoY and NAND 64% YoY in our latest estimates.

Our residual income based Price Target is revised up by 9.5% to W230,000, implying 35% upside. Stay OW.
