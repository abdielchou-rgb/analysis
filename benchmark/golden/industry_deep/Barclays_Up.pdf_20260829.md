# March MPM: Excessive monetary easing finally comes to an end – expanded version

Naohiko Baba, BSJL, Japan | Ryuichiro Hashimoto, BSJL, Japan

Excerpted from Japan Economic Focus: March MPM: Excessive monetary easing finally comes to an end - expanded version, published on March 19, 2024

The BoJ exited both NIRP and YCC, terminated ETF/J-REIT purchases, scrapped its inflation-overshooting commitment and rewrote its forward guidance, in line with our expectations. The NIRP exit should have little impact on the economy and our base case remains a further rate hike to 0.25% in July.

This supplements our earlier report (March MPM: Excessive monetary easing finally comes to an end, 19 March) to account for the post-MPM press conference.

• The BoJ wrapped up its 18-19 March monetary policy meeting (MPM) with an overhaul of its large-scale easing scheme, including an exit of its negative interest rate policy (NIRP) and yield curve controls (YCC), concluding that these programs have fulfilled their roles. The policy changes largely matched our expectations. In terms of NIRP, the BoJ shifted from three tiers back to its previous single-tier structure for current account deposits and lifted the interest on excess reserves (IOER) to +0.1%. For the policy rate, it shifted back to the uncollateralized overnight call rate with the range rising by 0.1pp to 0.0-0.1%.

• YCC was dissolved with a return to JGB buying under regular outright purchases (rinban operations). The “around JPY6trn” target for monthly purchases appears intentionally vague (implying scope for stealth reductions) and the upper bound of ofer sizes were revised sharply downward, especially in the long/superlong sectors, suggesting the BoJ is looking to gradually reduce it JGB buying, especially in these sectors. Fixed-rate buying operations and funds-supplying operations against pooled collateral were retained as a backstop in order to avoid an abrupt spike in long-term yields after the scrapping of YCC.

• New ETF and J-REIT purchases also came to an end. However, we believe the BoJ will continue to hold its existing stock and use related dividend income to defray the costs of normalization.

• The inflation-overshooting commitment linked to the monetary base and the forward guidance related to YCC were both scrapped and the new guidance noted that “given the current outlook for economic activity and prices, the Bank anticipates that accommodative financial conditions will be maintained for the time being.”

• The historically large hikes decided at the annual “shunto” wage negotiations (initial JTUC-Rengo tally) were the most decisive factor behind the latest policy revisions. We expect an average hike of 4.6% in the final tally, the largest in around 30 years, likely leading to positive y/y growth in real wages around June. We expect this to bring about an improvement in recently sluggish consumption.

• Any impact on the economy from the NIRP exit should be quite limited. Instead, we believe the BoJ should be focused primarily on the smooth functioning of the new current account deposit system and JGB buying scheme. In this sense, it should not take long to gauge the efects of the NIRP exit. Also, we believe the new forward guidance could be interpreted as paving the way for adjustments to accommodative financial conditions if there is a change in the outlook for economic activity and prices. Despite great uncertainty, we continue to expect an additional rate hike to 0.25% at the July MPM as our base case.

• In his post-MPM press conference, BoJ Governor Ueda remarked that the extraordinary easing policies had fulfilled their purposes and that the BoJ would now return to conventional monetary policy that adjusts “short-term rates in accordance with the economic/price outlook, like that of central banks in the US and Europe.” In addition, he noted that real interest rates will remain quite negative even after exiting NIRP and that there is scope to adjust the degree of accommodation depending on the economic/price outlook. Based on Ueda’s remarks, the upcoming checkpoints will likely be: 1) the extent to which wage hikes/personnel costs are passed through to sales prices, including at small companies; 2) developments in service prices and inflation expectations; and 3) the extent to which domestic demand in areas such as consumption recovers. We expect the BoJ to check these points at the April and July MPMs, when it will also release quarterly Outlook Reports, and judge whether it is suitable to raise rates any further. At the same time, we expect JGB purchases to be adjusted gradually in accordance with market conditions, separately from the economic/price outlook.

• Our next focus will be the 27 March speech by Policy Board member Naoki Tamura, widely viewed as a hawk.

## Profit margins can stay high for longer

Emmanuel Cau, CFA, Barclays, UK | Matthew Joyce, CFA, BBI, Paris | Magesh Kumar Chandrasekaran, CFA, Barclays, UK | Arihanth Bohra Jain, Barclays, UK

Excerpted from European Equity Strategy: Profit margins can stay high for longer, published on March 20, 2024

EPS revisions are stabilizing and '24 IBES estimates of 3% growth in Europe look more sensible post ytd cuts. With nominal GDP growth at trend and cost pressures easing, profit margins should be resilient. Even if upside from current high levels is limited, PMI recovery may help margins for short cycle plays like Chemicals.

Q4 earnings overall reassuring. The reporting season was a mixed bag, but in aggregate better than expected, albeit more in US than Europe. Ex-Energy EPS growth was positive in the two regions and higher than in Q3. Company commentaries showed a more positive view on the economy, improved guidance outlook, a material pick-up in share buyback/dividend announcements and increased capex intentions. Positive share price reaction suggests expectations were low and investors are willing to give earnings the benefit of the doubt.

FY24 earnings recovery on track amid resilient margins. ## Buybacks Pick Up Into Year-End

Venu Krishna, CFA, BCI, US | Japinder Chawla, CFA, BCI, US | Rex Feng, BCI, US | Madhuresh Rai, CFA, BCI, US | Young Chen, BCI, US

Excerpted from U.S. Equity Strategy: Buybacks Pick Up Into Year-End, published on March 20, 2024

4Q23 earnings supported strong cash generation, helping to ofset lower net debt & equity issuance. Companies drew down cash reserves in 4Q23 for the first time in roughly a year, funding a small number of large M&A transactions but also incremental buybacks and capex.

In 4Q23, earnings continued to support cash flow growth. Last quarter, cash flow from operations (CFO) for SPX ex-Financials grew at +7.3% y/y, modestly trailing EPS growth of +7.7%. As was the case with earnings, Big Tech is driving most of the upside to cash flow; without Big Tech, CFO for the rest of the Tech sector contracted on a y/y basis, and CFO for the rest of the S&P 500 grew +LSD. Strong CFO was ofset by muted net debt and equity issuance as sources of cash.

Companies drew down cash reserves last quarter for the first time since 2022. The most notable line item for cash deployment in 4Q23 was M&A, which was up strongly on both a q/q and y/y basis. However, the data are being skewed by three very large transactions in Tech and Pharma that closed in 4Q23. As a result, we think deal activity last quarter does not necessarily indicate a general pickup in M&A spending. Cash also went to a year-end pickup in buybacks and a modest y/y increase in capex.

Buybacks picked up toward the end of 2023. Aggregate buybacks were down \~15% y/y in 2023 but the fourth quarter made up \~30% of the total, indicating a slight pickup in repurchase to close out the year. For the first three quarters of 2023, buybacks were down from 2022's record pace amid global growth concerns and record high valuations. The year-end uptick amid resilient US macro and closer-to-fair multiples could signal more incremental repurchase activity in 2024.

Capex continues to expand at a measured pace. Interestingly, while CFO growth in 2023 was largely driven by Tech, the increase in net capex last year came from other sectors. Tech sector capex increased dramatically from 2019-2022, and it appears that the group has since moved to curtail capital spending while other sectors catch up.

## Party like it's 1995

Emmanuel Cau, CFA, Barclays, UK | Matthew Joyce, CFA, BBI, Paris | Magesh Kumar Chandrasekaran, CFA, Barclays, UK | Arihanth Bohra Jain, Barclays, UK

Excerpted from Equity Market Review - Party like it's 1995, published on March 22, 2024

Dovish central banks are further improving the growth/policy trade-of. Mid-cycle rate cuts amid a resilient economic activity is a positive environment for risk assets. Although still soft PMIs in Europe keep the short cycle trade in check, policy easing should help a broadening of the rally. Small caps may benefit.

Party like it's 1995. It was a busy week for central banks, with the BOJ, Fed, SNB and BOE all delivering policy. The Fed dot plot maintaining three expected cuts this year, despite upgraded growth and inflation forecasts, and was somewhat a welcome surprise to us. As we wrote last week, a more hawkish talk/rates projection from the Fed could have created a dent in the equity rally (see Equity Market Review - No cuts yet, but still a lot of liquidity, 15 Mar). Although its median dots show a shallower trajectory of the funds rate than in December, the Fed communication clearly suggests it wants to cut rates, which tilts the risk-reward positively for risk markets, in our view (i.e. Fed put). Meanwhile, the SNB's unexpected cut, the first of any major western CB since inflation kicked of post Covid, and a more dovish BOE rhetoric also confirmed a global central banks' easing cycle is on its way. And despite the BOJ's hike into positive territory, the messaging around it was also dovish. Markets were relieved as a result, with renewed Yen depreciation and positive Japanese stock performance. History shows that when central banks' cuts come without a recession ensuing, like in 1995, it typically puts us into a 'mid-cycle' situation: the economic cycle tends to get extended as growth is stimulated by the cuts. Past instances of mid-cycle cuts have seen both activity pick up and risk assets rally, so bullish market reaction this week makes sense to us. Arguably, a lot of good news must be priced in now, but we think the path of least resistance remains to the upside. Pre-emptive rate cuts raise the probability of a soft landing, and this may prompt more cash deployment into risk assets (see Reflation fizz – March Chart Pack, 6 Mar).

A dovish Fed fueled risk-on sentiment this week, lifting equities to all-time highs and driving a further tightening in credit spreads. Technicals remain strong, with low vol and healthy demand. Fed skews dovish and bolsters the "no landing" narrative. Despite some firming in inflation in recent months, the dot plot maintained the three cuts December's SEP had penciled in for 2024. The S&P closed at an all-time high on Wednesday (the 19th time it has done so this year), and the Nasdaq and Russell closed 1.2% and 1.9% higher, respectively. In credit, CDX.IG (S41) rallied tighter, trading inside of 46bp as of midday on Thursday, while CDX.HY gained 40c on Wednesday to close at \$107.23.

High yield spreads closed Tuesday inside of 300bp for the first time since January 2022. The HY index excluding CCCs closed Wednesday at 226bp, only 2bp wide of the post-GFC/pre-COVID tights of 224bp reached in January 2020.

Complacency continues to creep up. Our Complacency Signal spiked five more points last week, to 86%, its highest reading since January 2022. Implied vols across asset classes remain very low, with the MOVE Index falling below 93pts as of midday Thursday, its lowest level since March 2022. Similarly, implied vols across LQD and HYG have dropped to their lowest since 1Q22.

Still, technicals remain robust, and credit fundamentals are not a significant headwind (see last week's investment grade section and this week's high yield section). Despite the compression in spreads, the marginal investor in US credit remains yield-driven, and the all-in yield ofering across IG and HY continues to appear attractive at 5.36%/7.72%. Chair Powell's commentary that the "policy rate is likely at its peak for this time in the cycle" provides confidence that total returns will be strong over the near to medium term. High subscription rates and low new issue concessions in the IG primary market underscore the strong demand backdrop, and investors continue to have ample cash to deploy. On the foreign investor front, US credit received tailwinds from a less-hawkish-than-expected BOJ decision, which led JGBs to rally.

Inflation risks continue to lurk. Inflation measures such as the Atlanta Fed's "sticky" price index and the prices paid components of the ISM PMIs suggest that underlying inflation remains stubbornly elevated. Furthermore, the S&P flash PMI report released on Wednesday shows that "rates of output price inflation accelerated sharply across both manufacturing and services, quickening to 13- and 8-month highs." Taken in conjunction with the Fed's increased projections for core PCE inflation, risks remain skewed to the upside that higher inflation (Figure 8) will lead to fewer cuts than the three Barclays Economics has in its 2024 forecast.

So what looks cheap? In investment grade, we think front-end spreads look cheap versus the index. The 3y point appears most attractive, especially in BBBs (see this week's IG section). In high yield, CCCs continue to screen very cheap versus BBs/Bs, and we think there are opportunities in portions of that ratings bucket (see Opportunities in CCCs). Loan yields still screen as attractive versus high yield on a spot (200bp+) and a curve-adjusted basis (40bp+), as we discussed earlier this month in Take comfort in carry.

## Economic Outlook

## Optimism overflow

Christian Keller, Barclays, UK | Mark Cus Babic, Barclays, UK | Akash Utsav, Barclays, UK

Excerpted from Global Economics Weekly, published on March 22, 2024

Central banks fueled a market rally as they embraced optimism about inflation without sacrificing growth. While PMIs confirmed a gradual recovery in Europe, mixed activity data in China, coupled with sudden currency depreciation, left the outlook uncertain. Next week, US PCE inflation and spending data will be in focus.

This week has been marked by optimism in the markets, with equities reaching new all-time highs, yields falling, and the VIX dropping below 13, supported by a number of central banks communicating their expectations that inflation is on the right path, despite sometimes bumpy data. At the same time, in Europe, where activity has disappointed for over a year, surveys point to a gradual recovery. That said, this week's developments again demonstrate that China's outlook remains complicated, with mixed activity data and a sudden currency depreciation.

## Central banks optimistic about inflation

Fed communication suggests it is itching to cut...

The FOMC held rates steady this week and left the statement largely unchanged, reiterating guidance that it requires greater confidence before initiating cuts. However, other aspects of the meeting sent a dovish message. Most important, contrary to our expectations, the Summary of Economic Projections retained three cuts in 2024, despite a stronger trajectory for GDP, alongside upward revisions to core PCE and a downward revision to the unemployment rate in 2024. This was predicated on a benign interpretation of inflation developments, with Chair Powell suggesting that the committee regarded the firming in inflation at the beginning of the year as more of a bump in the road, from which it did not infer any additional momentum in the coming months.

In our view, this hints at a shift in the FOMC's reaction function that favours cuts sooner, rather than later. Similarly, in the press conference, Chair Powell could have easily made the case that this dot plot was more hawkish than in December by highlighting that the average revision to the dots was up in each year (0.11pp in 2024, 0.17pp in 2025, and 0.12pp in 2026); the central tendency in funds rate projections was increased (by about 0.25pp in both 2024 and 2025); and the median longer-run dot was revised slightly higher, formalizing a shift higher in views about the neutral rate. But he did not and instead chose to communicate a more soothing message to markets.

In all, this suggests the FOMC is itching to ease policy this year, likely seeking an opening in the data flow to initiate cuts at upcoming meetings. As before, we think this will come in June, when base efects are projected to imply favourable declines in y/y inflation, despite only gradual moderation in sequential monthly inflation prints. Thereafter, we expect every-othermeeting cuts, implying three 25bp cuts in total in 2024 and another four in 2025. However, risks continue to tilt toward fewer or later cuts, especially if disinflationary pressures stall, and the easing of financial conditions following this week's dovish messaging intensified these risks.

Indeed, as soon as next week, we expect February core PCE to post another firm print, rising 0.32% m/m, led by strength in core goods prices, even as services inflation likely moderated. On growth, we think the February personal income estimates will show a solid 0.5% m/m increase in income and 0.1% m/m increase in real PCE. We will also watch the March UofMich consumer sentiment and expect a 0.4pp upward revision to Q4 GDP.

## ...the BoE believes inflation is on the right path...

In the same vein, the Bank of England this week held the Bank Rate at 5.25%, but also made a step closer to cuts. First, the vote split was 1 to 8, with the two previously hawkish dissenters moving into the majority to vote for a hold, while Prof. Swati Dhingra continued to vote for a cut. Second, the MPC made changes to its guidance, adding that the degree of restrictiveness will be considered "at each meeting," signalling that some MPC members will be more actively weighing the option to cut. Also, the guidance that restrictive policy will need to be maintained for an extended time was softened to make explicit that we are already some way through this period. Finally, after the meeting, Governor Bailey expressed confidence about the path of inflation, saying that "we've had very encouraging and good news so I think we can say we're on the way," and endorsed market pricing, which now sees the Bank of England cutting from June.

That said, there were clearly some on the committee who are concerned about services inflation falling suficiently fast, and there was broad agreement that more evidence is needed to warrant a shift in the monetary policy stance. Despite this week's dovish tilt, we continue to think it will take until June for the majority on the MPC to gain suficient confidence to cut, with the committee taking advantage of two additional data cycles between the May and June meetings, when the levels of key variables will likely fall materially lower. This includes inflation, which already eased meaningfully this week and which we expect to continue to fall sharply in the coming months, with the headline falling below 2% in April and core decelerating from the current 4.5% y/y into the 2% handle by the May print.

## ...and the BoJ concludes that extraordinary measures have done their job

BoJ oficials also were optimistic. In their case, it meant that their extremely accommodative policies over many years had finally fulfilled their purpose, bringing sustainable achievement of its price stability target into view. Consequently, the BoJ overhauled its large-scale easing program, exiting the world's last negative interest rate policy, as well as yield curve control. New ETF and J-REIT purchases also came to an end, and the BoJ shifted its policy rate back to the uncollaterialised overnight call rate. That said, it retained its fixed-rate JGB buying operations and funds-supplying operations against pooled collateral as a backstop to avoid a spike in longterm yields.

However, as historic as this shift in policy is, we think it will have very little efect on the macroeconomy, as it is unlikely to afect mortgage rates or broader financial conditions meaningfully. Instead, we believe the BoJ's primary focus is on the smooth functioning of the new current account deposit system and the JGB buying scheme, suggesting it will not take long to gauge the efects of the NIRP exit.

We also think it is unlikely that the BoJ will now implement a series of rate hikes or large reductions in JGB buying, given its emphasis on avoiding "discontinuous" policy adjustments. Also speaking against substantial further tightening is the BoJ's revised forward guidance, which states that "the Bank anticipates that accommodative financial conditions will be maintained for the time being." Similarly, in the post-meeting press conference, Governor Ueda remarked that underlying inflation remained below 2% and that there was an element of launching into the revisions “without clear prospects,” suggesting an easing bias would be maintained for some time. For these reasons, we expect only one additional hike (of 25bp), delivered at the July meeting.

## SNB surprises with cut among the DMs, while EM central banks were mixed

Besides the largest central banks, this week also brought shifts in some smaller developed economies. Most notably, the Swiss National Bank surprised markets with a 25bp cut, becoming the first DM to ease policy, as President Jordan declared that "the fight against inflation over the past 2 1/2 years has been efective" and the SNB made significant downward revisions to its inflation projections. In addition, the RBA this week softened its forward guidance by scrapping any reference to possible further hikes. Meanwhile, Norges reiterated its message that its monetary policy is unlikely to change for "some time."

In the EM space, the moves were more mixed, with the central banks of Indonesia and Russia remaining on hold and Czechia and Brazil continuing to cut alongside Mexico, which initiated its easing cycle. In contrast, the central banks of Taiwan and Turkey surprised with interest rate hikes.

European activity surveys point to gradual recovery ...

This week's activity data supported our forecast of a gradual recovery of the euro area economy after more than a year of stagnation. Next week, we will look for more signals for economic growth in the European Commission survey, money and credit data, German retail sales, and the French household consumption of goods.

## ...while ECB communication remains cautious

Unlike the dovish signals from a number of other central banks, the ECB this week remained relatively cautious. Speaking at the ECB Watchers' conference, President Lagarde reiterated the signal that the first cut is likely to come in June, saying that "we will know a bit more by April and a lot more by June." However, she also emphasised that "after the first rate cut, we cannot pre-commit to a particular rate path."

Instead, the Governing Council intends to make decisions meeting-by-meeting and take a datadependent approach. On this front, the past and upcoming inflation data will be critical. This week, Eurostat released February's final HICP, which confirmed the slowing of headline and core inflation from January. Next week, we will watch flash March HICP in France, Italy, and Spain, ahead of the German and euro area releases the week after. We expect euro area headline inflation to hold steady at 2.6% y/y in March, with firmer underlying momentum and the Easter efect keeping services at 4% y/y for the fifth consecutive month. Core inflation as a whole could edge slightly lower, to 3.0% y/y, due to softer core goods.

## China outlook remains complicated

Robust IP is supportive for Q1 GDP growth...

Finally, in China, this week brought a slew of activity data, which were mixed. On the positive side, industrial production beat expectations by a wide margin in January-February, improving to 0.9% m/m from 0.5% m/m in December and adding to the signal from better export data. Similarly, fixed asset investment rose 4.2% y/y, from 3.9% y/y in December.

Given China's production-based GDP statistics, the strong industrial production will likely outweigh any weakness in domestic demand indicators, and the incoming data are also consistent with the idea that policy support is gradually passing through to the economy. For example, the beat in fixed asset investment was led by state-owned enterprises. As the delayed efects of policy easing take efect, we expect China's GDP to sequentially accelerate, picking up from 4.3% in H2 23 to 4.7% in H1 24 and to 5.3% H2 24, with the peak in Q3. Even this, however, would still add to only 4.4% annual growth for 2024, thus missing the government's 5% target.

## ... but the negative consumer-property sector nexus remains

Indeed, this week also reconfirmed that the negative nexus between a slumping property sector and weak consumer confidence remains a drag on China's economy. Retail sales disappointed expectations and softened to 0.1% m/m on average in January-February, compared with 0.25% m/m growth in December, against the backdrop of a deteriorating labour market. In addition, key property indicators worsened further.

The PBoC's decision on Friday to allow a weaker CNY against the USD also points to concerns among policy makers. The move efectively broke a de facto peg that had been in place over the past few months, which the PBoC had defended aggressively through stronger fixes (see China macro: Markets too sanguine about CNY depreciation risks?). A weaker currency will not help with consumer confidence, but likely rather reflects the authorities' intention to allow the value of the exchange rate to adjust to the performance of the economy.

## Thematic

Canada & Latin America Banks (Pos)

Initiating Coverage: Sunny in Mexico but Cool on Canada

20 March 2024

Brian Morton, CFA

Canada & Latin America Banks

We initiate coverage on Canada & Latin America Banks with a Positive industry view. Specifically, we initiate coverage on six Canadian banks and three Mexican banks. Given diferences in loan and deposit spreads, Mexico banks have been able to generate net interest margins in excess of 6% compared to roughly 1.5-2.0% for Canada banks. These wider margins drive higher overall profitability with relatively lower leverage. While the economies of all three large North American countries appear to be slowing, Mexico seems relatively better positioned given the continued positive impact from the near-shoring trend. Conversely, Canada is showing weaker trends with a sequential decline in quarterly GDP in 3Q23 and 4Q23. Deposit growth for the Mexican banks in our coverage universe is approaching 20% y-o-y, while overall industry deposit growth for the Canadian banks is in the low single digit range y-o-y. Driven by stronger growth in both the economy and deposits, loan growth for the Mexican banks has been above that of the Canadian banks. Similar to deposits, loan balances for the Mexican banks under coverage have been in the mid- to high teens y-o-y compared to mid-single digits y-o-y for the Canadian banks. ROEs for the Mexican bank industry have been improving post pandemic and are approaching the 20% range. Notably, this is driven by underlying ROAs above 2%. For the Canadian banks, ROEs are in the mid-teens although ROAs are sub-1%. Credit quality remains benign for the Mexican banks, with relatively stable delinquency trends and coverage ratios. While still very manageable, credit quality trends are normalizing for the Canadian banks, creating a slight headwind. On risk-based capital ratios, both Canadian and Mexican banks remain well capitalized. In terms of CET1 ratios, the Mexican banks range from the mid teens to over 20%, while the Canadian banks are in the low to mid-teens. Still, looking at leverage ratios, Canada banks are hovering at just over 4% compared to 10%+ for Mexico banks. At 9x forward P/E, the Mexican banks are trading at a modest discount compared to the Canadian banks at 10.5x. On a price-to-book basis, the Mexican banks trade at 1.6x compared to the Canadian banks at 1.3x. We are initiating on BMO and NA in Canada and GFNORTEO and GENTERA in Mexico with Overweight ratings, while rating BNS and CM Underweight. We are also initiating on RY and TD in Canada and GFINBURO in Mexico with Equal Weight ratings.

Electronics markets

U.S. Multi-Industry

We upgrade our rating on MMM to OW from EW.
