# Remain EW on IT and OW Consumer Staples

We think there is increasing client awareness of the issues we raised in our July note that recommended a switch out of IT and into Staples. We think it is too soon to turn more constructive on IT despite recent underperformance and lower valuations.

\- The EM IT sector peaked on 11 July and has fallen 15% in the past two months. At the same time, EM Consumer Staples has been flat, while the overall EM index is down \~6%.

\- We think the market will remain cautious on the global growth outlook and note the market's ongoing debate about the extent of upcoming Fed rate cuts (25bps versus 50bps). In this context, we remind clients that IT underperformance and Staples outperformance has typically persisted for at least 6 months after the beginning of a Fed cutting cycle.

\- On a forward P/E basis EM IT is now back in line with long-run average valuations - due to price declines and continued analyst upward estimates revisions - trading on \~15x +12m P/E on consensus. However, typically for a trough to form it needs to trade one SD cheap on this metric.

\- We also caution that on our preferred P/Sales metric, key names such as TSMC have adjusted only marginally thus far versus prior valuation cycles. Typically, once IT has peaked in absolute and relative terms IT takes around a year before it begins to outperform again. Moreover, investor positioning remains OW versus benchmark on the sector, albeit less so than two months ago. For details see our July note: Asia EM Equity Strategy: Correction: Shifting Risk-Reward – Take Profits on IT, Upgrade Consumer Staples to OW (24 Jul 2024).

## Remain EW on IT and OW Consumer Staples

In July, we downgraded our long-standing OW on EM IT to EW and recommended a straight switch to a more defensive stance by moving Consumer Staples to OW (see here for details). In terms of implementation we removed SK Hynix, TSMC and Mediatek from our AxJ and GEM focus lists retaining Samsung Electronics, Hon Hai Precision, and Naura Technology. We also removed Tokyo Electron and Tokyo Seimitsu from our Japan focus list. For our current AXJ, GEM and Japan focus lists see Exhibit 14 and Exhibit 15 . Key defensive Consumer names included are CP All, JBS and Woolworths in AXJ and EM, and Calbee, Kao and Cosmos Pharmaceutical in Japan.

Since peaking on July 11, the EM IT industry group is down 15%, while EM Consumer Staples is flat over the same period.

We have discussed the call extensively with clients during a marketing trip in Asia, Europe and US. Our observations on their feedback is as follows:

Tech specialists have, in general, been sceptical on our downgrade and continue to be so, as key companies in the industry are guiding positively on revenues and earnings.

Generalist investors have been more receptive, particularly at the experienced CIO level for two reasons. Firstly, scepticism that bottom-up IT consensus revenue growth numbers are sustainable given the growing debate on global growth and AI adoption (near term rather than long term where most are positive). Secondly, absolute valuations and relative valuations to other sectors.

Initially, clients also wondered why we have not recommended a switch to late cycle cyclicals, such as Energy. However, this has diminished as the oil price has fallen. See Martijn Rats' The Oil Manual: Are Oil Prices Signalling Recession? (9 Sep 2024).

There has also been debate over which other defensive sectors could perform in a slowing global growth / lower rates environment. We think Healthcare, Gold within Materials, and select Real Estate exposure fits the bill. See our recent note on the playbook for lower global rates here.
