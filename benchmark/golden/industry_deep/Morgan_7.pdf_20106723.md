# Commodity Market Positioning & Flows

Global commodity open interest declines for a second consecutive week as energy and EUA carbon prices soften

The estimated value of open interest across global commodity markets declined for a second consecutive week, falling by 1.2% WOW (\$14.5 billion) to \$1.19 trillion as declines across oil & refined products and grains & oilseeds markets led the complex lower (as of 23 Feb, Table 1). Global commodity markets experienced \$2 billion in net outflows (calculated across all trader types) as outflows from base metals and agriculture were only partially offset by inflows into crude oil and bulk commodities (Figure 1 204YTDgloba cmdites fwrp \$67n  x u h vk).

• The estimated value of the net investor position aggregated across commodity markets increased by 13.3% WOW, as net lengthening across metals, agri and environment markets outweighed net shortening in energy markets (20 Feb, Figure 3The valuoft ngisr pc bmdy k  13%WO ). Investors lengthened their net long notional position across precious metals markets by 14% (+\$4 billion) over the week. Energy market notional net investor length declined by 7% WOW (-\$1.2 billion) but remained overall net long. The aggregate notional net investor position across major base metals flipped again from net short (-\$2 billion) in the previous week to a small net long (+\$132 million) last week.

The estimated value of open interest across energy markets declined by -2% WOW to \$536 billion (23 Feb, Figure 6). The decrease was primarily driven by weak price action in crude oil and refined products last week, as modest net inflows into crude were broadly offset by outflows from products and natural gas. The estimated value of open interest across natural gas markets remained broadly flat over the week (23 Feb, Figure 13The stimadvlu ofpnr gk b w c) as producer moves to defer production have stabilized the US market while signs of industrial demand recovery have begun to appear in Europe.

The estimated value of open interest in precious metals was broadly stable at \~\$142 billion for a third consecutive week (23 Feb, Figure 8). Across all trader types, contract-based outflows totalled \$186 million as inflows into gold (\$1 billion) were more than offset by outflows from silver and PGMs. The latest CFTC data show Managed Money positioning in COMEX Gold futures lengthened by 15.7k contracts to 49k contracts net long, bouncing longer after a substantial decline to YTD lows over the previous week. The minutes from the latest January FOMC meeting reinforce the patient approach towards cutting rates which Chair Powell voiced in the postmeeting press conference, leaving our economists maintaining their call for a first Fed rate cut in June.

● The estimated value of open interest across environmental markets declined by -6% WOW to \~\$39.2 billion (23 Feb, Figure 7). Contract-based inflows were \$895 million WOW, mainly absorbed by EUAs. Investments funds reduced their net short position by 670 lots across EUAs over the week to -37.6k lots (as of 16 Feb).

• Global agricultural market estimated open interest declined by -0.6% over the week to \~\$298 billion (23 Feb, Figure 10, Figure 14). Contract-based net outflows were \$1.9 billion over the week, with outflows from grains & oilseeds and softs markets more than offsetting inflows into livestock markets. ICE NY Cocoa prices are tracking record high levels, having increased by over 20% over the week as unfavorable weather conditions in major exporting locations have heightened expectations of a significant supply deficit this year. This would mark the third consecutive year of deficit, with expectations for a shortfall of 800k tonnes. The Ivory Coast has oversold their 2023/24 crop with cocoa shipments from the ports declining by 35% over the year (Oct-Jan). Meanwhile, Ghana has adjusted its current season’s production estimate downward by 24% due to adverse weather and the effects of swollen shoot disease, potentially leading to the lowest output in 14 years for the country.

Price momentum generally declined across oil, petroleum products and softs markets but increased for natural gas, metals and grains market over the week (23 Feb, Table 2). Notably, the short-term lookback trading signals on COMEX Gold and NYMEX Palladium switched from sell-to-buy, while the short-term lookback trading signals on ICE Sugar and ICE Coffee switched from buy-to-sell. The shortterm lookback trading signal on ICE NY Cocoa surpassed the upside threshold of +3 SD, indicating trend exhaustion.
