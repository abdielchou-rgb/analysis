# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 5

BoC cuts rate; USTs fall amid mixed ISM Services; USD/CAD rises as Canadian rates diverge from Fed; Japan wage data weaken JPY; USD/THB rises after reports of Thai gov't efforts to exert more control over BoT; IDR falls despite intervention comments; DXY at 104.3 (0.2%); US 10y at 4.276% (-5.0bp).

• BoC reduces its policy rate by 25bp; CGB yields rally (2y: -12bp) alongside dovish opening statement, which states “it is reasonable to expect further cuts to our policy interest rate.”

• USTs trade choppily before continuing their rally amid mixed US ISM Services data for May; headline index rebounds, but underlying details exhibit declines in inflationary pressure and weak employment.

• USD/CAD (0.1%) rises as much as 0.5% after BoC cut, but pares gains as BoC Governor Macklem notes the BoC does not need to move in “lockstep” with the Fed, but it is not close to the limit of how far Canadian rates can diverge.

• Lower expectations for early normalization from the BoJ, given the April Japanese wage data, have yet to confirm acceleration, support JGB rally, and send USD/JPY (+0.8%) higher.

• USD/THB (+0.3%) surges, erasing earlier losses after media reports of the Thai administration considering ways to exert more control over the Bank of Thailand.

• IDR (-0.4%) underperforms other AxJ currencies despite BI Governor Warjiyo saying the central bank will continue to intervene in FX markets.

USTs traded choppily overnight, with 10y yields up over 3bps in the Tokyo session before returning to their tendency to rally during the London session. Mixed data in the New York morning added to the volatility, but the dovish BoC meeting and data signaling cooling price pressures ultimately led USTs to continue their decline. UST yields fell in a parallel manner and ended the day \~5bp lower across the curve.

In the NY morning, ISM services had a stronger-than-expected rebound to 53.8 in May (C: 51.0; P 49.4), and initially USTs sold off. However, the underlying details did not necessarily support the higher-for-longer policy rate narrative. The Prices Paid Index fell to 58.1 (C: 59.0; P: 59.2), which signaled a decline in inflationary pressures. Additionally, employment was weaker than expected. Further, survey response details suggested a weaker economy and indicated that higher costs and interest rates impeded business growth, and certain adjustments would need to be made in response. The soft underlying details reversed the UST move, which continued to trend lower throughout the day.

Market participants quickly turned their attention to the June BoC meeting where, as expected, the BoC became the first G7 central bank to cut rates. The policy rate was lowered by 25bp, and the opening statement and press conference both had a dovish lean as the Bank noted that if inflation continues to ease towards its 2% inflation target, "it is reasonable to expect further cuts" to the policy rate. CGB yields rallied and 2y yields fell 12 bp. The rally spilled over into USTs and accelerated their move lower.

CAD (-0.1%) initially weakened against the USD after the BoC cut, but the decelerating inflationary pressures exhibited by ISM data pulled USD/CAD in the opposite direction. Further, BoC Governor Macklem’s remarks also supported CAD; he noted that while the BoC does not need to move in “lockstep” with the Fed, there are limits to how far the Canadian rate policy can diverge from the Fed, though they are nowhere near those limits.

A pickup in usage of the Standing Repo Facility (SRF) to \$100mn suggested the presence of a small value exercise by counterparties to test functionality. The size is similar to spikes seen in February, March, April, and May, where usage then returned to near zero levels. Currently, there are 33 eligible SRF counterparties.

The rebound in ISM services, despite the softer underlying details, may have eased some market participants' fears that the economy is slowing, and sent equities higher (S&P 500: +1.2%). Technology stocks led the gains, with particular strength in an artificial intelligence company that hit \$3tr in market value.

## Japan:

JGBs rallied strongly in the Tokyo morning following the continued run in USTs, and as demand to square short positions amplified the fall in yields. The Bloomberg report regarding the BoJ, which suggested the Bank would discuss a cut in JGB purchase pace at the June meeting at earliest, was released on Tuesday. However, the tone was not decisive and market participants’ reaction was limited in the Tokyo morning. The super-long sector benefited the most in the curve. There was also better demand in the short-end sector on the back of lower expectations for early normalization from the BoJ, given that nominal wage data for April did not yet

confirm acceleration. In the Tokyo afternoon, the super-long sector partly retraced the rally ahead of the 30y auction on Thursday. JGB curve bull-flattened; 2y JGB rallied 3.0bp to 0.35%, 10y JGB rallied 3.0bp to 1.0%, and 30y JGB rallied 4.5bp to 2.175%. For Thursday’s 30y auction, we expect there could be decent demand to square short positions, especially if the UST rally continues on the back of slowing economic growth data. However, lifers may not aggressively buy ahead of the US employment data on Friday and the June FOMC meeting next week.

TONA-OIS curve also bull-flattened and underperformed JGBs for short-end and super-long sectors, while 10y ASW cheapened. XCCY basis widened broadly, particularly in super-long sectors.

USD/JPY bounced back from below 155.0 to \~155.5 in the Tokyo morning, after the sharp decline in the pair on Tuesday due to the rally in USTs on the back of weak US data coupled with reaction to remarks from BoJ/MoF officials. Lower expectations for BoJ’s early normalization given the April wage data, combined with the higherfor-longer narrative in the US, may have supported carry-focused trades on Wednesday which weakened JPY. The trend continued into the London afternoon, and USD/JPY ground higher to over 156. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY. GBP/JPY rose to 198.8 in the London morning.

## Europe:

G10 FX price action was confined to relatively small moves for the most part. CHF sold off -0.5%, as market participants re-engaged in carry trades after the recent sell-off, and turned to one of their funding favorites for carry. The EUR was little changed and fell -0.2% ahead of Thursday’s ECB meeting. The surprising outlier on Wednesday was SEK, which posted a gain of +0.3%, seemingly driven by its beta to equities. NOK erred on the side of the rest of G10 and fell 0.2%, as US dollar moves and oil and gas price action once again pulled the currency in separate directions. Sterling ended the day flat.

European duration remained better bid, in sympathy with Treasuries. In the US, ISM Services surprised to the upside, which weighed on European duration, but the move was short-lived and 10y Bund yields closed 2bp lower at 2.51%. Curves bull flattened slightly and 5s10s30s swap richened \~1bp. Ahead of the ECB meeting on Thursday, market-implied pricing indicated \~65bp of cuts in 2024, with \~24bp of cuts implied for June. Market-implied pricing continued to assign a higher probability of cuts for forecasting meetings. Risk sentiment improved while oil stabilized; 10y BTP/Bund retraced Wednesday’s widening, and closed at 131bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

The dollar bloc also exhibited smaller moves on Wednesday. A beat in the US ISM services index generated some US dollar strength across the board, but it was limited as the prices-paid component of the index was below expectations. Nonetheless, the DXY USD Index ultimately gained +0.2%. In Canada, the BoC kicked of its cutting cycle and provided dovish guidance, which drove a rally in USD/ CAD, that was partially overshadowed by the ISM release 15 minutes later. This ultimately resulted in CAD ending the day just 0.1% weaker.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies exhibited mixed moves post weaker-than-expected US JOLTS data. USD/CNH gained 0.1% to \~7.2560 amid a higher USD/CNY fixing of 7.1097 (P: 7.1083). After underperforming on Tuesday, INR turned into an outperformer. USD/ INR fell 0.2% to 83.3 and erased roughly half of Tuesday’s losses alongside a recovery in local equities (NIFTY: +3.4%) as two key parties reportedly pledged their support for the National Democratic Alliance. KRW and TWD also gained 0.2% amid the strength in local equities (KOSPI: +1%; TAIEX: +0.6%). USD/THB erased earlier losses of 0.3% after media reports of the Thai administration considering ways to exert more influence over the central bank emerged; the pair eventually finished the Tokyo session higher by 0.2%. IDR underperformed the most, down 0.4% to 16,286.5, even as BI said it will continue to intervene in FX markets.

Most AxJ rates bull-flattened following the trend in USTs on Tuesday overnight. In China, 5y CNY NDIRS yields fell 2bp while the 1s5s curve bull-flattened 1bp, amid weakness in local equity markets (CSI 300: -0.6%). CGB yields remained largely stable. In India, 10y G-sec held steady at 7.04% and underperformed the upturn seen in FX and equities. However, a recovery was seen in the NDOIS curve; 5y INR NDOIS yields fell 4bp and the 1s5s curve bull-flattened 1bp. Korea was the main outperformer in the region; 10y KRW NDIRS yields fell 5bp and the 2s10s curve bullflattened 1bp, aided in part by a small downward revision in final 1Q24 GDP. Hong Kong rates diverged from the rally in USTs and yields rose across the HKD IRS curve; 1y yield rose the most by 3bp as it headed into the dividend season. Similar to the FX market, Indonesia rates were the main underperformer; 10y IndoGBs sold off 3bp to 6.90%.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The BoC delivered its first 25bp cut to its policy rate and cut to 4.75% at its June meeting (C: 4.75%; P: 5.0%). In the opening statement of the press conference, the Bank highlighted that restrictive monetary policy continued to relieve price pressures, and with “further and more sustained evidence underlying inflation is easing, monetary policy no longer needs to be as restrictive.” In terms of forward guidance, the Bank noted that if inflation continues to ease, and the Bank’s confidence that inflation is headed sustainably to the 2% inflation target continues to rise, “it is reasonable to expect further cuts to our policy interest rate.” Nonetheless, the Bank is taking its rate decisions “one meeting at a time,” as it cautions that if it

lowers its policy rate too quickly, the progress on inflation could be jeopardized. Looking ahead, our economists expect three more 25bp cuts this year (Sept, Oct, Dec) to reach 4.00% by end of 2024

## Emerging Markets

• BI Governor Warjiyo said the central bank will continue to intervene in FX markets and keep yields attractive to prevent outflows especially in shortterm securities. He reiterated the IDR should strengthen going forward and through next year given more clarity on the Fed’s policy rate path, stronger portfolio inflows, and better domestic economic prospects

• BI Executive Director for Monetary and Security Asset Management Susianto confirmed the central bank intervened in the FX spot and DNDF markets on Wednesday to stabilize IDR. He attributed the IDR weakness to volatile global conditions and relatively high USD demand for dividend repatriation

• In a report to lawmakers seen by Bloomberg, CBC said the weak JPY is having only a limited impact on the Taiwan economy. It highlighted Taiwan’s exports and economy are affected predominantly by the global economy, though traditional industries will likely be affected by the weak JPY. It noted it is necessary to maintain the relative stability of TWD as it benefits product pricing and the operation of manufacturers, and supports domestic growth and financial stability

• BSP said the Philippines’ May inflation rate of 3.9% y/y is consistent with the central bank’s projections that inflation could temporarily accelerate above the target range in the near term due to the impact of adverse weather conditions on domestic agricultural output and positive base effects. It continues to see risks to the inflation outlook leaning toward the upside. Nonetheless, BSP expects full year average inflation for 2024 and 2025 to settle within the target range

• HKMA Chief Executive Yue said the HKMA is seeking to expand the Wealth Management Connect, as the current RMB 3mn investment quota is “not quite enough for private bank clients,” who have big needs to diversify assets. Yue also noted other possible areas to upgrade include further streamlining the selling process, allowing more active marketing, and having better investor education

• The NBP kept its policy rate unchanged at 5.75% (C: 5.75%). The statement showed no major change in forward guidance, with future rates decisions dependent on the prospects of economic activity and inflation. The NBP continued to say that the inflation outlook is associated with substantial uncertainties linked to the impact of fiscal and regulatory policies. Our economists maintain their call for the NBP to start easing monetary policy only in 1Q25, but the weak price pressures suggest that a 4Q24 rate cut remains a possibility (for more, read here)

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
