## February 6

Tsy Secretary Yellen expresses "concern" about CRE; Front-end UST gains extend on strong 3y auction; USD reverses weaker, risk-sensitive currencies gain; RBA retains tightening bias, AUD climbs; China risk assets surge; EGBs climb into London close; DXY at 104.17 (-0.3%); US 10y at 4.100% (-5.8bp).

• US Treasury Secretary Yellen expresses "concern" about commercial real estate and says it requires "careful supervisory attention" as it could create financial stability risks or banking system losses.

• US Treasuries richen across the curve after CRE risks return to the forefront, front-end gains later extend after the \$54bn 3y auction comes 0.8bp through.

• Risk-sensitive currencies find comfort in the US dollar's weakness intraday, as the spillover from steep regional bank losses are limited; SEK and NOK are among the G10 outperformers.

• AUD (+0.6%) also strengthens after the RBA keeps the cash rate target at 4.25% (C: 4.25%) but cautions "further increase in interest rates cannot be ruled out."

• China risk asset resurgence is led by A-share small caps (CSI 1000: +7.0%) amid media reports the CSRC plans to update top leadership on market conditions.

• Renewed deterioration in risk sentiment supports European duration into the London close.

House of Representatives Financial Services Committee happened to touch on commercial real estate risks. Yellen said, “many small banks” have a commercial property concentration and properties in “some cities are of special concern.” She expressed "concern" about commercial real estate and said it requires "careful supervisory attention" as it could create financial stability risks or banking system losses, given the rise in interest rates, higher vacancy rates, and increase in remote work.

At noon, in prepared remarks, Cleveland Fed President Mester described both the economy and monetary policy as “in a good place.” She said the labor market report for January was “remarkably resilient,” noting the revisions upward to jobs in October, November, and December. She echoed a sentiment shared by Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari, that strong output and employment growth could be an indication the neutral rate of interest might remain high.

In terms of the risks around her forecast, she highlighted re-emergence of bank stress and in particular “sizable exposures to CRE assets that will need to be repriced at higher interest rates.” She noted that, while labor markets are currently strong, the FOMC needs to “remain attentive to the possibility that conditions could deteriorate faster than expected.”

As the FOMC needs to balance risks to achieving both sides of their dual mandate, Mester said, “it would be a mistake to move rates down too soon or too quickly.” If the economy evolves as expected, she believes the FOMC “will gain that confidence later this year” and begin to move rates down. Her base case calls for rate cuts at a “gradual pace” and for three rate cuts this year. She added, “of course, if downside risks materialize, we would have the opportunity to move rates down more quickly, just as we raised rates more aggressively than usual to combat rising inflation. Or if inflation appears to be stalling at a level above our goal, we would have the opportunity to maintain a restrictive stance for longer.”

Regarding the balance sheet, Mester acknowledged the uncertainty about what constitutes an ample level of reserves, since it depends on the banking sector’s demand for reserves and distribution of the demand across institutions. She viewed the current level and distribution of reserves as “more than ample.” She later remarked there is “no urgency” to slow the pace of balance sheet runoff right now.

The main NY afternoon catalyst was the start of February UST coupon supply with a new \$54bn 3y auction. The 3y auction stopped-out at 4.169%, 0.8bp through, with solid auction internals. The allotment to indirect bidders inched up to 66.0% (P: 65.3%), above the one-year average (63.1%). The allotment to direct bidders ticked up to 18.3% (P: 16.8%), a tad below the one-year average (19.6%). The primary dealer allotment fell to 15.7% (P: 17.8%), below the one-year average (17.3%). A slight drop in the bid-to-cover ratio to 2.57x (P: 2.67) was a function of an auction size 3.8% larger than the January auction.

Post-auction, front-end USTs extended to session highs but gave back some gains into the NY close. Yet, USD sustained its weakness, and the DXY USD Index (-0.3%) closed at session lows, juxtaposed against a strong equity market close. The risksensitive Scandinavian currencies (NOK and SEK) were among the top performers in the G10, joined by AUD (+0.6%), which was supported by a cautionary tone from

the RBA that "further increase in interest rates cannot be ruled out." Broader market participation helped the S&P 500 (+0.23%) and Russell 2000 (+0.85%) weather renewed downside momentum in regional banks (KRX: -1.41%).

In a moderated discussion, Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari referenced microeconomic data from the NY Fed’s Quarterly Report on Household Debt and Credit. The report noted aggregate delinquency rates increased in 4Q23, with 3.1% of outstanding debt in some stage of delinquency at the end of December. Delinquency transition rates increased for all debt types, except for student loans. Annualized, \~8.5% of credit card balances and \~7.7% of auto loans transitioned into delinquency. Kashkari referenced the rise in credit card and auto loan delinquencies, above pre-pandemic levels, and concluded “the trajectory is concerning but the level isn’t that concerning yet.”

Of the many legal developments on Tuesday, one in particular was important for US Treasury market structure. The SEC adopted two rules that “require market participants who engage in certain dealer roles, in particular those who take on significant liquidity-providing roles in the markets, to register with the SEC, become members of a self-regulatory organization (SRO), and comply with federal securities laws and regulatory obligations.” This will increase oversight of firms that buy and sell US Treasury securities for their own account as part of the regular course of their business. The compliance date for the final rules will be one year after the effective date of the final rules.

## Japan:

Another sell-off in USTs initially drove JGB yields higher across the curve on Tuesday, but JGBs retraced initial weakness as dip-buying demand gradually emerged. This was particularly evident in the front end; 2y JGBs rallied by 0.5bp d/d, to 0.105%, on a simple yield basis. Although long-end JGBs initially lagged ahead of the 30y JGB auction on Wednesday, they gradually outperformed on the curve, supported by dip-buying demand; 30y JGBs rallied by 1.0bp d/d, to 1.78%. We expect some demand from domestic real money market participants but wonder if it will be able to absorb JPY900bn of supply. With market positioning seemingly skewed toward flatteners, we highlight risk of long-end underperformance post-auction. TONA OIS rallied across the curve, with the belly better received. XCCY basis continued to tighten, led by the front end.

It was another quiet session in FX markets; USD/JPY essentially traded sideways \~148.50 given limited catalysts in Tokyo trading hours. AUD/JPY rose toward 96.80 after a hawkish status quo outcome by the RBA.

## Europe:

The Scandinavian currencies outperformed, 0.8% stronger, against both USD and EUR, even as US equities were little changed in the New York afternoon.

European duration continued to sell off in the London morning, amid few data releases. In the London afternoon, price action reversed with the renewed deterioration in risk sentiment, alongside a sell-off in US regional bank stocks. 10y Bunds rallied 2.4bp d/d, to 2.29%, and 2s10s bull-flattened by 0.5bp, while 10s30s bull-steepened by 1.3bp. The rally was led by real yields and inflation breakevens widened. In inflation swaps, 2y2y rose by 2bp (+5bp since Friday’s close). Core ASW widened alongside the “risk-off” move, but this was short-lived and ASW continued to tighten. The whole complex closed cheaper (Bund ASW: -0.7bp) and boxes flattened. 10y BTP-Bund spreads opened tighter and traded in a \~1bp range afterwards. In the 10y sector, OATs and Bonos underperformed Bunds by \~1bp and 0.5bp, respectively.

## Dollar Bloc:

The DXY USD Index (-0.3%) ground lower, as US Treasuries rallied throughout NY trading hours. Despite limited macro catalysts, G10 currencies exhibited relatively large moves. Lower US yields pushed USD/JPY (-0.5%) back below 148.00. The RBA held the cash rate at 4.35% (C: 4.35%); however, the central bank maintained a stronger bias than market participants had anticipated after a 4Q23 downside inflation surprise.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies rallied broadly, aided by a recovery in Chinese equities and lower US yields. The key theme in Asia markets was optimism around government support for Chinese equities, as media reports suggested regulators plan to update the top leadership on market conditions as soon as Tuesday. Chinese risk assets rallied, with USD/CNH down 0.3%, near the 7.20 level, as small-cap stocks (CSI 1000) rallied 7% – and more than reversed the prior session’s losses – amid US \$2.1bn of equity inflows via Northbound Stock Connect. KRW rallied by a similar magnitude despite KOSPI (-0.6%) underperformance, as there were still US\$ 229mn of foreign equity inflows. THB led regional gains, up 0.4% to 35.66, as PM Srettha’s call for the BoT to cut rates by 25bp were outweighed by lower US yields, better sentiment for China, and relative outperformance of the local equity market. MYR and IDR underperformed, down 0.4% and 0.1%, respectively.

AxJ rates exhibited mixed moves. China rates underperformed, as 5y NDIRS yields rose 6bp, the most since late July 2023; the 1s5s curve bear-steepened by 3bp amid better risk sentiment for local assets. Hong Kong and Singapore rates also underperformed, as 10y swap yields rose 4bp. The 2s10s curve bear-steepened by 3bp in Singapore, and twist-steepened by 5bp in Hong Kong, where liquidity remained ample. In Thailand, 1y swap yields fell 2bp, to 2.23%, and the curve bullflattened amid PM Srettha’s call for a 25bp rate cut ahead of Wednesday’s BoT meeting. India rates outperformed, the NDOIS curve shifted 2bp lower even though RBI has drained liquidity from the banking system since last Friday.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies rallied, largely driven by a weaker US dollar. CLP (+0.7%) broke back below 950 and retraced the move from the prior session. BRL (+0.4%) and MXN (+0.3%) ended stronger, amid few local developments. LatAm rates largely followed US rates lower. DIs ended \~7bp lower across the curve and IBR rallied, with gains concentrated in the belly (5y: -15bp). TIIE ended 4bp higher in the long end, as Mexico rates caught up after local markets were closed on Monday for a holiday.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The RBA maintained the cash rate at 4.35% (C: 4.35%) at its February meeting. Forecasts were revised down modestly, with core inflation expected to be at 3.1% by end-24 (P: 3.3%), 2.8% by end-25 (P: 2.9%), and 2.6% by June-26 (P: 2.6%). These forecasts were conditioned on a cash rate broadly in line with market pricing of 3.9% by end-24 and 3.4% by end-25. Despite these changes, the statement kept a tightening bias (albeit softer on the margin) and noted “a further increase in interest rates cannot be ruled out.” Our

economists characterized the RBA as still very data dependent – emphasizing the uncertainty in the outlook and forecasts, showing little inclination to guide market pricing, and waiting to see how the data evolves. Our economists expect resilient services inflation will continue this year and concerns around the balance of risks will keep the RBA cautious in pivoting to an easing stance. Our economists expect the RBA will remain on hold through 2024 and begin easing policy in February 2025

• The ECB’s Consumer Expectations Survey for December showed 3y ahead inflation expectations at 2.5% (P: 2.4%), with a 20bp upward revision to the prior month

• Cleveland Fed President Mester said if the economy evolves as expected, the Fed would be able to gain confidence later this year “to begin moving rates down.” However, she cautioned it would be mistake for the Fed to move rates down too soon or too quickly without enough evidence that inflation was on a sustainable path back to 2%

• BoC Governor Macklem continued to acknowledge monetary policy is working to bring down price pressures in durable and nondurable goods prices, as well as in services inflation (ex-shelter). However, he continued to note shelter inflation remains elevated. Consequently, Macklem stated progress will be slow in terms of the path back to the Bank’s 2% inflation target. He reiterated his stance from the BoC meeting and said “that more time is needed to let monetary policy do its work to relieve underlying price pressures”, and the Bank wants to “see inflationary pressures continue to ease and clear downward momentum in underlying inflation”

• BoE External MPC Member Dhingra explained her rationale for voting for a cut at last week’s MPC decision, she noted the central bank may be “underplaying the downside risks” to the UK economy

## Emerging Markets

• BSP said it is necessary to keep monetary policy sufficiently tight until a sustained downtrend in inflation becomes evident. It reiterated the balance of risks to the inflation outlook still lean significantly toward the upside. The central bank noted that, while January data was consistent with its projections that inflation will moderate in 1Q24, it could accelerate above target range from 2Q24 due to El Niño and positive base effects

• Minutes from the January BCB meeting and comments from Governor Campos Neto brought few surprises, including forward guidance continuity that the 50bp pace is appropriate. There was a cautious tone about inflation progress; the board noted concern of de-anchored inflation expectations, services inflation, and a tight labor market. Additionally, they noted lack of a mechanical link between the Selic and fed funds rate. Rates seem fairly priced, in-line with these minutes. The next two meetings are priced for 50bp, with more likely odds of a deacceleration in the June meeting (-38bp)

• Minutes from the January BanRep meeting were largely in-line with the statement, but the minutes doubled down on hawkish concerns. The group who voted for the 25bp cut continued to cite inflation risks from high expectations and El Nino; the minutes also cited uncertainty on fiscals.

However, they noted an openness to increase policy rate reductions in the future. This, combined with two members who already voted for a 50bp cut, supports market pricing for an acceleration at the March meeting (-40bp)

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. ## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
