# How to track and quantify inflow from the 'national team'?

## "National team" net inflows via ETFs into A-share YTD may exceed Rmb410bn

Since mid-to-late January, news that the "national team" has entered the market has drawn investor attention. To gauge the inflow, we tracked recent transactions in 19 CSI 300 ETFs, 19 CSI 500 ETFs, seven CSI 1000 ETFs and nine CSI 2000 ETFs, estimating "national team" net buying based on the daily adjusted amount of "excess transactions" in those ETFs. This has led to estimated net inflows of >Rmb410bn from the "national team" via ETFs into the A-share market YTD, of which a respective 75.9%/12.9%/6.7%/4.5% went to the CSI 300/CSI 500/CSI 1000/CSI 2000.

## "National team" holdings could rise further under extreme market conditions

In our All you need to know about the 'national team' report, we estimated "national team" holdings as of the end of Q3-2015 may have amounted to cRmb1.24trn, or 7.7% of the total A-share free-float market cap at the time. Because only "national team"- related entities (Central Huijin/China Securities Finance/investment platforms under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange) as the top-10 holders of free-float shares of listed companies were incorporated in our estimates, "national team" holdings for 2015 may have actually been much greater than our Rmb1.24trn estimate. That means such holdings YTD remain well below the historical level, with the potential to rise further under extreme conditions, in our view.

As a contrarian trader, "national team" unlikely to reduce holdings in the near term

The "national team" tends to buck the trend, as shown by a time-series chart on the number of stocks it holds (Figure 8). Historically, "national team" holdings showed no meaningful changes from H2-2015 to late 2020, before some reductions in 2021 when the A-share market cap was high. Now, it still has significant holdings in heavily weighted sectors, including finance (Figure 13 . Currently, the A-share CSI 300 index is trading at 11.3x trailing P/E, 1 SD below the five-year average, while its P/BV is close to the historical trough. In an announcement, Central Huijin stated it "is fully recognising the value of the A-share market for allocation". As a long-term investor, the "national team" is very unlikely to reduce holdings in the near term.

## Valuation Method and Risk Statement

For various stocks across the industries we cover in the Hong Kong and mainland China stock markets, we use a variety of valuation approaches, including DCF models, Gordon growth model analysis and relative valuation analysis using various multiples such as PE, EV/EBITDA and P/BV.

We think the risks facing China's equities include a hard landing for the property market, a capital exodus associated with currency depreciation and slow structural reform progress. In our view, any government policies that do not adequately address these risks could result in a shock to the market. For example, an excess of stimulus policies could pose a risk to the transition from an investment-driven to a consumption-driven economy and increase the debt of government and state-owned enterprises.
