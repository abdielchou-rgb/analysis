# Edge AI: Product Releases on Track, but Where Are Killer Apps?

Edge AI is progressing faster than we would have anticipated 2 months ago. From our conversations, we believe investors are too focused on Killer Apps and not appreciating the sweeping effects of AI in hardware devices. 4G to 5G provides the precedent. Samsung is emerging as the catalyst stock.

## Key Takeaways

Edge AI is moving into the hardware verticals we outlined in our report two months ago.

Smartphones and PCs are seeing the biggest jumps in product releases and pipeline.

■ We think investors are too narrowly focused on searching for Killer Apps. Instead, look for OEM-led hardware upgrades for OS, camera & other native features.

■ The shift from 4G to 5G PC & Smartphone gives investors a roadmap for replacement cycles.

# Gathering Momentum

The one question we frequently hear from investors is: where are the Killer Apps? While we believe that downloadable apps taking advantage of on-device processing are important to the thesis, investors may be focusing too much on finding a "ChatGPT equivalent". More than anything (especially early on for Edge AI), we think the native functionality on consumer hardware devices will embed AI features processed locally. We believe this is the most likely path forward, for a few reasons:

1. Native applications (camera, text summary, image rendering, smart OS features) needs to be available offline. Hence Edge AI is an attractive way of improving the offering whilst not requiring users to be connected to the internet to use AI-enabled features for their basic phone applications.

2. Embedding these functions with AI features puts new requirements on the hardware, effectively aging phones and notebooks faster. Whilst Edge AI features in their current state may not compel the majority of users to upgrade their hardware, the slow creep of AI onto devices' basic features will create a wider capability gap between each model. This may mean that users holding on to older models are unable to upgrade to current features (new software roll-outs, for example), or experience faster degrading batteries and slowing phones as chips are outdated at an increasing rate.

3. Security and cost are key factors. We believe AI-enabled features will provide new ways for consumers to interact with their hardware. If these features become truly personalised, access to potentially sensitive user data could become acute. We believe Edge AI can help solve the security aspect in fine-tuning AI features to each individual user, providing a level of personalisation that is harder to obtain solely through cloud-based GenAI. Longer term, we believe the benefit of zero marginal cost for computing to the model provider will incentivise model providers to shift some of the compute workload to devices that use a Hybrid AI approach.

Our analysts note a similar effect following the move from 4G to 5G for smartphones: While most consumers may not see a noticeable difference between 4G and 5G, additional workload requirements mean that devices have become dated more quickly, driving an increase in replacement cycle speeds. We believe the same effect will be at work here, driving an opportunity for our consumer-exposed Edge AI Enablers. As we stressed in our Edge AI primer, this theme is about upgrading hardware for the next Tech shift, not about replacing data centres, nor about ChatGPT.

## Edge AI Thesis Revisit

For our full primer on Edge AI, please see: Thematics: Tech Diffusion: Edge AI – Growing Impetus (7 Nov 2023)

What is Edge AI? Edge refers to the field of running AI algorithms locally on hardware devices rather in centralised data centres in the cloud. Edge simply refers to inference or computation at the "edge" of a network, by definition closer to the point at which data is generated. This approach can help to save costs, reduce latency, rationalise bandwidth, enhance privacy, and allow for real-time processing at the point where data is collected. Every day, examples of Edge AI include facial recognition on a smart phone, voice recognition in smart speakers and anomaly detection in industrial equipment.

Is this a new phenomenon? Edge AI is not new. "The Edge" has historically been loosely interchangeable with the "Internet of Things" or "Industrial IoT", which has been through its own hype cycles over time. However, with the advent of Generative AI, the impetus for device upgrades to enable greater computational power natively on consumer hardware is accelerating and spanning beyond often narrowly used smart speakers, for example. As demonstrated by our European semis analyst, Lee Simpson, in his work on "TinyML" – a field of machine learning where models are small enough to be implemented on microcontroller units (MCUs) – Morgan Stanley Research into Edge AI has been gathering pace. Our November primer serves as a primer for generalists encountering the topic for the first time.

What are the benefits of Edge AI? In the context of this note, we focus on edge computing on battery powered devices (i.e. not mains powered). This can be a smartphone, smartwatch, drone – anything with the capability to process data. Doing ondevice processing – edge computing – offers different advantages to processing data in the cloud. We believe these advantages will fuel new applications in technology hardware. In our view, the key advantages to edge computing vs cloud computing are as follows: (1) personalisation; (2) low cost per query; (3) availability offline/on-demand; (4) low latency versus data centres; and (5) privacy and security with anomaly detection.

![](images/ebb52cc63887e69ad26dc93b1fb36b6ad66f48c5d3f0e6a04d046d3fbf8c96cb.jpg)
Source: Morgan Stanley Research

However, running AI models directly on device offers a set of challenges versus processing models in the cloud.

1. Battery & power consumption – Tests indicate that running AI models on device can require 30-50x as much of the battery. How limited is the device's battery without applying high level AI models? We assess the battery life, relative importance to the device of having more/less. Enablers in this category either A) expand the battery capacity or B) reduce the power consumption of components.

2. Processing power & memory – How processing and memory constrained is the device? We assess the current processing capacity and potential for increasing it to facilitate more complex models. Enablers in this category improve the processing power of the device.

3. Form factor – How size constrained is the device? What about the durability of the device and/or the harsh conditions an edge device could be subject to (think of an oil & gas drill). We assess whether the products' volume/weight limitations and durability are a barrier to enabling AI functionally. Enablers in this category allow devices to free up space for upgraded hardware.

4. Application use cases – What is the device used for? How central is it to AI applications? What is the proverbial ROIC for running inference at the edge on this device? For smartphones, this category isn't a constraint – it's clear why consumers would use a smartphone with Gen AI. But for Wearables, it is a constraint. Similarly, we view the AI use cases for drones as less well established at this point in time. Enablers in this category are leveraging developer kits for building killer apps for constrained devices.

Embedded in the constraints lies the investment opportunity. We believe companies that can solve for these constraints will help AI models diffuse into objects and devices in different ways than if models were accessed purely via the cloud.
