## Key Takeaways

US rates: Neutral duration, neutral curve: We revise our UST forecasts and now expect 10-year yields to end 2024 at 4.15%, 20bp higher than our previous forecast of 3.95%. Our forecast is nearly 45bp lower than market-implied forwards. We see 2-yea yields at 4.30% by the end of 2024.

FXEM: Modest USD strength: The new Fed forecasts put downward pressure on USD, but are offset by the expected supply side-driven outperformance of the US economy. We expect modest USD strength. Investor positioning in carry currencies within EM has reduced substantially over the past week and we believe that investor appetite for this theme will return. We remain short EUR/USD, long USD/CNH, short EUR/COP, long USD/MYR and long INR vs CNH and TWD. We continue to see EUR/PLN lower too.

Global credit: Loans, loans on the range: So long as growth is ok, we think that credit will care more that rate cuts are coming eventually, rather than the exact timing/magnitude. We’d further note that the modern-era tights for credit didn’t occur during the QT or CDO eras, but rather in the mid-1990s, and occurred with modest cuts and ‘higher-for-longer’ rates. We like leveraged loans (and CLO equity), which are well placed for a somewhat stronger, higher-for-longer world.

US equities: Constructive on Energy and Quality: The path of rates should continue to be an important driver of equity valuations. Large caps are fairly valued for the rate move we’ve had, but 10-year rates close to 5.0% would be a headwind.

Cross-asset: Closer to home, prefer carry and convexity in credit. EW across global equities and global fixed income, but with a strong preference for credit, which has good all-in yields and can do well if growth is better and spreads grind tighter from here, or if growth disappoints and rates rally. We close out of our commodities UW as the outlook for Brent and copper is better, with improving global growth and tight supply.
