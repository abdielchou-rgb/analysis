# Is CNH’s breakout just getting started?

## Is CNH’s breakout just getting started?

CNY fixings’ ongoing grind since Q1 recently stepped up its pace, as CNHJPY surged to a new all-time high (through 22) and CNY CFETS basket tested the 100 figure mark. These adjustments are, however, still too gradual in the context of CNH’s YTD outperformance vs. most G20 currencies (10% vs yen) and TWI’s 3% appreciation. Having been short CNH as one of our core trades for ’24, we continue to find it as expensive and find options implied probability of depreciation, over next 12 months, as too low.

That said, we expect the fixings’ crawl to remain managed and gradual - especially amidst the renminbi's historical characteristic of steadiness around major political events like 15th July Plenum. Growing expectations of JPY intervention, and tapering US exceptionalism could be other speed bumps. Nonetheless, with markets already priced for about seven Fed rate cuts in the coming two years, and lacklustre progress in China's activity/credit data, we see the bar for a reversal as quite high and expect 7.35-40 range to be the landing point for Q3.

Meanwhile, US elections (and tariffs') related risk premia is likely to firm up in coming weeks. Even if the renminbi adjusts only reactively and gradually to the tariffs, and not pre-emptively, CNH derivatives and regional low yielders could begin building this premium sooner. Sticking with our long 9m \$CNH OTM digi calls recommendation, we highlight USD/TWD ratio call spreads as an asymmetric opportunity into the July dividends. Vice-versa, for investors looking to hedge JPY intervention/US activity data risks in the near-term, KRW still screens stretched on our fair value metrics.

## Executive Summary

## CNY fixings’ adjustment gathers pace as CNH/JPY makes new highs

The recent adjustment in fixings’ followed two major developments – CNH/JPY cross surged to an all-time high, trading through a psychologically important level of 22 (10% YTD). At the same time, the RMB CFETS basket occasionally traded through the 100 figure mark in Q2. With Yen making up for about 9% weight in PBoC’s CFETS basket, exchange rate competitiveness preservation could potentially have been a catalyst behind the recent trend.

## Modest adjustment or a shift in policy priorities?

With CNY fixings' CCF factor at record wides of 1400+ pips, it might be premature to read the recent fixings' movements as a shift in policy priorities. In fact, we find the recent developments as congruous to a 3Q22 style managed catch down (i.e. a NEER re-calibrating exercise that sent fixings from \~7.10 to \~7.25 back then). Mounting signs of tapering US exceptionalism could also limit greenback upside.

 As such, ongoing CNY adjustment would continue to be a managed and orderly process, with 7.35-7.40 likely to be the next landing zone for USD/CNH in Q3.

## US elections/Tariffs risks are brewing: Lessons from ‘18/19

Even if an extended run of US downside data surprises were to take some pressure off USD/CNH, we believe the US elections/ tariffs expectations would be a headwind for the CNH and TWI basket. With ex-President Trump's lead in polls widening recently, his intent to significantly raise tariffs on China, and CNH's track record of steep depreciation with the '18-19 tariffs, we expect downside pressure on the renminbi to persist. Looking back in history, we'd note that:

a) RMB lost \~6.5% due to the '18/19 tariffs (a % TWI depreciation for every 3ppt surge in tariffs). Note: Expectations for tariffs' surge this round are higher than in '18-19, while TWI has also pared back all of that decline.

b) Back then, CNY moves followed official tariff announcements, rather than pre-empting them. Although history doesn't have to repeat itself, we expect it would be a pressure point for the CNH and TWI. Even if the renminbi is only allowed to reactively, and gradually, adjust to any future tariffs, pressure on CNH derivatives and regional low yielders could firm up. While sticking with our view to be long 9m \$CNH OTM digi calls, we also highlight USD/TWD call spreads as an opportunity into the dividends season.

## Upcoming Plenum meeting could be a speed breaker in the near term

Even though CNH’s spread over fixings has widened to about 10y highs, our CNH stress indicator suggests little risk of further policy pushback. However, we note that historically the RMB has generally stayed stable during important political events like the 15th July Plenum

Thereafter, if the dollar strength persists at the same pace, and there is no material policy easing announcement to revive growth expectations, the recent trend of CNY alignment would resurface to correct TWI’s YTD strength.

## Potential JPY intervention/US payrolls pull back are two imminent risks...

… that we see to the short CNH trades. Exactly two months ago, these two risks teamed up to cause a short-lived scare of \$/Asia unwinds. Of the two, we believe the former is a more potent risk today given growing MoF warnings on JPY intervention. As a reference, during JPY’s late April/early May 3.5% pullback, CNH, KRW and SGD strengthened a % each. For investors looking to hedge this event risk, USD/SGD puts offer the cheapest convex hedge.

The risk of a pull-back today is much lower than in early May, amidst a very different set up for US growth/inflation expectations. The Apr/May \$/Asia pullback coincided with: a) 2y UST yields’ YTD peak, b) almost a 50bp surge in 2y yields in the preceding month, c) peak momentum in US GDP growth upgrades, with upside beats in all NFP prints through Q1. Today, a) 2y UST yields have already receded 25bps in June, b) markets are priced for more than five cuts by end ’25 vis-à-vis about three on the May FOMC day, and c) tapering US growth exceptionalism is already a widespread narrative with carry/ policy divergence narrative/political risks elsewhere keeping the greenback resilient.

That’s not to say that CNH would be completely unimpacted in a scenario of very weak payrolls. But, we see risk of such an outcome as low given stability in the employment sub-indices of June flash PMIs. Nonetheless, acknowledging this risk, we'd stick with a relative value strategy in Asia with INR, PHP and KRW our most preferred currencies and CNH the least alongside THB and IDR.

## Consider USDTWD ratio call spreads into July dividends

Since early '23, KRW, THB and TWD have been most sensitive to CNH with betas of 1, 1, 0.8, respectively. This sensitivities’ ranking of the low yielders, however, is similar in scenarios of a JPY pull back. As such, a defensive/asymmetric way to position for a period of extended CNH weakness in the near term, over and above 9m \$CNH OTM digi calls, could be \$TWD.

Seasonally, \$/TWD tends to find an asymmetrical support from the large dividends payout in July. We note that July '24’s 8bn\$ dividends’ payout pipeline would be the most crowded cluster of the year, likely explaining TWD TWI’s July underperformance in the past few years. As such, we find USD/TWD 1m ATMS/1.5% OTMS ratio call spreads (1:1.5x), with max payout at expiry of 5x, as an asymmetric near-term hedge. On a relative basis as well, we see scope for TWD to pare back its recent outperformance vs. KRW, with the latter likely finding support from BoK’s likely support at 1400 and signs of momentum on Korea’s progress on index inclusion.
