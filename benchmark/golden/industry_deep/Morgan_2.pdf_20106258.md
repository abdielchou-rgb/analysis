## Oil Demand Tracker

## Unseasonably warm weather continues to negatively impact January oil demand

• Our high-frequency demand indicators are tracking a 1.6 month-over-month decline in global oil demand in January (Figure 1). While this reading compares favorably to the pre-COVID five-year average MoM drop of 2.3 mbd for January, on an absolute level demand for the month is likely to end 400 kbd below our expectations. On an annualized basis this equates to a 30 kbd hit to our 1.5 mbd global oil demand growth. The deviation from our estimates was likely caused by lower heating oil usage due to an unseasonably warm January.

Regionally, the US and Japan reported their oil consumption statistics.

Our Europe demand tracker now sees demand undershooting our 13.5 mbd estimate for January by 100 kbd, while early in the month it was tracking higher by as much as 300 kbd. This is likely due to warmer than expected weather and lower flight activity affected by strikes across the region (Figure 18).

• US PSM statistics for November reported a 496 kbd YoY gain in demand led by higher LPG and “other products<sup>”</sup> consumption. In terms of the fuel mix, most of the gains in demand were in the liquids, while petroleum-based products reported only a modest 40 kbd YoY increase.

US gasoline demand improved over the week as weather normalized but continues to track 8.0 mbd for January (Figure 8). EIA statistics suggest US gasoline demand averaged 8.2 mbd so far in January. Both figures are tracking below our published estimates by 400 kbd. Meanwhile, distillates demand in the US continues to improve with the four-week average now at a five-week high of 3.7 mbd.

China demand in January was tracking 15.5 mbd, 500 kbd below our published estimates as heating fuel demand remains poor. For comparison, winter weather in East Asia, China included, recorded 741 HDDs, 40 HDDs lower than the 10-year average in January. Meanwhile, trucks on Chinese expressways were above the 52-million mark for a third week in a row.

• Japan<sup>’</sup>s oil demand contracted sharply by 234 kbd YoY in December, leading to an overall 59 kbd YoY contraction in 2023. Despite normal winter weather in the region in December, including Japan, heating fuel demand declined as increased use of nuclear power displaced fuel oil and kerosene.

Global jet fuel demand expanded 500 kbd YoY for the week ending January 26. Global flights improved to 97.1% of 2019 as US international flights rose to an eight-week high of 100.4% and Chinese flights climbed to 106.4% of 2019 volumes. European flights stagnated close to their March 2023 levels at 89.8% of 2019 levels for a second week in a row.

## Methodology

Our global demand tracker calculates implied demand formulated as:

Daily total oil products demand = total refinery output<sup>(1)</sup> + biofuels blending + daily net imports of products + daily change in products stocks

<sup>(1)</sup> Total refinery output is computed as: daily crude runs \* products yie ${ \mathrm { \Delta \ l d ^ { ( 2 ) } + N G L s ^ { ( 3 ) * } } }$ products yields

<sup>(2)</sup> Product yields are for LPG, Naphtha, Gasoline, Diesel/Gasoil, Fuel Oil, and other products

<sup>(3)</sup>NGLs are used to generate ethane, propane, butane, and pentane. We have applied different yields based on that for LPG and other products to estimate NGLs derived liquids demand.

Our US gasoline demand tracker calculates daily implied gasoline consumption using the formula:

implied gasoline demand = daily crude runs \* US gasoline yield + ethanol blending + net gasoline imports

Data source: Refinery runs and outages are sourced from IIR, Product yields are sourced from WoodMac, Trade flows from Kpler, Biofuels from Rystad, and products stocks from Platts.
