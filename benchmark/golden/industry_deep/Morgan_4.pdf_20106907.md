# China: Strong growth in Jan-Feb exports

Expect steady growth trend in export activity this year

China<sup>’</sup>s Jan-Feb merchandise exports (in USD terms) came in significantly above expectations, growing 7.1%oya, or rising by average 7.5% m/m sa compared to the December level. Nominal imports came in moderately above expectations, growing 3.5%oya in Jan-Feb, or rising 1.6% m/m sa from the December level. The combined Jan-Feb trade surplus came in at US\$125.2bn.

By product breakdown, exports of low-end consumer goods continued to outperform, showing average increase of 11.5% m/m sa in Jan-Feb. Meanwhile, exports of exports of high-tech exports rose by average 3.5% m/m sa.

By major markets, exports to the EU showed strong growth at average 5.6% m/m sa in Jan-Feb, along with solid export growth to the US (+3.7% m/m sa). Regional trade also showed strong growth pace, as China<sup>’</sup>s exports to ASEAN rose by average 8.0% m/m sa in Jan-Feb, with exports to Taiwan up 8.2% m/m sa. Exports to Latam and Africa also showed strong growth momentum.

On the import side, processing imports showed significant growth in Jan-Feb, while major commodity imports (crude oil, iron ore, copper) showed somewhat mixed performance (following solid gain in December).

China<sup>’</sup>s export activity generally held up better than expected in 2023, benefiting from its diversification efforts across major markets (EM Asia, LatAm, Russia and Africa) and by products (with rapid expansion in new energy products and motor vehicles).

Going into 1Q24, the strong Jan-Feb export growth came on the back of constructive signals from a few recent indicators, including China<sup>’</sup>s manufacturing PMI, latest signals from alt-data, and positive signals from other Asian exports (including Taiwan<sup>’</sup>s export orders report).

Looking ahead, we look for relatively steady growth trend on China<sup>’</sup>s export activity for 2024 (with some possible payback to the significant Jan-Feb strength in the coming months) amid steady global growth outlook. Following solid manufacturing FAI growth and capacity expansion in the past two years, and amid lingering softness in domestic demand conditions, Chinese exporters will continue to push for expanding their global market share. Trade partners potential reactions will however become a growing concern this year.

China<sup>’</sup>s export activity beat expectations significantly in January-February. Merchandise exports (in USD terms) showed strong growth of 7.1%oya in Jan-Feb (J.P. Morgan: 0.3%; consensus: 1.9%), compared to the moderate rise of 2.3%oya in December. In sequential terms, we estimate that average Jan-Feb exports jumped 7.5% m/m sa compared to the December level.

Imports also came in moderately above expectations, rising 3.5%oya (J.P. Morgan: +2.1%; consensus: +2.0%), following growth of 0.2%oya in December. Seasonally adjusted, we estimate average Jan-Feb nominal imports increased 1.6%m/m sa from the December level. As a result,the trade surplus came in at US \$125.2 billion in Jan-Feb (J.P. Morgan: \$110.0bn; consensus: \$106.8bn), compared to \$75.3bn in December (and \$823.2 billion for full-year 2023).

Broad-based, strong export growth to major markets; exports of consumer goods outperformed

To some extent, the strong Jan-Feb export growth data came on the back of constructive signals from a few other indicators, including the manufacturing PMIs (the export order component of Caixin manufacturing PMI has risen for three consecutive months, coming in at 50.8 in February, highest level in 12 months), latest signals from alt-data, as well as constructive messages from other major Asian exporters (including significant upside surprise in Taiwan<sup>’</sup>s January export orders report). On the global front, there have been signs of recovery in global industry activity as suggested by the latest global manufacturing PMI readings.

In further breakdown, low-end consumer goods exports continued to outperform, while exports of mechanical and electrical products as well as high-end products also showed solid growth. Specifically, exports of low-end consumer goods (including garments, clothing, footwear and toys) showed strong average increase of 11.5% m/m sa in Jan-Feb from the December level (adding to the gain of 2.4% m/m sa in December). Besides, exports of highend products showed an average increase of 3.5% m/m sa in Jan-Feb from the December level (after edging up 0.1% m/m sa in December): mobile phone exports showed an average decline of 0.6% m/m sa (following the fall of 12.0% m/m sa in December); exports of integrated circuits showed average increase of 5.6% m/m sa, and exports of data processing machines showed average increase of 3.0% m/m sa. Besides, China<sup>’</sup>s auto exports (in US\$ terms) showed average decline of 6.4% m/m sa in Jan-Feb, following increase of 2.3% m/m sa in December.

By major market, for the DM markets, exports to Europe showed solid average gain of 5.6% m/m sa in Jan-Feb (vs. +5.6% m/m sa in December), and exports to the US grew by average 3.7% m/m sa (vs. -1.6% m/m sa in December), while exports to Japan fell by average 1.5% m/m sa (vs. +2.7% m/m sa in December). Meanwhile, exports to EM Asia showed average increase of 5.0% m/m sa in Jan-Feb (vs. +3.1% m/m sa in December), with an average 8.0% m/m sa rise in exports to ASEAN, 8.2% m/m sa gain in exports to Taiwan, along with an average decline of 3.8% m/m sa in exports to Korea. Regarding exports to other key EM economies, exports to Latin America showed average 17.6% m/m sa growth in Jan-Feb, exports to Africa showed average 21.8% m/m sa growth, while exports to Russia showed average decline of 0.4% m/m sa.

## Decent import growth in Jan-Feb, led by processing imports

Regarding the broad import categories, process imports, which accounted for 15.1% of total imports in 2023 (vs. 16.9% in 2022), showed significant average increase of 14.2% m/m sa in Jan-Feb (vs. -2.7% m/m sa in December). Meanwhile, ordinary imports (which accounted for 64.9% of total imports in 2023 or 63.7% in 2022) showed average decline of 2.7% m/m sa (vs. +6.5% m/m sa in December).

A breakdown by trading partner suggests imports from the US fell by average 1.8% m/m sa in Jan-Feb (vs. +5.7% m/m sa in December), imports from the EU fell 4.1% m/m sa (vs. +3.2% m/m sa in December), imports from Japan fell 3.6% m/m sa (vs. +1.9% m/m sa in December). Meanwhile, imports from EM Asia edged up by average 0.1% m/m sa in Jan-Feb, with solid gain in imports from Korea (+2.9% m/m sa) and Taiwan (+2.8% m/m sa). In addition, regarding imports from major commodity exporting countries, imports from Brazil fell by average 2.8% m/m sa in Jan-Feb (vs. +15.3% m/m sa in December), imports from Australia fell 2.8% m/m sa (vs. +11.7% m/m sa in December), while imports from Russia fell 3.5% m/ sa (vs. +3.5% m/m sa in December).

Regarding energy commodity imports, in volume terms, imports of crude oil fell by average 11.3% m/m sa in Jan-Feb (vs. +18.1% m/m sa in December), while coal imports fell 13.3% m/m sa (vs. -0.3% m/m sa in December). Regarding imports of key industrial metals, import volumes of iron ore edged down by average 0.2% m/m sa in Jan-Feb (vs. +5.9% m/m sa in December), while copper imports increased 7.1% m/m sa (vs. -13.8% m/m sa in December). Besides, imports of mechanical and electrical products fell by average 1.4% m/m sa in Jan-Feb (vs. +2.8% m/m sa in December), and imports of high-tech products increased 1.9%m/m sa (vs. +1.3% in December).

## Outlook for steady growth in export activity for 2024

Looking back, China<sup>’</sup>s export sector performance held up better than expected in 2023: although exports fell 4.6%yoy in nominal US\$ terms, it was largely driven by price effect, while export volume grew 4.2%%yoy, outperforming the sluggish trend in global manufacturing activity. China<sup>’</sup>s share of the global exports market remained relatively stable at 14.4% in the first three quarters of 2023 (vs. 14.2% in 2022). The resilience in China<sup>’</sup>s export sector performance reflects diversification in China<sup>’</sup>s export markets (from G3 to EM economies, including EM Asia, LatAm, Africa and Russia), as well as product group diversification towards middle- and upper-middle value-added products (including auto, solar cell and lithium battery, and in the broader category of mechanical and electrical products). In addition to trade diversification, Chinese companies have also accelerated the pace of overseas investment, e.g., opening factories in ASEAN, Mexico and other countries, considering lower production costs, bypassing tariff concerns, along with support from the government<sup>’</sup>s Belt-and-Road Initiatives.

Going ahead, we look for relatively steady growth in China<sup>’</sup>s export activity for 2024 (with some possible payback to the significant Jan-Feb strength in the coming months). On the external front, the J.P. Morgan global team looks for the global economy to show steady growth in the coming quarters, with global growth at 2.3% ar in 1H24, along with signs of recovery in global industry activity as suggested by the latest global manufacturing PMI readings. In addition, following solid manufacturing investment, and hence capacity expansion growth, in the past two years, and amid lingering softness in domestic demand conditions, Chinese exports will continue to push for expanding their share of the global export market. Meanwhile, aggressive price competition and China<sup>’</sup>s large manufacturing capacity may lead to a disruptive impact and trigger counter measures by competing trading partners. For instance, in the auto sector, the EU and the US have raised concerns about unfair competition related to government subsidy and low-price dumping. Such developments could backfire on China<sup>’</sup>s export sector.
