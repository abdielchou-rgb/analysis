# A Look at 1QTD Active Mutual Fund Performance and Recent Flow Trends

## Net flows were mixed 1QTD, Jan. MF fund performance weaker

In this report, we assess investment performance trends of the largest activelymanaged mutual fund categories for each traditional asset manager in our coverage universe, using data from Morningstar Direct. Overall, relative investment performance in January mostly weakened versus 4Q23 across our coverage (albeit it is still early in the quarter). However, we note that Janus Henderson Group (JHG-Hold) has exhibited notable performance improvement across all periods in 1Q so far. In contrast, performance for T.Rowe Price (TROW-Hold) in 4Q23 and January appears to remain challenged. We see the active fund industry as having broadly underperformed passive ETFs in 1Q so far, though with a lesser magnitude compared to 4Q23. Net flows across our coverage are mixed YTD so far, however, there are some bright spots including robust iShares inflows at BlackRock (BLK-Buy), positive long-term flows reported in January for Invesco (IVZ-Hold), and strong money market inflows for Federated Hermes (FHI-Buy).

## Our methodology on tracking relative performance

In the analysis, we aggregate each firms' mutual funds by its Morningstar investment objective category, and then determine the percentile investment performance ranking in that category that those funds represent, asset-weighting those funds within each category over the periods cited above. We then color code the percentile rankings, with a ranking in the top 1% being the best (bright green) and 100% being the worst (bright red). We assess the largest fund categories across equity, fixed income, and blended investment objectives, to the extent we can measure \~50-75% of each firm's total long-term mutual fund AuM available in Morningstar, on a globally domiciled basis (with the US representing the largest domicile). Lastly, we sum the total AuM measured and asset-weight the relative percentile rankings at the bottom to determine an overall asset-weighted percentile ranking for each firm and for each period (quarterly, 1-year and 3-year) and going back quarterly to 1Q19.

While this is only one measure toward assessing each firm's competitiveness and future organic growth potential based on performance ranking, we think it can be helpful in future potential organic growth across various products for each manager, and how competitive a firm's performance stands overall, both historically and currently, as well as how it is trending. In particular, short-term performance trends (over 1, 2, or 3 quarters) can be leading indicators of more sustainable longer-term performance, especially where 1-year and 3-year trends begin to significantly impact sales and redemption trends.

## Active mutual fund performance trending weaker than passive 1QTD

In Figures 22 and 23, we show industry-wide aggregated performance of 24 of the largest investment objective categories for active US-domiciled mutual funds (12 for equity and 12 for fixed income), and compare the aggregated asset-weighted performance of these active mutual funds vs. the closest passive ETF product substitute, in our view. We then show the "alpha" for each strategy over each period (quarterly back to 1Q23 and annually back to 2009), as well as the % of funds in each category beating the passive ETF substitute. We then asset weight the alpha across categories for equity and fixed income to determine whether active equity and active fixed income are broadly outperforming ETF "benchmarks".

We see active equity funds have underperformed their passive ETF substitutes in 1Q so far (data through Feb 9), though by a smaller margin compared to 4Q23. This is consistent with a trend of underperformance for active equity funds in most years since the last 10 years, including the three most recent years from 2021 to 2023. We also see active fixed income underperforming passive substitutes in 1QTD, though with a very modest miss. We note that recent data for fixed income funds is a divergence from when active funds generated positive alpha in the first three quarters of 2023, and also outperformed passive substitutes in 2022 and 2021.

## Observable flow trends for asset managers in 1QTD

Below, we highlight observable net flow trends for each of the asset managers in our coverage for 1Q so far (through 02/09), using a combination of monthly reported flows and data from Morningstar Direct. We make the following observations:

AMG: Morningstar data shows mutual fund net outflows of \~\$2.7bn for 1QTD, driven mostly by Parnassus outflows of \$2.0bn.

BEN: The company cited in its monthly report that the increase in AUM from the acquisition of Putnam Investments, was partially offset by slight long-term net outflows in January. We see \~\$4.9bn of long-term mutual fund outflows that we can observe from Morningstar Direct data for 1Q so far (although BEN's institutional products are not captured).

BLK: Morningstar data shows BLK had inflows in iShares of \$21.3bn in 1QTD, driven by \$5.5bn of equity inflows & \$13.7bn of fixed income inflows.

FHI: Morningstar data shows \~\$0.3bn of long-term MF net outflows for 1QTD, driven mostly by \$0.6bn of equity outflows, while fixed income inflows were at \$0.3bn, multi-asset outflows were modest at \$31mn, and alternative flows were relatively flat. There was a \$9.1bn increase in money market mutual fund AuM in 1Q so far.

IVZ: The company reported \$1.7bn of net long-term inflows, and \$5.8bn of QQQ inflows for January (we estimate \$12.6bn of inflows for 1Q). Money market outflows were \$1.3bn during last month.

JHG: Morningstar data for 1QTD suggests net long-term outflows of \~\$0.3bn, which were led primarily by equity outflows of \$0.9bn and multi-asset outflows of \$0.2bn, partially offset by \$0.8bn of fixed income inflows, while alternatives outflows were modest at \$2mn. We estimate total inflows of \$3.2bn for the quarter.

TROW: The company reported \$4.8bn of net long-term outflows for January and we observe \$5.8bn of equity mutual fund outflows using data from Morningstar Direct (however, a portion these equity outflows may have been transitioned to collective investment trust vehicles).

For an investor who is long fixed-rate instruments (thus receiving these cash flows), increases in interest rates naturally lift the discount factors applied to the expected cash flows and thus cause a loss. The longer the maturity of a certain cash flow and the higher the move in the discount factor, the higher will be the loss. Upside surprises in inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most common adverse macroeconomic shocks to receivers. But counterparty exposure, issuer creditworthiness, client segmentation, regulation (including changes in assets holding limits for different types of investors), changes in tax policies, currency convertibility (which may constrain currency conversion, repatriation of profits and/or liquidation of positions), and settlement issues related to local clearing houses are also important risk factors. The sensitivity of fixed-income instruments to macroeconomic shocks may be mitigated by indexing the contracted cash flows to inflation, to FX depreciation, or to specified interest rates - these are common in emerging markets. The index fixings may - by construction - lag or mis-measure the actual move in the underlying variables they are intended to track. The choice of the proper fixing (or metric) is particularly important in swaps markets, where floating coupon rates (i.e., coupons indexed to a typically short-dated interest rate reference index) are exchanged for fixed coupons. Funding in a currency that differs from the currency in which coupons are denominated carries FX risk.
