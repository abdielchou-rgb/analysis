## May 13

Tsys trade in narrow range; Weak China credit, inflation data rekindle debt deflation risks; European rates trade sideways; NOK shows slight gains, with rise in Brent; PHP weakens with BSP in focus; April NBP Minutes cite case for on-hold decision; DXY at 105.22 (-0.1%); US 10y at 4.487% (-1.0bp).

• A narrow trading range defines moves in US Treasuries; yields return near Friday's closing levels after NY Fed median one-year-ahead inflation expectations rise to 3.26% in April (P: 3.00%).

• Very weak China credit and inflation data for April keep risk of debtdeflation alive, with an unexpected contraction in new total social financing for the first time since October 2005.

• European duration trades sideways; 10y BTP-Bund spread widens \~1bp to 135bp.

• NOK flashes slight outperformance against USD and EUR, alongside a recovery in Brent crude oil prices above \$83/bbl.

• USD/PHP gains 0.8% to 57.87 as market participants anticipate a possible dovish turn by the BSP in its policy meeting on Thursday.

• Minutes from the April NBP meeting reveal “it was emphasised that should energy prices be raised, inflation might rise significantly in the second half of 2024," and it was against this background that the Council kept interest rates unchanged.

## Japan:

JGBs sold off modestly early in the Tokyo morning on Monday, after USTs bearflattened on Friday, ahead of the BoJ JGB purchase operations for the 1-3y, 5-10y, and 10-25y sectors. Although market participants had expected a status quo outcome for the purchase sizes, the BoJ surprisingly reduced the purchase size for the 5-10y sector, from JPY 475bn to JPY 425bn. After the announcement at 10:10am JST, the 10y sector began to underperform on the curve; JGB futures also sold off instantaneously. In the Tokyo afternoon session, the 20y sector began to underperform on the curve after a weak JGB purchase outcome for the 10-25y sector. 20y JGBs underperformed and sold off by 4.5bp d/d to 1.740% on a simple yield basis. Although 30y JGB yields rose to \~2.0%, there seemingly was less demand to buy the dip from the real money community; 30y JGBs sold off by 3.0bp d/d to 2.025% on a simple yield basis.

Upcoming supply events may be catalysts for upward pressure on yields: the 5y JGB auction on Tuesday, the 20y JGB auction on Thursday, and another BoJ purchase operation on Friday. Market participants are focused on whether the BoJ will reduce the purchase size for the 3-5y sector. TONA OIS bear-steepened across the curve, but outperformed JGBs broadly. XCCY basis was essentially unchanged from Friday, across the curve.

During Tokyo trading hours on Monday, USD/JPY traded choppy. Early in the Tokyo morning, USD/JPY climbed toward the 155.96 level after the rise in UST yields on Friday. After the BoJ announced a reduction in its JGB purchase amount, USD/JPY fell to \~155.50 instantaneously, but the pair quickly rebounded toward the 155.90 level and then traded range-bound. Although some market participants argued that the possibility of a reduction in BoJ JGB purchase sizes would come because of JPY weakness, there seemingly was a less persistent impact. More primary drivers of price action will be US CPI and the direction of UST yields. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY; EUR/JPY continued to rise to 168.10 toward the London morning.

## Europe:

G10 currencies traded in relatively tight ranges at the start of the week. Performance was mixed, as low-yielding currencies weakened against the US dollar while the remainder posted small gains; USD/JPY (+0.3%) rose back above the 156.00 level. A rise in the New York Fed’s 1y consumer inflation expectations measure was a source of strength to the US dollar at 11:00am ET, although the DXY USD Index (-0.1%) still ended the session slightly lower. NOK outperformed, 0.2% stronger against USD and 0.1% against EUR, as Brent crude oil prices rebounded above \$83/bbl.

European duration traded sideways without data releases or comments from ECB members. 10y Bund yields slipped \~1bp to 2.51%, with an intra-session low of 2.48%.

In the London afternoon, the release of New York Fed 1y inflation expectations at 3.26% in April (P: 3.00%) weighed somewhat on duration. In EUR swaps, curves closed little changed while the 2s5s10s butterfly richened \~1bp. Core ASW cheapened; Bobl and Bund ASW led (-0.5bp). In the G4, 10y BTP-Bund spreads widened over 1bp to 135bp, while semi-core spreads were little changed.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly on Monday after weak China credit data. China new total social financing contracted for the first time since October 2005, a reflection of still sluggish domestic demand; CNY depreciated 0.1% to 7.2345 per USD. MYR also depreciated 0.1% to 4.75 per USD, largely due to spillover effects from the China credit data. PHP was the main underperformer; USD/PHP rose 0.8% to 57.87 as market participants anticipated a possible dovish turn by the BSP in its policy meeting. Both THB and IDR depreciated 0.2% alongside flat equity prices and underperformance of local bonds. TWD and KRW relatively underperformed, unchanged at 32.42 and 1,368 per USD, respectively. USD/INR remained at 83.52.

Most AxJ rates sold off on Monday, following the rise in UST yields on Friday. China was the exception as weak credit data increased the possibility of more monetary easing; the CNY NDIRS curve shifted 6bp lower. The 1s30s CGB curve bullsteepened by 4bp amid China’s issuance plan for ultra-long special CGBs. India rates also rallied after the RBI began to buy back short-term G-sec with a ceiling of US \$7.2bn; 2y G-sec yields fell 1bp while the NDOIS curve fell 2bp. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bear-steepened by 1bp while the 3s10s KTB yield curve shifted 2bp higher. Both Singapore and Hong Kong swap curves shifted 4bp higher. In Indonesia, 10y INDOGB yields rose 2bp to 6.97%.

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
