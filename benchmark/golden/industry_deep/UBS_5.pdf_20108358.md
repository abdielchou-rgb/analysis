# China Economic Perspectives China by the Numbers (May 2024)

## Our guide to Chinese monthly data

What the numbers are, what they mean, and the outlook going forward.

## April domestic growth momentum moderated

We estimate that the five-year CAGR of major domestic economic activities slowed in April, including IP, retail sales, and infrastructure FAI. In particular, two more working days in April 2024 than April 2023 helped lift better-than-expected IP y/y growth, while depressing holiday consumption and service growth. Property activities stayed in deep y/ y contraction, with seasonally adjusted property sales dipping down further from March, while new starts showed a tentative sign of stabilization but still well below the Q1 level. Infrastructure FAI softened. On the positive side, manufacturing FAI growth stayed robust y/y and edged up on a CAGR basis. In addition, export growth picked up and beat expectations, thanks to robust global demand and global tech upcycle.

## Property purchase intention weak, price expectation soft

In the latest UBS China housing survey, property purchase intentions for the next 2 years stayed weak at 23%, the same level as in Mar 2023 while edging up seasonally from Aug 2022 (18%). Confidence in committing to buying a property stayed soft, and more respondents than before expect property prices to decline rather than increase in the next 12 months. While most people still have paper gain on their property and mortgage LTV remains low, property price decline has already suppressed consumer confidence and spending, and further price declines would mean an additional negative impact.

## More property policy easing on the way

Following the April Politburo meeting, several cities relaxed HPR and/or announced plans to acquire existing homes for social housing or subsidize household purchase of new properties. On May 17th, the government cut nationwide mortgage down-payment ratio by 5ppts, removed mortgage rate floors, and cut housing provident fund rate by 25bps. The central government encourages local governments to directly purchase or fund the purchase of unsold properties from developers. We think low-cost funding by the central bank through the banking system could help destocking, though total funding scale remains unclear. For a start, the PBC set up a RMB 300bn lending facility on May 17th, which can lever up RMB 500bn bank lending. Meanwhile, issuance of special CGB started last week, and that of special LGB is also set to accelerate. We expect the PBC to ensure ample liquidity by cutting RRR and using other liquidity facilities. We don’t expect any MLF rate cut, while LPR cut is possible in the rest of 2024.

## Growth may slow sequentially in Q2 but lower base will support y/y growth

Property activities and prices have been falling further, and housing purchase sentiment remains weak. Our baseline remains that property sales and new starts will likely bottom in the next 2-3 months, with the help of ongoing policy easing. This can help stabilize the weak household confidence and consumption. With base effect turning favorable in the coming months, we believe exports should stay in decent y/y growth in Q2 and early Q3. Overall, following a better-than-expected Q1 growth, we expect the sequential momentum to slow in Q2, though the y/y growth may stay at around 5.3% thanks to a low base last year. In the base case of property being stabilized, we expect growth momentum to stabilize at 4-5% in the second half of the year, and full year GDP growth to record 4.9%.

## UBS Activity Indicators

 UBS Physical Activity Index (3mma) picked up in April (to 2.5%y/y from 1% on 3mma basis), mainly helped by stronger-than-expected industrial production, which more the offset the weakness in construction, slower transportation activities and softer power generation.

 UBS Expenditure index moderated in April, o/w investment, consumption, and net exports growth all edged down on 3mma basis from March.

## Business Indicators

April NBS manufacturing PMI edged down by 0.4ppt to 50.4, after a surprising jump in March. New orders slid by 1.9ppt to 51.1 while production index improved by 0.7ppt to 52.9, indicating a mixed picture of manufacturing activities. New export orders also weakened by 0.7ppt to 50.6, while import index dipped by 2.3ppt to 48.1. Raw material inventory stayed below 50 at 48.1. Quantity of purchase and finished goods slid by 2.2/1.6ppt to 50.5/47.3, respectively. Input price rebounded by 3.5ppt to 54, while output price turned less bad by 1.7ppt to 49.1. Employment index edged down by 0.1ppt to 48, pointing to lingering labor market pressures. On the other hand, Caixin manufacturing PMI edged up by 0.3ppt to a 14-month-high reading of 51.4, with both new orders and output index picking up, especially the former. As we discussed before, such divergence between NBS and Caixin PMIs is not uncommon, largely due to the difference in survey sample selection, while we may need other high frequency data to cross-check the underlying strength of the manufacturing sector.

NBS non-manufacturing PMI softened by 1.8ppt to 51.2, staying >50 consecutively since January 2023. In particular, services PMI slid by 2.1ppt to 50.3, while construction PMI held up at 56.3, reflecting robust momentum in construction activities. By sector, PMIs of railway transport, road transport, post, telecom / broadcast / TV were above 55, while capital market and real estate services were much weaker, the latter echoing continued property downturn yearto-date.

## Property

Property sales stayed in deep y/y contraction. April property sales declined by -22.8%y/y from -18.3%y/y in March, while new starts contracted by another - 14%y/y. After seasonal adjustment, new starts volume showed a tentative sign of stabilization with its April reading marginally better than March (though still notably lower than Q1 average), while property sales volume clearly has not bottomed yet, dropping down further from March and Q1 level. Property completion also stayed weak at -19%y/y (vs -22%y/y before). Property investment stayed weak at -10.5%y/y (vs -10.1%y/y previously). Developers’ source of funds remained in deep contraction of -21%y/y in April (slightly smaller than -29%y/y in March). The NBS revised up 2023 level base of property sales and investment by 11% and 4% in April (after revising down both by 6% in Q1).

Property sales volume to stabilize in the coming months; downside risks remain. The current sharp property downturn was driven by a combination of weakening fundamentals, previous policy tightening, and subdued home purchase intention. The government started to ease policies in Q4 2022 and more notably after the July 2023 Politburo meeting, The April 2024 Politburo meeting called for ensuring housing delivery and destocking. On May 17th, the government cut nationwide mortgage down-payment ratio by 5ppts, removed mortgage rate floors, and cut housing provident fund rate by 25bps. The central government encourages local governments to directly purchase or fund the purchase of unsold properties from developers. We think low-cost funding by the central bank through the banking system could help destocking, though total funding scale remains unclear. For a start, PBC set up a RMB 300bn lending facility, which can lever up RMB 500bn bank lending. Our baseline remains that property sales and new starts will likely bottom in the next 2-3 months, with the help of ongoing policy easing. Overall, we expect a similar fall in property sales/starts/ investment in 2024, by 5-10%/15-20%/5-10%, respectively, vs -8.5%/-20.4%/- 9.6% in 2023.

## Fixed Asset Investment

Overall FAI growth slowed to 3.6%y/y in April from 4.7%y/y in March. Infrastructure FAI growth softened to 5.9%y/y (vs 8.6%y/y in March), with its CAGR since 2019 slowing. Previous funds raised from the RMB 1trn special CGB issued in Q4 2023 likely continued to support public investment, but new issuance of special LG bonds has been slow YTD. LGFV financing has been restricted, and local government land sales revenue declined in Jan-Apr. Meanwhile, manufacturing investment growth stayed robust and only edged down to 9.3%y/y in April (vs 10.3%y/y in March), while its five-year CAGR since 2019 edged up slightly. Further considering the softness in property investment, overall FAI growth slowed on both y/y and CAGR basis. Note that NBS continued to revise down 2023 FAI level base by 3% in April following its previous cut of -11% in Q1.

Expect both infrastructure and manufacturing growth to slow in 2024. The central government plans to provide more explicit support to infrastructure FAI, with RMB 1trn of special CGB and RMB 3.9trn of special LG bonds in 2024. However, LGFV financing remains restricted, while local land sales is still under pressure. We see modest deceleration of infrastructure investment in the coming months to mid-single-digit y/y growth from 8.8%y/y in Q1. We expect full year growth slowing to 5-6% in 2024 (vs 8.2% in 2023) with downside risks. We see more growth potential in new energy investment, hydraulic & water projects, citycluster transport networks and public utility and facilities. In the manufacturing sector, we believe FAI in the sectors along the property supply chain will likely be held back by weak demand and profits, although FAI related to the green transition should stay robust while FAI along the tech supply chain could improve. Export recovery could also support manufacturing FAI. Overall, we see manufacturing investment growing by 5-6% in 2024E (6.5% in 2023). See more in our 2024-25 outlook.

## Industrial Production

Industrial production growth improved to 6.7%y/y, better than expected. The official seasonally adjusted IP level picked up again by 1%m/m, after a shortlived marginal decline of -0.1%m/m in March. With two more working days in April 2024 than April 2023, holiday effect likely contributed meaningfully to the sequential strength. Meanwhile, we estimate that five-year CAGR of IP softened modestly from March, suggesting that underlying momentum weakened. In particular, VA growth of the auto sector rebounded to 16.3%y/y from 9.4%y/y previously, likely boosted by the expected demand from trade-in policy, while auto production growth bounced. VA growth of communication appliance also strengthened to 15.6%y/y from 10.6%, thanks to the upward tech cycle and robust external demand. Echoing this, production of cell phones rebounded to 6.9%y/y (vs -3.1% previously) and that of electronic ICs picked up to 31.9%y/y from 28.4%. Meanwhile, VA growth of ferrous metal and minerals stayed weak, while production of cement and steel stayed in y/y contraction, mirroring softness in construction activities.

China's inventory growth edged up further in March, to 2.5%y/y in nominal terms (vs 2.4%y/y previously) and to 4.9% in real terms (vs 4.7% previously). Industrial inventory to sales ratio (seasonally adjusted, 6mma) has risen notably since 2022, now still well above the historical average, which means it takes a few more months to destock.

## Consumption

Retail sales were surprisingly weak in April, growing only 2.3%y/y (vs 3.1% in March and 4.7%y/y in Q1). We estimate that the seasonally adjusted retail sales level (in both nominal and real terms) declined slightly m/m in April, while the official estimate suggests a positive but much slower sequential growth momentum (in nominal terms). The NBS explained that the shift in Labor Day holiday timing meant two fewer holidays in April 2024 vs April 2023, which contributed to over 1ppts to retail sales weakness. However, the five-year CAGR of nominal retail sales growth softened markedly in April from March, despite a lower CAGR base, suggesting weak underlying momentum beyond the holiday effect. By key category, auto sales (from -3.7%y/y to -5.6%y/y) dragged down headline y/ y growth by -0.6ppt. Property related products stayed weak, while going-out related products (cosmetics, clothing) turned to y/y decline. Catering sales and services sales growth slowed partly on high base y/y and holiday distortion.

Consumption recovery should continue at a slightly slower pace in 2024. Post-Covid consumption recovery in 2023 lagged that in other economies due to subdued income growth recovery and weak consumer confidence. Our estimated real retail sales and the official consumer confidence both bottomed around Q323 and have been improving but only slightly since then. Labour market remains stable with lingering pressures, while surveyed firm hiring expectation softened for Q224. Later in the year, the expected stabilization in property activities should prevent household confidence from sliding down further. That will likely help to support continued post-Covid consumption recovery along with the economy and labor market, though at a slower pace. Household saving rate stayed above pre-Covid 2019 level by 2ppt in Q124, indicating lingering cautious consumer sentiment and further accumulation of excess savings. Overall, we expect annual real consumption (GDP basis) to slow to 5.1% in 2024E from 6.4% in 2023.

## Inflation

CPI edged up to 0.3%y/y in April from 0.1% previously, slightly stronger than expected. Food inflation stabilised at -2.7%y/y despite lower base, o/w pork and vegetable price both improved to 1.3-1.4%y/y. Non-food inflation edged up to 0.9%y/y on a low base, o/w touring & outgoing price growth moderated to 4.1%y/y on high base, while fuel price picked up to 6.9%y/y thanks to low base. Core CPI edged up by 0.1ppt to 0.7%y/y. Sequentially, CPI edged up by 0.1%m/m in April, vs -1%m/m before. Food price declined by 1%m/m with largely unchanged pork prices. Non-food price rebounded to +0.3%m/m previously, o/w touring & outgoing price +2.7%m/m due to holiday demand, fuel price +2.9%m/ m, while utility prices stayed muted despite recent price hikes in some cities.

PPI declined less by 0.3ppt to -2.5%y/y in April thanks to low base, while it declined by -0.2%m/m sequentially (vs -0.1%m/m before). Investment goods improved by 0.4ppt to -3.1%y/y (-0.2%m/m), o/w mining & quarrying price narrowed its decline to -4.8%y/y (-1%m/m), raw material improved to -1.9%y/y (0.3%m/m), manufacturing price stabilized at -3.6%y/y (-0.4%m/m). Consumer goods edged up by 0.1ppt to -0.9%y/y (-0.1%m/m)

Expect positive but soft CPI inflation in 2024. Despite the uptick in April, YTD CPI growth stayed weaker-than-expected at 0.1%y/y and PPI declined by -2.6%y/ y, echoing still soft demand recovery but robust supply of goods and services. We continue to expect non-food consumer goods prices to increase slightly and pork prices to bottom out gradually in the rest of 2024. Full year CPI may only print 0.4%y/y in 2024 (vs 0.2% in 2023), given YTD softness, robust production activities and slow recovery of domestic demand. Manufacturing and consumer goods under PPI may stay soft in the coming months given relatively low capacityutilization rate. Raw materials prices may stabilize gradually after a sharp decline since end 2023. We therefore expect PPI to stay in y/y contraction in most of 2024 and only turn positive y/y in early 2025, printing full year with a decline of 1- 1.5% (from -3%y/y in 2023).

## Money & Credit

April new TSF turned negative surprisingly. New TSF printed a contraction of RMB 199bn in April, the first decline since Oct 2005. Slow government bond issuance is the key factor, with April's print much softer than a year ago (at RMB -98bn, -553bn y/y). Meanwhile, domestic credit demand stayed weak, with corporate bond issuance staying soft (RMB 49bn, -245bn y/y), and both M&L corporate loans (at RMB 410, -257bn y/y) and M&L household loans (at RMB - 167bn, -51bn y/y) fell short of a year ago. Undiscounted bills also saw a notable decline of RMB 449bn (-314bn y/y). In addition, regulation and statistical rule changes may have also weighed on credit growth, as the government just adjusted the estimation methodology of value added for the financial sector based on profit growth rather than deposit and loan growth before, and regulators forbade banks from offering deposit rates above the upper limit strictly. As such, credit growth slid further by another 0.3-0.4ppt to 8.4%y/y, and credit impulse declined more to -2.6% of GDP from -1.6% in March. See more in our comment.

Credit demand may improve modestly in the rest of 2024. Our baseline expects property sales and mortgage loans to stabilize gradually in the coming months, corporate credit demand to improve later in the year as growth momentum stabilizes and equipment upgrading demand materializes, and government bond issuance to pick up pace in the next few months. However, the new GDP estimation methodology and tighter financial regulation will continue to suppress credit growth in the rest of 2024, especially in Q2. Given the sharp disappointment in April credit data, the lingering headwinds from weak property activities and regulation rules changes, we revise down our 2024 year-end credit growth forecast from 9.3% previously to around 9%, albeit still with more downside risk to our new baseline forecast.

## Base Money & Liquidity Operations

 Base money growth slid by 0.6ppt to 4.8%y/y in April (3mma) from March, after adjusting for RRR cuts (e.g. Jan 2020, July/Dec 2021, Apr/Dec 2022, Mar/Sep 2023, Feb 2024 and various targeted RRR cuts) and changes from other liquidity facilities.

The PBC withdrew liquidity in the interbank market in April. The PBC withdrew liquidity in April, by RMB -400bn through OMO, RMB -70bn through MLF and RMB -343bn through PSL. The PBC cut RRR by 50bps on Feb 5, which brought down the overall weighted average RRR for financial institutions to 7.0% from 7.4% and released base money liquidity of around RMB 1trn, helping to keep overall liquidity ample (see RRR cut comment).

## Interest Rates & Bond Market

 REPO rate unchanged in 2024. Interbank rates typically move up near monthends on seasonal liquidity demand and edge down at the start of the following month. PBC cut REPO rate by 20bps in 2023 and kept it unchanged in 2024. DR007 averaged 1.94% in 2023 and hovered around 1.8-1.9% lately.

Yields edged down as property downturn deepened. 10-year CGB yield trended down since Q2 2023 due to subdued growth momentum, except for a period of modest rebound in Aug-Oct 2023 on signs of growth bottoming. Since late 2023, 10yr CGB yield has trended down again on expected policy easing and investors’ desire for safety assets, hovering around 2.3% lately. Corporate yields dropped as well, and corporate credit spreads narrowed modestly. April issuance of government bond was softer than a year ago while that of corporate bonds rebounded, as expected.

Likely no policy rate cut in 2024. In 2023, the PBC cut MLF rate by 25bps, 1yr LPR by 20bps and 5yr LPR by 10bps. Commercial banks were asked to lower rates of outstanding mortgages and deposit rates. In Feb 2024, PBC cut 5yr LPR by 25bps without cutting MLF rate. In light of strong USD and expectations of Fed rate cut only in Q3, we no longer expect the PBC to cut MLF policy rates this year. There may be room to guide banks’ LPR rate by another 10-20bps alongside with more reduction of bank deposit rates.

## Trade

Exports accelerated to 1.5%y/y growth in April from prior contraction of 7.5%. Imports also accelerated to 8.4%y/y growth. Monthly trade balance widened to \$72.4bn in April from \$58.5bn in March. Taking 2019 as the base, exports and imports grew by 8.6% and 4.0% in April, vs. priors of 7.1% and 5.8%, respectively. Full year exports contracted by 4.6%y/y and imports contracted by 5.5%y/y in 2023, after growing 5.6% and 0.7% in 2022. Real exports and imports expanded by 4.6%/2.9% in 2023.

Shipment to major destinations mostly improved, IT export growth accelerated. Shipments to the G3 (the US, the EU & Japan) registered narrower y/ y contraction at 2.9%. Shipments to the US only registered a 0.2%y/y contraction despite the high base. Export growth to ASEAN economics outperformed again, with y/y shipment growth accelerating to 11.1%y/y. Consistent with the tech cycle improvement, shipment growth of the IT basket accelerated to 11.4%y/y in April (from prior of 7.4%), with broad-based improvements across major products. Shipments of consumer goods displayed much narrower y/y contraction at 3.5% vs. prior of 17.2%.

A slight trade recovery in 2024. Trade growth data from neighbouring economies continued to suggest steady momentum of external demand. On the other hand, survey data shows a mixed picture of new orders. In our view, current data combination suggests that the external demand recovery has continued for China into 2Q24 with steady sequential momentum, though there is still limited evidence to suggest a boom for export demand. Given the statistical base is turning lower for the coming months, the steady sequential momentum shall likely support exports to stay in y/y growth in the coming months. We expect full year exports to record 3.5%y/y in 2024 (vs -5% in 2023, see latest view).

## FX Reserves and Capital Flows

 Headline FX reserves contracted by \$44.8bn to \$3.2trn in April vs. March. China’s FX reserves increased by \$110bn in 2023, declined by \$123bn in 2022, increased by \$34bn in 2021, following gains in 2019-20 and losses in 2018.

Valuation effect remained negative, widening trade balance provided support. We estimate the negative valuation effect from major reserve currencies' movement in April has likely been around \$19bn due to incremental USD strength at end-Apr vs end-Mar. Global financial market fluctuations might have brought additional valuation losses too, but widening trade balance from March should have offered some supports. Overall capital outflow pressure has likely continued. Meanwhile, the balance of total foreign holdings of China's onshore bonds registered another expansion of RMB 328bn in 1Q24, after RMB 474bn expansion in 4Q23. Foreign holding balance recorded a RMB 282bn gain in 2023 (contractions in most of the early months but turning positive since Sep), after a RMB 616bn loss in 2022 (including a sharp loss of RMB 740bn in the 10 consecutive months over Feb-Nov 2022). Partly helped by this, Q124 saw an inflow in overall non-FDI capital flows.

We expect the new normal to continue, with larger 2-way capital flows. The rise in capital outflows will mainly reflect non-government entities’ accumulation of overseas assets, especially as controls on capital outflows gradually ease over longer term. Recent sharp drop in FDI into China is partly due to decline in retained profits and high USD costs, but also reflects pressures of decoupling. We expect FDI to recover in 2024-25, but not back to its pre-pandemic levels. Meanwhile, we believe China's continued financial market opening and decent economic growth potential should continue to attract more inflows (see our deep dive).

## Exchange Rate

RMB depreciated by 0.3% against the USD by end-Apr vs. end-Mar, hovering around 7.23 recently. The strengthening USD DXY index is a key driver behind the CNY depreciation against the USD, while the CNY appreciated against the CFETs basket over April. Full year RMB weakened by 2% vs USD while strengthened by 1% against the basket in 2023. This follows 9% depreciation vs the USD and 2.4% weakness vs the basket in 2022.

RMB exchange rate has been driven by various factors. Resilient trade balance is a supporting factor, while the negative China-US rate differentials and concerns on China's growth momentum slowdown are main headwinds. The PBC lowered RRR for FX deposits by 200bp/100bp in Sep/Apr 2022. The SAFE raised the macroprudential adjustment parameter for cross-border financing from 1 to 1.25 in Oct 2022, and further to 1.5 in July 2023.

RMB may strengthen to 7.1-7.2 range by end 2024E. Similar to the recent depreciations, the dynamics of the interest rate differentials, USD index, as well as China's growth data and property market weakness, may still bring episodes of CNY weakness ahead. That said, PBC will likely use various tools (including counter-cyclical factor in daily fixing) to prevent further CNY weakness much beyond 7.3 amid rapid episodes of depreciation and significant increase of volatility. Given our expectation of a stabilization of China’s growth momentum as well as the expected Fed interest rate cuts, we continue to expect RMB to strengthen a bit by end-2024E to 7.1-7.2.

## Fiscal

Fiscal revenue weakened further while expenditure rebounded y/y in April. Headline fiscal revenue declined by -3.7%y/y In April from -2.4%y/y in March (-2.3%y/y in Q1). On a comparable basis (after controlling a high base due to delayed SME tax collection and carry-over of tax cut since mid-2023), the underlying fiscal revenue grew +2%y/y in Jan-Apr (vs headline -2.7%y/y). Fiscal expenditure rebounded to 6.1%y/y in April from -2.9%y/y in March (+2.9%y/y in Q1). Corporate income tax revenue cooled to 0.7%y/y in April (1%y/y in Q1), while personal income tax slid again to -18.8%y/y on a high base (-4.5%y/y in Q1). As such, April saw a headline fiscal surplus of RMB 42bn, smaller than the average level in previous years. Local land revenue declined by another -21%y/y in April from -19%y/y in March (-6.7%y/y in Q1).

Further progress in addressing LGFV debt issue. Local governments issued RMB 1.39 trillion of special refinancing LG bonds to swap for qualified LGFV debt and/or pay arrears to corporate suppliers in H2 2023. In 2024, we expect additional issuance of special refinancing LG bonds and banks to increase restructuring of LGFV bank loans, while YTD issuance has been very limited at RMB 84bn. We think the long-term solution (e.g. adjustment of central-local fiscal relations and debt structure) may not come out soon but possibly be discussed in the upcoming 3rd Plenum (see NPC takeaway and Politburo meeting takeaway).

Modest fiscal support in a more explicit way. China announced a headline general fiscal deficit of 3% of GDP, additional RMB 1 trillion special CGB and RMB 3.9 trillion special LG bonds for 2024. The combined budget deficit (general fiscal + govt funds) on a cash basis is estimated to be 8.2% of GDP (+1.2ppts larger 2023 outturn). We expect the Augmented Fiscal Deficit (AFD) to expand by 1ppt in 2024 after no increase in 2023. The government called for faster issuance of special CGB and special LGB, and avoiding fiscal tightening like H1 2023. Overall fiscal support will be more explicit in stronger on-budget spending, especially from central government. We think local government land sales revenue may decline by another 10% in 2024, while off-budget LGFV financing will likely be muted amid the ongoing emphasis of controlling implicit debt risk (see NPC takeaway).
