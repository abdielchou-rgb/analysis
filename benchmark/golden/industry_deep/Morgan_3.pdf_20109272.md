## Japan Equity Strategy

## Downward pressure on stocks from stronger yen shortlived; focus on weak yen beneficiaries/financials during results

Japanese equity prices have continued to rise from the June 17 low, though with some volatility (Figure 1). Foreign investors’ buying also recovered to ¥0.9 trillion in the week to July 4 (Figure 2). Share prices swung widely last week (July 8-12). Downward pressure on the yen eased in the latter half of the week as July 5 US employment data raised expectations of FRB interest rates cuts, and upward momentum in Japanese share prices gained pace. Electrical appliances & precision instruments, information & communication, chemicals, and insurance drove this market upswing. However, the US dollar subsequently weakened/yen strengthened, affected by soft US CPI data on July 11 and speculation about currency intervention, and both TOPIX and the Nikkei 225 fell sharply on July 12. Electrical appliances & precision instruments, insurance, machinery, and automobiles were the drivers of this decline (Figure 3). We stick firmly with our view that the Japanese stock market will break out of its trading range and rise in the latter half of the year even under this high share price volatility. This is because Japan’s Economic Surprise Index has started to improve as European and US momentum falters (though real wages announced on July 8 continued to decline), and upward revisions to the Japanese corporate EPS revision index have also started to become evident amid fading European and US momentum.

Downward pressure on stock prices due to yen appreciation to ¥155/\$ short-lived: Rising forex volatility is heavily influencing share prices, as evident in steep TOPIX/Nikkei 225 declines when the yen moved from ¥161/\$ to the ¥158/\$ level on July 12. However, as we have noted in the past, the correlation between yen weakness and rising equity prices has broken down since May, when the yen weakened beyond ¥155/\$, and share prices did not continue upward (Figure 5). Consequently, we do not think there would be any great downward pressure on Japanese equities if the yen were to conversely backtrack and revert from ¥161/\$ to ¥155/\$ (stronger yen, weaker dollar). Even if the stock market were to fall because of the yen appreciation, we doubt that this pattern would last long.

Currency intervention implications for BOJ rate hikes, stock prices: There is growing speculation that currency intervention took place after the US CPI announcement on July 11, but some observers argue that intervention forceful enough to ensure dollar weakness/yen strength signaled the authorities concerns about yen depreciation, increasing the probability that the BOJ will hike rates at its July Monetary Policy Meeting. On the other hand, looking at forecasts factored in by interest futures markets, the probability of a rate hike at the July Monetary Policy Meeting has receded from 60% on July 11 to around 40% at present because of the weak US CPI and the growing likelihood of an FRB rate cut (Figure 6). ● Strategy by sector assuming companies posting strong progress in results support share prices: We expect upward revisions to companies’ guidance and strong progress in earnings will support Japanese share prices as we move into the Apr-Jun results season. We set out details in our April-Jun earnings outlook report, but Apr-Jun 2024 (1Q) results is a period with seasonally few revisions to full-year guidance, and share prices tend to react strongly to progress rates at this time. We envision generally high progress owing to yen depreciation and cautious full-year guidance (Apr-Jun average exchange rate: ¥156/\$, putting the yen 13% weaker YoY; guidance: 8% weaker YoY; Figure 7). By sector, we think overseas demand-driven companies benefiting from the weaker yen will report strong progress. Corporate business conditions by sector (BOJ Tankan DI) are improving in communications and materials, whereas the EPS revision index has risen for finance, precision instruments, automobiles and others, and our focus is therefore on whether there is upside not yet priced in by the market in materials and communications (Figure 8). Turning to domestic investor sentiment by sector, we highlight the possibility of an improvement for automobiles on the back of good results, in addition to materials (Figures 9, 10). On top of this kind of tactical strategy, we cannot exclude financial stocks since BOJ quantitative tightening and rate hikes will advance at Jul-Sep policy meetings, and this should continue to support the performance of value stocks.
