## TSE reforms: Second chapter of corporate governance reforms

TSE-led corporate governance reform efforts continue to gain momentum. On Feb 1, the TSE released best practices to date in corporate disclosures regarding management with awareness of the cost of capital and stock price. This follows the release of a list of companies disclosing actions in response to TSE initiatives and should lead to further corporate moves and a market pickup by encouraging companies to enhance dialogue and engagement with market participants. TSE-led reform represents the second chapter of corporate governance reform that began as part of Abenomics and has been a driver of the rally in Japanese stocks in general and value stocks in particular since 2023. This note provides an overview of TSE reforms and their history, a review of corporate governance reforms to date, and investment ideas based on these reforms. Near-term, we expect a focus on companies which have yet to disclose initiatives around TSE reform and are therefore likely to do so, but think high capital efficiency will impact longer-term performance.

• The TSE published a list of companies with best practices on Feb 1, allowing investors and companies to engage in dialogue more effectively than before. It plans to update the list every six months, and we hope this will improve the quality of companies<sup>’</sup> initiatives.

• TSE reform drove Japan<sup>’</sup>s value stock rally in 2023 (even as growth stocks led in the U.S.) and thus the rally in Japanese stocks overall. It is not a stand-alone initiative, but part of a series of corporate governance reforms underway since Abenomics. Following an initial phase including the introduction of the Corporate Governance Code in 2015, corporate reforms can be seen as being in a second chapter, with TSE reforms symbolizing this phase.

Corporate governance reforms were the most successful area of reforms launched during Abenomics, and over the past decade, there has been significant progress. The amount of share buybacks announced by companies has tripled; the ratio of outside board directors has doubled to 40%; and the number of companies held as strategic shareholdings has fallen 20%. The percentage of companies listed on the TSE Prime Market with P/B above 1x has also risen from 50% to 60% over the past year owing to TSE reforms.

We believe TSE reforms have been successful despite having no binding force as: (1) they successfully use peer pressure to encourage Japanese companies to act; (2) Japanese companies tend to move seriously once they determine a direction; (3) companies<sup>’</sup> engagement abilities have improved since Abenomics; and (4) activist investors have become increasingly involved.

Our analysis of share-price performance based on capital efficiency (ROE − COE) as emphasized in TSE reforms along with disclosure status shows those with high capital efficiency (ROE > COE) and existing disclosure outperforming the market. Companies with low capital efficiency (ROE < COE), low P/B, and no disclosure are drawing near-term interest in expectation of future announcements, investing in companies with high capital efficiency is most effective for the longer term. In this note, we screen for names with high capital efficiency (both with and without existing disclosures).

## TSE reform has driven value rally

TSE reform refers to the exchange<sup>’</sup>s March 2023 request to companies to “implement management that is conscious of cost of capital and stock price<sup>”</sup> and the actions taken by companies in response, and is now recognized as P/B reform in the global market. TSE reform was the driving force behind Japan<sup>’</sup>s value stock rally in 2023 (even as growth stocks led in the U.S.) and the rally in Japanese stocks overall. A review of share-price performance by sector since 2023 shows value stocks including trading companies, autos, steel, transportation, and banks generally outperforming. Rising longterm interest rates as Japan exits deflation and normalizes monetary policy have generally been seen as a key investment theme for Japanese stocks, but we think outperformance by a wide range of sectors, not just financial,　indicates that TSE reform is likewise being recognized and welcomed by the market as an important theme. As shown in Figure 1, Japanese value stocks have outperformed from 2023 despite fluctuations in U.S. long-term interest rates and despite growth stocks dominating overseas. We think this indicates that investors view this Japan-specific theme as effective and are accordingly pricing it in (Figure 2).

## Results of corporate governance reforms since Abenomics era

Corporate governance reforms were initiated in 2014 as one of the main aspects of Abenomics under the second Abe administration. In the initial period, the first chapter, Japan<sup>’</sup>s Stewardship Code and a corporate governance code were drafted, and the socalled Ito Review report (version 1.0) was released. These became major driving forces in raising companies<sup>’</sup> awareness of corporate governance and RoE improvement (Figure 3). The Corporate Governance Code spells out guidelines for the diversification of board members and reductions of strategic shareholdings and requires companies to comply or explain why they are not taking these steps. Partly in connection with changes to Basel requirements, banks made serious efforts to encourage nonfinancial companies to reduce strategic shareholdings. As a result, over the 10 years through

2023, banks<sup>’</sup> cross-shareholdings have declined by about 40% (book value basis). In addition, TOPIX companies<sup>’</sup> number of strategic shareholdings declined by 20% (Figures 2 and 3). The percentage of outside board directors doubled, from 22% in March 2015 to 41% in March 2023 (Figures 4 and 5), and the percentage of women directors also rose substantially, albeit from a low level of 3%, to 13% (Figure 4), with help from the Abe administration<sup>’</sup>s Womenomics policies to promote women in the workplace (Figures 6 and 7).

## TSE reforms symbolic of second chapter of corporate governance reforms

The TSE reforms, which got underway in March 2023, are symbolic of a second chapter of corporate governance reforms. A year ago, in late January 2023, the TSE released an outline of this initiative in a summary of a follow-up meeting after the changes to market sections. In late March, so-called P/B reforms, unusual by global standards also, got underway. In October 2023, as a follow-up on corporate actions in response to this call, the TSE released for the first time the ratio of companies disclosing actions they intend to take with regard to their cost of capital and share price. It then published in January 2024 a list of these companies disclosing actions they intend to take (to be updated monthly) and decided in February to publish best practices (to be updated semiannually). The TSE has indicated plans to take necessary additional measures every six months.

## Pickup in corporate initiatives

In response to the TSE reforms, companies are starting to make changes that can be described as reforms. We see this as evidence of heightened awareness of and expectations for corporate governance among companies and market participants. In Oct-Dec 2023, six months after the start of the TSE reforms, share buybacks exceeded the amount in Oct-Dec 2022, the record high. The likelihood that the full fiscal year amount will reach a record high has thus risen considerably (Figure 10). TOPIX companies<sup>’</sup> aggregate DPS is also expected to reach an all-time high in FY2023 (Figure 11, Bloomberg consensus basis). As companies review their business portfolios and capital allocations, they are increasing not only shareholder returns but also growth investments that lead to longer-term growth. The amount of M&A in 2023 rose about 50% YoY (based on a Recof survey). As market expectations for corporate governance required of listed companies have increased, a number of companies have gone private through MBOs (Figures 12 and 13). Oct-Dec 2023 results announcements included some corporate governance-related moves, such as Mitsubishi Corp.<sup>’</sup>s announcement of a large buyback of ¥500 billion (10% of total outstanding), industry restructuring moves such as KDDI<sup>’</sup>s tender offer for Lawson and Dai-ichi Life<sup>’</sup>s tender offer for Benefit One, and other tender offer deals without an agreement. Steady progress has been made on corporate reforms and market revitalization.

## The keys to the success of TSE reforms

One question, then, is why the TSE reforms have been successful in influencing corporate behavior. The TSE reforms are now recognized internationally as P/B reforms, and this question is being asked again, with similar market reform efforts broadening to South Korea, China, and other Asian countries (Figure 14, report). Global market participants may be wondering how the TSE has succeeded in getting companies to make the changes it wants to see, without having any binding force. The TSE is a platform provider and does not have the power that regulators have to make companies take certain steps, nor does it delist companies or take other tough steps against companies that do not act on its requests. In fact, the TSE<sup>’</sup>s January 15 list of companies that have disclosed actions they intend to take does not include such leading Japanese companies as Toyota Motor and Fast Retailing. We attribute the success of the TSE reforms to several factors: (1) they encourage Japanese companies to take action by

leveraging peer pressure; (2) Japanese companies tend to move seriously once they determine a direction to go in; (3) companies have improved their market engagement capabilities since the Abenomics era; and (4) activist investors have become increasingly involved in Japan<sup>’</sup>s market (Figure 15: the number of activist investors shareholder proposals reached a record high in 2023).

## Stocks with upside and those worth looking at in terms of capital efficiency, market valuation

To link the best practices of companies released by the TSE to investment ideas, we categorized companies into four groups based on returns on capital and market valuation (four quadrants in Figure 18) . These groups are (1) companies with both high return on capital and market valuation that are viewed as having best practices, and are thus expected to have upside (Figure 19); (2) companies with high return on capital but low market valuation (generally expected to have upside, but improvement in market valuation is limited when corporate CoE assumptions are low; Figure 20); (3) companies with low return on capital and high market valuation (companies where future growth potential is generally priced in; Figure 21); and (4) companies with low return on capital and low market valuation (Figure 22). Our screening is based on (1) the average of the historical Z-score (calculated using three years of quarterly data; hereinafter referred to as the TS Z-score) and the intra-sector Z-score (based on TSE 17- sector classifications; hereinafter referred to as the CS Z-score) of the gap between the latest 12-month RoE and the Bloomberg estimated COE; and(2) the average of the TS Z-score and CS Z-score of the latest P/B.

In addition to the companies disclosed by the TSE, we include Toyota Motor, NTT, Fast Retailing, Shin-Etsu Chemical, Hitachi , Oriental Land, Sumitomo Mitsui FG, Mizuho FG, and Mitsui & Co., Ltd as best practice companies (1st quadrant) that could be medium-term investment candidates.

Companies where market valuations have not kept up with high capital efficiency (companies in 4th quadrant) include Nintendo, Honda, Renesas Electronics, and Komatsu.

Companies that have already factored in future prospects for current capital efficiency (companies in 2nd quadrant) include Itochu Corporation, Fujitsu, TDK, Kao, and NEC.

In contrast, companies where capital efficiency and market valuation over the past 12 months have not been as good as their own historical and industry averages (companies in 3rd quadrant) include SoftBank Group, Murata Manufacturing, and Daikin Industries.

From a short-term perspective, companies with low capital efficiency (ROE>COE) and low P/B and undisclosed initiatives are attracting attention as those that are likely to announce some initiatives in the future, but from a longer-term perspective, it can be seen that investing in companies with high capital efficiency is effective (stock prices rise on execution of measures at companies that have already disclosed them, while at companies that have not disclosed measures, share prices rise in two stages, first as expectations are factored in and then as implementation of measures occurs). In particular, companies that have high capital efficiency but have not disclosed measures are expected to have upside. Conversely, stock price of companies with low capital efficiency but disclosed initiatives are rising, so it is necessary to check the effectiveness of their efforts.
