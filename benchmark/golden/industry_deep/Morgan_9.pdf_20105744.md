# 2024 Outlook: Cycle Recovery Takes Time

Capital goods demand is likely to remain at a cyclical low in 2024. Traditional and staples-related capex demand could be better than new energy. We prefer names with market share gains in China, overseas expansion and less new energy exposure: Weichai H, Dingli, Haitian, LK Tech, Hengli, Inovance.

Where are we in the cycle? We think the China industrials segment will remain at a cyclical low in 2024, given a backdrop of subpar macro growth and overcapacity issues in China. Overseas expansion is a common strategy for industrials companies, and we think this will be a critical long-term growth driver, albeit with challenges. Various sub-sectors are at slightly different parts of the cycle curve ( Exhibit 1 ):

• Bottomed out – Heavy-duty trucks and railway equipment reached lows in 2022, and turned around in 2023. We expect the recovery to continue in 2024. Laser is likely to bottom in 2024, but we think the uptrend will be flatter than before.

• Bottoming at cyclical low – General machinery, automation, testing equipment and lithium battery equipment (ranked in order of cycle position) need more time to recover. Traditional industries' subpar recovery is being dragged by a slowdown in new energy capex.

• Down-cycle – Construction machinery is in the later phases of a four- to fiveyear down-cycle that is set to continue in 2024, while solar equipment has fallen from the peak cycle since 2H23, and should see a down-cycle for 2024.

What's our framework for stock picking? Segment cycle, company growth potential (share gains in China, competitive product to expand overseas, new initiatives), operational efficiency, balance sheet and valuation all matter in a challenging environment.

• Key OW stocks – Weichai H (2338.HK), Focuslight (688167.SS) should be market share gainers amid China demand recovery. Dingli (603338.SS), Hengli Hydraulic (601100.SS), Haitian (1882.HK), LK Tech (0558.HK) are key plays on overseas expansion with solid track records. Inovance (300124.SZ) should continue to gain share in China, and increase activity in overseas markets.

• Key UW stocks – We remain negative on all solar equipment stocks (Maxwell (300751.SZ), Autowell (688516.SS), etc.). Sany (600031.SS) is still seeing industry cycle headwinds and more competition, while LeaderDrive (688017.SS) looks expensive even with humanoid robots. Raycus (300747.SZ) will likely continue to face intense price competition.

## Automation and general equipment

## Automation – Cycle bottoming, but turning point is unclear

We think demand stabilized in 2H23, and expect an L-shaped recovery into 2024 with no clear inflection point in sight, at least in 1H24, considering sluggish property investment and weakening domestic consumption. The decline in demand from the new energy segment is also unlikely to be fully offset by the mild recovery in traditional industries, in our view. Ongoing government stimulus is key to watch with regard the pace of recovery, and could also be a short-term stock catalyst. Overseas expansion is a long-term theme, and not only in EM as companies are also aiming to penetrate DMs by offering better products.

De-stocking approaching an end. Automation market demand has remained lackluster, down 2% YoY in 2023 vs. +1% YoY in 2022, after +17% YoY in 2021. After six quarters of sales growth deceleration, we think demand has stabilized on improving industrial enterprise profit and capacity utilization as well as a 20-month de-stocking period, and we now expect some near-term re-stocking for certain industries (i.e., 3C, food & beverage, packaging).

However, the road to recovery is bumpy given lukewarm demand. Into 2024, we expect an L-shaped recovery for automation given ongoing headwinds from the housing market, LGFV deleveraging and weakening domestic consumption, although we do see exports likely improving from lows. Our China economist, Robin Xing, expects reflation in 2024-25 to be slower than the 2016-17 cycle, and PPI YoY may only exit deflation decisively from 2025. He believes policy support in both fiscal (financial deficit expansion) and monetary (rate cut) easing will be executed to cushion the economy slowdown, but in a reactive manner. This would keep China's reflation journey bumpy and gradual.

## Automation outlook in 2024 – mild recovery, localization trend continues albeit at slower pace

Manufacturing FAI remained resilient in 2023 at around 6% YoY vs. 9-14% in 2021-22, thanks to strong policy support. However, we expect it to normalize at \~4% in 2024, given sustaining high-end manufacturing and green capex. Accordingly, we expect the total automation market size to drop to 2% YoY in 2024 to Rmb284bn, with market size for both OEM and projects declining 2% YoY to Rmb100bn and Rmb182bn, respectively, vs. - 9% and +2% YoY in 2023.

Meanwhile, we think Chinese brands will continue to gain market share, thanks to their improving products with broad client bases and experience as well as higher client stickiness with solutions that offer value-added products at a good price to value. However, we think the pace of market share gains will slow, considering: 1) lower demand from new energy downstream, which is dominated by local players; and 2) local peers are entering mid-high end manufacturing, which require higher product quality.

## PCB Equipment

Modest recovery into 2024: On the demand side, top-tier downstream PCB manufacturers, including Shennan and Shengyi, believe PCB manufacturing is likely past the trough. However, 2024 should continue to be a challenging year even with the likelihood of positive yoy growth. AI remains a demand tailwind, and automotive continues to be key growth driver in 2024, supporting high-end PCB sales (HDI and flex). ## Construction Machinery

Heading into 2024, we expect construction machinery (CM) sales to decline another 4% YoY to 589K units, after the 10% YoY drop in 2023. By product, in 2024, we expect excavators, wheel loaders and crane sales to fall 12%, 7% and 3% YoY, respectively, with concrete machinery flat YoY and AWP (aerial working platform) to see positive growth at 5% YoY.

China domestic demand will remain in a down-cycle: Construction activity has slowed, dragged down by property construction, and capital has remained tight for infrastructure construction. In 11M23, property and infrastructure contributed 23% and 66% of total FAI, respectively. Channel inventory has been piling up again, which will likely result in a further 12% YoY drop in excavator sales, for example, following the 24% and 25% YoY in declines reported in 2022 and 2023, respectively.

• Continued contraction in housing investment: Our China property team expects developers' property investment to remain weak (-7% YoY in 2024 vs. -9% YoY in 2023), and national property new starts to be down 11% YoY in 2024 (vs. -40% YoY in 2023), driven by developers' weak sales outlook, tight liquidity for new construction starts and insufficient land acquisitions. (Link)

• Infrastructure likely to slow down: We expect infrastructure FAI of 5% YoY in 2024 vs. 8% in 2023, given ongoing LGFV deleveraging. Power and green projects are likely to be the key growth drivers.

Exports slow down as well on rising competition: We expect excavator exports to be - 6% YoY in 2024, after turning negative in June 2023 (-1% YoY) and as monthly sales declined widened to >10% in July-November 2023. Chinese players are expanding product lines, sales channels and overseas capacity for a longer-term growth in global markets. We believe the competition in overseas markets will intensify both among Chinese brands and at global brands.

More AD/CVD (Anti-dumping/Countervailing Duty) investigations initiated in overseas markets, including investigations for AWP (Aerial Working Platform) in the excavators in the UK and wheel loaders in India, which could be negative to Chinese CM players near term.

Stock ideas: Overweight-rated Hengli (603915.SS) is our most-preferred stock given its continuous product upgrades to substitute for imports. The non-excavator expansion will continue in both China and overseas markets, while some delayed projects (e.g., hydraulic system supply to agriculture machine clients) could start delivery in 2024. In addition, precision component ball screws and roller screws will start validation at downstream clients in 2024.

## New Energy Equipment

## Solar Equipment

We expect demand growth to slow down in 2024 after a significant increase in 2023: Our China utilities team estimates global solar installations of 427GW in 2024, +15% YoY vs. +61% YoY in 2023. In China, the team expects total installations to reach 198GW in 2024, +10% YoY vs. +105% YoY in 2023, noting the utility scale is constrained by power grids and plant facilities' reaching peak output. As well, distributed solar energy may be constrained by rising solar penetration, curtailment and lower project IRRs with a duck curve impact.

For supply, we see equipment upgrade demand continuing into 2024, but new orders could be half 2023's level: We believe orders for solar equipment peaked in 1H23, with QoQ erosion since 3Q23 across the solar value chain (ingot, wafer, cell and module). Most equipment suppliers we cover did not see order withdrawals in 2H23, but we remain cautious about 2024 given tightened capital-raising policies and persistent downstream margin pressure.

With capacity gradually coming online in 2H23 and 2024, overcapacity remains a concern. We estimate ingot, wafer, cell and module nameplate capacity will be 1000-1400GW+ as of end-2024 vs. demand of just 530-570GW. We also see equipment demand rising as toptier clients expand capacity and upgrade equipment to remain cost competitive. However, we estimate orders for new capacity expansion could be half of 2023's level based on our channel checks with solar equipment companies.

• Crystal growing furnace (CGF) should see \~100GW of new orders industry-wide in 2024, per JSG. Given that ongoing improvements in solar cell conversion efficiency will require higher ingot quality, CGF upgrade demand should remain solid in 2024.

• For wafer slicing equipment, visibility on demand is lower for 2024, as wafer manufacturing faces higher overcapacity risks due to substantial capacity expansions in 2022-23.

• Cell equipment demand for TOPCon should reach \~200GW from top-tier clients in 2024, we estimate, while demand from SMEs and new players is highly uncertain (\~300GW from top-tier clients vs. total \~500GW orders in 2023). Industry-wide, HJT new orders should be flat or rise slightly YoY in 2024, per Maxwell. However, we note that HJT demand is sensitive to cost-reduction progress in 2024, as results from trial runs of multiple technologies that would lower user costs should be available in 2024 (e.g., 0BB stringer, turnkey solution at 1GW unit capacity, stencil printing, copper plating, low-indium target material, etc.).

• Module equipment demand in 2024 is supported by equipment upgrades (e.g., replacing regular stringers with multi-busbars stringers) for domestic capacity and solid overseas module capacity expansion.

Stock ideas – remain cautious on solar equipment segment: We remain cautious on the solar equipment segment, with UW ratings on most solar equipment stocks we cover,

including Maxwell (300751.SZ), Autowell (688516.SS), Gaoce (688556.SS) and Metron (300861.SZ), with a negative view on order momentum and/or technology. JSG (300316.SZ) and SC New Energy (300724.SZ) are the only two EW-rated stocks among our solar equipment coverage. We expect JSG and SC to achieve strong growth in 2024. JSG should benefit from Rmb25bn+ order backlogs for solar equipment and solid sales growth from quartz crucibles, which remain at historically high ASP levels given the shortage in imported quartz sands. SC is seeing strong sales growth and improving gross margin with higher contribution from TOPCon orders in 2024.

In the domestic market, LiB equipment demand is likely to stay low for another year, as overcapacity concerns in both EV and ESS (energy storage systems) markets linger.
