# Hong Kong Strategy After 20% rally, what's next?

## Equity Strategy

## Hong Kong

## Mean reversion of equity risk premium; earnings revision stabilising

Over the past two months, MSCI HK and HSI have rallied strongly, up 19-20% from their respective troughs, driven by fund rotation from other regional markets, supportive government policies, and, more recently, good Internet earnings. After the rally, MSCI HK's forward PE has re-rated from 10.5x to 12.7x (1.4SD below historical mean) and equity risk premium has mean reverted. With interest rates projected to remain elevated, we foresee limited room for multiple to re-rate in the remainder of 2024, and the performance of HK equities should be largely dictated by earnings. Encouragingly, we begin to see earnings downgrades stabilising. For the first time since December 2023, we see positive earnings revision in May for HSI for 2024-25F. Similarly, MSCI HK has recorded positive earnings revisions for 2025F for the first time since July 2023. We maintain our targets of 9,500 for MSCI HK and 20,600 for and HSI, based on target PEs of 12.8x (-1.3SD below mean) and 9.6x (-1.0SD below mean), respectively.

## HK macro remains challenging but consensus earnings appear modest

We expect HK's current challenging macro narrative to extend over the remainder of 2024. As more local residents travel abroad, the leakage of consumption to overseas markets will continue to weigh on domestic spending, in our view. In March 2024, daily resident departures exceeded the average level from July 2018 to June 2019 by close to 15%. At the same time, interest rates are likely to stay high. UBS economists expect only a 50bp cut in the Fed Fund rate for 2024E. Nonetheless, after 10 consecutive months of downgrades, consensus expects only a modest 4% growth in MSCI HK earnings for 2024, undershooting the UBS forecast of 8%. Also, proof of the effectiveness of recent policy support – for example, the abolishment of all demand-side management measures for HK residential properties, and policy supportive of the Hong Kong stock exchange, such as southbound inclusion of REITs and potential removal of dividend tax for southbound investors – could drive positive earnings revisions and multiple re-rating.

## Adding Link REIT to least preferred

In terms of stock picks, we add Link REIT to our list of least preferred names, on structural challenges related to northbound spending. We remove Hang Seng Bank from least preferred list, due to higher for longer rates to support NIM and high shareholder return.
