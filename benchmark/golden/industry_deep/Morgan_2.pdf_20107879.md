# Lessons learned from 15 years of stock picking in Asia ex Japan

It has been 15 years since we launched our APxJ Focus List. US dollar return has been 406.4% (CAGR of 11.4%) with alpha generation of 350bps per annum versus the MSCI Asia Pacific Ex Japan benchmark. We present current names included, recap our investment process and outline some lessons learned.

15 years of Alpha Generation: We started our 20-stock MS Asia ex Japan Focus List in April 2009 in the aftermath of the GFC. Current Asia ex Japan Focus List names are given in Exhibit 1 with detailed Risk / Reward analysis later in the report.

Over the 15 years that we have run this process live Absolute returns have been positive in 59% of months and Relative returns (versus the MSCI Asia Pacific ex Japan benchmark "MAXPJ") in 58% of months. The total cumulative absolute US\$ return (including dividends) has been 406.4% (annual CAGR of 11.4%). Excess returns versus the MSCI Asia Pacific ex Japan index have been 350bps per annum. The Sharpe Ratio versus the US 3-month T bill rate has been 0.70 and Information Ratio versus MXAPJ has been 0.62. These numbers are presented on an un-audited basis excluding transaction costs\*.

Process: Combining Top Down and Bottom-up with a Quality Bias: Our APxJ Focus List (and Global EM Focus List launched in December 2006) comprises of 20 stocks selected by our team with reference to our market, sector, style, and thematic preferences. They are typically preferred names within their industry coverage as OW-rated names by Morgan Stanley bottom-up analysts within their coverage. Our style bias has been to prefer Quality in the form of our Best Business Models approach, which targets superior RNOA / ROE, sustainable competitive advantage and balance sheet strength versus industry peers whilst controlling for valuation premium. We continuously review stock and portfolio performance in conjunction with the views of our bottom-up analysts, and target a 6- to 12-month holding period and aim to limit turnover to <100% per annum. Of the 20 stocks now included in APxJ, the average holding period since inclusion is nine months.

Lessons learned: 1) Collaboration and teamwork are effective. The best performance has come when our top-down themes and ideas meet the bottom-up stock-picking skill of Morgan Stanley's fundamental analysts. 2) Let winners run and cut losers earlier. While well-trodden investment advice, we have also learned this firsthand. 3) Applying historical context is useful, particularly amid sharp shifts in regulatory conditions or investment narratives. 4) Portfolio risk management techniques have become more accessible and advanced. We find them a useful complement to human judgment, particularly at market turning points.

The style of our Focus Lists has, on average, been Balanced (neither Growth nor Value style bias). Relative to their benchmarks, we have also tended towards larger-cap and more liquid companies. Over time we have also introduced more of a style bias – specifically to Quality through the cycle – drawing on our Best Business Models approach, which targets superior RNOA / ROE, sustainable competitive advantage and balance sheet strength versus industry peers, while controlling for valuation premium. For version 9 of our Asia Ex Japan / EM Best Business models approach see our note linked [Asia EM Equity Strategy: Launching APxJ/EM Best Business Models v9 (2 Feb 2023)].

While not a formal constraint, the stock ideas included in our Focus Lists are usually Overweight-rated by our analysts within their coverage, with upside to price target on inclusion. Occasionally, we have included Equal-weight-rated stocks to encompass a market, sector, or style view or in the context of overall portfolio characteristics of the list, or following a downgrade from Overweight. We do not add stocks rated Underweight by our analysts. Stocks are typically removed from the list on a coverage analyst downgrade of view and price target, but also if we think a new idea has better prospects in the context of our process and broader investment outlook. We aim for annual portfolio turnover of less than 100% with a target holding period of six to 12 months.

## Five key lessons learned after 15 years of Focus List management

While not a comprehensive memoir of our experience running these Focus Lists through booms and recessions and numerous local waves of euphoria and crisis, we make several observations on what has worked for us in running this process. Our hope is that this may help clients in their own investment processes. Some of the below are well-known general investment lessons, but others may be less obvious:

## 1) Collaboration with ideas of bottom-up analysts

We've tended to do best when our thematic and market/sector/style preferences overlapped with idea generation from a Morgan Stanley analyst covering the stock. We have also benefitted from the perspectives from our country strategist colleagues specializing in individual markets. A recent example was our October 2022 team decision to upgrade Korea, Taiwan and the Asia Semiconductor and Technology hardware universe, including adding key names to the Focus List.

## 2) Quality with a Valuation overlay has been a preferred style

Increasingly, over time, we have leveraged our insight that Quality (superior return on net operating assets vs. industry peers, above-average balance sheet strength and management) is the dominant style approach over the cycle in GEM and APxJ. But, it is important not to overpay for Quality – that's why we also look closely at a number of valuation metrics - particularly P/B.

A pure Growth approach can attract risk in GEM and APxJ, in our view, given the emerging nature of many companies in these areas. Likewise, a pure Value approach, historically favoured by many dedicated specialist managers, introduces a sector bias towards SOEs and sectors vulnerable to disruption and away from potential structural compounders.

## 3) Run the winners and cut losers

Early on in managing this list we were less effective at holding on to successful ideas that grew into their multiple, and equally not bold enough in challenging our thesis for owning a stock if the market began to turn against it.

## 4) Look for historical analogy

We find history can often rhyme across markets, regions and periods, even if the local context appears quite different. This is particularly true during periods of more intense changes in macro and regulatory policy.

## 5) Continuity and improvement

Running a weekly Focus List call has been a formal part of our process throughout the 15 years of running the Asia Pacific ex Japan Focus List. In this call we discuss performance statistics and incoming analyst notes to generate a consistent review process for Focus List constituents, looking for new ideas and reassessing the investment case for existing positions.

But, of equal importance, we also look to apply new perspectives, benefiting from the improvements in portfolio risk management tools, and the insights from our quantitative strategy and sustainability research teams, in addition to our market-specialist strategy and sector research coverage teams.
