## Fundamental Developments

◼ NII pressure eased as deposit beta continue to rise but at a slower pace.

◼ CRE and especially office stress remains, but banks are building reserves to 10% of portfolio.

◼ Asset quality normalization continues with minimal stress in C&I loan book.

◼ Regulatory changes should be finalized by end of year, but worst-case outcome are likely avoided.

◼ Market-based revenue bounced back as IB fees experienced volume growth.

## Issuance and Relative Value

◼ YTD 2024 GSIB senior holdco supply is running slightly below our run-rate forecast. USD Sr issuance of \$82.7bn is at 56% of our full forecast. The pace could increase as Bank of American and Goldman Sachs are running below our initial estimates.

◼ Regional Bank issuance at 51% of our full-year forecast is running in line with estimates. We think the pace will remain steady and not overwhelming.

◼ Yankee bank issuance has been a mixed bag, European banks and UK Banks are running higher than expected while Japanese banks are a little lower than our run-rate forecast.

◼ Money-center 10y spreads outperformed JULI index as did regional banks and Yankee Banks. We have been reducing our Overweight in Financials as the year progressed; we think large banks look fair and will trade around the index.

## We see the following forces impacting spreads for the rest of 2023:

◼ Valuations – Money-center 10-year spreads are trading at \~110p, about 2bp tighter to the JULI ex-EM 10-year index. Valuations for GSIBs are less compelling given the recent rally.

## ◼ Fundamentals

◼ Deposit story has stabilized, and competition easing as several banks have guided toward peaking deposit BETAs in 3Q’24.

◼ Net interest income and net interest margins should begin to stabilize over the course of the year. While rates cuts have been pushed out, the impact on NIMs has been more of an equity story than credit.

◼ Asset quality normalization has begun. We think reserves will increase for CRE and C&I portfolios, while consumer-related provisions are dependent on the unemployment rate.

◼ Capital will increase driven in part by the regulatory changes as well as SCBs remaining relatively steady or slightly higher.

## ◼ Technical

◼ Issuance – We reduce our senior holdco issuance of \$121bn or 50% of total run rate. The pace was heavy to start the year and has slowed down recently, mostly due to the lack of 2Q deals from BAC and GS. We do think issuance will remain steady through the course of the year. We note that bank level issuance has been higher than expected and should continue.

## Regulatory

◼ BIS III end game rules might be finalized by the end of the year. All indications point toward the rules being eased from the initial proposals. For CAT 3 and CAT 4 banks we expect AOCI is likely to be included in capital ratios, and TLAC/LTD requirements may be implemented. Importantly we think liquidity ratios will also change, but that is a 2025 story.

## System Deposit Trends

## Deposit Trends in U.S. Banking System

◼ Deposits trends have stabilized within the banking system.

◼ Deposits peaked in 1H’22 and steadily declined as QT impacted the system. The pace of the decline picked up in March following concerns about SIVB and other regional banks.

◼ Deposits at small regional banks stood at \$5.2tn in early June 2023, deposits declined through the course of the year, but have increased in 1H’24 to get back to March peaks.

◼ While some concerns remain around continued QT and RRP, we think the Fed is very focused on the level of reserves in the system.

## Cost of Interest-Bearing Deposits, Betas, and Balances

## Rising IBD Costs Contributes to Higher Deposit Beta

◼ Rising interest-bearing deposit costs seem to be easing in 1H’24. As the chart depicts, the deposit beta appears to have peaked.

◼ The decline in system deposit levels will be dependent on the reduction of the Federal Reserve’s balance sheet, the terminal rate, as well as the decline in RRP.

◼ Anecdotal evidence suggests that deposit competition has eased. We do note that the mix is still detrimental for the banks, which is putting pressure on NIM.

## Earnings Drivers

◼ The GSIBs are over earning on the NIM. The delay in rate cuts through the course of the year has helped GSIBs but at the detriment of regional banks. With only 1 cut expected in 2024, we expect GSIBs continue to guide higher on NII.

◼ Loan growth is muted in most categories except consumer. We don’t expect loan growth to accelerate through the course of the year. Consumer will be the driver.

◼ Capital markets revenue performance has bounced back. Management teams are commenting about green shoots in advisory and expect deal activity to pick up. DCM and ECM volumes are very strong and not expected to decline given the lack of volatility.

## Securities Portfolio in the Spotlight

## Declining Marks from Rising Rates

◼ The growth in deposits is directly correlated with the increase in securities portfolios at commercial banks.

◼ Management teams extended duration within the securities portfolio, taking on higher interest rate risk.

◼ The rapid move in interest rates resulted in mark-to-market losses for banks, and the spotlight moved to bank capital ratios.

◼ AFS opt-out for regional banks created concerns about CET1 and the focus moved to TCE ratios. Marks have continued to drop, but concerns have eased as banks are not feeling any liquidity pressures.

◼ The securities portfolio is now being reinvested at higher yields, which is acting like a tailwind for banks.

## Bank Reserve Levels

## Rising Reserve Levels

◼ Reserve levels continue to rise driven by loan growth on the consumer portfolio as well as exposure to CRE.

◼ Reserves at north of 2% of loan for the system are very healthy, especially given that NCOs remain at a low level.

◼ Although allowance levels and coverage ratios are above pre-pandemic levels, which reflects the banking system’s ability to withstand modest economic weakness, coverage ratios and reserve balances are well below GFC levels.

## Credit Cards

◼ Trends in consumer credit quality are normalizing. Average credit card 30+ day delinquency rates are declining in 2024 as tightening underwriting criteria begins to have an impact.

◼ The traditional relationship between unemployment and delinquencies/loan losses has been distorted by stimulus as well as forbearance/loan deferrals the last few years.

◼ The greater risk within cards is the threat of new products like BNPL and banks weakening the credit spectrum to compete.

## Auto

◼ Automotive loan balance growth was sequentially flat at \~\$1.6tn as of 1Q’24, while new 30+ day automotive delinquencies grew to nearly 8%% in 1Q’24.

◼ The Manheim used vehicle price index stands at 196.8 as of mid-June 2024, which is \~7% lower compared to June ’23 levels because of increasing new vehicle inventory. The index is now down to December 2020 levels.

## Commercial Real Estate Exposure

## Anticipated Asset Quality Deterioration in Office CRE

◼ Using Federal Reserve data, total system CRE loans amounted to \$2.8tn as of early June 2024, 60% of which were in non-farm, nonresidential loans and 16% in construction and land development.

◼ We expect CRE portfolio stress to arise in office CRE loan books, as vacancy rates are elevated and just below GFC levels. Small banks are more exposed to the assets class. We expect 10% losses on office; banks are now reserved at around that level.

◼ As a percentage of CET1 capital, CRE exposure excluding multifamily, farm, and owner-occupied properties is concentrated at small regional banks versus GSIBs.

## Capital Structure Views

## GSIB Senior Holdco Debt

◼ We remain Neutral for senior holdco debt at U.S. money-centers.

◼ We continue to prefer GSIB brokers over large depository institutions, which lack sizable loan books vulnerable to asset quality deterioration.

◼ Money-center senior holdco 10-year spreads outperformed the broader index by 15bp YTD. Valuations look fair, considering rising deposit funding costs will pressure profitability on the sector.

## Sub Holdco

◼ Sub holdco issuance is at 50% of our \$8.0bn forecast for FY23 YTD, which is typically a function of Tier 2 capital amortization.

◼ Higher balances of senior holdco debt should result in higher implied recoveries and put upward pressure on the sub/senior ratio at GSIBs.

◼ We remain Neutral on sub holdco paper as valuations remain rich to our ratio target, but moderate supply prevents underperformance.

## Regional Snr / Sub Bank

◼ We remain Neutral regional banks after having downgraded the sector due to relative value.

◼ Concerns have eased about the sector, but we don’t see any catalyst for them trading through GSIBs.

◼ The anticipated finalized ruling on TLAC/LTD requirements is expected to apply to Category IV banks in addition to large regional banks and will likely result in increased issuance of eligible total loss-absorbing capital.

## Preferred Stock / AT1

◼ Our preferred YTW index declined \~34bp year to date to 6.62%.

◼ Spreads have been supported by muted preferred issuance YTD of only \$11.0bn.

◼ We continue to like shortcall, high reset preferred securities, and we do not believe the sector is subject to write-down language risk.

## Yankee Bank Rating and Issuance Overview

◼ U.K. Banks in the 10-year tenor have outperformed the JULI ex EM index by 33bp YTD. It has been the best performing sector among Yankee Banks

◼ We have been Overweight U.K. banks through the course of the year reflecting diminished concerns about residential real estate and relative value.

## Bank Sector Views by Geography

## Core European Banks

◼ We are Overweight core European Banks, and our issuance forecast for the jurisdiction is unchanged at \$80bn.

◼ Sector spreads trade nearly 55bp behind the JULI ex EM index in the 10-year tenor, which appears compelling.

## U.K. Banks

◼ We remain Overweight UK banks; the sector has been the best performers among global financial institutions as concerns about residential real estate have eroded.

◼ U.K. holdco bank trade 20bp behind GSIBs, still attractive but not as much as at the start of the year.

◼ Our issuance forecast for the jurisdiction in 2024 is unchanged at \$40bn.

## Aussie/ NZ Banks

◼ We are Neutral on Australian and New Zealand Banks, and our \$27.5bn issuance forecast is unchanged.

◼ We are Neutral on Canadian Banks.

◼ The sector trades 5bp through the JULI ex EM index in the 5-year tenor and is basically unchanged vs the index through the year

## Japanese Banks

◼ We remain Neutral on Japanese Banks but keep our supply forecast.

◼ We downgraded Japanese banks in 1H’24 given the outperformance vs JULI index and other defensive Yankee banks. We do expect issuance to pick up in 2H’24.

## Explanation of Credit Research Valuation Methodology, Ratings and Risk to Ratings:

J.P. Morgan uses a bond-level rating system that incorporates valuations (relative value) and our fundamental view on the security.
