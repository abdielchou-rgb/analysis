# AlphaWise US Consumer Pulse Survey Wave 54

## alphawiseα

This report provides a full breakdown of data from our 54th survey of \~2,000 consumers in the U.S., which ran May 24th - 27th to gauge consumer behavior, attitudes and outlook.

• Travel Momentum Continues. Domestic and international leisure travel spending outlook continues to trend net positive and skewing higher among upper income consumers. Roughly 60% of consumers are planning to travel over the next six months, similar to last month and the same time last year. Mid- and upper-income households display higher intentions to travel compared to lower income households.

• Consumers Are Spending Moderately; with 31% of consumers expecting to spend more next month and 17% expecting to spend less, yielding a net of +14% (vs. +16% last month and similar to the same time last year). Over half (51%) of consumers don’t plan to change their spending habits. Worth noting, we see more consumers reporting missed bill payments (37% vs. 31% earlier this year) and average savings reserves are lower (4.0 months down from 4.5 in March and 4.8 same time last year).

• The Economy, Healthcare, and Immigration are Key Election Issues. Jobs & the economy are by far the most important issue in the upcoming 2024 Presidential Election, cited by 53% of registered voters (in-line with the past few months and slightly down from 57% in October ’23). Healthcare is the second most important issue to 36% of voters. Immigration further ticked down to 29% of voters citing it as the most important issue (down from 36% two months ago and 31% last month); while taxes ticked up to 32% (vs. 30% last month). When asked about how these issues are currently going in the U.S., the majority of voters believe things are not going in the right direction.

## Household Savings Reserves

This month, 32% of consumers indicated they do not have any savings, 35% have 3 months or less in savings, 22% have 4-12 months and 11% have more than a year; this yields an average savings reserve of 4.0 months (down from 4.5 in March and 4.8 same time last year).

Savings reserves vary significantly by income, with upper income consumers having on average \~7 months worth of expenses in savings compared to \~5 months for mid-income and \~2-3 months for lower income cohorts.

## Bill / Loan Payments

Overall, 37% of consumers reported a missed or late payment on any bill/loan tested in the past three months; up from \~31% earlier in the year. Low-income (42%) and mid-income (34%) consumers are more likely to have missed or made late payments vs. high-income (22%) consumers.

A clear priority for consumers continues to be their cell phone bill. Among the \~90% of consumers who have the payment obligation, only 15% reported a missed or late payment. Another priority for consumers is their mortgage: 34% have a mortgage and, among those, 87% are on time with payments. Household utility, car and internet bills are also important and tend to be mostly paid on-time.

Student (35%), BNPL (25%) and personal loans (28%) are more likely to be missed/paid late, but fewer consumers have these types of loans.

## Engagement in Activities: Out of Home

Participation in most out of home activities is broadly consistent with past surveys. The proportion of consumers who report eating out at restaurants is holding at \~70%, but the net engagement outlook (% who expect to engage in activity more minus less) is still net negative -14% (down from -9% last month).

The net engagement outlook for mall visits is in-line with last month at -15%, while entertainment activities ticked down to +3% from +6% last month. Visits to gyms and indoor fitness clubs are similar to last month (20%), and the net engagement outlook is +31%.

## Delay or Cancelation of Planned Activities

Consumers continue to report robust intentions to follow through on travel: without flights (66% flat with last wave), with flights (56%, flat) and internationally (41% vs. 38%).

Consumers continue to be more cautious to follow through with major plans and purchases (such as the purchase of a home). Among the 15% of consumers who were considering purchasing a home, the majority (72%) are delaying or canceling those plans.

The majority of consumers are following through with routine healthcare visits and job hunting.
