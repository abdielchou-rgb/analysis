# FX & EM Fixed Income Strategy: Preference for Rates Over FX Remains

James Lord

We maintain a preference for EM rates over FX. Last week’s US presidential debate has raised the chances of President Trump winning a second term modestly, according to an average of betting markets. This should support USD at the expense of currencies elsewhere, including EM. Our US rates team have entered a 2s20s steepener and see risks of both bull steepening and higher long-end term premia. While the latter would not be positive for EM rates, core CPI inflation remains largely under control in EM.

In EM local markets we continue to have a preference for rates over FX: The wide range of potential policy outcomes from the US presidential election, and the noise surrounding the campaigns, means that investors will need to monitor polling closely and potential market impacts of the various policy agendas.

Following Thursday night's first presidential election debate, betting markets lowered the probability of President Biden securing a second term substantially. However, we should take this signal with a pinch of salt, as it wasn't mirrored by a similar increase in the probability of former President Trump gaining a second term. Instead, the probability of alternative Democratic candidates increased by more, suggesting that the majority of the decline in the betting market average for President Biden is more a reflection of the probability of him dropping out of the race than whether he would win in November should he remain the Democratic nominee.

Nonetheless, former President Trump has increased his margin as the betting market's favourite ( Exhibit 1 ), and he retains an advantage in seven key swing states ( Exhibit 2 ), and so investors should consider the implications of a higher probability of a Republican president under a divided Congress or a Republican sweep scenario. We see the following:

• A higher USD: We see a higher USD in particular in a Republican sweep scenario with deficit expansion and higher import tariffs the drivers but also in a divided government with a Republican president scenario, with higher import tariffs the main driver. This scenario should keep EM FX on the back foot.

• A steeper curve: Our US rates team expects to see a steeper UST curve and has positioned via 2s20s. A higher probability of import tariffs and changes to

immigration policy will likely lower growth expectations in the US economy, leaving the market to price more rate cuts from the Fed. A higher probability of deficit expansion could put upward pressure on long-end term premia.

• EM macro markets – rates > FX: The first point supports our generally bullish USD forecasts versus both G10 and EM currencies and so we are comfortable with our current long USD recommendations. A steeper US curve, if driven by the front end, should support our preference for EM rates over FX. Yet, a steeper curve driven by an expansion of term premia would be less helpful. The latter has the highest probability in a Republican sweep scenario an so for now we maintain our preference for EM rates over EM FX, particularly given the ongoing stability in EM inflation (see below).

• FX diverging from rate differentials: We noted last week that USD/Asia currency pairs are diverging from interest rate differentials. It is interesting to note that many currency markets have diverged from what interest rate differentials would imply. It's unclear exactly why, but we think that the prospect of US import tariffs may be playing a role. As such, this phenomenon could remain a feature of the market.

## India's inclusion impact on the rest of EM

India will join the GBI-EM index later this month. As discussed below, investors have already pre-positioned for the index inclusion to some extent. Their holding is 3.6% in bonds and 2.9% in FX as of end-May. This reduces their need to strictly follow the index by adding 1pp per month.

Should India keep INR stable, the RBI keep a hawkish stance to tame inflation and the Treasury keep a moderate fiscal deficit, we suspect that investors could have some moderate OW in both rates and FX in the size of 1-2%. This suggests that investors still need to add 8-9% of their AUM in India in the next 10 months.

Based on end-May data, we estimate that India's inclusion will be at the expense of Thailand (-1.6%), South Africa (-1.4%), Poland (-1.4%), the Czech Republic (-1.1%), Brazil (-0.9%) and Colombia (-0.8%).

During such a period, GBI-EM investors would need to either sell these markets' bonds to fund India trades or use any new inflows to buy into India.

This could prove to be a headwind for some of the biggest GBI-EM countries in the next 10 months.

That said, there have been key pockets of real underperformance: Despite index spreads only being marginally wider, there have been some credits that have underperformed significantly. We review the key drivers and review our positioning for each.

1) Africa HY – Kenya and Angola the standouts: We think that Africa's underperformance in the recent sell-off can be explained by three factors. First, the region had strong outperformance in 1Q24, hence the push by some investors to now take some chips off the table. Second, a lot of the positive catalysts that investors were expecting 6-9 months ago have now played out, i.e., Egypt FDI funding, Angola and Nigeria reforms, etc. Lastly, any US elections concerns manifesting in higher core rates would largely have an impact on credits who need market access. This affects a large chunk of Africa single B credits like Angola, Nigeria and Kenya whose financing needs depend on market access.

Kenya and Angola stand out as the notable underpeformers since mid-May ( Exhibit 18 ).

Kenya's substantial spread widening was more idiosyncratic in nature as the events over the last two weeks which resulted in the Finance Bill being withdrawn have put the spotlight back on Kenya's fiscal trajectory. The sell-off would have also been exacerbated by positioning, which had increased steadily in recent months from 0.05% UW in November 2023 to the current 0.28% OW.

Fundamentally the Finance Bill was meant to be the main reason for a smaller deficit in 2024 and 2025 but with this removed the heavy lifting now has to be done via reduced expenditure, which could result in lower growth. The 30-year bonds are now pricing in over one downgrade as a result ( Exhibit 19 ).

With the 10s30s curve now flat, we do not see any advantage holding long-end bonds, hence belly bonds like the 2032 would be our preference at these levels. The catalyst for us moving from neutral to negative would be fiscal driven should be authorities fail to come up with a credible detailed plan on how they plan to curb expenditure.

Angola's underperformance on the other hand has been purely beta driven, now 70bp wide at a country index level from May 15. There has not been any fundamental change to the underlying economy to warrant this as reserves continue to improve steadily and gross external financing needs are set to reduce in 2025. What's more, oil production has also improved steadily (June production average 1.2mbpd versus 2024 budget target of 1.060mbpd).

A possible reason for the underperformance is positioning. Angola's 0.59% OW is at its highest since 2018 and hence susceptible to selling pressure.

With no material change to the fundamental backdrop, we see the weakness as an opportunity to add, hence we retain our like stance on Angola with a preference for the long end (2049), where the current yield is higher at 11.1% versus the 2028 at 8.9%.

## What comes next?

Although BRL positioning looks somewhat short and valuations look cheap (though not extreme), we are hesitant to fade the ongoing sell-off. Ultimately, as BRL's underperformance relative to EM peers has been broadly driven by concern about the government's commitment to fiscal orthodoxy, we do not think that it falls on the BCB to stabilise the currency (barring operational dysfunction in the markets).

## Our current stance on Asia FX and rates

In a quiet two weeks without much US data, the market shifted the focus back to Asia: It is the weak CNY that gets most investors' attention. Over the past two weeks, the PBOC has allowed its fixing to gradually move from 7.1135 to 7.1268.

The absolute move is just 133 pips, which is nothing compared to other EM currencies. But the last time when DXY was close to 106 in April, the PBOC put the fixing firmly around 7.10. Why would the PBOC let fixing move higher now? We believe that there are a number of reasons:

First, USD has been quite strong in recent weeks: DXY is back above 106.0 now. Some Asian currencies are already past or close to the key level: 160 for USD/JPY, 1,400 for USD/KRW, 83.5 for USD/INR, 16,500 for USD/IDR and 59.0 for USD/PHP. The PBOC might feel that it is the broad USD which triggers CNY weakness rather than China's idiosyncratic factors.

Second, the PBOC might want to converge the fixing versus spot: But onshore CNY and offshore CNH have been above the fixing for a while. While a stable fixing provides an anchor for the currency, the deviation shows where the pressure is. Converging these two could reduce depreciation pressure in the medium term.

Third, the PBOC wants to inject some flexibility into CNY: Since mid-2023, CNY has been relatively stable. Its stability has yielded some good results such as anchoring expectations or stabilising the onshore stock market. But it raises the concern as to whether the currency is more pegged now versus USD and whether the currency is the stabiliser of the balance of payments. Moving the fixing higher in line with the fundamentals would be welcomed by the market.

How does it square with a strong currency required by the top leadership? The deputy governor of the PBOC explained her understanding of President Xi's comments about a strong currency: It should be interpreted as "stable" and "easy to use". "Easy to use" could refer to further internationalisation of CNY.

"Stable" doesn’t necessarily mean stable or no volatility versus USD – rather it should be put in the context of the CNY basket versus its trading partners. In such a sense, CNY CFETS has been quite stable and stronger in recent weeks. This suggests that there is room for USD/CNH to go higher even if USD/CNH is above 7.30.

This gradualism is in line with what we discussed in our mid-year outlook (see Asia Macro Strategy Mid-Year Outlook: Stress Testing Our Three Themes, June 3, 2024): the PBOC could let CNY weaken before the US election to alleviate the external pressure on the currency.

In addition, the presidential debate last week could increase concerns about tariffs, which we believe could be one of the catalysts to drive a higher USD/CNH in 3Q24.

INR a currency to watch: A higher USD/CNH could prompt weakness in broad Asia FX, as seen in the past two weeks. One currency to watch would be INR. It has been our top recommendation to be long from the start of 2024 and short CNH/INR and short TWD/ INR have generated a significant amount of carry and capital gain.

Triggers for INR weakness: There are many reasons why INR would be stable versus USD but there is a possible trigger for INR's weakness: CNY. India does see China as a competitor in its export sector. Since India wants to strengthen its manufacturing sector and compete with China. the CNY/INR level would be a key pair for policymakers to watch, in our view.

With a higher USD/CNH, the authority might be concerned about INR being a bit expensive versus CNH ( Exhibit 37 ). This could prompt the RBI to be less aggressive in capping the topside of USD/INR. In other words, USD/INR could start to move higher towards to 84.0 should CNH continue to depreciate. We are still comfortable with our short CNH/INR recommendation and would like tighten the stop to 11.60.

We continue to hold short TWD/INR and long USD/CNH 6m 7.40/7.60 call spread.

Adding long USD exposure: We have been holding long USD/THB with the view that USD could be stronger into 3Q. We would like to add long USD/PHP 3m NDF. This would make the BSP the first central bank in Asia to cut interest rates despite a relatively cautious stance from the Fed about cutting rates.

While it is right to cut interest rates due to lower domestic inflation, it would also put the Philippines' rates lower than its Asian peers. A combination of a current account deficit and fewer capital inflows could weigh on PHP, in our view. We set the target at 60.5 with a stop at 57.5.

## When to buy Indonesia?

Bottom line: IDR and INDOGBs have underperformed lately but we don't think that it is time to buy yet. IDR is facing headwinds from a narrowing current account surplus, portfolio outflows and fiscal concerns. INDOGBs don't look attractive compared to other EM markets, which makes global EM investors reluctant to buy. We think that the opportunity to buy INDOGBs and IDR will come once USD starts to weaken at a faster pace and the Fed commences its cutting cycle.

Indonesia used to be one of the EM darlings for many years: For context, there were one or two EM darlings in each region across CEEMEA, LatAm and Asia. In CEEMEA, it used to be Russia given its high real yield and current account surplus. In recent years, it has been South Africa bonds thanks to its steep yield curve which allows investors to gain carry without FX risks.

In LatAm, it is normally Mexico or Brazil taking turns to be the consensus OW due to their monetary policy cycle or high real rates. In Asia, it used to be Indonesia, which normally enjoys stable growth, prudent fiscal policy and high real rates. This explains why foreign ownership of INDOGBs used to be quite high.

However, this phenomenon started to change during/post the pandemic, with foreigners selling INDOGBs as foreign ownership of INDOGBs dropped from close to IDR 1,000 trillion in 2021 to IDR 800 trillion now ( Exhibit 39 ). Meanwhile, IDR also struggled to rally. During 4Q23, when DXY weakened from 106.5 to 101, USD/IDR only moved from 15,900 to 15,400.

## Why hasn't IDR performed well?

## The IDR underperformance can be explained by a few factors:

1) Indonesia's current account has been weakening: The current account balance peaked in 1Q23 at a surplus of 1.1% of GDP and has turned into a 0.5% of GDP deficit in 1Q24 ( Exhibit 41 ). The deterioration was driven mainly by the goods trade component, with exports falling faster than imports as commodity prices fell. This suggests that without a pick-up in commodity prices, exports may continue to soften, pushing the current account further into deficit.

Importantly, exporters have also been reluctant to convert their USD receipts into local currency. As the trade balance improved from 2019-23, corporates' foreign currency deposits rose ( Exhibit 42 ). However, even as the trade surplus was narrowing from 2023, corporates' foreign currency deposits have been increasing since July 2023.

## When will foreigners buy INDOGBs?

A circular argument: When we ask investors when they would start to buy INDOGBs, we often get the answer of "when IDR starts to appreciate". But when we push further on when IDR could start to appreciate, their answers would be when there would be inflows to INDOGBs.

The argument is a bit circular but it shows the drivers of the market. We agree that bond inflows are important for IDR, as we discussed in Asia Economics & Macro Strategy: Quantifying Macroeconomic Drivers of Asia’s Portfolio Flows, July 24, 2023. Hence, when foreigners would need to buy Indonesia would be important for both bond and FX markets.

How important are GBI-EM investors for INDOGBs: Foreign ownership of INDOGBs is IDR 800 trillion or close to US\$50 billion. GBI-EM investors own roughly US\$300 billion AUM and have almost 11% in Indonesia. Hence, GBI-EM investors account for over 60% of foreign investors in Indonesia. Understanding their behaviour would be important for INDOGBs and therefore IDR.

Therefore, when foreigners will buy Indonesia is a decision based on: 1) Indonesia's fundamentals; and 2) How investors see Indonesia compared to other EM markets in the global EM portfolio.

Regarding Indonesia's fundamentals, our economists will be discussing them in more detail in another note. Based on their mid-year outlook (see Asia Economics Mid-Year Outlook: Syncing Up, May 19, 2024), they see the capex cycle in Indonesia rising, which could create a self-sustaining virtuous growth cycle.

However, our conversations with clients suggest that investors are concerned about: 1) Whether BI needs to deliver more rate hikes to contain the IDR move; and 2) Whether the fiscal deficit would widen, raising the risk of rating downgrade.

In other words, investors don’t disagree with the structural story but there are two factors that prevent them from buying into Indonesia.

Meanwhile, Indonesia doesn't seem to stand out in its valuation and attractiveness compared to other EM markets: For example, INDOGBs yield is ranked sixth among major EM bond markets. But its yield isn't high as most CEE markets also offer 6%+ yield ( Exhibit 45 ).

When measuring yield per duration, a measure to gauge the risk/reward for every duration risk, we saw Indonesia's ranking moving towards most of the low yielders in Asia.

Overall, Indonesia no longer stands out as an EM darling as it did a few years ago: There are two implications. First, investors no longer treat Indonesia as a default OW or they will not allocate their AUM to Indonesia as a default choice when they decide to readjust the portfolio.

When will Indonesia outperform? This would allow BI to cut rates, which could trigger bond inflows. Bond inflows could prompt IDR to appreciate. This virtuous cycle could allow Indonesia to perform well.

...and HKD: Second, Chinese firms listed in Hong Kong will need to make their dividend payments to H-share holders in HKD, which could result in CNH/HKD conversion, weighing on CNH and tightening HKD liquidity at the same time.

...but H-share payments are set to increase in July and August: However, the downside pressure on RMB from dividend payments may not ease yet. Instead, RMB could face greater downward pressure in July and August, as Chinese companies' payments to Hong Kong shareholders are set to ramp up.

Combining both A-shares' payment to Stock Connect Northbound shareholders and Hshares' payment to Hong Kong shareholders, Chinese companies will need to pay out RMB

73 billion in July and RMB 197 billion in August, compared to RMB 34 billion in June ( Exhibit 50 ). This pick-up in payouts will happen mainly from the third week of July and stay high through August ( Exhibit 51 ).

More reinvestment and less repatriation? Historically, foreign investors usually reinvest about 50-60% of the dividend receipts. A key question relating to the TWD impact is whether there could be higher reinvestment and therefore less repatriation of dividend receipts by foreign investors this year given the AI rally. It is difficult to have a clear conclusion on this, in our view.

While it is true that the AI rally could encourage higher reinvestment of dividend receipts, foreign ownership of Taiwan equities has also increased substantially in recent months ( Exhibit 56 ). At the trough in September 2023, foreign ownership of TAIEX stood at 39.7%. This ratio has now risen to 43%, the highest since January 2022. All else equal, this points to potential for higher nominal amount of TWD outflows from dividend repatriation.

## Definition of terms

Buy/Long: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be positive over the relevant time period.

Sell/Short: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be negative over the relevant time period.

Selling Protection or Buying Risk: The analyst expects that the price of protection against the event occurring will decrease over the relevant time period.

Buying Protection or Selling Risk: The analyst expects the price of protection against the event occurring will increase over the relevant time period.

Pay: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will increase.

Receive: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will decrease.

Like: Based on current market conditions as of the date of this report the analyst expects that the relevant securities of the issuer that is subject of the recommendation will perform favorably over the relevant time period as compared to the overall market of comparable securities by other issuers. This is not intended to be, nor should it be interpreted as a formal fundamental rating of the issuer or its creditworthiness.

Dislike: Based on current market conditions as of the date of this report the analyst expects that the relevant securities of the issuer that is subject of the recommendation will perform unfavorably over the relevant time period as compared to the overall market of comparable securities by other issuers. This is not intended to be, nor should it be interpreted as a formal fundamental rating of the issuer or its creditworthiness.
