# Global Equity Strategy Quantitative Framework Update

Each month we update the three key parts of our quantitative framework:

1) Scorecards - Our individual scorecards rank sectors based on valuations, sensitivity to our proposed macro scenario, earnings momentum, price momentum (where extreme), positioning, and quality fundamentals. The composite scorecard combines all of these factors into one overall score.

2) Earnings versus Trend - We look at the EPS versus trend to evaluate one element of earnings risk as well as the fit with trend.

3) Machine Learning - This looks at which macro variables are the most important to the relative sector returns by refitting and training a machine learning model on a threemonth rolling basis. The variables we look at are largely daily macro proxies (such as 2-10 Yield Curve, Hard data, AI Narrative, China Proxy, Soft data, US 10y breakeven and US 10y real yield) and are supplied by Sean Simonds (see Tactical Dashboard).

This quantitative framework constitutes only one part of our overall weightings analysis. Our view remains that:

Regions: Our most preferred regions are, in order, GEM, up 1 place to number 2 on our scorecard, Japan (remains number 1 on our scorecard), and the UK (which we like tactically) is now at the bottom. Cont. Europe has moved up 2 places to be number 3 on our scorecard and thus we place it under review.

Sectors: In general we are overweight 'growth' defensives, with software followed by healthcare equipment being our most preferred sectors (and smaller areas, such as European defence, industrial gases and T&D-related utilities). Globally, healthcare equipment is number 2 and software slips one place to number 8. In Europe, utilities climb to number 2. Food producers have climbed to be top of the scorecard (up 1 place); we are selective in this sector. On the cyclical side we like copper, UK retailing, European/ Japanese banks (European banks are 2.1 std cheap on P/E, cheaper than any other sector, but have the 4th best earnings momentum - a good combination) and tyres.

Some of our least favoured sectors are luxury, global autos, capital goods telecoms and energy. Capital goods ranks second bottom on our global scorecard (and more middling in Europe), while energy ranks third bottom.

Style: Price momentum had been very extreme last time and in general is now less worrisome.

Market Direction: We don’t cover market direction in this scorecard. The tactical indicators are still elevated (and we would expect them to normalise), but we lifted our Global MSCI AC World year-end target to 800, from 750, on the back of AI and the rise in US inflation being temporary (see here).

Our Risk Appetite scorecard ranks regions based on equity risk appetite (reward for higher volatility sectors). We look at the rolling cross-sectional 6m linear regression coefficient of average return on standard deviation for each region. We work out the same for global sectors and subtract it from the absolute risk appetite of each region. Finally, we then standardise relative risk appetites against their 5-year history. Regions with lower risk appetite relative to global equity markets relative to history are ranked at the top.

## Narrative Radar ML Model

In order to understand which factors are currently having the largest impact on market moves, and which areas have over/undershot macro fundamentals, we deploy a machine learning model to understand and explain what is driving equity returns. Our Narrative Radar suite is published weekly in our Tactical Dashboard. We use our model for three main purposes:

1. What's driving markets? To understand what macro variables are the most important for market returns, we refit and train our model on a one-month rolling basis. We then deploy an algorithm known as SHAP, which is an impressive tool that can extract the extent to which a variable has impacted a model's prediction. The macro factors that we look at are: 2-10 Yield Curve, Hard data, AI Narrative, China Proxy, Soft data, US 10y breakeven, and US 10y real yield.

2. What's over/undershot fundamentals? Based on our model's learned relationships, we extrapolate the expected relative performance of industry groups and factors given the changes in macro drivers. We then compare this with actual performance to identify areas that are most disconnected with fundamentals.

3. Industry group and factor fan charts. We provide charts comparing the relative performance of each industry group and our machine learning model's expected performance. These charts also include confidence intervals to provide further context on how extreme these deviations are. The confidence intervals are calculated by using bootstrap sampling from our cross-validated errors in order to simulate errors around the prediction our model makes on the test data. For each bootstrap simulation we take the prediction made by the model and add on a randomly selected error. We perform this bootstrap simulation thousands of times to get our confidence range. We employ the same process and show the related output fan charts/confidence intervals for factor returns as well.
