# Review of F3/24 3Q Results

Companies on the TSE Prime Market have reported F3/24 3Q results. While the results confirmed healthy corporate earnings with a 20% YoY rise in RP, skewing toward certain industries stood out too. Total value of buyback programs established in F3/24 set a new all-time high amid TSE’s market reforms.

## Key Takeaways

At the all-industry level, F3/24 1-3Q (Apr-Dec) results were increases (YoY) of 5.3% in sales, 19.7% in RP, and 24.2% in NP.

While announcements confirmed healthy corporate earnings, large contributions from utilities (electricity/gas) and transport equipment industries stood out.

The ratios of above/below consensus results were roughly the same at 50%. Multiple cases of missing consensus by firms in industries with high exposure to China.

We forecast increases (YoY) of 4.0% in sales and 17.2% in RP in F3/24 and 2.7% in sales and 7.0% in RP in F3/25 (consensus estimates).

Total value of buyback programs set in F3/24 was an all-time high ¥9.4trn. Despite record-high stock prices, firms are actively conducting shareholder return.

Yen appreciation (yen depreciation (appreciation) is a positive (negative) factor for Japan’s overall corporate earnings)

Weakening of the external environment, particularly Chinese and US economies Renewed inflation and delays passing along higher costs to prices.

## F3/24 1-3Q (Apr-Dec) results

In 1-3Q (Apr-Dec) results for companies listed on the TSE Prime Market with March fiscal years, the all-industry level had increases (YoY) of 5.3% in sales, 19.7% in RP, and 24.2% in NP.

Forex effect contributed positively on yen depreciation in major currencies with the USD/ JPY shifting from an Apr-Dec 2022 average of ¥136.4 to an Apr-Dec 2023 average of ¥143.3 and the EUR/JPY moving from ¥140.6 to ¥155.3. RP increase rates (YoY) by industry were manufacturing at 14.4%, non-manufacturing at 17.7%, and financials at 45.7%. All industries (ex. financials) posted gains of 3.6% in sales and 15.7% in RP.

Within the 19.7% rise in all-industry RP, industry contributions were manufacturing at +7.4ppt, non-manufacturing at +6.3ppt, and financials at +6.0ppt ( Exhibit 2 ).

Individual industries that contributed positively to RP included utilities (electricity and gas) at +8.6ppt, transportation equipment at +8.6ppt, insurers at +2.7ppt, and banks at +2.7ppt. Large positive contributions by the top two industries are a key feature of the latest results announcements. RP only rose slightly without these boosts. In transportation equipment, Toyota (7203) provided the bulk of the increase at a 5.8ppt contribution thanks to the profit lift from higher sales volume, improved mix, and yen depreciation, and Honda (7267), Subaru (7270), and other auto OEMs also posted higher profits ( Exhibit 4 ). Utilities (electricity and gas) mainly benefited from a technical factor of period disparity in the fuel cost adjustment program.

Individual industries that contributed negatively to RP included marine transportation (- 4.4ppt), wholesale (-1.8ppt), and chemicals (-1.0ppt). Decline in container ship rates (YoY) heavily affected marine transportation.

## Share buybacks: F3/24 program establishment value set a new all-time high

Share buyback programs established in F3/24 (April 2023 through March 2024) by companies listed on the TSE Prime market totaled ¥9.4trn as of February 14. With just over a month left until the fiscal year-end, the total already exceeds F3/23’s level (¥9.3trn) and set an all-time high. The number of programs thus far is 610, coming in below levels from February in F3/22 and F3/20. Nevertheless, the latter two cases were prior to TSE’s market reorganization. Since the former TSE-1 market had more companies than the current Prime market, we think the F3/24 volume is effectively the largest.

Program establishment value trailed F3/23 levels through November 2023, even with TSE’s request for “Action to Implement Management that is Conscious of Cost of Capital and Stock Price” (disclosed on March 31, 2023). This happened because of large upswing by the stock market and resulting decline in share buyback incentives. Since 2024, however, it is surprising that companies are actively conducting shareholder return despite record-high stock prices. TSE has been firmly standing by the reforms, including issuing a list of companies that have provided disclosure regarding “Action to Implement Management that is Conscious of Cost of Capital and Stock Price” since January 2024. In response, we think listed companies have adopted a shared recognition of the need to implement shareholder return.

Stock splits

JPX disclosed “Initiatives to Invigorate the Securities Market in Preparation for the Start of the New NISA” on December 15, 2023. In this document, JPX states that it “will promote efforts to further reduce investment units to allow investment with a relatively small amount of funds.” This encourages stock splits assuming continuation of 100 shares as the unit amount.

Stock splits generally exhibit correlation to stock market movement ( Exhibit 22 ). The roughly 160 splits in 2023 exceeded the about 100 splits in 2022, but this increase was on track with the rise in stock prices and hence actually not that much higher than in the past. JPX issued a “Request for Consideration of Reducing the Investment Unit” on October 27, 2022. While some stocks at high prices announced stock splits following the request, the impact was limited.

Nevertheless, we expect more stock splits as momentum strengthens in the Japanese stock market with the new NISA tailwind. The number of stock splits thus far in 2024 is outpacing the level from the past five years for the same timing with a boost from rising stock prices ( Exhibit 23 ). We present stocks with large minimum investment value that are relatively likely to implement stock splits in Exhibit 24 .

Japan had two previous peaks in stock splits. The first was 2004-05 during the phenomenon of strong gains by stock prices of companies that announced splits. This was known as the stock-split bubble. The second was 2013. Following guideline releases by securities exchanges nationwide in 2007, companies revised the unit amount through April 2014 with consolidation at either 100 shares or 1,000 shares. In the case of a stock with a unit amount of one share, meanwhile, a change to 100 shares meant a 100-fold rise in minimum investment value and tougher investment hurdle. There was a surge of lastminute actions by companies simultaneously changing the unit amount and splitting the stock to avoid a tougher hurdle in 2013. Coincidentally, the next year (2014) was the year in which general NISA, the former system, started.
