# Growth Holds Up; Benign Macro-stability

Domestic demand slowed sequentially in February, while macro stability reflects strength in the fundamentals. We maintain our constructive outlook on the economy. Risks emanate from global factors and elections in May 2024.

1) Growth: Domestic demand for February was largely steady on a YoY basis, even as it declined on a sequential basis. Revenue from GST collections came in at Rs1.68tn, up 12.5% YoY, and taking the FYTD average to Rs1.67bn. Manufacturing PMI improved to 56.9, remaining expansionary since Jul-21, while Services PMI tracked at 60.6, supported by positive demand trends, reflected in strong sales and business activity. On the external demand front, exports expanded 11.9% YoY in February from 3.1% in the previous month.

2) Inflation: CPI inflation remained steady at 5.1% YoY in February, similar to January levels, while core inflation slowed to an all-time low on the current base of 3.4%. WPI moderated to 0.2% YoY in February from 0.3% in the previous month.

3) Trade deficit: Trade deficit widened to US\$18.7bn in February from US\$16.5bn in January. On a monthly annualised basis, the trade deficit expanded to 6.4% of GDP from 5.6% of GDP in January, while the services trade surplus is tracking at 5.7% of GDP in Feb-24 vs. 5.5% of GDP in Jan-24. We expect the current account to be in surplus in QE Mar-24.

4) Tracking policy: Interbank liquidity swung to surplus in March (month to date), and is tracking at US\$6.5bn, while the weighted average interbank call rate has moderated a tad below the repo rate to 6.4%. On the fiscal side, the 12-month trailing deficit moderated to 5.7% of GDP in January from 6% in December. The FYTD fiscal deficit is tracking at \~63.6% of the F2024 budgeted target.

5) Outlook: On growth, we remain constructive on the growth outlook, given support from domestic demand, as reflected in the robust trend in high-frequency growth data. As per the advance estimate, GDP growth is pegged at 7.6% in F2024. We retain our growth estimate of 6.5% for F2025e. With regard to macro-stability, we anticipate headline inflation to remain range-bound around 5.0% YoY in March, supported by the recent cutting of fuel prices and favourable base effect, and thus average 5.4% YoY in F2024 and 4.5% in F2025. The current account deficit is likely to remain benign, supported by strength in services exports and softening global commodity prices, especially oil, and track at 0.9% of GDP in F2024 and 1% of GDP in F2025. On monetary policy, we see the possibility of a shallow easing cycle (cumulative 50bp), but we are watchful of risks from slower-than-expected moderation in inflation, faster-than-expected growth trend and external risks from global financial conditions/commodity prices.
