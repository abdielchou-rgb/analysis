# 2024 China Equity Strategy Mid-year Outlook | Asia Pacific

# Seeking Certainty Amid Change

We expect a range-bound market in near term, as recent rally has largely priced in the positive developments since February. Despite positive policy pivots, we caution against persistent deflation pressure and rising complexity from trade protection and geopolitics. Prefer A-shares over offshore.

We stay equal-weight on Chinese equities within an EM/APxJ context: YTD China has been the best performing market globally, but with ups and downs. We believe the current index level already priced in the improvement on multiple fronts, including macro stabilization, flows and positioning, and government policy pivots. However, deflationary pressure is likely to persist with policy focusing more on the manufacturing side, and housing inventory digestion facing uncertainty. Geopolitical complexity is likely to rise under trade protection disputes and ahead of the US election. We believe a range-bound market is more likely in the coming months.

Our June 2025 targets are, respectively, 19,150 (+1%), 6,800 (+1%), 61 (-1%) and 3,900 (+7%) for Hang Seng, HSCEI, MSCI China and CSI300: We have relatively flat price targets for June 2025 except for CSI300, implying no upside for the offshore space. We have revised up our earnings growth forecast for 2024 to reflect a better-than-expected macro stabilization trend and less cautious view on USDCNY. Our bear/bull scenarios for June 2025 targets continue to represent a wide range of performance outcomes (for MSCI China from bear -31% to bull +21%), with the bull/ bear skew more tilted towards the downside in absolute scale and relative to EM.

We advise OW A-shares within China allocation and expect its outperformance vs. the offshore space to resume. Key reasons include: 1) external factors behind offshore outperformance since mid April have cooled; 2) the A-share market is less impacted by geopolitical uncertainty; and 3) reforms targeting corporate governance and shareholder return enhancement are being rolled out on the back of a firmer stance by regulators to stabilize the A-share market.

Sector preferences - We maintain our preference for selected Materials and Consumer, and turn less cautious on Banks: We turn less cautious on Banks from a top-down perspective given incremental government initiatives to contain macro and property risk, as well as a potential tax cut catalyst for HK listed dividend plays. We retain an underweight on Real Estate given still weak fundamentals and our sector analyst's more cautious view.

Key trades for 2H2024: 1) Include more A-shares in a broad China equity portfolio. 2) See our China/Hong Kong Focus List and A-share Thematic lists, which take into consideration our top-down sector preference and macro cyclicality in the near/ medium term. 3) Focus on thematic investing: SOE Reform Beneficiaries & China Going Global. 4) In the event a Southbound dividend tax cut is confirmed, see our screen of stocks best positioned as dividend plays.

## Executive Summary

YTD China has been the best performing market globally, but not without ups and downs: YTD MSCI China, Hang Seng, and CSI300 have respectively delivered 11%, 12%, and 6% on a total return basis in USD terms, compared to S&P (11%), Topix (18%), MSCI ACWI (10%), MSCI EM (8%). That said, the trajectory for Chinese equity market indices has not been a straight upward line, but with severe outflows and index drawdowns in January before rebounding from the start of February. The recovery had been largely gradual and measured until mid-April, after which a confluence of external (non-China) factors led to an accelerated rally.

From here we see hurdles for the market to climb higher: Many of those external factors, such as concerns over higher yield and FX volatility, have largely been digested by the market, while the Chinese equity market's valuation has also recovered to long-term average levels, with UW positions reduced across the board by global mutual fund investors. Policy pivots by the government are taking place, but so far we think they are still not enough to solve housing inventory issue or reflate economy forcefully. Geopolitical complexity may also rise with potential trade protection escalation and the US election drawing closer. Therefore, we stay equal-weight within an EM/APxJ context and expect a range-bound market from here, especially for the offshore space.

Our June 2025 targets for the Hang Seng, HSCEI, MSCI China and CSI300 are 19,150, 6,800, 61 and 3,900, respectively, implying no upside for the offshore space. We have revised up our earnings growth forecast for 2024 to reflect better-than-expected macro stabilization trend, as well as our China economics team's recently upgraded China 2024 GDP growth forecast, and less caution on USDCNY towards year-end. Earnings growth, however, will remain subpar vs. historical levels under persistent deflation pressure and won't recover to double-digits (13% for MSCI China) until 2026, by our estimate. Meanwhile, we see no further valuation re-rating upside, especially for the offshore space.

Our bull/bear scenarios for June 2025 targets continue to represent a wide range of Chinese equity market performance outcomes - Bull/bear skew is more tilted towards the downside in absolute terms and relative to EM (for MSCI China from bear -31% to bull +21%). The bull case entails more forceful and prompt reflationary efforts to rebalance the economy and digest housing inventories, on top of more positive engagement by and with China in a global geopolitical context. The bear case is a scenario where China fails to effectively break out of the current move towards a debt-deflation loop, and both corporates and consumers start to behave in somewhat austerity mode, and try to delever further and hold on to cash.

We advise to OW A-shares within China allocation and expect its outperformance vs. the offshore space to resume. Key reasons include: 1) external factors behind offshore outperformance since mid April have cooled; 2) the A-share market is less impacted by geopolitical uncertainty; and 3) reform measures targeting corporate governance and shareholder return enhancement are being rolled out for the A-share market, on the back of a firmer stance by regulators to stabilize the A-share market. Since the start of 2018, CSI300 on a currency hedged, total return basis has cumulatively outperformed MSCI China by 16%.

## YTD strong performance assisted by confluence of external/ domestic factors

2024 YTD China has been the best performing market globally, but not without ups and downs: YTD MSCI China, Hang Seng, and CSI300 have respectively delivered 11%, 12%, and 6% on total return basis in USD terms, compared to S&P (11%), Topix (18%), MSCI ACWI (10%), MSCI EM (8%) ( Exhibit 2 ). That said, the trajectory for Chinese equity market indices has not been a straight upward line, but with severe outflows and index drawdowns in January before rebounding from the start of February until mid-April.

A confluence of external and internal factors have contributed to the rally since February and the acceleration since mid-April: As discussed in greater detail in the recent rally report, we believe the following reasons collectively have led to it:

1) Domestically, we have seen some early signs of macro stabilization, especially in exports and manufacturing, together with stepped-up efforts by policymakers to stabilize the stock market (with a focus on A-shares).

2) Positioning: global long-only investors hit their lowest underweight positions around the end of January. Continuous outflows from China/HK finally took a break.

3) From a technical perspective, Chinese equities' realized volatility peaked in late January, forcing fund managers to reduce their active risk by trimming underweights and covering shorts.

4) Global/external factors played a critical role, especially in explaining the rally acceleration after mid April. The context behind this rally includes diversification away from geopolitical uncertainty, and rising volatility in the US and Japanese markets. Investors took profits on key US Tech/Semi outperformers and adjusted portfolios as the VIX spiked on Friday, 19 April, ahead of anticipated military conflict in the Middle-East. The expectation of a higher yield for longer ahead of the May FOMC meeting and yen weakness also exacerbated such actions.

Modest policy easing by the government moving in the right direction; however, scale and timeline uncertain over meaningful economy reflation: We note that Chinese policy makers have made incremental progress in stabilizing the macro and containing a potential debt-deflation loop, including a push for a faster budget rollout (urging an acceleration in government bond issuance into 2H2024, as well as the launch of the auto trade-in program), initial moves towards reining in overcapacity in the lithium battery market, and increasing efforts to support housing inventory digestion (including for the first time a PBOC facilitated relending program to help resolve the housing inventory situation). More measures on the fiscal and monetary side could be added if existing efforts turn out to be insufficient to reflate the economy, including additional an RRR cut, rate cut and/or fiscal deficit widening (see details in 2024 China Economics Mid-Year outlook).

However, more will be needed to put the economy back on a reflationary track, as estimated by our China economics team. In particular, given the government's focus has remained on pushing investment in manufacturing rather than boosting consumption, deflationary pressure will likely still linger for longer. Our China economics team expects the GDP deflator to stay subdued at -0.3% for 2024 (-0.5% in 2023) and rebound only modestly in 2025, to 0.4%, keeping nominal GDP growth subpar at <5%. This suggests a still challenging macro environment for corporate earnings growth, which is more correlated with nominal GDP growth (more detailed discussion in later sections).

As a result we see an improved equity market environment for alpha opportunities compared with 6-12 months ago, while more is needed to push the index level from here. More stabilized investor positioning as well as sentiment, and more stabilized macro growth in our view, will accommodate stock level performance dispersion driven by fundamentals and thematics, whereas stock moves tend to conform when fund flow factors take over, and/or the market becomes seriously concerned over policy direction/ economic growth model, such as worries over China deviating from a market-oriented economy, or concerns about China falling into long-term deflation like Japan, which had effectively derated MSCI China to 10.5x on a 12-month forward P/E basis during late 2022 and early 2024.

## Stay equal-weight and monitor for better macro visibility; external factors worth watching in a year of election and rate cycle change

We therefore decide to stay equal-weight on China within global EM context as we believe the current index level has already largely priced in the improvement on multiple fronts, which means a range-bound market is more likely from here.

The bull case entails more forceful and prompt reflationary efforts to rebalance the economy (with an increased step-up in actions targeting consumption), and digest housing inventories, on top of more positive engagement by and with China in a global geopolitical context. In such a scenario, we see earnings revisions finally wrapping up the downward cycle, while margin improvement and top-line growth lead to healthier borrowing and Capex expansion. ROE rebounds, while valuations re-rate closer to the

historical median level since 2017, pushing Chinese equities (MSCI China) closer to the global trendline ( Exhibit 10 ). We project 19% upside for MSCI China in the bull case, slightly above the 17% upside for the broad EM index. We expect MSCI China's valuation discount vs. MSCI EM to narrow (from 13% suggested by the base case to 8% based on the bull case) but not entirely close. We do expect a relative valuation discount for MSCI China vs. EM to persist much longer, given the general slower macro growth path and global Multipolar World backdrop (more discussed in the following sections).

In the bear case, however, China sees minimal earnings growth in the next couple of years at least, while valuations are pushed closer to historical trough levels. This is a scenario where China fails to effectively break out of the current debt-deflation pressure loop, and both corporates and consumers start to behave in austerity mode, and try to delever further and hold on to cash. Key triggers for this case could be over tightening due to LGFV and housing market deleveraging, combined with a serious deterioration in the trade environment. We see 3%, 4% and 3% earnings growth for MSCI China in 2024, 2025, and 2026, respectively, as a result. We also see MSCI China's valuation dropping to 9.0x, closer to the recent cycle valuation troughs (i.e., 8.0x during end January 2024, and 8.4x during October 2022).

2024 is also an important year from an external development perspective given: 1) a major US election is coming up; 2) Fed rate cut cycle is to kick off in 2H (September for the first cut, followed by two more in Nov. and Dec., as forecast by MS); and 3) rising trade protection dispute with EU. All developments, depending on how things play out, could also have significant implications for the China market, potentially impacting equity risk premium, FX, fund flows, and global investor risk appetite, among others. ## Maintain relative preference for A-shares vs. offshore

We have been largely recommending that investors overweight China A-shares within their broad Chinese equity portfolios over the past four years, except for the short period between November 2022 and February 2023 when we recommended the opposite. CSI 300, A-shares have also exhibited consistent outperformance vs. the offshore market on a 3, 5, 10 and 15-year CAGR basis, but underperformed on 1-year CAGR basis (-10%). ## 2024 a defining year for growth stabilization; yet reflation journey could still be challenging

Our MS China economists expect real GDP growth to stabilize at 4.8% in 2024 from exports and manufacturing capex strength (vs. the 2022-23 average of 4.1%). Export volume growth is likely to stay robust at 8%Y in 2024 (versus 2%Y in 2023 and 10%Y in

However, gradual stabilization in the housing market in response to incrementally stronger policy support could mitigate the headwinds.

Exports remain a key growth driver for 2024; uncertainty could rise in 2025: MS China economics team expects volume growth to remain robust at 8%YoY in 2024, thanks to global trade recovery and lower export prices. Central government moving in the right direction to stabilize domestic demand and cushion for macro pressure; scale and timing remain key: We also acknowledge the fact that Chinese policy makers are making incremental efforts to stabilize domestic demand. ## Valuation already reflecting improved sentiment towards China by global investors

Valuation back above 10x 12-month forward P/E, first time since summer 2023: MSCI China now trades at 10.3x 12-month forward P/E with an 18% discount vs. MSCI EM. \~10x had been historically been the lower bound of MSCI China's P/E valuation range since 2017 (after Chinese ADRs were fully included in MSCI indices; we consider the valuation level prior to that as less comparable given the index universe difference). The two times that MSCI China's forward P/E had broken below this level – October 2022 and the most recent cycle after summer 2023 – were both tied to concerns about China's long-term growth policy and trajectory (Covid lockdown in 2022 and persistent deflation since 2H2023). Meanwhile, the 29% discount vs. EM had also been the maximum discount since October 2015, until the most recent cycle.

Further valuation move subject to multiple factors: Therefore, we think that this valuation reflects that global investors' perception towards investing in Chinese equities has already improved meaningfully (also echoed by the latest global fund positioning data which we will discuss later in the note), and has largely priced in the recent positive development of macro stabilization, as well as policy pivot efforts. It will be important to see whether China's valuation and the equity risk premium associated with investing in China can recover to visibly above 10x with a much narrower discount vs. EM, or if China will be subject to a persistently lower valuation regime than before. We believe the following developments will bear monitoring:

1. The fundamentals: It remains to be seen whether China can successfully reflate its economy, and allow positive and faster earnings growth drive equity market returns higher.

2. Policy direction: The priority of economic growth in the policy agenda is still being debated by investors as the housing market slowdown drags on and lackluster consumption momentum continues.

3. Global rate/liquidity environment and risk appetite: Rate cut cycle's timing and magnitude, and USD strength could impact the attractiveness of EM risk assets in general.

4. Geopolitical concerns: Geopolitical complexity is more likely to rise than abate as we go through the US election season and as China faces more headwinds around overseas trade and transactions.

## Policy cycle, regulation and liquidity: Incremental pivot welcome; More is needed

Modest fiscal and monetary easing ongoing: The March NPC set an augmented fiscal deficit widening at 0.5% of 2024 GDP, the same as 2023. On top of that, initial fiscal rollout so far this year has been lagging the schedule, given stronger-than-expected real GDP growth led by exports. The government has already started to accelerate support, evidenced by the start of an Rmb1trn ultra-long STB issuance from May 17 and the planned issuance of Rmb1.1-1.2trn in LGSB for May-Jun (vs. \~Rmb700bn in January-April). The key policy focus will be on tech localization, energy safety, and urbanization. On the monetary front, MS China economics team expects a 25-50bps RRR cut in the coming

couple of months to accommodate faster government bond issuance, followed by a 20bps policy rate cut in 2H24, facilitated by a Fed pivot this September.

## Geopolitical dynamics – Election season unfolding

Global investor concerns about geopolitical uncertainty seem to have been receding recently despite global multilateral developments that suggest ongoing risk factors: Investor sentiment seems to have become less sensitive to negative headlines that arguably would have triggered market moves at other times. The most recent examples include the US announcement of tariff hikes targeting certain Chinese products on May 14th, Russian President Putin's Beijing visit during May 16-17, and the recent inauguration of Taiwan's new leader.

However, we do not expect such 'immunity' to be long-lasting. Investors should still be, in our view, prepared for sustained geopolitical complexity in the coming months as the US election draws closer, and as EU's probe into imports from China intensifies. Ripple effects of trade protectionism particularly bear watching, given the recent US tariff hike event. For example, more than a third of China's 'new trio' and semiconductor exports go to the EU, where an anti-subsidy probe into China's green exports is in progress given rising concerns of a 'China Shock 2.0', in which China's low export prices undercut local manufacturers in other countries. There seems to have been some acceleration of traderelated probes between the EU and China recently:

\- The European Commission is contemplating potential measures against Chinese EVs with a deadline of July 4th;

\- The European Commission has just closed another subsidy investigation against two Chinese solar panel makers after they withdrew bids for the Romanian photovoltaic park contracts;

\- On May 17th the European Union announced that it would launch an investigation into Chinese tinplate steel;

\- A Chinese auto research expert from a government-affiliated auto research body (Mr. Liu Bin, China Automotive Technology & Research Center [CATARC] and deputy director of China Automotive Strategy and Policy Research Center) said that China should raise its import tariffs on large gasoline-powered cars to 25%, a tariff level in-line with WTO rules.

The other key risk is whether the US will take further action to dis-incentivize Chinese companies from moving assembly and manufacturing to other countries for final export to the US as China promotes outbound investment. In addition, a bipartisan hawkish view towards China could escalate further as the US election season officially unfolds in the next few months.

## What could get us more bullish from here?

At the current juncture, we look for a combination of the following signals before potentially turning more bullish on Chinese equities:

\- Timely step-up of stabilization measures with more aggressive fiscal stimulus targeting private consumption and housing inventory digestion. Whether policy makers are ready to expand the inventory digestion relending program to achieve a faster and broader solution could be critical to seeing an earlier bottoming out in housing sales. More targeted easing for domestic consumption would also provide a boost for Chinese households still struggling with declining home asset value, and gloomy job and income outlooks.

\- The consensus estimates downward revision trend slows down continually or even bottoms out. We look for at least another quarter of slowdown in earnings estimate cuts to confirm the trend. Earnings growth re-acceleration could help further solidify investors confidence as well.

\- More forceful implementation of shareholder return enhancement measures and corporate governance reforms.

\- Structural reform announcements at the Third Plenum in July: These could include more detailed structural SOE, pension and benefit network reforms, over-capacity management, etc.

\- Improved multilateral geopolitical relationships on a global basis: This could include more progress on communications (trade, economic, financial market, tourism, education, services, healthcare, etc.) with the EU, Asean, and other non-US regions.

## We believe the A-share market could resume outperformance vs. offshore in the coming months

We believe that the broad market setup still favors the A-share market in the coming months, and expect its outperformance vs. offshore space to resume. Our base case price target for June 2025 for CSI300 is 3,900, suggesting another 7% upside, compared to what we have for MSCI China at 61 with a 1% downside. The key reasons are:

• The key drivers behind performance divergence between A-shares and offshore space have largely cooled or faded away, as discussed earlier;

• The upcoming US election season and associated geopolitical uncertainty tends to cause less market impact to A-shares vs. offshore given the A-share market is still dominated by domestic investors;

• Firmer stance by policy makers to stabilize the stock market with a focus on Ashares: Such efforts by Chinese policy makers date back to late August 2023, when a series of measures was announced, including halving stamp duty and restricting share disposals by controlling shareholders. On October 12, 2023, the Chinese government announced direct buying of stocks in the A-share market through Central Huijin. Since late January 2024 there has been a major step-up in efforts. A dedicated market stabilization package was reported and new leadership for the CSRC was announced. There has been a stronger push for shareholder return enhancement (including dividend payment and share buybacks) and corporate governance reforms, as well as tighter regulatory measures against market manipulative, non-compliant and/or illegal activities. The latest new Nine Articles for the capital markets have also put an institutionalized focus on these initiatives.

\- Major expansion of investment restrictions towards IT, healthcare and semi by the US: Many such companies are listed in the A-share market;

\- Much stronger and sooner reflation success under a major policy pivot and recovery of confidence in China's long-term growth outlook by global investors;

\- Much stronger/stabler CNY relative to other EM currencies and/or spike in demand to diversify away from major equity markets, such as US and Japan;

\- Relative valuation vs. offshore getting a lot more stretched: CSI 300 now trades at a 16% premium vs. MSCI China on a 12-month forward P/E basis. ## Turning less bearish on Banks

We turn less cautious on Banks from a top-down perspective given incremental initiatives to contain macro and property risk, as well as a potential catalyst for dividend plays: Earlier-than-expected macro stabilization signs since February have helped support a more stable economic environment, suggesting potentially smaller erosion to banks' profits than the market had expected. Moreover, the recently announced property market stabilization measures, particularly the inventory digestion relending program, in our Bank analyst's view - could serve to cushion property prices in core areas by taking out some inventory there, without having to worry about overly aggressive credit issuance as a result. This is because the properties with little resale or rental value will remain impaired assets and gradually be digested as the cycle runs its course. Therefore, banks could benefit from more stable property credit demand without sacrificing credit quality or allocating more credit to low-return projects.

Meanwhile, recent market debate around a potential dividend tax cut for Stock Connect Southbound could potentially support Hong Kong listed stocks with high dividend payments, see our detailed report China Equity Strategy: Reported Dividend Tax Cut for Southbound - Our Take (10 May 2024). Out of the 29 names that screened well to benefit from the potential tax cut, if reports turn out to be true, 7 are banks.
