# APAC Economic Perspectives BSP: Turning a little less hawkish

## BSP left rates on hold, but flags less upside risks to inflation

The BSP left the target reverse repurchase (RRP) rate unchanged at 6.5% p.a. at today's policy meeting, in line with consensus and UBS expectations. It lowered its 2024 baseline and risk-adjusted inflation forecasts by 0.1ppt and 0.5ppt, respectively, to 3.6% and 3.9%. Both are now within the central bank's 3±1% inflation target. The reduction in the risk-adjusted forecast reflects BSP's assessment of less acute upside risks, even though its baseline outlook was "broadly unchanged", as per the monetary policy statement (MPS). The MPS also dropped the reference to the"need to keep monetary policy settings sufficiently tight", merely noting that it would be "appropriate to keep the monetary policy settings unchanged in the near term". The tone is slightly less hawkish, in our view.

## Inflation is now within target, even though rice prices remain elevated

Headline inflation has fallen within the central bank's 3±1% inflation target for two consecutive months (Jan/Dec: 2.8%/3.9%). Meanwhile, core inflation fell anothe 0.3ppt to 3.4% y/y (+0.5% m/m) in January. Food inflation has eased rapidly, from a high of 10% y/y in Sep'23, to just 3.3% y/y in Jan'24. This was mainly due to base effects (recall the onion price surge last Jan and the typhoon-triggered spike in vegetable prices in Q4'22). Sequentially, food prices rose 0.8% m/m in January, mainly led by rice (+2.3% m/m). This was offset by lower vegetable prices (-2.0% m/m), oils and fats (-0.2% m/m), and sugar and confectionery (-0.5% m/m).

## BSP still expects inflation to breach 4% in Q2, as do we

At the media conference, BSP said it expects inflation to rise above 4% in April-July 2024 (due to base effects), before subsiding to around 3% in Q4. Our forecasts for inflation are generally similar to the BSP's. However, factoring in the weaker-than-expected January print, we shave down our full-year forecast for inflation to 3.5% in this note (Prev: 3.6%). We expect inflation to average 3.0% in Q1, 4.0%, in Q2, 3.6% in Q3, and 3.4% in Q4. In our forecasts, inflation hovers slightly over 4% in May-July 2024. We expect rice prices to ease gradually after the rice harvest which comes on market in March (see our previous report here). Downside risks to inflation might materialize if the government ramps up imports (rice imports in December increased by 29% y/y to 387,210 metric tons), or if the rice harvest is ample. The fact that core inflation remains well contained, and that inflationary pressures are not broad-based, supports our view that this supply-driven inflation will eventually normalize.

## Scope for rate cuts from around mid-year, RRR cut also possible

At the media conference, BSP officials reiterated that risks to inflation still lean to the upside, and that the BSP would see scope for easing once inflation expectations are well anchored and inflation has reverted to the target band. It added that the monetary policy decision gives "more weight to domestic events", rather than being beholden to the timing of the Fed's pivot. We still expect BSP to cut interest rates by 100bp this year. The BSP also said that it intends to reduce the reserve requirement ratio to be more in line with neighbouring economies, but the timing depends on when inflationary risks subside.

## Thesis Map: Bangko Sentral ng Pilippinas Q: What policy direction does inflation signal?

Neutral. The BSP's decision to stay on hold at 6.50% was based on a moderated inflation outlook over the policy horizon. The BSP revised down its risk-adjusted 2024 CPI inflation forecast to 3.9% (versus the official forecast of 4.4% at the November meeting). In its view, supply-side inflation pressures continue to ease, partly helped by the national government's non-monetary interventions as well as seasonal factors. However, risks to inflation are still skewed to the upside. Key upside risks are associated with higher transport charges, electricity rates, and international oil prices, and domestic food prices (incl. El Nino). The BSP’s latest survey of external forecasters shows that inflation expectations became more firmly anchored, with a mean forecast that is within range for both 2024 and 2025. The government recently signed a five-year trade MOU with Vietnam, under which Vietnam is committed to supply 1.5-2mn metric tons of white rice annually for the next five years. The National Irrigation Administration (NIA) is also ramping up efforts in production and aims to reach rice self-sufficiency by 2028 (see here).

## Q: What policy direction does resource utilization signal?

Neutral to looser. The government projects 6.5-7.5% growth in 2024. The policy statement said that the sustained expansion in output in Q4 2023 reaffirms the BSP’s view that the country’s growth momentum remains intact over the medium term. However, recent indicators also suggest that economic activity could moderate in the near term as the full impact of the BSP’s prior monetary policy tightening continues to manifest. In response to queries, BSP officials said that the expectation is for growth to be "slightly below potential" in 2024.

## Q: What policy direction does the financial stability situation signal?

Neutral. The BSP's policy adjustments are aimed in part at mitigating the risk of financial sector volatility. Official concerns over asset quality in the banking system are declining as the gross NPL ratio has fallen from 4.5% in August 2021 to 3.5% in October 2022, and further to 3.2% in December 2023. The peso has remained generally stable despite broad dollar strength.

The median forecast is for the BSP's key policy rate to fall to 5.50% by end 2024. The upper end of the forecast range is 6.25% for end-2024 and the lower end of the forecast range is 4.5%.

We expect BSP policy rates to remain on hold in the near term. We expect 100bps of rate cuts in 2024, and for the BSP's policy rate to close the year at 5.5%.

Reaccelerating inflation pressures promising headline inflation well above the target range in 2024 could lead the BSP to introduce more rate hikes. Policy tightening could turn into rate cuts sooner than expected if inflation cools faster than expected, or if growth disappoints due to the fallout from domestic policy tightening (less likely). We think the main constraint to earlier rate cuts is inflation, rather than the Fed's policy.
