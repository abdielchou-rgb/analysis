# Global Rates Strategy June/September Eurex Futures Rolls

## Relative value and curve dynamics

The first notice day for all June Eurex fixed income futures is on June 6, coinciding with the ECB meeting on the same day. Currently, the front and back contracts of Bobl, IK, and OAT futures have the same CTDs. However, Bund, Schatz, and Buxl futures have different CTDs, making relative value and curve dynamics more relevant for this specific futures roll.

## Positioning dynamics

We cannot draw significant conclusions regarding positioning based on our positioning metrics and price/open interest correlations for most of the contracts. Our positioning indicator suggests a mild long positioning bias for the Bund, Schatz, and Buxl contracts, while positioning leans more towards the neutral side in the Bobl and OAT contracts. In contrast, the correlations between price and open interest are negative in most of the contracts, possibly indicating a negative positioning bias. The hedged roll dynamics suggest a richening bias in the Bund, Buxl, and Schatz contracts, which is more pronounced compared to previous rolls for Bund and Buxl contracts. In summary, our analysis of positioning and hedged rolls does not indicate a strong positioning bias. Liquidity or valuation considerations might be important factors to take into account this roll.

## Open interest dynamics

Since the last roll in February, open interest in the Bund, Schatz, and OAT futures contracts has surged. The Schatz contract saw the most significant increase, with approximately 180k contracts. In contrast, the Bobl contract has seen a decrease in open interest of around 63k contracts. Currently, the open interest in the front Bund, Schatz, Buxl, and OAT futures contracts is above the average of the past four rolls. This indicates relatively stronger liquidity conditions for these specific contracts in this cycle. In contrast, the open interest in the IK contract remains below the average open interest volumes of the past four rolls by approximately 10k contracts.

## Repo effects

We expect that the ECB will cut its policy rate for the first time in this cycle in June, followed by a long string of 24bps cuts per quarter, bringing the depo rate from the current 4% to 3.25% by the end of the year. This may have an impact in funding costs during this roll due to a change in policy rate expectations. We also note that June ECB meeting coincides with the first notice day of the June Eurex contracts. We conducted a sensitivity analysis to evaluate the potential impact on futures prices resulting from a reduction in repo rates following a rate cut by the ECB. Specifically, we have projected that each 25bp repo rate cut could move all back contracts by approximately 5-7 cents, assuming all other factors remain equal.

## Duration gaps

In addition to liquidity considerations, there are also some notable duration gaps. Of particular interest are the RX, DU and UB contracts. We estimate implied durationneutral hedge ratios of 100:95 for RX, 100:88 for DU and 100:94 for UB contracts.

## Switch risks

There are no significant switch risks for the majority of Eurex futures contracts in this roll. There is a small risk of switch in the front IK contract. A parallel curve sell-off of nearly 90bp would be required for a switch to occur.

## OE M4-U4 Roll: Buy June / Sell September

So far, there has been very little open interest in the front Bobl contract that has been rolled into the back contract, in line with previous rolls. Further, the open interest in this roll appears in line with the the historical average of the past four rolls (Figure 9 and Figure 10). Our positioning indicator suggests that positioning is neutral (Figure 12), while the correlation between open interest and price indicates a mild short positioning bias (Figure 13 and Figure 14).

The OBL 2.1% Apr-29 is the CTD in both contracts. Therefore, the duration gap between the two contracts is very small. We estimate that approximately 0.86% of additional September contracts will be required to clean up the tail.

Similar to RX futures, there is limited switch potential for OE futures. From a net basis analysis, the front contract appears cheaper to its CTD relative to the back contract. Additionally, we highlight that a decrease in funding costs could affect the roll, especially the back contract. We estimate that each 25bp decline in funding cost would reduce the value of the September contract by approximately 6 cents, assuming all else remains equal.

In recent cycles, the hedged Bobl roll has shown a significant richening bias of approximately 5 cents in the last 24 days preceding the first notice day. This was mainly driven by OEU3/OEZ3 and 0EH4/OEM4 rolls. For the upcoming cycle, the hedged roll has shown a cheapening pattern, as demonstrated in Figure 11. Overall, we think there is no significant positioning bias. Based on liquidity and valuation considerations, we recommend buying June / selling September contracts.

## DU M4-U4 Roll: Buy June / Sell September

Open interest volumes in the Schatz contract have been on an upward trend over the past few months, currently standing at 2.2m contracts (Figure 2). This could be also seen when comparing to the average volumes of past rolls. For example, the open interest in the front DU contract is currently higher by approximately 400k contracts relative to the historical average of the past four rolls (Figure 15 and Figure 16).

The front and back DU contracts currently do not share the same CTD. The BKO 2.5% Mar-26 bond is the CTD in the front contract, while the BKO 2.9% Jun-26 is the CTD in the back contract. There is a significant duration gap between the front and back futures, with around 11.64% more September contracts required to clean up the tail. Similar to RX and OE contracts, the switching potential in any of the Schatz contracts is not significant. A 25bps decrease in funding costs could move September contract by around 6 cents. The June contract appears cheaper to its CTD relative to the September contract, from a net basis point of view.

Our positioning indicator suggests a long bias (Figure 18), while price and open interest correlations indicate a short bias in the front Schatz contract. Futures prices have been on a declining trend, while open interest has declined more recently, following a strong pick up in April (Figure 19 and Figure 20).

In past cycles, the hedged DU roll has shown a very strong richening bias of 11-13 cents in the 24 days leading up to the first notice day. For the upcoming cycle, the hedged roll appears to have begun displaying a richening bias of approximately 11 cents in recent days, similar to previous cycles (Figure 15). We prefer to buy June /sell September futures contracts.

## UB M4-U4 Roll: Buy June / Sell September

At present, the open interest in the front Buxl contract remains slightly above its historical average from the previous four rolls (Figure 22). However, the share of front Buxl contracts rolled into the back contract is generally consistent with the four previous rolls (Figure 21). Liquidity dynamics should not be a major concern in this specific roll.

The front and back UB contracts currently do not share the same CTD. The DBR 1.25% Aug-48 is the CTD in the front contract, while the DBR 2.5% Aug-54 is the CTD in the back Buxl contract. This implies a significant duration gap between the two contracts. As it currently stands, the September contract carries more risk than the June contract, requiring approximately 5.74% fewer September contracts to clean up the tail. However, for the upcoming cycle, hedged UB roll has been displaying a strong richening bias. Purely from a valuation point of view, we recommend buying June / selling September futures contract. Liquidity is also relatively stronger compared to previous rolls.

## IK M4-U4 Roll: Sell June/Buy September

At present, open interest in the front IK contract remains slightly below the average levels of the last four rolls, with approximately 10k contracts lower than the average. At this stage, very little open interest has been rolled into the back IK contract, mirroring previous cycles (Figure 27 and Figure 28).

Both the front and back futures IK contracts currently have the same CTD, which is the BTPS 4.4% May-33 bond. As a result, there is not a significant duration gap between the two contracts, with a duration-neutral ratio of approximately 100:100. There is a small risk of switch in the front IK contract. A parallel curve sell-off of nearly 90bp would be required for a switch to occur, leading to a change in the CTD to BTPS 4.35% Nov-33.

A decrease of 25 basis points in funding costs is estimated to move the September contract by approximately 6 cents, assuming all else remains equal. From a net basis perspective, the front contract appears to be cheaper to its CTD compared to the back contract.

In recent cycles, the hedged IK roll has displayed a richening pattern in the 24 days preceding the first notice day (Figure 25IK futres- HdgRol). For the upcoming cycle, the hedged IK roll has not shown a significant bias so far.

Recent price and open interest dynamics in the front IK contract does not suggest a significant bias (Figure 31 and Figure 32). Furthermore, our positioning indicator (Figure 30) displays a similar pattern. Based on slightly weaker liquidity and (low) switch risk in the front IK contract, we recommend selling June / buying September contracts.
