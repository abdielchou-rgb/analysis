## When will Eurozone equities be able to move on?

After meaningfully lagging the US in 2<sup>nd</sup> half of last year, partly driven by the much weaker growth delivery, Eurozone equities showed some relative stabilization in Q1/Q2, before breaking lower most recently on a spike in French political uncertainty - top chart. We have closed the UW on Eurozone vs the US in Q1, driven by more positive China stance and the improving Growth - Policy tradeoff in the region, but we believed that it was too early to expect Eurozone to move to an OW vs the US - see report. This was in contrast to the consensus of pundits who pretty unanimously turned bullish on the region earlier in the year. Now, should one use the current dip to go long?

On the positive side, fundamental backdrop is continuing to track better in Euro Area vs the US this year, with Eurozone CESI above the US one. At the same time, ECB has started easing, while Fed could stay higher for longer. Better China performance helps Eurozone and its valuations relative to the US are as cheap currently as at the extremes of TMT sell-off, GFC and Euro peripheral crises, among other.

• Having said that, current uncertainty still needs to lift, with Eurozone equities not showing oversold extremes. In fact, on equal weighted basis, Eurozone stocks were trading above US in Q2, and that is still the case, even fully taking into account the French driven weakness - middle chart. Vdax vs Vix spread is likewise far from extremes.

• The key will be any improving visibility with respect to the political backdrop. Here, we do not see the current French risks as a game changer for the region. The institutional setup is much more robust currently than during initial Euro crises, and the example of the latest Italian elections shows that the fallout might be manageable for the markets. While the snap elections offer increased near term risks, they might be reducing longer term ones. French government bond spreads to Germany are up 30bp, only higher during 2011 extremes - bottom chart.

Now, with all of the above in mind, and barring a surprise Macron win, we do fear that proverbially things might need to “get worse in order to get better”. The chances are that RN gets bigger share of vote than currently assumed, and also that a potential new French government will likely try to test the boundaries of what they can do. Financial markets might end up needing to push back against the more aggressive fiscal easing. Given this, the risk of further drawdowns will likely not be elevated only between the two rounds of voting and in the immediate aftermath, but also for a while post elections.

Put together, we think that, as we move through 2H, there is likely to be a good entry point to buy Eurozone, to go OW vs the US, but for now we stay on the sidelines given the elevated risk of further drawdowns and no capitulation visible. We keep our Defensive sector tilt, we are cautious on Consumer plays and think that Banks can look increasingly more vulnerable. Stylewise, we keep OW Growth vs Value stance, believing that it will continue to build on 14% ytd performance in the US and 7% in Europe.

We downgraded the region all the way to UW in May ‘23, post the strong run, and expecting the activity airpocket. As Eurozone has underperformed the US by almost 20% in 2H of last year, and as there was a manufacturing recession underway, we used this as an opportunity to neutralize the trade in Q1, also helped by our tactical more bullish China call. Still, we argued in Q1 that it was too early to go outright OW Eurozone equities, in contrast to the prevailing consensus opinion, and were looking for a better entry point sometime in 2H. The question is whether the current drawdown is an opportunity to buy Eurozone? Not yet, in our view, the risk of further weakness is high.

The mechanics of the election make it hard to call, but polls hint at a possible Le Pen victory. This would result in a period of cohabitation, where Macron would have to work with a government (prime minister and cabinet members) from the opposition. This has happened on three occasions in the past (1986-88, 1993-95, 1997-2002), where the prime minister runs domestic affairs, while the president’s role is limited to external affairs, defense and justice .

This is complicated further by the introduction of the Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP). As expected, France was on the list of countries announced by the European Commission that would be required to execute a 0.5% structural deficit adjustment annually over the coming years. According to our economists, party programs suggest a significant likelihood of fiscal slippage in the event of cohabitation, with France possibly breaching these new European fiscal rules, that are set to kick in next year - see report. On the budget, more specifically, there are ways to push a budget proposal, even in the case of relative majority. The government could bypass the lower house or push the budget proposal as an ordinance over time - see details in the same report linked in this paragraph.

On the positive side, many point to the fact that Le Pen’s party seem to have already toned down their rhetoric. In the recent party program released, they have indicated that the retirement age would not be lowered, and that the party wants to only reduce electricity prices and lower the VAT rate on gas and fuel prices, compared to their initial intention of lowering VAT rate for a much larger set of items - see report.

In addition, they argue that since the sovereign crisis, the Euro Area has developed more sophisticated tools, built stronger institutions, and created a greater joint understanding of how to manage a monetary union. For example, the ECB’s TPI relies on the compliance of member countries to work towards debt and macroeconomic sustainability. Volatility and market stress resulting from violation of conditions, or from confrontation with the EC, would likely serve as a deterrent, considering this would hurt their chances of maintaining power in the 2027 elections.

Finally, one could draw comparisons to the recent Italian elections, where Meloni was forced to be more prudent in her policies. Notably, Italian spreads widened into the elections, but quickly tightened thereafter.

Given all the above, we do not think the current political risks are a game changer. However, one might need to navigate further turbulence in the market in the short term, as uncertainty around the election outcome, as well as around the policy approach adopted post the election is still high. We continue to believe that it is too early to move Eurozone to an outright OW vs the US, and think that there would be a better entry point sometime in 2H, once we get more clarity on French politics. The risk of further drawdowns is likely elevated over the next weeks.

## Equity Strategy Key Calls and Drivers

Equities rallied almost 30% from last October’s lows, driven in Nov-Dec by the expectation of a Fed pivot, but these projections have fully reversed, to be now below October low levels. Equities are ignoring this, and consequently, the gap that has opened up ytd between Fed futures and the equity market is getting wider. The assumption that the market is likely making here is one of growth acceleration coming to the rescue in 2H, but CESIs are negative. Regionally, Eurozone risk-reward has improved in our view, but we wait for a better entry point to upgrade to OW, likely in 2H. Eurozone valuations appear attractive, relative growth momentum could be bottoming out and ECB has moved ahead of the Fed, in rate cut cycle, which is atypical. We also have a tactical buy on China given extreme cheapness and UW positioning by most investors. In terms of sector/style leadership, Growth is outperforming Value and large caps are again beating small, in all key regions. We stay OW Growth vs Value, but note the opportunity to add to Small caps in Eurozone and UK, as the group tends to rebound post the start of rate cuts. Defensives were the worst performing sectors in Q1, but are now outperforming so far in Q2 . We reiterate our barbell of OW Defensives and Commodities.
