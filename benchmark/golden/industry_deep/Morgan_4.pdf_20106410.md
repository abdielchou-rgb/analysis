3) How Much Investment is Needed to Drive Rides Growth, AKA What Will The Forward Rides Incremental Margin Range Be? Our new model assumes '24-'26 Mobility incremental margins range from 9%-10% as the company invests for growth in both Core UberX (pricing, supply, insurance) and Non-UberX mobility businesses (to address the growth opportunities above). Please see Exhibit 1 for our Mobility segment model estimates and please let us know if interested in our interactive model. In our view, we think our incremental margin estimates (assuming they improve in '25 with scale) may be conservative, but it is an area of uncertainty given our lack of understanding around elasticity and the investments needed to drive our \~20% '23-'26 Mobility bookings CAGR.

4) Delivery: What Will Forward Growth and Unit Economics on Restaurant vs Grocery & Retail Be?: UBER has executed nicely on its Delivery platform and supply driven initiatives since the '22 Analyst day as (one example), its most recent quarter highlights share gains in the US (against industry leader DASH), improving profitability, and a strong growing advertising business and burgeoning grocery/ retail delivery business. That said, we look for more color on forward drivers of growth between restaurants (users, frequency, cross sell assumptions, etc.) and grocery/retail (supply, cross sell, users, etc.)...as well as the investment required in grocery/retail to drive this growth. Early signals are constructive (with UBER's global delivery business generating 8% fully loaded incremental margins in 4Q:23 and with management expecting grocery/retail losses to decline Y/Y in '24) but more clarity on how UBER intends to drive profitable segment growth will be helpful to investors.

5) How Large and When Will the Capital Returns Program Be? UBER has discussed the notion of capital returns and with the company now GAAP profitable and its FCF yield now 5%/6% on '25/'26 FCF, we think it's time for UBER to outline a clear capital return strategy. In our view, disclosure around the policy being focused on consistent share count reduction vs. offsetting dilution, when we can expect to see the impacts compound in the model, and sources of funding (we expect existing and future cash) will be key. We note that share repurchases may start small (UBER ends '23 with a net debt position of \$4bn (\~\$2bn net cash including equity stakes) with an expectation for \$5.3bn of FCF in '24), but we would expect capital returns to scale with growing FCF/share balances in '25 and '26. Further, we believe a more consistent capital return strategy could lead to a higher multiple investors are willing to pay given comfort around capitalizing return of free cash flow. We have seen AAPL, MSFT, META, and GOOGL all start more aggressive share repurchase programs over the past \~3 years, and we believe now is the time for UBER to join the club.

## 4Q Results Highlights and Model/PT Changes:

Strong Execution in Core Products… UBER continues to execute around its core Mobility and Delivery products with users and frequency both hitting all-time highs and trips growth notably outpacing Gross Bookings growth for the fourth quarter in a row pointing to healthy consumer activity. In Delivery, bookings accelerated for the 4th consecutive quarter, with users, frequency and basket size all up. UBER cited growing category position in 10 out of its top-10 markets and notably, in the US, spoke to solid merchant supply growth, closing the gap in the suburbs (vs. leader DASH), and other improving customer metrics (reliable ETAs, decreasing defect rate, courier efficiencies, etc.). In Mobility, Core UberX continues to demonstrate durable \~20%+ Y/Y bookings growth (\~70% contribution to Y/Y mobility bookings growth) as the company gains share globally (including in the US).

...Augmented by Growth Plays... UBER is also seeing nice contribution from its new businesses/verticals as well. In Grocery & Retail, top-line is growing \~40% Y/Y and

generating \$7 billion in run rate gross bookings (contributing \~20%/\$500mn+ to Y/ Y Delivery growth). Further, the number of Delivery users also ordering Grocery & Retail is now at 14% (up \~100bps vs 2Q), and although not yet expected to be profitable, the company expects the unit economics of Grocery & Retail to continue to improve in '24. In addition, non-UberX mobility products like Reserve, Taxi, Moto, U4B, UberX Share, etc. grew 80%+ Y/Y in 4Q, generated \$11 billion in run rate gross bookings. Notably, Non-UberX products now account for 14% of mobility bookings (up \~100bp Q/Q and 400bps Y/Y) and in aggregate are EBITDA profitable (although lower margin).

…With a Growing, Loyal Membership Base and Strong Ad Business: Uber One membership continues to grow nicely, now at 19 million members (+27% Q/Q and +58% Y/Y) across 25 markets globally. This matters, because, as a reminder, members have higher frequency and retention. Members accounted for 30% of total gross bookings and 45% of delivery gross bookings in 4Q. A global, engaged and loyal user base is attractive for advertisers too and to that effect run rate advertising revenue grew to \$900 million in 4Q (up from \$650mn in 2Q23 and \$500mn in 4Q22), with 550K active ad merchants (mostly Delivery SMBs) +75% Y/ Y. The company also spoke to faster growing ad areas like enterprise, CPG, Journey Ads (in-app mobility ads), etc. and is on track to exceed its \$1bn '24 ad run rate guide from the '22 Analyst day.

Expect More Near-term Investment for Growth as We Raise PT to \$82 and Remain OW: On Mobility, we expect UBER to continue invest for more durable multi-year growth (in both core X and emerging New Mobility) which causes us to lower our '24 Mobility incremental EBITDA margins by 80bps and '24/'25 rides EBITDA by \~1%/3%. That said, continued stronger than expected Delivery growth and incremental margins cause us to raise our Delivery '24/'25 Gross Bookings 1%/2% and EBITDA by 6%/5%. In aggregate our '25 EBITDA/FCF falls by 2%/3%… although we will be closely monitoring UBER's Analyst day next week, which may provide some offset to this lower EBITDA and FCF. We remain OW and increase our PT to \$82 PT (16% upside), in line with our regression of peer growth and long term EBITDA margins, driven by the roll forward in valuation to year end 2024. Our new \$82 PT implies \~20x '25 EBITDA.
