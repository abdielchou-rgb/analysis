## Watch Out for Near-Term Headwinds and Signposts to Monitor Before Turning More Bullish on China

## Near-term headwinds

• Macro headwinds and earnings cut pressure persisting;

• Liquidity remaining relatively tight as real interest rate stays high and credit multiplier effect is missing;

• Another US\$2 billion or so of fund outflow pressure for Chinese equities;

• Potential liquidity tightness ahead of Chinese New Year.

## Signposts of potential positive signals

• Any early signs of stabilization of the housing market;

• Incremental step-up of market stabilization effort and, more importantly, macro stimulus measures;

• Structural reform announcement related to SOE reform implementation, Social Welfare improvement, and more holistic roadmap of local government debt solution.

## Key Takeaways

Economics: Growth momentum is slowing, and deflationary pressures are persisting in China. But the policy response remains hesitant as there are concerns about renewed misallocation. We continue to expect reactive easing, which should keep the risks of a debt-deflation loop alive.

China equities: Prefer A shares to offshore China given the sector skew and historical record of greater reaction to onshore policy easing. For MSCI China we now expect 7% EPS growth for 2024 and 9% for 2025 (cumulatively 21% below consensus). We see the valuation multiple at 9.5x, an approximate 15% discount to MSCI EM due to China’s 3D challenges.

Asia equities: Asia/EM equities performance divergence continues and we revise our TOPIX end-2024 target to 2,800. We raise our EPS forecast for TOPIX for both 2024 and 2025. We think that markets can sustain a 15.0x forward P/E multiple given recent and prospective strong investor flows.

Asia macro: We are cautious on Asia FX and rates via selectively long USD and paying rates exposure. In rates, we recommend receiving 1-year CNY NDIRS and long onshore CNY-hedged 1-year CGBs, as the growth momentum remains weak and the Chinese stock market is under pressure.

Cross-asset strategy: We remain OW in Japan equities and UW in EM equities. We prefer taking on EM risk exposure via local rates and sovereign credit than via equities.
