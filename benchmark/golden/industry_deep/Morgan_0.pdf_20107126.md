## March 19

BoJ exits NIRP, YCC; Ueda emphasizes accommodative conditions will remain, JPY weakens; AUD slides, RBA removes tightening bias; USTs modestly bull-steepen; Soft Canada CPI drives GoC rally; CLP exhibits sharp weakness amid Costa remarks; DXY at 103.82 (+0.2%); US 10y at 4.293% (-3.2bp).

• The BoJ ends, NIRP, YCC, and equity ETF purchases as the 2% price stability target, in a sustainable and stable manner, "came in sight." JGB purchases will continue at \~JPY6tr per month.

• JPY weakens on a broad basis as the statement and BoJ Governor Ueda emphasize the BoJ will need to maintain accommodative monetary conditions; USD/JPY (+1.1%) approaches 151.00.

• AUD (-0.4%) slides after the RBA leaves the cash rate target at 4.35% (C: 4.35%) but removes reference in the statement to "a further increase in interest rates."

• Modest UST bull-steepening ahead of the March FOMC meeting, and accompanying Summary of Economic Projections, ends the Treasury sell-off streak at six sessions.

• Downside Canadian inflation surprise catalyzes a double-digit rally in front-end GoCs; this was the first month since October 2021 that grocery prices rose less than the rate of overall headline CPI.

• CLP (-1.8%) weakens substantially as BCCh Governor Costa focuses on progress in non-volatile CPI and sounds comfortable with decoupling their rate cycle from other central banks.

The BoJ then claimed the spotlight with its decision to raise its policy rate for the first time since 2007. In a 7:2 majority vote, the BoJ set guidelines to encourage the uncollateralized overnight call rate to remain “at around 0 to 0.1%.” In addition, the BoJ decided to end its YCC framework, overshoot commitment, and ETF and J-REIT purchases. Yet, given a continuation of \~JPY6tr per month in JGB purchases and an emphasis on maintaining accommodative monetary conditions, market participants sold the fact in the FX market (USD/JPY: +1.1%) and bought the fact in the rates market (10y JGBs rallied by 3.0bp d/d to 0.725% on a simple yield basis).

Before rates and currency markets could turn attention squarely to the March FOMC meeting, there was a downside inflation surprise in Canada. Headline CPI unexpectedly decelerated to 2.8% y/y in February (C: 3.1%; P: 2.9%), while measures of core inflation (Median and Trim) also slowed. Our economists’ note this was the first month since October 2021 grocery prices rose less than the rate of overall headline CPI. Front-end GoCs surged and staged a double-digit rally. This tightened front-end US-Canadian yield differentials and pushed USD/CAD (+0.2%) temporarily through the 1.360 level.

The magnitude of the rally in Canadian government bonds spilled over to US Treasuries, as yields ground lower in an orderly fashion. In addition, possible squaring of short positions (following the recent sell-off) ahead of the FOMC meeting likely also supported UST duration. Overall, this mild bull-steepening ended the Treasury sell-off streak at six sessions as focus turned to the March Summary of Economic Projections.

After reducing the size of its three-month bill and 42-day cash management bill auctions last week, Treasury reduced the size (-\$10bn) of the four-week bill auction to \$85bn and the eight-week bill auction (-\$5bn) to \$85bn. The 17-week bill auction size was unchanged at \$60bn.

Better breadth to equity gains brought the S&P 500 (+0.56%) to a fresh all-time high, and stocks sensitive to the housing cycle relatively outperformed after US housing starts rose by 10.7% m/m (C: 8.2%; P: -12.3%) in February.

For the March FOMC SEP, our economists expect an unchanged dot plot (median end-24 dot: 4.625%; median end-25 dot: 3.625%). With their expectation the Fed will continue to show a median of three 25bp rate cuts in 2024, they expect growth to be marked higher while the inflation forecasts remain unchanged.

We think the recent revelation of the strong rise in labor supply means the Fed could still have room to cut rates, despite strong growth, because of supply-side gains from immigration. Should the Fed reinforce this significant supply-side impact, it will likely be interpreted dovishly by market participants. This would help increase the number of rate cuts priced in this year (for more, read here).

In-depth discussions on the balance sheet are also slated for the March meeting. We expect the Fed will initiate a taper in June and move the run-off cap on Treasury securities from \$60bn to \$30bn per month, and leave the MBS cap unchanged.

A slower pace of run-off could help get to a more efficient balance sheet in the long run by smoothing the redistribution of reserves, which reduces the likelihood of a premature end. We expect the RRP to be depleted mid-year and the end of QT in early 2025 when reserves are \~\$3.2tr (for more, read here).

## Japan:

Initially, JGBs retraced their strength from Monday, but then gradually rallied into the BoJ meeting. The outcome of the March BoJ meeting aligned with consensus expectations, and the BoJ maintained the current JGB purchase size through for at least April-June. Demand to square short positions as well as demand to restore duration exposure from domestic market participants drove the broad JGB rally. 10- 20y JGBs outperformed on the curve, although there was decent selling pressure in the 30y+ sector. 10y JGBs rallied by 3.0bp d/d to 0.725% on a simple yield basis, while 30y JGBs only rallied by 1.0bp d/d to 1.800%.

OIS price action was in stark contrast as there was broad underperformance in the front-end. The funding tenor for the BoJ’s various funding support programs shortened to 1y while the borrowing rate increased to 0.1%. Given less incentive for financial institutions to use these funding supply operations, fast money market participants began to pay the 2-5y sector; 2y OIS sold off by 1.1bp d/d to 0.26%. ASW richened across the curve. XCCY basis curve tightened, led by the front-end.

The focus of the Tokyo session was on the BoJ policy outcome. USD/JPY had essentially traded range-bound before the outcome. Once market participants confirmed it was a dovish BoJ hike, USD/JPY quickly rallied and broke above 150.00 during Tokyo trading hours. The BoJ indicated accommodative monetary conditions will continue for a while based on their economic and policy outlook in the statement. This may have encouraged JPY carry market participants to initiate new carry trades. Strong performance of Japanese equities may have also generated demand for additional FX hedges from overseas market participants. In London hours, USD/JPY continued to trade higher, above 150.50, driven by broad USD strength.

## Europe:

Lack of local catalysts and equity gains supported NOK (+0.1%), while SEK and GBP closed the London session flat. EUR (-0.1%) weakened, despite an improvement in ZEW sentiment for Germany and Europe. CHF struggled for direction and ended the London session flat. Price action disregarded front-end rates markets, which reflected increased likelihood of a rate cut on Thursday after a downward revision to the government’s inflation forecast to 1.5% (P: 1.9%).

European duration traded in a relatively tight range on Tuesday amid few data releases and ahead of the Fed and UK CPI on Wednesday. Soft Canadian CPI drove a rally in the US, which also spilled over into Bunds. This was short-lived, and 10y Bunds closed 1bp lower at 2.45%. In the front end, ECB OIS rallied \~2bp, led by reds. Further out, EUR 2s10s (against 6m) bull-steepened 1bp, while 10s30s twiststeepened over 1bp. The German curve out-steepened swaps and the Schatz-Bund ASW box flattened 1bp. Semi-core and peripheral duration underperformed core and the 10y BTP-Bund spread widened 3bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

It was an eventful session in G10 FX with two central bank meetings. Despite a hike by the BoJ, the main G10 underperformer was JPY (-1.2%) as market participants seemingly sold the fact. USD/JPY pushed up through the 150 level and ended the London session at 150.95. The DXY USD Index ended the London session 0.2% higher. AUD (-0.4%) sold off after the RBA kept rates unchanged but removed reference to the possibility of further rate hikes. This was interpreted dovishly by markets as front-end yields fell. NZD (-0.5%) was weighed down in part by AUD performance and in part by comments from the New Zealand Treasury, which claimed the country is “in the midst of a severe economic slowdown.” CAD (-0.3%) also weakened against the US dollar after February inflation data surprised to the downside.

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened alongside JPY losses after the BoJ’s decision to end NIRP and YCC. The upcoming Fed rate decision also weighed on AxJ currencies, as the US dollar strengthened. KRW underperformed due to its high beta. USD/KRW rose 0.5% to 1340, and local equities sold off (KOSPI: -1.10%). CNY was steady at 7.20 after a strong fixing at 7.09, while USD/CNH rose to 7.211. PHP also underperformed, and USD/PHP rose 0.7% to 55.92 while local equities sold off. TWD depreciated by 0.4%, and USD/TWD ended the AxJ session at 31.78. MYR and IDR also depreciated as the Indonesian bond market had its eighth consecutive session of losses. SGD weakened, and USD/SGD rose to 1.342 while local equities exhibited less volatility. THB pared gains for the third consecutive session, and USD/THB rose 0.3% to 36.09.

AxJ rates continued to sell-off, but China was once again an exception as 2s10s bullflattened ahead of a local prime rate decision; 30y CGB yields fell 3.8bp. On the NDIRS curve, 10y yields rose 0.7bp. South Korea rates underperformed with a selloff across the curve; 2s10s bear-steepened, with 2y yields up 2.4bp and 10y yields up 3.1bp. In Singapore, 10y yields rose 1.3bp to 3.09%. In Thailand, the 5s10s curve rose 1.5bp, while the front-end remained relatively unchanged. In Hong Kong, 2y and 5y yields rose \~1.5bp. India G-Secs, Malaysia, and Indonesia 10y rates exhibited less volatility.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mixed. COP (+0.4%) strengthened, despite broader USD strength while MXN (+0.1%) and BRL (-0.04%) were broadly flat. CLP (-1.8%) weakened substantially as BCCh Governor Costa focused on progress in nonvolatile CPI and sounded comfortable with decoupling their rate cycle from other central banks. LatAm rates were generally lower. The front end of the IBR curve rallied (1y: -6bp) and curve bull-flattened (2s10s: +3bp), while DIs outperformed (January 2027s: -7bp). The TIIE curve ended the session mostly unchanged as markets opened after a local holiday (10y: +1bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• RBA held its cash rate target at 4.35% (C: 4.35%) but moved to a balanced bias as the economy has moved in-line with expectations. Uncertainty and data dependency were key messages from the press conference. Our economists expect slower progress to targets, and more fiscal stimulus with rates on hold until early 2025

• As widely reported, the BoJ removed its negative interest rate policy and YCC framework, and its overshoot commitment to the 2% price stability target in a stable and sustainable manner has come into sight with a high degree of certainty. There were various changes in the future monetary policy operations, including: 1) the short-term rate to 0-0.1%, 2) no more ETF/J-REIT purchases, and 3) a change in terms and conditions for usage of various funding supply operations. However, the BoJ will continue to

purchase JGBs in the amount of JPY 6tr per month, with unlimited JGB purchase operations left as a backstop measure. At the press conference, BoJ Governor Ueda suggested any monetary policy adjustment will be conducted by changing the short-term rate. MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
