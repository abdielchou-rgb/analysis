## March 8

NFPs show strong, concentrated job gains with soft AHE; USTs twist-steepen; Equity gains fade in momentum reversal; JPY exhibits strength, BoJ in focus; European rates bull-steepen amid ECB rhetoric; Fed's Williams says neutral still seems low; DXY at 102.71 (-0.1%); US 10y at 4.075% (-0.8bp).

• February US job report shows strong, yet concentrated nonfarm payrolls – led by healthcare and social assistance – but a softer labor input from a large slowdown in average hourly earnings growth.

• Volatility around nonfarm payrolls subsides and US Treasuries settle close to closing levels of the prior session; the curve modestly twiststeepens.

• Initial strength in equities fades as a reversal in the momentum factor grips the broader market and sends both the Nasdaq 100 (-1.53%) and S&P 500 (-0.65%) into the red.

• JPY (+0.7%) strengthens against the US dollar following several BoJrelated media reports, which suggest the BoJ is leaning toward policy normalization.

• European duration bull-steepens as ECB rhetoric warms to rate cuts, ECB Governing Council Member Rehn says "risks of premature interest rate cuts in terms of inflation control have substantially decreased."

• NY Fed President Williams says it is not yet clear whether the neutral rate has risen. In his view, the neutral rate still seems quite low.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

A brimful February US nonfarm payrolls report ensured the NY session kicked off with a lively start. The knee-jerk reaction to the large upside surprise in total nonfarm payrolls of 275k (C: 200k; P: 229k) was a sharp US duration sell-off.

Yet a cumulative downward revision to job gains over the two prior months that totaled 167k took the shine off the headline beat. Private payrolls rose by 223k (C: 165k; P: 177k), but job gains continued to show narrow breadth as the healthcare and social assistance sector accounted for over 40% of the jobs added.

There was also clear divergence between the establishment and the household surveys. The latter painted a less rosy picture of the labor market. Employed persons fell by 184k in the household survey, unemployed persons rose 334k, and the unemployment rate rose to 3.9% (C: 3.7%; P: 3.7%). This was despite a drop in the labor force participation rate to 62.5% (C: 62.6%; P: 62.6%).

The labor input to the economy was soft as average hourly earnings slowed to 0.1% m/m (C: 0.2%; P: 0.5%), following the outsized increase in the prior month. Average weekly hours did pick back up to 34.3 (C: 34.3; P: 34.2) and, as a result, translated to a pickup in the labor income proxy. Indeed, our economists forecast control retail sales returned to slight growth in February.

As the details of the report were digested, US duration quickly reversed and the curve bull-steepened as the front end rallied hard. Yet Treasuries came off the richest levels of the session, likely as attention turned toward release of Tuesday’s February US CPI report. This has scope to be the primary input to whether the Fed’s median end-2024 dot in the March SEP continues to show three cuts or moves to one fewer. Overall, the curve twist-steepened while yields gravitated back to closing levels from the prior session.

The main theme across G10 currencies was pronounced JPY strength following several BoJ-related media reports, which suggested the BoJ is leaning toward policy normalization. The latest, released late in the NY evening, reported the BoJ would mull whether to scrap its YCC program. JPY gains pushed USD/JPY (-0.7%) down to the 147 level as the DXY USD Index (-0.1%) closed below the 103 level, at its lowest since mid-January.

US equities displayed the most volatility after initial strength was faded as a reversal in the momentum factor gripped the broader market and sends both the Nasdaq 100 (-1.53%) and S&P 500 (-0.65%) into the red.

## Japan:

JGBs continued to bear-steepen as market participants remained focused on a March BoJ lift-off; 2y JGBs traded above 0.2% for the first time since 2011. However, JGBs gradually recovered from initial weakness, supported by decent demand to buy the dip in the front-end given cheap valuations; 5y JGB yields were unchanged at 0.38% d/d. Long-end JGBs continued to lag, absent real money demand, and underperformed on the curve; 30y JGBs sold off by 2.5bp d/d to 1.785%. A media report which suggested both a March hike and QT were possible was released in the Tokyo evening session and JGB futures instantaneously sold off by 30c. Another media report which suggested status quo purchases mitigated concern among market participants and JGBs retraced roughly half of the initial weakness. TONA OIS bear-steepened alongside JGBs but continued to outperform JGBs as concern about QT weighed on cash JGB performance. XCCY basis tightened, led by the longend.

Initially, USD/JPY traded lower from the Tokyo morning alongside the weaker USD, but quickly recovered back to the 148.00 level as Japan rates retraced their initial weakness. From the London session, JPY suddenly began to outperform as media reports suggested the BoJ is leaning toward ending negative rates and will introduce a new quantitative framework for future JGB purchases. Changing the JGB purchase framework seemingly invoked discussion the BoJ may also end its overshoot commitment at the March meeting and USD/JPY quickly fell below the 147.00 level. Although JGB futures recovered initial losses after another media report suggested the BoJ would consider to keeping \~JPY 6tn per month under the new quantitative framework, CCY/JPY pairs struggled to rally. FX market participants were seemingly more focused on the timing of the rate hike, rather than QT. Other CCY/JPY pairs also traded lower after the BoJ-related media reports. EUR/JPY sold off from the 162.00 level to 160.70, aided by dovish comments from ECB officials.

## Europe:

A decline in Brent crude oil prices to \~\$82/bbl weighed on NOK, which underperformed in the G10 complex. EUR (-0.1%) held steady \~1.0900 in the NY afternoon, even as more hawkish-leaning ECB Governing Council members softened their tone on risks to premature rate cuts.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar whipsawed after strong headline nonfarm payrolls were coupled with details which showed signs of easing in the labor market. The DXY USD Index (- 0.1%) retraced losses, to an extent, but ended the week below 103.00 for the first time since late January. The upside surprise in employment growth in Canada did not boost CAD, as employment gains remained below the replacement rate and wage growth ease more than expected. USD/CAD (+0.2%) encountered resistance at 1.3500. JPY (USD/JPY: -0.7%) outperformed amid further media reports which suggested the BoJ may exit negative rates at its March meeting.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies continued to strengthen as equities and USTs rallied following Fed Chair Powell’s comments on Thursday. KRW led the gains due to its high beta, up 0.8% to \~1,320 alongside KOSPI (+1.2%) outperformance. TWD gained 0.4% to 31.4, its first weekly gain in a month, due to large foreign equity inflows (US\$1.4bn). PHP also outperformed, up 0.5% to 55.6 alongside a rally in its local equity market (+1.5%). MYR extended its recent rally by another 0.5%. CNH underperformed and remained \~7.20, as media reports that China called for the facilitation of foreign visitors to China by improving payment services and waiving visa requirements had limited impact. INR markets were closed for a local holiday.

AxJ rates rallied broadly following USTs. China was the exception, with bond yields stable to slightly higher following Thursday’s report that regulators scrutinized regional banks’ bond investments. 5y CNY NDIRS yields edged up by 1bp, while 30y CGBs continued to trade well after a strong auction. Indonesia rates were also relatively resilient, as 10y INDOGB rallied 1bp, although this did not prevent IDR from a 0.4% gain. Korea outperformed; 10y KRW NDIRS yields fell 7bp to 3.2% and the 2s10s curve bull-flattened 2bp. Hong Kong and Singapore rates outperformed given their high correlation to US rates; 10y swap yields fell 5bp and 3bp, respectively.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mostly stronger; MXN (+0.4%) and COP (+0.4%) were largely driven by a weaker USD. BRL (-1.0%) weakened after a stateowned oil company skipped an expected dividend payment which would have provided the government with much needed revenue as they try to hit their fiscal target. This drove DIs weaker and the January 2032 contract ended 10bp higher for the session. CLP (+2.1%) strengthened substantially and the front-end of the CAM curve sold off (1y: +14bp) following higher-than-expected CPI data of 0.6% m/m (C: 0.2%). The IBR curve also underperformed USTs as markets reacted to strongerthan-expected CPI data after market close on Thursday of 1.09% m/m (C: 1.01%). TIIE were generally unchanged.

He noted Thailand banks’ net interest margin are in the middle compared to regional peers, and they have higher cost from credit risk as they extend loans to SMEs more than other countries. Although, “higher nominal wage growth, including due to increases in public sector wages, would contribute to higher demand pressure in the economy.” Under these circumstances, the Council judged the current level of the NBP interest rates was conducive to meeting the NBP inflation target in the medium-term

## Economic Release Notes

## Developed Markets

• In Japan, the Monthly Ministry of Finance International Transactions in Securities data for February suggested trust accounts (on behalf of pension funds) had actively rebalanced from foreign equity (-JPY 1.16tn) to foreign bonds (+JPY 1.02tn) amid continued outperformance of equities. There continued to be decent inflows to foreign equities from investment trusts (+JPY 1.01tn), which suggests strong demand to buy overseas assets from retail market participants under the new NISA scheme. Banking accounts, for both banks and trust banks, remained net buyers of foreign bonds (+JPY 439.1bn and +JPY 133.8bn, respectively). As USTs sold off in response to the strong US labor and CPI data, they may have taken advantage of this opportunity to buy the dip. Activity in lifers was modestly skewed to sellers (-JPY 306.6bn), albeit on limited activity. The US rates market pushed back the sharp Fed rate cut cycle after strong US economic data and prospects of a period of higher FX hedge costs likely became prolonged. In this environment, it appears difficult for them to invest in foreign bonds on a FX hedged basis. Banks and lifers seemingly found it difficult to restore duration exposure in earnest, until the Fed starts its rate-cutting cycle

• In Japan, Balance of Payments data for January revealed Japanese market participants (mainly banking accounts) were decent net buyers of US sovereign bonds (+JPY 3.14tn) as yields rose after the year-end rally. They were decent net buyers of German sovereign bonds (+JPY 300.6bn) and French sovereign bonds (+JPY 163.7bn), likely due to rebalancing activity given the relatively large market value. Japanese market participants were large net sellers of Canadian (-JPY 192.4bn) and Australian bonds (-JPY 367bn), including SSG and IGs. Overseas market participants broadly were net buyers of Japan short-term debt securities, despite possible BoJ normalization in the near-term. China (JPY 3.9tn) and French-based market participants (JPY 2.2tn) were particularly active in buying short-dated paper. In longer maturities, US-based market participants were large net buyers (+JPY 389.3bn), while French market participants were decent net sellers (- JPY 577.2bn)

• In the US, Total Nonfarm Payrolls rose by 275k in February (C: 200k; P: 229k) but the labor input was not as strong with only a 0.1% m/m increase in Average Hourly Earnings (C: 0.2%; P: 0.5%). The acceleration in payrolls from January was revised out, in particular within professional and business services, retail trade, and transportation paired with upward revisions to government. There was little sign of a weather effect, as construction jobs moved sideways. Wage pressures do not appear elevated and the FOMC is more comfortable with higher payroll growth, given significant immigration flows (for more, read here)

• In Canada, Employment gains were 40.7k in February (C: 20.0k; P: 37.3k) and driven by full-time employment at 70.6k (P: -11.6k). Part-time jobs gave back the prior month’s gains and then some at -29.9k (P: 48.9k). The Unemployment Rate ticked up to 5.8% in February (C: 5.8%; P: 5.7%). Our economists believe this data allows the Bank of Canada to remain on the sidelines as it continues to monitor progress on inflation. Our economists believe even as the labor market comes into better balance, a bumpy path of inflation in 1H24, coupled with still elevated wage growth pressures, will keep the BoC from cutting rates before July, when they expect the first 25bp cut. Our economists acknowledge risks of an earlier start should the pace of progress for inflation prove faster-than-expected (for more, read here)

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
