# All together now: Gamma Rental candidates for Q2 earnings

## Dispersion emerging: Record single stock dispersion could persist through Q2 earnings [Figures 1-5]

We have extensively suggested that subdued levels of index correlations are the primary driver of the low volatility regime, amongst other factors. Index realized correlations are now at all-time lows heading into Q2 earnings season, eclipsing even 2017-style levels. We could continue to witness volatility 'under the hood' as names report more dispersed results, well reflected in the higher beat rates of realized vs. implied moves over the last several earnings seasons. However, we do not think it will necessarily manifest in higher index volatility if Big 6 Tech+ continues to dominate the earnings story.

The AI tailwind in the form of the momentum trade keeps trucking on, more recently driven by the short leg underperforming as the long leg has stalled. We think it will take a coordinated unwind in the AI tailwind to drive a substantial pickup in correlations. The AI narrative has dragged in a lot more names in its wake – healthy for now in terms of increased breadth, until something breaks. For now, we expect dispersion to persist through Q2 earnings season as long as nothing does.

## Rent money: Screening for Gamma Rental opportunities in US & EU single stocks [Figures 6-15]

This is the fourth run of our Gamma Rentals framework. Post-mortem, over half of the top 'Moocher/Mover' pairs by sector produced positive delta-hedged P&L post-Mover earnings over the last few earnings seasons. We screen for US and EU stocks with direct peers reporting earnings prior to their own to capture 'sympathy' moves via deltahedged ATM straddles – more details on full methodology in Appendix.

In Figures 6-7, we show the top five US Moocher/Mover pairs by sector and by earnings calendar week, respectively, based on an equal-weighted composite ranking of pairwise backtest, quantamental signal, and volatility carry ranking scores. Per our methodology, the top 20 US candidates in which to own pairwise volatility are: MU, AVGO, WDC, PH, NVDA, HD, NDSN, WTW, APD, OXY, CHD, AZO, AJG, QCOM, MKC, OKE, CB, LOW, QRVO, and RJF.

In Figures 8-10, we show the average rank across all pairwise candidates at the aggregate US sector, subsector, and industry level (GICS Level 1-3), respectively. On a sector basis, pairwise volatility within Energy, Tech, and Staples appear the most attractive to own in the US.

We run the same analyses for EU stocks, per Figures 11-15. The top 20 candidates in which to own pairwise volatility are: ALFA SS, ACS SQ, AFX GY, LAND LN, JYSK DC, GTT FP, BG AV, BVI FP, AMUN FP, BPE IM, AI FP, ISP IM, ENGI FP, HNR1 GY, INGA NA, CABK SQ, BC IM, EBS AV, HELN SE, and AKZA NA. Sector-wise, Real Estate, Financials, and Industrials appear the most attractive EU sectors in which to own pairwise volatility.

## Appendix: Gamma Rentals screening methodology

We screen for stocks with direct peers reporting earnings prior to their own to capture 'sympathy' moves. A direct peer is defined as having the same GICS Level 3 classification We define the first reporter as the 'Mover' and the second reporter as

We look to establish a long volatility position in the Moochers, whilst mitigating the potential for a vega 'crush' by holding positions through Movers' earnings reports, and subsequently exiting positions prior to Moochers' earnings reports (Figures 16-19). The preferred method for owning the Moochers' volatility to capture the Movers' earnings is via delta-hedged ATM straddles, with listed expiries falling closest to (but following) Movers' report dates to maximize gamma exposure. Alternatively, expiries falling after Moocher earnings may be considered as the embedded event risk could provide a bid to the Moocher straddle implied volatility. However, the position would ideally be closed prior to Moocher earnings.

Methodology: We take a three-tiered approach to screening for Gamma Rental candidates ('Moochers') across all current S&P 500 (US) and Stoxx 600 (EU) constituents, respectively –

1. Pairwise backtest: Strong historical pairwise correlation/covariance to Mover earnings, and historically better Moocher volatility performance (realized-implied, measured in volatility point 'P&L'<sup>(3)</sup> through Mover earnings, taken as medians

2. Quantamental signals: Higher model-implied probability ('degree') of forward movement based on a suite of quantamental scores, specifically an average across the absolute values of CTA, Crowding, Earnings Growth, and Earnings Momentum scores (where 1 = highest degree, 0 = lowest).

3. Volatility carry: Measure of trailing volatility carry based on the current z-score of 1m implied/realized ratio (where lower z-score = better carry, and vice versa).

Lastly, we may take into consideration UBS Analysts' expectations for degree of earnings surprises for the current reporting season, where 0 = in line, 1 = slightly positive/negative surprise, and 2 = very positive/negative surprise), as applicable.
