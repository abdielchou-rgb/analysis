• The FOMC remains on hold at 5.375%. The FOMC statement acknowledges the considerable progress in inflation and rising risks to the labor market.

• In the press conference, we expect Chair Powell to signal that the Fed is close to lowering its target rate, but not commit to a cadence for cuts.

## July meeting

We expect the Federal Reserve to open the door to a September rate cut. We expect the FOMC statement to acknowledge the considerable progress in inflation and rising risks to the labor market. In the press conference, we expect Chair Powell to add to the signal that the Fed is close to lowering its target rate, but not go so far as committing to a cadence of cuts.

## FOMC policy statement

Current conditions - we look for important changes in the first paragraph of the statement to characterizations of the labor market and inflation that acknowledge the rebalancing in demand/supply and considerable disinflationary progress.

GDP continues to grow at a "solid pace". We forecast GDP growth at 2.0% 4Q/4Q this year and 2.1% 4Q/4Q next. The economy expanded by 1.4% Q/Q annualized in the first quarter and we are tracking 2Q GDP growth at a solid 2.2% annualized pace (as of July 19) vs Atlanta Fed's GDPNow at 2.7% and NY Fed Nowcast at 2.01%.

Job gains "have slowed but remain strong". As of June, the 3-month average of monthly net new jobs was 177k, down from 243k at the beginning of the year. We are forecasting 195k nonfarm payrolls in July (released August 2). Since the start of the year the unemployment rate has moved up to 4.1% and is likely to remain there in July. We think the unemployment rate will gradually converge to 4.2% by the end of the year and 4.5% next year, higher than what the Fed considers the long-run unemployment rate and the level projected in the June Summary of Economic Projections.

Core PCE inflation has eased considerably over the past year, and though progress flattened out in 1Q24, disinflation resumed in the second quarter. The last CPI/PCE prints were well aligned with our long-standing out-of-consensus view that the Fed will cut three times this year, starting in September, followed by two more cuts in November and December. April and May were the first two data points of a descending inflation path, which we think is giving the Fed the confidence it needs that inflation is on a track toward target. We think June's core PCE (released this Friday) will further add to the convincing evidence the Fed is looking for – we forecast a 0.18%M print. Four additional cuts through mid-2025 bring the mid-point of the federal funds rate target range to

3.625%. On a 4Q/4Q basis, we forecast core PCE inflation slows from 3.2% last year to 2.6% this year and 2.1% in 2025. Our 2024 forecast is two-tenths lower than the rate projected in the June Summary of Economic Projections.

Chair Powell and the FOMC have highlighted that risks to the outlook – on both growth and the labor market – are more two-sided now. Even though inflation is still central, labor markets have become more important to the Fed's reaction function as the economy and inflation cool.

## Press conference

We expect the press conference to signal that the Fed is close to lowering its target rate. We think Chair Powell will say that the latest inflation readings added to the Fed’s confidence that inflation will head towards the 2% inflation target. On the labor market, we think he will again note that the labor market is in better balance – strong like 2019 but not much tighter than that. We think he will also reiterate that an unexpected weakening in the labor market could cause the Fed to cut rates sooner or quicker. We do not expect the Chair to give guidance on the pace of the eventual cutting cycle.

## Fedspeak

Last week, right before the Blackout Period, Fed Governor Kugler and Chicago Fed President Goolsbee offered their views on the economy and inflation.

In her prepared remarks, Governor Kugler stressed considerable rebalancing in the labor market and rapid disinflation over the past three months. She thinks it is appropriate to begin cutting rates later this year, but added that cutting earlier than expected could be tied to rising layoffs driving the unemployment rate higher. Similarly, Chicago Fed President Goolsbee pointed to the last few months of disinflation and expressed concern over the labor market and the Fed triggering a recession if policy remains too tight for too long.

## From timing to pace

Market context into the meeting: The US economy has shown some signs of cooling, and inflation in 2Q24 has looked much cooler vs. what we saw in 1Q24 - a reminder of the possible residual seasonality that may be affecting the progress on core PCE inflation. Additionally, cooler rent inflation in June has opened the door to continued cooler CPI prints going forward, and should have added to confidence on inflation by both investors and the Fed.

This backdrop has allowed the market to gravitate toward a September 25bp cut as a given. The market has generally been skeptical of pricing a series of continuous cuts, and even now the focus has shifted from the timing of cuts to the pace of cuts after September.

Our base case remains for there to be three \*25bp cuts in 2024, starting in September, through November and December. The market currently prices a shallower cutting cycle, spaced in a pattern that shows markets pricing a higher probability of a cut every alternate meeting (see Exhibit 2 ) – a once-a-quarter pace. On the other hand, our base case is that the Fed delivers 7 back-to-back cuts through June 2025 – opening a gap between our pricing of rate cuts and the market's (see Exhibit 3 ).

Investors will look for key phrases like "mid-cycle adjustment" or "insurance cuts" – words that were used during the rate cuts in 2019, when the Fed delivered three back-to-back 25bp cuts from July through November.

Elections are also likely to loom large heading into the meeting, and we do not expect the Fed itself to make any comments about the presidential election as is the historical practice. However, markets will continue to grapple and consider the elections – and we think this will keep the long-end of the Treasury curve under pressure to price higher yields (see Exhibit 4 ). Adding to that, the risk of August/September seasonality in which investor focus on positive carry (i.e., short duration currently) (see Exhibit 5 ) drives longend yields higher and the curve steeper.

Overall, we see a bullish bias to short-term yields driven by the Fed meeting, and some bearish election and seasonality-driven bias higher into long term yields – increasing the chance that the yield curve can continue to steepen. We maintain 2s20s steepeners into the July FOMC.

## Disclosure Section

Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)

Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any premium paid
