# China Economic Perspectives Consecutive export growth

## Acceleration of both export growth and import growth at the end of 2023

Export growth accelerated to 2.3% in December from prior growth of 0.5%, slightly better than the Bloomberg consensus expectation of 1.7% growth. The statistical base was set high for the month, sequential export expansion helped to drive the headline growth acceleration. Imports returned to a mild y/y expansion by 0.2%, vs. Bloomberg consensus expectation of 0.3% growth. For full year 2023, exports and imports contracted by 4.6% and 5.5% respectively, much worse than the 5.6% export growth and 0.7% import growth recorded for 2022.

## Shipments to smaller trading partners drove the headline acceleration, faded smart phone boost

Exports to G3 (the US, EU, Japan) registered wider y/y contraction, as shipments to the US fell back to y/y contraction again after a jump in November. Shipment growth to ASEAN recorded narrower y/y contraction while export growth to BRI economies moderated. Exports to "others"/smaller trading partners accelerated significantly to support the overall export growth, the latter helped by low base. Mobile shipments turned to y/y contraction with sequential contraction in both export value and volume, a sign of faded boost from smart phone new product cycle. Auto exports accelerated, consumer goods exports recorded narrower contraction.

## Import growth turned positive with stronger non-energy imports

Oil and gas imports continued to underperform with y/y contraction, high statistical base is a dragging factor behind this. Non-energy imports showed significant acceleration to support overall import growth. Import growth of iron ore and copper ore accelerated to 48.6%y/y and 27.4%y/y respectively; while low base helped, our estimation also suggests sequential expansion in import volume for these items on seasonally adjusted basis towards the year-end.

## Trade stabilization for the new year

Recent data points continue to show a mixed picture for external trade conditions. Hard data in neighbouring economies continued to show steady improvements, but the survey data is turning more negative. In the near term, we expect export growth to hover around current levels while the chance of a sharp rebound is still limited. The projected US recession and a high base could place downward pressure on exports in Q2. Nonetheless, for full year 2024, we expect exports to end this year's contraction and record the same level as 2023. Imports may also turn to a small positive reading of 0.5% (from -5.5% in 2023).

## CNY appreciation amidst weaker USD, steady CNY by year-end 2024

The CNY appreciated further to the range of 7.1 by end-December vs. 7.13 at end-November, amidst further weakness in the USD DXY index. Looking ahead, the dynamics of the interest rate differentials, USD yields and USD index could still bring episodes of volatility. That said, we also maintain our expectation that the PBC will use various tools to prevent further CNY weakness much beyond 7.3. In the medium term, considering factors such as our projection of stabilization of China’s growth momentum, UBS US economics team’s expectation of policy rate cuts from the US Fed, we project the CNY to fluctuate around 7.10 by year-end 2024. FX reserves expanded again in December, supported by valuation effect, wider trade balance while capital outflow pressure might have eased further.

## Exports stayed in y/y growth for the 2nd month in row

Headline y/y export growth accelerated to 2.3% in December from prior of 0.5%; Bloomberg consensus expected 1.7% growth. Imports switched to y/y expansion again by 0.2%, against prior contraction of 0.6% while Bloomberg consensus expected 0.3%y/y growth. Trade balance expanded to US\$75.3bn from US\$68.4bn.

Our estimation suggests export level expanded by 1.2% m/m on a seasonally adjusted basis vs. prior expansion of 2.1% m/m. Continued sequential improvement helped to yield the positive headline despite the base set slightly higher for the month.

In real terms, our estimation suggests that real export has registered positive y/y growth for the fifth consecutive month with growth rate accelerating to 17.9% from 12%. Real import has managed the y/y growth stream for the 11th month in a row, and the growth rate picked up to 2.9%y/y from 0.6%y/y. Our estimation shows that y/y export and import price drags both widened over the month after previous improvements.

For full year 2023, exports contracted by 4.6% and imports contracted by 5.5%, much worse than the 5.6% nominal export growth and 0.7% import growth recorded for 2022. In real terms, we estimate exports and imports expanded by 4.6% and 2.9% respectively in 2023, a significant improvement vs. 1.6% real export contraction and 6.7% real import contraction in 2022.

## Shipment growth to smaller trading partners improved, smart phone boost faded, autos reaccelerated

Exports to G3 (the US, EU, Japan) registered a slightly wider y/y contraction at 4.9% y/y (vs. prior contraction of 4.7%). Shipments to the US dropped back to 6.9% y/y contraction after the one-off jump to 7.3% y/y growth in the previous month. Y/y shipment contraction to the EU and Japan narrowed. In particular, shipment contraction to the EU narrowed to only 1.9% y/y from prior 14.5% with strong sequential expansion. Exports to North Asia economies dropped back to y/y contraction again (1.9%) after the mild growth in the prior month.

Shipment contraction to ASEAN economies narrowed to 6.1% y/y (vs. prior contraction of 7.1%). Export growth to the BRI economies (Brazil, Russia and India) moderated slightly to 15.3% y/y from 17.9% y/y. Exports to other trading partners accelerated significantly to 18.2% from 7.2%, offering the key support to the acceleration of headline export growth, partly thanks to low base.

Mobile phone exports swung down sharply to 0.4%y/y contraction in December, from 54.6%y/y growth in November. Sequentially, both shipment value and volume contracted on a m/m basis, and average unit value declined, suggesting the initial boost from the new product cycle may have begun to fade. Exports of LCD panels and ICs both maintained y/y growth. Exports of computers registered a narrower y/y contraction again despite higher base. Overall, the IT basket export managed to retain y/y growth for a second month. Whereas the slowing mobile phone export growth and high base leading a growth rate moderation, the continuing growth in ICs exports and narrowing contraction of PC exports are reflections of tech cycle improvements.

Exports of auto and auto parts reaccelerated to 27.6% from 19.6%. Sequentially, seasonally adjusted auto shipment volume level moderated from historical high after rather fast expansions in the previous months. The y/y export volume growth of auto stayed at 36%, reflecting continuing strength of China's auto industry along the global auto supply chain.

Exports of consumer goods recorded a narrower y/y drag at 0.8% vs. prior contraction of 6.7%. The base was set high for the consumer goods export, such improvement in headline should reflect sequential expansion and likely be driven by smaller emerging markets trading partners. Within the consumer basket, exports of footwear saw the biggest y/y contraction at 11.5% y/y, whereas that of furniture continued to outperform other consumer items with 12.4% growth.

## Imports returned to mild growth

Imports of key commodity basket improved to 5.1%y/y growth to support the overall import improvement. Oil and gas imports continued to underperform. Crude oil imports registered y/y contraction again but at a narrower pace at 4.5% vs. prior of 12.8%. Natural gas imports recorded 2%y/y contraction. High base is a factor behind the y/y contraction of oil and gas imports. On the other hand, coal import growth accelerated further to 23.4%y/y with strong import volume expansion.

Imports of non-energy commodities continued to register resilient growth. Import growth of iron ore accelerated to 48.6% y/y vs. prior of 29.1%, with significant improvements in both price and volume growth. Imports of copper accelerated significantly to 27.4% from 11.6%. Low base is a factor supporting accelerations in headline base metal import growth. Our estimation also suggests sequential expansion in import volume for these items on a seasonally adjusted basis towards the year-end.

Imports of machine tools registered y/y growth by 5.8%, import price of machine tools dropped to y/y contraction but import volume saw strong growth. Imports of IT components dropped back to y/y contraction, partly due to the drag from high base. Imports of automatic data processing machines accelerated to 27%y/y in December after growing by 14.4% in November and 60% y/y in October, such could be reflecting continued intakes of Generative Artificial Intelligence related computers.

## Trade stabilization in 2024

Recent data points continue to show a mixed picture for external trade conditions. Hard data in neighbouring economies is showing continuing improvements, with Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam all registering continuation y/y export growth in their latest data points.

However, survey data is turning negative, the ISM manufacturing PMI new orders, the DM PMI new orders index, German Ifo index for industry and trade all moderated. China's NBS manufacturing PMI and Caixin manufacturing new export orders showed divergence again, with the NBS manufacturing PMI new export orders dropping while Caixin new export orders improved.

In our assessment, current data combination reflects suggests a strong rebound in the overall external demand momentum is still missing for China though the tech cycle is remaining positive to support exporters along the supply chain. In the near term, we expect export growth to hover around current levels while the chance of a sharp rebound is still limited. The projected US recession and a high base could place downward pressure on exports in Q2. Nonetheless, for full year 2024, we expect exports to end this year's contraction and record the same level as 2023. Imports may also turn to a small positive reading of 0.5% (from -5.5% in 2023).

## CNY appreciation amidst weaker USD, steady CNY by year-end 2024

The CNY appreciated further to the range of 7.1 by end-December vs. 7.13 at end-November, amidst further weakness in the USD DXY index. On the other hand, the CNY depreciated against the CEFTs basket by around 0.8% for the month. Since the wave of notable depreciation in September and October, the CNY has appreciated vs. the USD for two months in a row against the backdrop of weakening USD DXY index.

Looking ahead, the dynamics of the interest rate differentials, USD yields and USD index could still bring episodes of volatility. That said, we also maintain our expectation that the PBC will use various tools (including reintroducing counter-cyclical factor in daily fixing) to prevent further CNY weakness much beyond 7.3. In the medium term, considering factors such as our projection of stabilization of China’s growth momentum, UBS US economics team's expected policy rate cuts from the US Fed, we project the CNY to fluctuate around 7.10 by year-end 2024.

FX reserves rose by US\$66.2bn to US\$3.24trn over December. Against the backdrop of weaker USD index and global financial market rally, valuation effect should be one of the most important factors driving the expansion while wider trade surplus helped too. Capital outflow pressure likely eased as USDCNY strengthened. For full year 2023, FX reserve balance expanded by US\$110bn, almost fully reversing the US\$122bn losses in 2022.
