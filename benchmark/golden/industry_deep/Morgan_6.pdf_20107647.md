# Global Macro Commentary | Global

## April 16

Powell accentuates recent data has not met bar for cuts; Tsys sell-off led by belly; GoCs twist-steepen on third straight weak CPI, USD/CAD at local high; EUR/USD downside continues, Lagarde talks cuts; USD/JPY recovers after sharp drop; BRL weakens; DXY at 106.26 (+0.0%); US 10y at 4.667% (+6.6bp).

• Fed Chair Powell accentuates that the "more recent data" have clearly not provided the Fed "greater confidence" it needs to ease policy restrictiveness and instead "indicate it is likely to take longer than expected to achieve that confidence."

• BRL (-1.9%) weakens substantially, to the 5.28 level, the weakest level in over a year.

## Japan:

After another sell-off in USTs overnight on Monday, JGBs bear-steepened on Monday amid a repricing higher in term premium. Front and belly JGBs traded relatively resiliently, as market participants seemingly attempted to square short ASW positions into the liquidity tap auction. The liquidity tap auction for the 5-15.5y sector was strong due to strong demand to square short positions as well as demand from real money market participants, in particular for the 15y tenor. JGBs continued to trade relatively resiliently after the strong tap auction and 5y JGBs sold off only 0.5bp d/d to 0.485% on a simple yield basis. Long-end JGBs lagged given an absence of interest to buy the dip; 30y JGBs sold off by 1.0bp d/d to 1.925%.

TONA OIS sold off, led by the belly of the curve which was in contrast with resilient performance in cash JGBs of the same maturity. This suggests market participants sought to square short JGB ASW positions as ASW levels are seemingly on the cheap end of recent ranges. Paying pressure became particularly pronounced in the Tokyo evening session as USD/JPY approached 155.00. XCCY basis widened, led by the belly of the curve.

USD/JPY broke above the 154.00 level after strong US retail sales data overnight on Monday. Although USD/JPY traded rangebound around 154.20-30 during Tokyo hours on Tuesday, the pair ground up toward 155.00 as USTs sold off from London hours. Despite a further rally in USD/JPY, verbal intervention from Japanese Ministry of Finance officials was somewhat muted. The MoF has seemingly made it difficult to justify intervention, given the recent rise in USD/JPY has been driven by a change in US fundamentals, in response to strong US economic data. Market participants increasingly believed it may be difficult for the MoF to intervene in the FX market until after the G20 summit. We believe any JPY weakness, without specific catalysts, could be regarded as a speculative move, and provide a reason for the MoF to intervene in the FX market.

## Europe:

Risk-sensitive currencies underperformed in the G10. EUR reached a fresh year-todate low but failed to break through 1.0600 after Fed Chair Powell indicated continued patience in holding rates at the current level until the Fed will have gained enough confidence on bringing inflation back to target. The Scandinavian currencies weakened the most against the US dollar.

The sell-off in European duration gained momentum and 10y Bunds closed \~5bp lower at 2.48%. In the London morning, strong German ZEW data likely supported the sell-off while an upward revision to US industrial production was a catalyst for another short-lived leg lower. The 10y UST-Bund spread widened 2bp; however, in the front-end reds underperformed in Europe (ERM5: +9bp). In two weeks, markets have priced out one 25bp cut by the end of 2025 as pricing now implies \~140bp. Risk sentiment deteriorated again but this did not prevent a sell-off in duration. Core ASW widened, led by Schatz (+2bp) and the 10y BTP-Bund spread widened 2bp to 145bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

GoCs outperformed among DM bonds after the third consecutive weak Canadian CPI report. A widening in yield differentials between the US and Canada (US 2y: +6bp; CA 2y: -2bp) lifted USD/CAD (+0.2%) to a new 2024 high above the 1.380 level. The DXY USD index traded \~106.3000 in the NY afternoon. The Antipodean currencies weakened the most against the US dollar.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies continued to weaken on Tuesday amid geopolitical tensions, a stronger USD and higher UST yields after robust US retail sales data on Monday, and a higher USD/CNY fixing above 7.10. USD/CNH finished the AxJ session 0.2% higher at \~7.2750, while USD/CNY remained relatively stable just under 7.24 amid media reports which suggested state banks had sold large volumes of USD to support onshore CNY. IDR was the major underperformer in the AxJ region after markets opened after a week of holidays. USD/IDR spiked 1.8% higher to 16,176, which prompted BI to intervene in the spot and DNDF markets; local equities fell 1.6%. KRW continued to weaken for the fourth consecutive session, with USD/KRW as high as 1,400 and closed 0.8% higher, at 1,394. Local equities declined (KOSPI: - 2.3%). TWD, PHP, MYR weakened by \~0.3%. USD/INR breached above the 83.50 level, although gains were more modest at 0.1%. THB outperformed, 0.2% stronger amid a local holiday.

AxJ rates sold off broadly on Tuesday following the surge in UST yields overnight on Monday. China was an exception, as yields remained stable across the NDOIRS curve despite higher-than-expected 1Q24 GDP. CGB yields rallied \~1bp across the curve. Like FX, Indonesia was the major underperformer, as the yield curve moved sharply higher as a catch-up to the move in yields when the local market was closed. 10y INDOGBs sold off 22bp to 6.87%, the highest since mid-November. Korea rates also underperformed, as 10y and 30y KTB yields rose 6bp and 4bp, respectively. The 2s10s NDIRS curve bear-steepened 4bp. Singapore front-end rates rallied 1-2bp, while 10y IRS sold off 5bp, a divergence from the trend in Hong Kong, where 1y IRS yields rose 6bp. 10y HKD IRS yields rose 6bp. India G-Secs sold-off 1-2bp across the curve.

## Latin America:

In a continuation of moves from Monday, LatAm currencies weakened further. MXN (-2.1%) underperformed peers, heavy long positioning exacerbated the move. BRL (- 1.9%) also weakened substantially, to the 5.28 level, the weakest point in over a year. The Andean currencies performed better than peers despite few local developments; CLP fell 0.3% and COP fell 0.2%. Rates in the region continued to sell-off. TIIEs ended 14bp higher in the belly and the CAM curve had losses concentrated in the front-end (2y: +20bp). DIs underperformed meaningfully as markets continued to react to the change of the 2025 and 2026 fiscal targets (January 2027s: +31bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• BoE Deputy Governor Lombardelli said rate cuts are in “the direction of travel,” although she refrained on when she will vote for a rate cut

• BoE Governor Bailey said the US and Europe have a different inflation dynamic

• ECB Governing Council Member Makhlouf said “our next meeting is in June and if everything continues to move in the direction that it appears to be moving I think we’ll be in a much better position in June, I’m hoping that we’re going to be much more confident about the data to feel that actually we can make a move in June.” With respect to the size of the cut, he noted “the working assumption is that we do 25, I’d never rule out a 50 basis points but it would be pretty unusual”

• ECB Governing Council Member Rehn explained the ECB cutting in June also “assumes there will be no further setbacks — for instance in the geopolitical situation and therefore in energy prices.” In such case, the June cut might be at risk

• Fed Vice Chair Jefferson said he expects inflation to decline with the funds rate steady, but if inflation persists, the Fed may need to hold rates for longer

• ECB President Lagarde reiterated “we are not pre-committing to any rate path — we are data-dependent” but “if we don’t have a major shock in developments, we are heading towards a moment where we have to moderate the restrictive monetary policy that we have”

• Fed Chair Powell delivered an intentional message the data has not met the bar for the FOMC to gain confidence, and the Fed will need to wait longer before cutting its policy rate

• BoC Governor Macklem argued US-Canadian monetary policy can diverge even though there are limits. He described recent progress on inflation data as “reasonably good” and he wants evidence the “downward momentum in core inflation” is going to be sustained

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
