# China Economic Perspectives China by the Numbers (July 2024)

## Our guide to Chinese monthly data

What the numbers are, what they mean, and the outlook going forward.

## Q2 GDP growth disappointed; mixed picture in June

Q2 GDP growth slowed more than market expected to 4.7% YoY, despite a low base. Sequential GDP growth sharply fell to 2.8% from 6.1% QoQ (seasonally adjusted annual rate) in Q1. The Q2 GDP deflator turned slightly less negative at -0.7% YoY, suggesting lingering deflation pressure. The growth picture in June remained mixed. YoY growth of exports and infrastructure FAI picked up more than market expected, and manufacturing FAI and IP growth remained robust. However, retail sales growth further slowed. Seasonally adjusted property sales volume stabilised but at a low level.

## GDP growth to recover QoQ but slow YoY in H2

The official 2024 growth target of around 5% looks challenging following the disappointing Q2 GDP data. We expect the government to roll out additional policy support in H2 to stabilise growth. Possible policy measures include more support for property destocking, and the faster issuance and disbursement of government bonds. Following the latest rate cut in July, we expect another 10bps cut in the 7-day reverse REPO and LPR, and an additional 25bps RRR cut in the rest of 2024. With more policy easing, we expect QoQ GDP growth to recover modestly in H2, although YoY growth may moderate to around 4.5% YoY in H2 due to a high base. Our full-year 2024 GDP forecast of 4.9% faces some downside risks, mainly from elevated uncertainty amid the ongoing property downturn.

## The 3rd Plenum on long-term goals and reforms

The much-awaited China's 3rd Plenum concluded with a short Communique on July 18, and released the full Plenum Report on July 21th. The Plenum reiterated equal access to production factors, fair competition, and equal protection under the law for both SOEs and non-SOEs. High-quality development was deemed the top task for China’s future development with innovation and education as key focuses. The Plenum emphasised new urbanisation with hukou and land reforms, and improving social welfare & safety net. Amid the risk of trade frictions, the government pledged to continue to deepen its opening-up and support foreign investment. The Plenum plans to adjust central-local government fiscal relations by increasing LG revenue and expand CG's spending responsibilities. As expected, consumption tax collection will move from production to consumption end, with more revenue to be allocated to local governments. Please see more details in our Plenum takeaways.

## A 60% US tariff hike a significant downside risk for 2025-26

Evidence since 2018 shows trade reshuffling and supply chain reconfiguration. To gauge the impact of potential US tariff hike, we built a simple scenario that assumes the US hikes tariffs in mid-2025 on imports from China only, China does not retaliate in kind, some trade diversion takes place, and other economies do not impose additional tariffs on Chinese exports. We estimate that a 60% US tariff hike would lead to a 250bp drag on China’s GDP growth over the following 12-month period. In our scenario, we expect the government would rely mostly on fiscal policy to mitigate the impact of a tariff hike. Monetary policy would be relaxed further, but we do not expect outright QE or a zero interest rate. The Renminbi would be left to depreciate 5-10% against the US dollar, but USDCNY is unlikely to break 8. Policy support may help to mitigate the negative impact to about 150bp. GDP growth in 2025E and 2026E may fall to around 3%, assuming the hike is implemented in mid-year 2025 vs our baseline of 4.6% and 4.2%, respectively. Please refer to our detailed analysis.

## Business Indicators

June NBS manufacturing PMI stayed soft, while Caixin PMI edged up. NBS manufacturing PMI stabilized at 49.5 in June, with slightly softer new orders and production by 0.1/0.2ppt to 49.5/50.6. New export orders stayed unchanged at 48.3. Raw material inventory and quantity of purchase both cooled by 0.2/1.2ppt to 47.6/48.1. Finished goods inventory was less bad at 48.3. Input and output prices both softened by 5.2/2.5ppt to 51.7/47.9, indicating a somewhat cooler price momentum. Employment stayed weak at 48.1, reflecting lingering labour market pressure. Meanwhile, Caixin PMI edged up by 0.1ppt to 51.8, with better production index (+0.2ppt) but softer new orders (-0.4ppt) again. As we discussed before, such divergence between NBS and Caixin PMIs in Q2 is not uncommon, largely due to the difference in survey sample selection, while we may need other high frequency data to cross-check the underlying strength of the manufacturing sector .

NBS non-mfg PMI weakened by 0.6ppt to 50.5 in June, albeit staying >50 consecutively since January 2023. Services PMI edged down by 0.3ppt to 50.2, while construction PMI cooled further by 2.1ppt to 52.3 partly due to heavy rains in South China. By sector, PMIs of civil aviation, post, telecom / TV / satellite, monetary service, and insurance stayed above 55, while that of capital market and real estate sectors remained subdued.

## Property

Property sales growth was less bad on a low base in June, but stayed in deep y/y decline in Q2. June property sales declined by -14.5% y/y, less bad than -21% y/y in May, helped by a low base. New starts declined by another -22% y/y. After seasonal adjustment, new starts volume continued to fall from May, while that of property sales largely stabilized at low levels. Property investment stayed weak at -10.1% y/y (vs -11% previously). Meanwhile, secondary property prices in 70 major cities continued to decline sequentially albeit at a slightly slower pace. Overall, Q2 average property sales stayed in deep y/y decline of -19% in Q2, new starts declined by a lesser -20% y/y (vs -28% y/y in Q1), while property investment dropped more by another -11% y/y (vs -10% y/y in Q1). Seasonally adjusted Q2 property sales and new starts volume were both well below the Q1 averages.

Property sales volumes to stabilise in the coming months; downside risks remain. A combination of weakening fundamentals, previous policy tightening, and subdued home purchase intentions has driven the current sharp property downturn. The government started to ease policies in Q4 2022 and more notably after the July 2023 Politburo meeting. The April 2024 Politburo meeting called for ensuring housing delivery and destocking. On 17 May 2024, the government relaxed property policies further, and all 4 tier-1 cities followed through with local easing measures. The PBC rolled out a new re-lending facility in May to support property destocking, but progress appears limited thus far. We believe the government needs to provide more low-cost funding and roll out top-down guidance to clarify implementation details. High frequency data shows that property sales in 30 major cities declined much less in the last week of June (-5% y/y) and the first 17 days of July (-8% y/y) than the full month of June (-20%y/y). Our baseline remains that property sales and new starts will likely bottom in the next few months, with the help of ongoing policy easing. Overall, we expect property sales/starts/investment to fall by 5-10%/15-20%/5-10% in 2024, respectively, vs -8.5%/-20.4%/-9.6% in 2023.

## Fixed Asset Investment

Overall FAI growth largely stabilized in June but softened in Q2. Surprisingly, infrastructure FAI growth rebounded to 10.2% y/y in June (vs 3.8% in May), despite a high base, slow issuance of special LGB YTD, and restricted LGFV financing. Such strength may have been supported by the RMB 1trn special CGB issuance in Q4 2023, as all related projects were required to start construction by end-June. Meanwhile, manufacturing FAI growth stayed robust at 9.3% y/y in June due to continued policy support, as FAI in equipment purchase grew strongly by 17.3% y/y in H1 2024. Considering the continued decline of property investment, overall FAI growth largely stabilized at 3.6% y/y in June. That said, Q2 average growth for manufacturing and infrastructure investment both cooled from Q1, leading to slower overall FAI growth of 3.6% y/y (vs 4.5% y/y in Q1).

We expect both infrastructure and manufacturing investment growth to slow in 2024. The central government plans to provide more explicit support to infrastructure FAI, with RMB 1trn of special CGB and RMB 3.9trn of special LG bonds in 2024. However, LGFV financing remains restricted, while local land sales are still under pressure YTD. We expect full-year infrastructure FAI　growth to slow to 5-6% in 2024 (vs 8.2% in 2023) with downside risks. We see further growth potential in new energy investment, hydraulic & water projects, city-cluster transport networks and public utility and facilities. In the manufacturing sector, we believe FAI in the sectors along the property supply chain will likely be held back by weak demand and profits, although FAI related to the green transition should remain robust, whereas FAI along the tech supply chain could improve. An export recovery could also support manufacturing FAI. Overall, we expect 5-6% growth in manufacturing investment in 2024 (6.5% in 2023). See more in our 2024-25 outlook.

## Industrial production

Industrial production growth edged down in June and Q2. Industrial production (IP) growth slowed slightly by 0.3ppt to 5.3% y/y in June, likely supported by resilient export demand and robust investment in equipment upgrade. The official seasonally adjusted IP level edged up by 0.4% m/m in June, vs 0.3% m/m in May and 1% m/m in April. We estimate the 5-year CAGR of IP improved to 5.3% in June (vs 4.6% in May). By sector, value-added (VA) growth in the auto sector softened slightly (6.8% y/y), while auto production stayed weak. VA growth of communication appliance remained resilient at 11.3% (vs 14.5% in May) due to the global tech upcycle and robust external demand, together with solid production of electronic ICs and cellphone. Meanwhile, y/y growth of ferrous metal VA and steel production both softened on a higher base, while VA of nonmetal minerals stayed weak and cement production declined (-10.7% y/y), both echoing subdued property investment. Overall Q2 average growth of IP softened modestly to 5.9% y/y from 6.2% in Q1.

China's inventory growth edged up in nominal terms in May (to 3.6%, vs 3.1% in April), while softer in a real basis (4.3%, vs 5% in April). Industrial inventory-to-sales ratio (seasonally adjusted, 6mma) has risen notably since 2022 and is still well above the historical average, which means destocking will take more time.

## Consumption

Retail sales growth unexpectedly slowed in June, leading to a weaker Q2 reading. June nominal retail sales growth slowed to 2% y/y from 3.7% y/y in May despite a low base, much weaker than expected (Bloomberg consensus: 3.3%), leading to weaker Q2 growth of 2.6% y/y (vs 4.7% y/y in Q1). The five-year CAGR of nominal retail sales growth stabilized at 3.6% in June. We estimate the seasonally adjusted retail sales level (in both nominal and real terms) declined from May, as did the official estimate (in nominal terms). Heavy rain in southern China likely weighed on consumer mobility and consumption activities in June, while soft income growth and negative wealth effect from housing price decline likely continued to depress household consumption. Q2 average retail sales growth softened to 2.7% y/y from 4.7% y/y in Q1,with the seasonally adjusted real retail sales level slightly below Q1 average. See more details in our Q2 data comment.

Consumption recovery should continue at a slightly slower pace in 2024. China's post-COVID consumption recovery in 2023 lagged other economies due to subdued income growth recovery and weak consumer confidence. The official unemployment rate appears to have stabilised in Q2, while our labour market survey indicated continued pressures on the labour market and income growth. Household income growth slowed y/y in Q2 on both nominal and real basis. The official consumer confidence index weakened again in April-May after a modest recovery in Q4 2023-Q1 2024. Echoing this, household savings rate stayed above pre-COVID 2019 levels by 1.9ppt in Q2 2024, indicating continued cautious consumer behaviour and further accumulation of excess savings. Later in the year, the expected stabilisation in property activities should prevent household confidence from sliding further. Overall, we expect annual real consumption (GDP basis) to slow to 5.1% in 2024E from 6.4% in 2023.

## Inflation

Headline CPI edged down by 0.1ppt to 0.2% y/y in June, weaker than expected (BBG average: 0.4%). While pork prices notably rebounded (11.4% m/ m, 18.1% y/y), prices of fresh vegetables (- 7.3% m/m) and fresh fruit (-3.8% m/ m) declined on stronger supply, leading to softer food CPI (-0.6% m/m, -2.1% y/y). Meanwhile, touring & outgoing prices declined more, fuel prices cooled, and June sales promotions led to weaker prices for auto and consumer goods. Overall core CPI and non-food CPI declined by 0.1/0.2% m/m (unchanged y/y at 0.6%/0.8%).

 PPI narrowed decline to -0.8% y/y in June from -1.4% y/y in May on a low base, but fell by -0.2% m/m (vs +0.2% m/m in May). The sequential weakness was mainly due to global oil prices decline and subdued industrial demand. Investment goods turned less negative by 0.8ppt to -0.8%y/y (-0.2%m/m), while consumer goods stabilized at -0.8%y/y (-0.1%m/m).

We expect positive but soft CPI inflation in 2024. We continue to expect nonfood consumer goods prices to increase slightly and pork prices to bottom out gradually in the rest of 2024. We continue to expect CPI inflation to rebound gradually to around 1% y/y in Q4, while full-year CPI may only print 0.4% y/y in 2024 (vs 0.2% in 2023), given YTD softness, robust production activities and slow recovery of domestic demand. Manufacturing and consumer goods under PPI could remain soft in the coming months given the relatively low capacityutilisation rate. Raw materials prices may stabilise gradually after a sharp decline since the end of 2023. We therefore expect PPI to remain in y/y contraction in most of 2024 and turn positive y/y only in early 2025, printing a full-year contraction of 1-1.5% (from -3% y/y in 2023).

## Money & Credit

Credit growth further decelerated, slightly below market expectations. New RMB loans were RMB 2.13trn in June, albeit RMB 921bn below a year ago. Both M&L household loans (at RMB 320bn, -143bn y/y) and M&L corporate loans (at RMB 970bn, -623bn y/y) were weaker than last June, reflecting soft credit demand and subdued property sales. Net issuance of government bonds was stronger than a year ago (at RMB 849bn, +312bn y/y) as we had expected, while corporate bonds and shadow credit were largely at par with last year. As such, official TSF credit growth slowed by 0.3ppt to 8.1% y/y, while credit impulse declined slightly less to -0.8% of GDP.

Revise down year-end credit growth forecast. Looking ahead, we expect a slight rebound in overall credit growth in H2 2024 as property activities stabilize sequentially (in volume terms) and corporate activities improve. That said, soft credit demand, statistical rule changes and tighter financial regulations will likely limit the upside. Given the weak credit growth in H1, we lower our 2024 end-year TSF credit growth forecast to 8.4-8.5% from 9%.

## Base Money & Liquidity Operations

Base money growth stabilised at 3.8% y/y in June (3mma), after adjusting for RRR cuts (eg, Jan 2020, July/Dec 2021, Apr/Dec 2022, Mar/Sep 2023, Feb 2024 and various targeted RRR cuts) and changes from other liquidity facilities.

The PBC withdrew liquidity in the interbank market in June. The PBC withdrew RMB 80bn of liquidity in June, with RMB 136bn of liquidity injection via open market operations (OMO) partly offsetting the withdrawal of RMB 127bn through maturing PSL (Pledged Supplementary Lending) and RMB 55bn through MLF. Nevertheless, interbank rates hovered within a narrow range, suggesting largely stable liquidity conditions. The PBC cut RRR by 50bp on 5 February, which brought down the overall weighted average RRR for financial institutions to 7.0% from 7.4% and released base money liquidity of around RMB 1trn, helping to keep overall liquidity ample (see RRR cut comment).

 We expect continued accommodative monetary and credit policy, but no aggressive easing. We expect the PBC to ensure ample liquidity by possibly cutting RRR once more and the use of other liquidity facilities in 2024, to accommodate the expected large bond issuance.

## Interest Rates & Bond Market

REPO rate cut by 10bps in July. The PBC cut the Reverse REPO rate by 10bp in July, after 20bp cut in 2023. The PBC also announced that it would conduct overnight REPO/ reverse REPO when needed, which will allow the central bank to narrow the range of interest rate fluctuation. We believe REPO rate may become the new policy rate. Interbank rates typically move up toward month-end on seasonal liquidity demand and edge down at the start of the following month. DR007 averaged 1.94% in 2023 and hovered around 1.8-1.9% recently .

Yields stayed relatively low recently. Since late 2023, 10yr CGB yield has trended down on expected policy easing and investors’ preference for safety assets. The PBC warned against long-term yields being too low since April 2024, yet 10yr CGB yield had been still hovering at low levels. In July, the PBC announced that it would borrow CGB, which would allow the central bank to sell these CGB and push up long-term yields. Nonetheless, the 10yr CGB yield stayed relatively low at 2.2-2.3% lately. Corporate bonds yields also edged down.

We see another 10bps cut in 7-day reverse REPO and LPR in 2024. In 2023, the PBC cut the MLF rate by 25bp, 1yr LPR by 20bp and 5yr LPR by 10bp. Commercial banks were asked to lower rates of outstanding mortgages and deposit rates. In 2024, the PBC cut 5yr LPR by 25bp in Feb, and cut 1yr/5yr LPR by 10bp in July, both without cutting the MLF rate. In consideration of exchange rate, we see another 10bps cut in 7-day reverse REPO and LPR in 2024, while the PBC may cut more notably in 2025 in case of 60% US tariff hike.

## Trade

Headline exports accelerated to 8.6% y/y growth in April from 7.6% in May. Imports dropped back to 2.3% y/y contraction. The monthly trade balance widened to USD 99.0bn in June from USD 82.6bn in May. Taking 2019 as the base, exports and imports grew by 7.7% and 5.1% in June, vs priors of 7.2% and 4.9%, respectively. Full-year exports contracted by 4.6% y/y and imports by 5.5% y/y in 2023, after growing 5.6% and 0.7% in 2022. Real exports and imports expanded by 4.6%/2.9% in 2023.

Shipment growth to the US picked up further; IT exports growth remained robust. Shipments to the G3 (the US, the EU and Japan) registered 4.5% y/y growth in June vs a 1.2% y/y contraction in May, shipment growth to the US picked up further to 7.5%y/y from 4.6%. Export growth to ASEAN economies softened to 15%y/y from 21.3%. Consistent with the tech upcycle, shipment growth of the IT basket remained robust at 12.8%y/y despite high base. Shipments of consumer goods dropped to contraction again at 0.8% y/y on a higher base, after a short-lived rebound to 1.6%.

 A slight trade recovery in 2024. Trade growth data from neighbouring economies continued to suggest steady momentum of external demand. Survey data has also stabilized in June after previous moderations. In our view, the softened sequential momentum in June affirms our view that an export boom is missing, but the still steady momentum suggest gradual export recovery has continued. In light of the potential US tariff hike in 2025, we could see some frontrunning of exports from China in Q3-Q4. We believe the steady sequential momentum should support exports to remain in y/y growth in the coming months. We expect full-year exports to record 3.5% y/y in 2024 (vs -5% in 2023, see latest view).

## FX Reserves and Capital Flows

 Headline FX reserves contracted by USD 9.7bn to USD 3.22trn in June vs May. China’s FX reserves increased by USD110bn in 2023, declined by USD 123bn in 2022, and increased by USD 34bn in 2021, following gains in 2019-20 and losses in 2018.

Valuation effect turned negative; widening trade balance offered some offsetting support. We estimate the valuation drag from major reserve currencies' movements in June has likely been around USD 17bn due to incremental USD gain at end-June vs end-May. Global financial market fluctuations might have brought additional valuation gains, a widening trade balance vs May should have offered some offsetting support. Overall, capital outflow pressure has likely continued in June, following a net inflow in Q124. Meanwhile, the balance of total foreign holdings of China's onshore bonds registered another expansion of RMB 172bn in May, following 51bn in April, after gaining RMB 328/474bn in Q124/Q423. Foreign holding balance recorded a RMB 282bn gain in 2023 (contractions in most of the early months but turning positive since Sep), after a RMB 616bn loss in 2022.

We expect the new normal to continue, with larger two-way capital flows. The rise in capital outflows will mainly reflect non-government entities’ accumulation of overseas assets, especially as controls on capital outflows gradually ease over the longer term. Continued supply chain shifts overseas will also drive outward direct investment (ODI). The recent sharp drop in foreign direct investment (FDI) into China is partly due to a decline in retained profits and high USD costs, but also reflects pressures of decoupling. We expect FDI to recover in 2024-25, but not back to pre-pandemic levels. Meanwhile, we believe the continued opening of China's financial market and solid economic growth potential should continue to attract more inflows (see our deep dive).

## Exchange Rate

RMB depreciated slightly further by 0.3% against the USD to the range around 7.265 comparing end-June to end-May. Mild deprecation has continued into early July to the range of 7.275. That said, amidst stronger USD DXY index, the CNY appreciated by 0.3% against the CFETs basket over June. In 2023, full-year RMB weakened by 2% vs USD, and strengthened by 1% against the basket. This followed 9% depreciation vs the USD and 2.4% weakness vs the basket in 2022.

RMB exchange rate has been driven by various factors. Resilient trade balance is a supporting factor, while the negative China-US rate differentials and concerns on China's growth momentum slowdown are main headwinds. The PBC lowered RRR for FX deposits by 200bp/100bp in Sep/Apr 2022. The SAFE raised the macroprudential adjustment parameter for cross-border financing from 1 to 1.25 in Oct 2022, and further to 1.5 in July 2023.

RMB may strengthen to 7.1-7.2 range by end 2024E. Similar to the recent depreciations, the dynamics of the interest rate differentials, USD index, as well as China's growth data and property market weakness, may still bring episodes of CNY weakness ahead. That said, the PBC will likely use various tools (including counter-cyclical factor in daily fixing) to prevent further CNY weakness much beyond 7.3, amid rapid episodes of depreciation and significant increases in volatility. Given our expectation of stabilisation in China’s growth momentum as well as expected US Fed interest rate cuts, we continue to expect RMB to strengthen a bit by end-2024E to 7.1-7.2.

## Fiscal

Fiscal revenue and expenditure both weakened in June. Headline fiscal revenue declined by -2.6% y/y in June from -3.2% y/y in May (-3.2% y/y in Q2 vs -2.3% y/y in Q1). On a comparable basis (after controlling a high base due to delayed SME tax collection and carry-over of tax cuts since mid-2023), the underlying fiscal revenue grew 1.5% y/y in H1 (vs headline -2.8% y/y). Fiscal expenditure slowed to -3% y/y in June from 2.6% y/y in May (1.1% y/y in Q2 vs 2.9% y/y in Q1). Corporate income tax revenue slid to -27% y/y in June (-10% y/y in Q2), while personal income tax cooled to -4% y/y (-7% y/y in Q2). As such, June saw a headline fiscal deficit of RMB 921bn, slightly smaller than a year ago. Local land revenue declined more by -35% y/y from -27% y/y in May (-29% y/y in Q2).

Progress in addressing LGFV debt issue, adjustment of central-local fiscal relations. Local governments issued RMB1.39 trillion of special refinancing LG bonds to swap for qualified LGFV debt and/or pay arrears to corporate suppliers in H2 2023. In 2024, we expect additional issuance of special refinancing LG bonds and banks to increase restructuring of LGFV bank loans. To adjust central-local fiscal relations, the 3rd Plenum vowed to increase local governments revenue by expanding more local tax revenue resources (e.g. consumption tax) and central governments increasing spending responsibilities and its share in overall fiscal expenditure (see Plenum takeaways).

Modest and explicit fiscal support ahead. China set a headline general fiscal deficit of 3% of GDP, additional RMB1trn special CGB and RMB3.9trn special LG bonds for 2024. The combined budget deficit (general fiscal + govt funds) on a cash basis is estimated to be 8.2% of GDP (+1.2ppt larger 2023 outturn). We expect the Augmented Fiscal Deficit (AFD) to expand by 1ppt in 2024 after no increase in 2023. Amid disappointing Q2 data, we see faster issuance and fund disbursement of special CGB and special LGB in H2. Overall, fiscal support will be more explicit in stronger on-budget spending, especially from central government. We think local government land sales revenue may decline by another 10% or more in 2024, while off-budget LGFV financing will likely be muted given tight control of implicit debt risk (see NPC takeaway).
