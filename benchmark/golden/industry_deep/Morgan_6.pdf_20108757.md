# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 18

Soft May US retail sales data send UST yields lower; strong 20y UST auction accelerates rally; AUD rises after RBA; Fedspeak reiterates data dependence; USD/JPY falls as BoJ's Ueda leaves potential for July hike; Korea rates relatively outperform; DXY at 105.27 (-0.%); US 10y at 4.223% (-5.8bp).

• Weaker-than-expected May US retail sales with downward revisions to the already soft April data suggest weakening in the consumer and a potential slowdown of growth, which send UST yields lower.

• UST rally accelerates after a strong 20y auction that stopped out 2.8bp through the when-issued yield, with the lowest recorded allotment to primary dealers (5.8%) since the tenor was reintroduced.

• AUD (+0.7%) rises and ACGBs sell off after the RBA keeps rate on hold, but the statement is modestly more hawkish, emphasizing that the RBA “will do what is necessary" to get inflation back to target.

• A slew of Fedspeak stresses the need for more data, with Boston Fed President Collins stating one should not overreact “to a month or two of promising news” and St. Louis Fed President Musalem saying it could take “quarters” of data to support a rate cut.

• USD/JPY falls below 157.5 during the Tokyo session as BoJ Governor Ueda reiterates that a July rate hike is possible alongside JGB purchase reductions.

• Korea rates outperform AxJ peers as BoK Governor Rhee says it is difficult to discuss rate cuts before July, but acknowledges a cut could be considered if inflation falls to \~2.3-2.4%.

During the London session, UST yields were able to continue to drift slightly higher in a continuation of the moves seen on Monday as the European risk-off sentiment softened.

In the NY morning, weaker-than-expected May US retail sales data immediately sent UST yields \~5-6bp lower across the curve. Retail sales barely grew month-overmonth, and the soft April data was revised even lower with fairly broad-based revisions. Headline retail sales grew just 0.1% m/m (P: -0.2%), and excluding auto retail sales resulted in a m/m decline. The retail sales control group showed a bit more strength and increased 0.4% m/m (C: 0.5%; P: -0.5%) but had a notable decline in food services/dining-out as households pulled back on discretionary spending.

Overall, the data pointed to some financial strain among consumers amidst the high rate environment. The soft data also resulted in lower 2Q24 consumption and GDP forecasts, and our economists now track consumption at 1.9% (P: 2.1%) and GDP at 2.2% (P: 2.4%).

UST yields settled lower and traded in a narrow range into the NY afternoon and ahead of the \$13bn 20y reopening auction. The auction was met with high demand and stopped out at a yield of 4.452%, 2.8bp through. The demand was led by indirect bidders whose allotment grew to 77.9% (P: 70.8%), well above the average from the last four auctions of 69.5%. Allotment to direct participants was down to 16.3% (P: 19.2%), which left primary dealers with an allotment of just 5.8%, the lowest on record since the reintroduction of the tenor in May 2020. The strong auction briefly accelerated the rally in the NY afternoon.

Tuesday was also met with a slew of Fedspeak with commentary from seven regional Fed presidents and Fed governors. Overall, the Fed officials acknowledged the soft May US CPI data as welcome news, but continued to stress the importance of needing more data to make policy decisions. Federal Reserve Governor Kugler stated that “it will likely become appropriate to begin easing policy sometime later this year,” while Dallas Fed President Logan said she needed “several months” of soft data to have confidence in inflation moving towards 2% and St. Louis Fed President Musalem noted that it could take “months, and more likely quarters,” to gain that confidence.

By the end of the NY session, USTs closed just shy of session lows, with yields down \~5-6bp across the curve. STIR repriced marginally lower, with the market implied probability of a September rate cut up to \~65% (P: 56%) and an additional 5bp of cuts for 2024 priced in d/d.

Despite an initial fall after soft May US retail sales data, which signaled weakening in the consumer and a potential slowdown of growth, equities continued their rally, and the S&P 500 (+0.3%) once again ended in the green. The wave of strength in the artificial intelligence ecosystem continued, which pushed one AI company to become the world’s largest company by market capitalization. The risk-on rally also continued to support oil prices, and Brent crude oil futures rose 1.3%.

On Tuesday, the CBO released an update to the Budget and Economic Outlook for 2024-2034, and the 2024 US budget deficit was raised to \$1.9tr (P: \$1.5tr). The largest contributor to the cumulative increase was the incorporation of recently enacted legislation into CBO’s baseline, which added \$1.6tr to projected deficits. The legislation included emergency supplemental appropriations that provided \$95bn for aid to Ukraine, Israel, and countries in the Indo-Pacific region. By law, that funding continues in future years in CBO’s projections (with adjustments for inflation), which boosted discretionary outlays by \$0.9tr through 2034.

## Japan:

The JGB curve bear-steepened throughout the Tokyo session amid JGB purchase operations. In the Tokyo morning, JGBs began to sell off, especially in the super long-end, in response to the sell-off in USTs on Monday. As expected, the BoJ did not reduce the volume of bond purchases in the 1-3y, 3-5y, 5-10y, and 10-25y sectors, yet the JGB sell-off momentum continued. The result of Tuesday’s operation was weak in the 10-25y sector, and accordingly the long end of JGBs continued to rise during the Tokyo afternoon. There seemingly was less demand from real money market participants, especially on the super long-end due to continued uncertainty regarding the upcoming reduction of BoJ purchases, which will be determined at the July monetary policy meeting.

By the Tokyo close, 5y JGB sold off 0.5bp d/d to 0.510%, 10y JGB sold off 1.5bp d/d to 0.940%, and 30y JGB sold off 2.5bp d/d to 2.145% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve bear-steepened and underperformed JGBs on the long end. JSCC-LCH clearing spread continued to tighten, which suggested there may have been paying pressure from domestic investors in the JSCC space and receiving pressure from overseas investors in the LCH space. XCCY basis tightened across the curve.

USD/JPY had traded largely in a range between 157.50 and 157.75 during the Tokyo morning, but the pair moved higher toward 158.20 level into the London morning. In the Tokyo morning, BoJ Governor Ueda reiterated that a July rate hike remains possible even with JGB purchase reductions, and accordingly USD/JPY moved slightly lower to 157.50. Carry-focused trades likely continued to support the pair, with limited downside on the back of higher UST yields on Monday.

During the Tokyo afternoon, USD/JPY gradually bounced back and climbed toward 158.20 immediately following the RBA’s decision to leave its cash rate at 4.35% and the signal that a hike is not out of the question. AUD/JPY moved higher from 104.10 to 104.80 into the London morning, and the other CCY/JPY pairs moved in tandem with AUD/JPY.

## Dollar Bloc:

In the dollar bloc, the US dollar gave up overnight gains after a weaker-thanexpected US retail sales release. The DXY USD Index traded roughly flat in the New York afternoon. A stronger tightening bias from the RBA at its June policy meeting, wherein the RBA emphasized the upside risks to inflation, lifted AUD. The currency gained 0.7% as ACGBs sold off across the curve and global risk demand remained relatively supported during NY trading hours.

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies weakened slightly against the USD, led by the low yielders. USD/CNH gained 0.1%, to \~7.2760, amid a steady USD/CNY fixing and the relative underperformance of Chinese equities (CSI 300: +0.3%; HSI: -0.1%). SGD and TWD underperformed the most, down 0.2% and 0.1%, respectively, despite the 1.2% rally in TAIEX and US\$550mn of foreign inflows into Taiwan equities. INR was the outperformer in the region, up 0.2% to 83.42, on the back of steady foreign inflows in local equities and bonds. MYR also gained 0.2%. THB was stable at 36.8 amid mixed news flow, with reports that year-to-date tourist arrivals jumped 37% y/y; continued focus on political developments; and the BoT saying its tolerance for THB movements has increased. IDR market remained closed.

AxJ rates mostly sold off following the Monday overnight weakness in USTs. China was an exception and outperformed amid the muted local equity market performance; 10y and 30y CGB yields fell 1bp and 2bp, respectively. 5y CNY NDIRS yield also edged down by 1bp. Korea rates also outperformed on a relative basis and stayed largely stable on the NDIRS curve as BoK Governor Rhee said it is difficult to discuss the outlook for rate cuts before July, but acknowledged a cut could be considered if inflation falls to \~2.3-2.4%. India rates were also relatively stable, and the 10y G-sec and 5y NDOIS yields rose just 1bp while front-end yields remained unchanged. Other AxJ rates sold off, led by Hong Kong and Singapore given their higher correlation with US rates. In Hong Kong, yields rose \~6bp across the 2-10y points of the IRS curve. In Singapore, 2y IRS yields rose 2bp and the 2s10s curve bear-flattened 1bp. Thailand yields rose \~2bp across the NDOIS curve as BoT Governor Sethaput argued against raising the inflation target range.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The RBA maintained its policy rate at 4.35% at its June meeting. The statement was modestly more hawkish, reflecting steady inflation, greater government spending, and upward consumer revisions. Forward datadependence was narrowed to inflation upside risks, making 2Q24 CPI the key catalyst into the August meeting (for more, read here)

• ECB Vice President de Guindos suggested that quarterly forecast meetings will be instrumental in setting interest rates

• ECB Governing Council Member Knot remarked on the data dependency of the ECB. He stated that inflation might pick up again due to strong wage growth dynamics; nevertheless, he also acknowledged the risks around the current economic outlook, with “uncertainty being a central concern”

• Richmond Fed President Barkin said that scenarios wherein the Fed does one rate cut and then holds may be sensible. Nonetheless, he noted the need to adjust the rate path based on the data

• Boston Fed President Collins stated that although the recent inflation data are encouraging, one should not overreact to a month or two of good data, and that it is too soon to determine whether inflation is on track to 2%

• Federal Reserve Governor Kugler stated that if the economy evolves as she expects, "it will likely become appropriate to begin easing policy sometime later this year.” She also noted that the May retail sales data "may be another signal that the long-expected deceleration in consumer spending may finally be upon us"

• St. Louis Fed President Musalem noted that he needs to observe "a period of favorable inflation, moderating demand and expanding supply before becoming confident that a reduction in the target range for the federal funds rate is appropriate," adding that these conditions "could take could take months, and more likely quarters to play out”

## Emerging Markets

• RBI Governor Das said it is premature to discuss a change in the RBI’s monetary policy stance as the last mile of disinflation is proving to be very sticky, arduous, and slow, and challenges on food inflation remain. He highlighted any drastic measures to cool down prices faster would lead to a sacrifice of growth. He also mentioned RBI would like to see even stronger FX reserves, and will continue to build up reserves opportunistically as strong buffers help to prevent market volatility

• RBI Deputy Governor Patra said the evolution of the deposit insurance function is expected to face more difficult obstacles in an environment of increased uncertainty. He highlighted climate change as an emerging overarching risk to the global economy and financial systems. He said the RBI is looking into ex-ante funding requirements for climate sustainability, climate risk-based differential premiums, and suitable coverage for green deposits

• BoK Governor Rhee expects growth and inflation to evolve in line with the May forecasts, even though there are still uncertainties. He pointed out that structural reforms are required to lower the cost of living. Rhee noted it is difficult to discuss the outlook for rate cuts before July, as more time is needed to judge if inflation will ease to target, but said a rate cut could be considered if inflation cools to \~2.3-2.4%. He reiterated the BoK is focused on lowering FX volatility rather than targeting a specific FX level

• BoT Governor Sethaput said the current inflation target range of 1-3% is appropriate and has worked well, noting that raising the inflation target range could risk unanchoring inflation expectations and lead to higher actual inflation and borrowing costs. He emphasized the BoT is more focused on FX volatility, rather than the level of the exchange rate. He sees the THB weakness thus far being underpinned by fundamentals, and said the central bank will intervene if there is excessive volatility, moves are not underpinned by fundamentals, and issues with market functioning start to emerge. That said, he highlighted the BoT is now more tolerant of FX movements than in the past (e.g., in 1997) as Thailand’s external vulnerabilities are now much lower

• The NBU lowered its base rate to 7.00% at the June meeting (C: 7.00%; P: 7.25%). Forward guidance kept the door open for further rate cuts but also made a pause possible (for more, read here)

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
