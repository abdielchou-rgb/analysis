# European Economic Perspectives Riksbank: Rates unchanged, first cut signalled for "May or June"

## Riksbank keeps rates unchanged at 4.0%, signals start of easing is near

As expected, the Riksbank kept its policy rate unchanged at 4.0% in its rate decision. After the shift to a dovish guidance in February, which opened up the possibility of rate cuts as soon as H1 2024, the Riksbank has now stated that the first rate cut will likely come in Q2 2024. It left the door open for a cut at the next meeting (8 May) by stating that "it is likely that the policy rate can be cut in May or June if inflation prospects remain favourable" and adjusting its rate path forecast to one cut in Q2 2024. It believes dialling back monetary policy restrictiveness is justified in light of the improving inflation outlook and weak economic environment: "The risk of inflation becoming entrenched at levels that are too high is continuing to decline, but inflationary pressures are still somewhat elevated. [...] The need for contractionary monetary policy declines when inflation falls towards the target and economic activity is weak." Indeed, CPIF inflation surprised to the downside in February, inflation expectations are coming back towards the 2% target and wage growth is moderate. Nevertheless, the Riksbank remains datadependent, wanting "further confirmation that inflation will stabilise close to the target." This makes the upcoming March inflation print on 12 April - the last before the May meeting - a critical data point to watch. As expected, there was no change to the pace of monthly sales of government bonds, which continue at SEK 6.5bn.

## Rate path revised down, medium-term inflation forecast reflects easing

The rate path was revised down to show one cut in Q2 2024 and 2.6% by the end of 2026, i.e. earlier and faster cuts compared to the last projections in November (which had unchanged rates through 2024 and the end-2026 policy rate at 3.5%). Governor Thedéen noted a 50:50 probability between May and June in the press conference. Despite weaker inflation outturns, the CPIF inflation forecast for 2024 was kept unchanged at 2.3% while 2025 was revised up by 20bp from 1.7% to 1.9%, likely on the back of the expected policy easing. Inflation in 2026 is still seen just at target at 2%. This compares to 2.3%/1.7%/2.0% in our CPIF inflation forecast. Meanwhile, the 2024 growth forecast was revised up by 0.5pp to 0.3% (vs. -0.1% UBSe) to reflect stronger Q4 GDP growth and the upcoming monetary easing. The 2025 forecast stayed unchanged at 1.9%, while 2026 was revised down to 2.4% (vs. 2.1% and 2.8% UBSe). The risks to the growth outlook outlined in the Monetary Policy Report seem broadly balanced, and the Riksbank's baseline is for a "relatively short-lived" economic slowdown with a relatively resilient labour market.

## We maintain our view of a first cut in May…

Given the Riksbank's guidance for a first cut "in May or June", combined with the fasterthan-expected inflation decline, we maintain our expectation for a May cut. We expect CPIF inflation to continue to fall close to 2% by June, which should give the Riksbank confidence that the inflation outlook has stabilized. Moreover, surveys show that companies have adjusted their price plans downwards significantly, and inflation expectations remain well anchored. Notably, wage growth is moderate and less of a concern than in the Eurozone. And while activity has likely troughed, the highly rate sensitive economy remains weak and in recession.

## …but will watch the data in April…

Admittedly, today's Monetary Policy Report does not provide explicit insights into the Riksbank's willingness to cut before other central banks. The reason lies in the experience of the past years: with the massive cost push and krona weakening in 2021/22, companies had adjusted their pricing behaviour and increased prices to a larger extent and more often than usual, which further fuelled the inflation overshoot. The inflationary pressures from renewed krona weakening were thus considered larger than usual, as long as corporate pricing power had not fully normalized. However, the Riksbank stated in the Monetary Policy Report today that "companies' pricing plans have fallen further and are assessed to be in line with price increases of 2 per cent." This might imply that the risks from krona weakness have somewhat decreased, albeit not dissipated. Moreover, the ECB meeting will take place a mere four weeks after the May Riksbank meeting and an ECB cut is by now well telegraphed, in our view, meaning the krona would only be exposed to temporary depreciation pressure at most.

## …and push out the second cut from June to August

We therefore stick to our view for a May cut, but will closely watch (i) the March inflation print out on 12 April, (ii) the ECB's communication and guidance at its April meeting, and (iii) the krona reaction over the coming weeks. We do, however, push out the second rate cut in our forecast from June to August. Given the Riksbank's guidance for only one cut in Q2 2024 and the risks surrounding the exchange rate, we consider it unlikely that the Riksbank will deliver two consecutive cuts in May and June, while the ECB has not committed to any particular pace of cuts past June. The rest of the rate path remains unchanged: we expect cuts every quarter until end-2025, such that policy rate reaches 3.0% by end-2024 and 2.0% by end-2025; i.e. 100bps of cuts each in 2024 and 2025. The market currently prices 14.2bps of cuts by May, 34.1bps by June, and 51.1bps by August. The total of cuts priced for 2024 is 90.2bps.
