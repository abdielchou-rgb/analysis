## Developed Markets

## United States:

USTs cheapened in a lackluster session with no major data releases and a lack of new guidance on monetary policy from FOMC members. The Conference Board's leading economic index for April decreased by 0.6% m/m (C: -0.3%, P: -0.3%) to 101.8 driven by weaker new orders and consumer expectations. Despite a weaker than expected print, the UST curve bear-steepened and 2y yields relatively outperformed.

In prepared remarks, FOMC Governor Bowman reiterated a willingness to further raise rates “should the incoming data indicate that progress on inflation has stalled or reversed” even though she believes “the current stance of monetary policy appears to be at a restrictive level.” She also noted that progress in the labor market coming into better balance has slowed, despite the April nonfarm payrolls report which showed lower than expected job gains and a rise in the unemployment rate.

Concentrated pockets in equity markets continued to show volatility and partially reversed gains from earlier this week, but broader indices closed largely unchanged (S&P 500: +0.12%).

The main theme throughout the week continued to be the April US CPI release which signaled additional progress on inflation, although the latter half of the week retraced some of UST gains from Wednesday. Overall, USTs richened slightly over the week led by the long-end with 2s10s over 3bp flatter.

Usage of the Standing Repo Facility returned to zero on Friday, which confirmed the \$100mn pick-up on Thursday was likely related to a small value exercise. On Sunday, Fed Chair Powell will now give a prerecorded speech during the Georgetown Law commencement ceremony.

## Japan:

Following the sell-off in USTs overnight on Thursday, JGBs opened lower on Friday, but activity was muted ahead of the BoJ JGB purchase operations for 1-3y, 3-5y, 5-10y and 25y+ sectors. The belly of the curve underperformed amid concern for a possible reduction in purchase sizes. After a surprise reduction in the purchase size for the 5-10y sector on Monday, market participants were concerned that the BoJ would further reduce purchase sizes, especially in the 3-5y sector, where the share of BoJ bond purchases has been relatively high.

However, the BoJ kept purchase sizes unchanged across all sectors, which provided some relief to market participants. Accordingly, tenors less than 10y broadly recovered. Meanwhile, long-end JGBs lagged, and the curve bear-steepened ahead of the 40y JGB auction next Wednesday. Although 30y JGB yields already broke above 2%, there seemingly was less demand to buy the dip from the real money community.

Into the 3:00pm Tokyo close, the curve began to sell-off, led by JGB futures; 5y JGBs sold off by 1.5bp d/d to 0.545% on a simple yield basis, while 40y JGBs sold off by 3.0bp d/d to 2.455%. TONA OIS bear-steepened and underperformed JGBs in the 30y+ sector. The belly of the XCCY basis curve underperformed.

Notable events next week include the 10y JGB linker auction on Monday, 40y JGB auction on Wednesday, BoJ bond purchases in the 1-3y, 3-5y, and 5-10y sectors on Thursday, and the MoF liquidity tap auction in 5-15.5y sector on Friday. Regarding the super long-end auctions, especially the 40y sector, we believe market participants may be concerned about supply and demand dynamics, given lack of demand from a specific market participant base.

Early in the Tokyo morning, USD/JPY traded sideways \~155.40, but quickly rallied to 155.90 after the BoJ kept purchase sizes unchanged across all sectors. Although some market participants expected a reduction in the size of BoJ purchases in response to further JPY weakness, at 10:10 am JST, the BoJ announced no changes. Although, USD/JPY failed to break above 156.00 and traded \~155.70 during Tokyo trading hours. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY; EUR/ JPY recovered to 169.00 level.

## Europe:

The US dollar reversed overnight gains and ended unchanged in a relatively muted NY trading session. The DXY USD Index traded \~104.45 in the NY afternoon. Performance across the G10 was mixed; GBP (+0.3%) outperformed while lowyielding currencies underperformed, CHF and JPY weakened 0.2% against USD.

European duration sold off on Friday, in a quiet session with respect to data releases on both sides of the Atlantic. Despite a plethora of ECB speakers, there was little new guidance provided aside from ECB Executive Board Member Schnabel who opined that “based on current data, a rate cut in July does not seem warranted.”

10y Bund yields rose by 6bp to 2.52% and curves flattened by \~0.5bp on 2s10s and \~1bp on 10s30s. Core ASW continued to trade in sympathy with duration and cheapened by \~0.5bp. In EGBs, spreads closed little changed.

## Dollar Bloc:

Media reports that suggested additional measures were taken to support the Chinese property sector failed to lift AUD and NZD overnight. However, the broad move higher in risk-sensitive currencies during NY hours pushed both Antipodean currencies 0.3% higher. USD/CAD found support at the 1.3600 level ahead of next week’s CPI report – the last inflation report before the June BoC meeting (-10bp priced).

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly amid comments from FOMC participants. KRW was the main underperformer. USD/KRW rose 0.8% to 1,355 alongside a decline in local equities (KOSPI: -1.03%). TWD depreciated 0.3% to 32.21 per USD alongside lower local equity prices (TWEX: -0.21%). PHP depreciated 0.3% to 57.62 per USD after the BSP governor was confident about possibly easing monetary policy in 2H24. Both IDR and THB weakened 0.2%, in-line with most AxJ peers. CNY relatively outperformed, alongside local equity outperformance (CSI 300: +1.03%), buoyed by PBoC support for the domestic property market. USD/CNY was steady at 7.2210 per USD. MYR remained at 4.68 per USD amid stronger-than-expected 1Q24 GDP growth. INR outperformed, 0.2% stronger to 83.33 per USD.

AxJ rates underperformed broadly. In China, the 1s30s CGB yield curve twiststeepened by 4bp. There was US\$5.5bn in 30y Special CGB issuance, which pushed 30y yields 2bp higher; the 2s10s CNY NDIRS curve twist-steepened by 3bp. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-flattened by 2bp while the KTB yield curve held stable. In Hong Kong, the 2s10s swap curve bear-flattened by 3bp. The Singapore swap curve shifted 2bp higher. In India, 10y G-sec yields rose 2bp to 7.09% while the INR NDOIS curve shifted 1bp higher. The Indonesian bond market remained subdued after a sharp rally in 10y INDOGBs in the previous session.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• ECB Executive Board Member Schnabel said “based on current data, a rate cut in July does not seem warranted” and the Council “should look very carefully at the data because there is a risk of easing prematurely.” With respect to a June cut, she admitted it “may be appropriate” but added “we should give ourselves sufficient time to see what is happening”

• ECB Vice President de Guindos reiterated “we have been very clear, for June we have been very transparent […] and subsequently we have also been very transparent and we have said that we are going to be dependent on the data and how this data arrives at our meetings”

• ECB Governing Council Member Vasle said “the time will soon come when we can begin to reduce the restrictiveness of our monetary policy”

• ECB Governing Council Member Holzmann appeared more cautious and emphasized “there’s the danger that it accelerates again [and] getting a grip on inflation again in that situation would be extremely difficult”

## Emerging Markets

• BSP Governor Remolona said their “less hawkish” stance has not affected the peso. The central bank is comfortable with the Philippines foreign reserves level and is confident about possibly easing monetary policy in 2H24. The central bank has not felt obliged to intervene recently but wants to keep the market orderly, given broad US dollar strength for the past several weeks. Remolona also added if there is stress or large peso movements, the central bank may intervene in the foreign exchange market

• RBI Deputy Governor Swaminathan said India’s shadow lenders, such as non-bank financial companies, have built up risks in their books by overrelying on unsecured loans. He noted non-bank financial companies have the lowest average number of compliance staff relative to their size, compared to other segments like commercial and cooperative banks, and their unsecured lending in some entities is way too high to be sustainable in the long run

• According to statements from the PBoC, China scrapped the nationwide mortgage rate floor for individuals’ first and second home purchases. The central bank lowered loan rates for the personal housing provident fund by 25bp and the reduced minimum down payment ratio to 15% for the first home and 20% for the second home

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
