## April 24

Treasuries bear-steepen before initial 1Q24 US GDP data; ACGBs bear-flatten, Aussie CPI is strong; USD/JPY trade through 155; European rates bear-steepen; BoC Summary of Deliberations show discussion on easing; COP lags regional peers; DXY at 105.82 (+0.1%); US 10y at 4.642% (+4.1bp).

• Treasuries bear-steepen in advance of the initial release of 1Q24 US GDP, with the latest Atlanta Fed GDPNow estimate at 2.7% q/q (C: 2.5%; P: 3.4%).

• ACGBs bear-flatten acutely after 1Q24 Australian CPI comes in strong and market participants fully price out implied RBA cuts this year.

• USD/JPY (+0.3%) trades cautiously above the 155 level, with market participants on alert for MoF intervention; Finance Minister Suzuki has said the environment is in place to intervene, if needed.

• European duration bear-steepens, Bunds underperform Treasuries on a cross-market basis.

• Summary of Deliberations from the April BoC meeting once again reveal disagreement among the Governing Council on when the rate cutting cycle should start but mention a "gradual easing."

• COP (-0.8%) lags regional peers, while independently a potentially controversial pension reform passes in the local senate.

## Japan:

JGBs bear-steepened in the Tokyo morning session, with underperformance in the super long-end ahead of the BoJ JGB purchase operations, but the weakness abated in the Tokyo afternoon session. The purchase sizes for the Rinban (3-5y, 5-10y, 10- 25y, and 25y+ sectors) were unchanged, as widely expected. Although the Rinban results were not solid for the super long-end relative to expectations, this sector recovered afterwards given demand for these higher yields; the JGB curve flattened in the Tokyo afternoon session. 20y JGBs rallied a marginal 0.5bp d/d to 1.65% on a simple yield basis and 5y JGBs were unchanged at 0.49% on a simple yield basis. TONA OIS outperformed compared to JGBs in tenors less than 10y. XCCY basis widened across the curve, largely in the long-end.

During the Tokyo session, USD/JPY rose to 154.90. Although the DXY USD Index fell on Tuesday after weaker-than-expected US PMIs, the impact to USD/JPY was limited. There was no escalatory warning from the Japanese Ministry of Finance, although Finance Minister Suzuki said on Tuesday the environment is in place for intervention if needed. In addition, a media report regarding this week’s BoJ meeting was less meaningful relative to reports from other media outlets. EUR/JPY and GBP/JPY rose to 165.50 and 192.80, respectively, following the PMI data but those pairs were rangebound during the Tokyo session on Wednesday.

## Europe:

In the G10, performance was mixed; rate differentials moved away from the US dollar’s favor while risk demand remained relatively subdued. The Scandinavian currencies underperformed; USD/NOK and EUR/NOK rose 0.6% as equities and oil prices moved lower.

10y Bunds sold off and underperformed 10y Treasuries (10y UST-Bund spread: +4bp), without a clear catalyst apart from position squaring of cross-market trades and strong German IFO data early in the London session. The 10y tenor led the selloff; 2s10s swaps steepened by 4bp while 5s10s30s cheapened over 1bp. In money markets, greens underperformed (+8bp) and market pricing now implies only five cuts in 2024 and 2025 combined. Peripheral spreads widened in line with a setback in risk assets; the 10y BTP-Bund spread retraced recent tightening and closed at 140bp. Core ASW cheapened and boxes steepened (Schatz/Bund: +0.5bp).

## Dollar Bloc:

Stronger core and headline Australian inflation supported AUD, as market participants fully priced out any cuts by the RBA in 2024. AUD gained 0.1% against the US dollar, while AUD/NZD (+0.1%) reached new year-to-date highs after Australian CPI. Weaker-than-expected retail sales in Canada had limited impact on USD/CAD; CAD followed the broader USD throughout the rest of the trading session. CAD also brushed off Summary of Deliberations from the April BoC meeting. The Summary underscored a disagreement the Governing Council had on the timing of the first rate cut – a point that had emerged from the March Summary of deliberations as well.

## Emerging Markets

## Latin America:

Most LatAm currencies were weaker in the session against a strengthened US dollar. COP (-0.8%) led losses in LatAm, while independently a potentially controversial pension reform passed the local senate. MXN (-0.7%) and BRL (-0.5%) were also weaker, while CLP (+0.4%) outperformed despite few local developments. Most rates in the region followed USTs higher. DIs were \~11bp higher across the curve, while the IBR curve bear-steepened (2s10s: +8bp). TIIEs also sold off following the higher-than-expected CPI (for more, read here) and ended 8bp higher in the 3y tenor.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The Summary of Deliberations from the April BoC meeting mentioned officials expect a “gradual easing” in the central bank’s policy stance. As in the March Summary, the latest discussion revealed disagreement among the Governing Council on when the rate-cutting cycle should start. Regarding inflation, the BoC acknowledged “further” progress has been made but wants it to be “sustained”

## Emerging Markets

• BCB Board Member Galipolo sounded comfortable with a continuation of the easing cycle. He acknowledged recent moves in local assets need to be viewed calmly and would wait to see the impact on inflation. He emphasized they only have an inflation target and inflation has behaved well. Markets have priced in a slowdown in the cutting cycle at the May BCB meeting (- 25bp)

## Economic Release Notes

## Developed Markets

• In Australia, Headline CPI rose by 1.0% q/q in 1Q24 (C: 0.8%; P: 0.6%) and Trimmed Mean CPI rose by 1.0% q/q in 1Q24 (C: 0.8%; P: 0.8%); the monthly indicator also showed sequential momentum. Alongside tight labor conditions and strong global growth, our economists believe this data supports their long-held view for the RBA to be on hold this year, with risks tilted more toward hikes than near-term cuts

• In the UK, Median Basic Pay Settlements rose 4.8% y/y in 1Q24 (P: 5.0%), the smallest rise since 3Q22. Separate data from the BCC show the percentage of businesses reporting recruitment difficulties is at the lowest in three years (66%)

• In Germany, the IFO Business Climate improved to 89.4 in April (C: 88.8; P: 87.9)

• In the US, preliminary March Durable Goods Orders rose by 2.6% m/m (C: 2.5%; P: 0.7%), boosted by aircraft orders. Core Capital Goods Shipments rose by 0.2% m/m (C: 0.2%; P: -0.6%). The stall in equipment investment likely will continue; orders for core capital goods remain below shipments. Inventories were flat m/m and pulled down our economists' 1Q24 US GDP tracking to 2.6%

• In Canada, Headline Retail Sales fell by 0.1% m/m in February (C: 0.1%; P: - 0.3%) as consumers generally pared back spending on discretionary items. Core Retail Sales (ex-gasoline stations and fuel vendors, and motor vehicle parts dealers) were unchanged m/m in February. In volume terms, retail sales fell by 0.3% m/m in February. Headwinds persist for Canadian consumers as macroeconomic uncertainty and tighter monetary policy remain challenges. Our economists expect consumer spending will remain soft as higher mortgage renewals weigh on household spend

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
