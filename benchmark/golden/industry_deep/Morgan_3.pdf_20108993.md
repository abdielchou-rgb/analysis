## Global Exposure Combining Quantamental Perspectives

The revenue exposures of companies operating in the EM/Asia Pacific/Japan region have intertwined across markets as well as across regions globally. The traditional single geographic classification, which is usually based on domicile, does not fully capture the geographic exposure faced by companies. We launched the Global Exposure Guide for the EM/Asia Pacific/Japan region in September 2014. We subsequently revamped our taxonomy and have published a Global Exposure Guide in the second quarter of each year since 2016.

Increasingly complex business models raise complications regarding not only geographic revenue exposures. Investors' concerns on cost exposures have also grown amid supply chain challenges post the Covid pandemic since 2020. Thus, in this year's version of Global Exposure Guide, we attempt to explore these different dimensions of exposures for stocks in the EM/Asia Pacific/Japan region. We highlight the key themes we will explore below:

1. We explore ways to tilt EM portfolio exposure towards US.

2. We identify companies that are most affected by Yen movements from revenue and cost exposure perspectives.

3. We identify companies that are most exposed to foreign revenue that may have higher sensitivity to dollar moves.

4. We highlight Chinese companies that are most exposed to US suppliers given ongoing geopolitical dynamics.

## Revenue Exposure Portfolios in the Context of Themes and Regimes

## Revenue Exposure #1 - Enhancing EM Returns by tilting towards US Revenue Exposure

In the past decade, EM equities have lagged their developed market peers, especially US equities. Since October 2010, MSCI USA has achieved a \~500% cumulative return outpacing MSCI World \~300% and MSCI EM \~40% returns. Although past performance is no guarantee of future results, we explore ways to tilt our portfolios towards US revenue exposure. The results indicate that EM investors could enhance returns by expressing a preference towards the US via a US revenue weighted methodology.

We explored two methodologies in this note: 1) enhanced weighting scheme based on revenue from the US and 2) by simply investing in the top 30 securities based on % of revenue from the US. Both methods rely on our MS sector analyst revenue estimates input, thus the investment universe is based on Morgan Stanley's equity coverage. In order to best reflect the actual trading behavior and data availability, we match the annual rebalance date with our GEG exposure guide publication date instead of calendar year.

## Revenue Exposure #3 - Exposure to USD Volatility

It is widely known among investors that EM moves in the opposite direction as USD weakness (i.e., EM stocks perform well as USD weakens), as a weakening USD tends to drive fund flows to EM pursuing more attractive growth prospects. However, the sensitivity to USD movements differs by foreign revenue exposure. To evaluate the sensitivity, we create two EM portfolios, weighted by Foreign and Domestic revenue similar to the US revenue weighted methodology in Revenue Exposure #1 - Enhancing EM Returns by tilting towards US Revenue Exposure . Exhibit 15 shows the regression of the monthly return of the two portfolios against the monthly change in the dollar index (DXY). As expected, both portfolios show a negative slope, such that USD depreciation is associated with stronger performance of EM. However, the EM portfolio weighted by foreign revenue has a steeper slope than that weighted by domestic revenue (-1.76 vs. - 1.59), and with a similar R-squared (0.36 vs. 0.35). This demonstrates that EM stocks with high foreign revenue exposure are more sensitive to USD movements.

## Cost Exposure of Asia/EM Stocks

Company filings and disclosures regarding geographic and industry perspectives have focused on top line exposures. Especially in Asia/EM, the amount of information available for the cost exposures is less rich than for top lines. At the same time, concerns on supply chains have been growing and de-risking is ongoing. In addition to various important thematic topics, we also introduce the potential uses of supply chain data sourced from FactSet. In this section, we attempt to extend the usage of supply chain data to gauge the cost exposures of stocks in the Asia/EM region.

## Brief introduction of FactSet Supply Chain data

FactSet classifies the business relationships and interconnections among global corporates, based on investor reports, annual filings, investor presentations, and press releases. While there are always two parties to each relationship, i.e. supplier and customer, disclosures may be available at one end only, rather than both. For example, corporate A may be an important customer of corporate B, hence this relationship is likely to be disclosed in corporate B’s filings. But corporate A may have many other suppliers, and corporate B may not be disclosed by corporate A as it is not a key supplier. Hence, we would have to refer to both relationships, and reverse relationships for a more complete picture.

In this report, we refer to MSCI AC World index constituents and identify all their suppliers and customers from FactSet Supply Chain data to derive the union of relationships and reverse relationships. We then create an adjacent matrix to contain all such customer-supplier relationships.

We acknowledge room for improvement for this analysis as data availability enhances. In particular, current data availability is mainly on "existence", but not "magnitude" (i.e., dollar amount) of the customer-supplier relationship, since many of the public data sources disclose only such existence without much quantification. If a corporate relies on some of its suppliers more heavily than others, the current dataset would not manage to capture that.

## Revenue exposure to corporate, government, and consumer expenditure

In our database, we collect companies' revenue exposure to three different sources of enddemand: government, consumer, and corporate expenditure. EM/APxJ corporates derive about half of their revenue from corporations/businesses ( Exhibit 51 ).
