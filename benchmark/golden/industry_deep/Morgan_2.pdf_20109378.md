# Venture Vision: The Rule of 40 - Europe

Don't underestimate the power of simplicity. Venture investors swear by the "Rule of 40". Meanwhile, public market investors are generally unaware of it. This global mini-series aims to cast a light on its importance for active managers.

What is the Rule of 40? The Rule of 40 is a rule of thumb in venture/growth markets (predominantly used for SaaS businesses) which simply ranks businesses on a combination of revenue growth YoY and EBITDA margin or cash margin (i.e. 60% revenue YoY & -15% EBITDA margin = 45%). Other iterations include the Rule of 20 and Rule of 60 – the same principle applies. It is a blunt tool. A high-level screen to homogenise and benchmark thousands of otherwise incomparable businesses. Principally it matters because it allows Venture GPs to screen but also to focus on corrective measures for those falling below this hurdle. But to what degree do public market companies deliver on this threshold and how does it translate into annualised returns?

In Europe, the annual average outperformance of the Rule of 40 strategies has been 10%. For the large cap rule of 40 these figures rise to 13% and 14% average and median annualised outperformance respectively versus STOXX50 back to 2010.

Private companies are now over the hurdle again: According to SVB's latest figures, the median company in private markets is now back into Rule of 40 territory (albeit based on US companies given greater numbers and disclosure). At peak in 2021, 20% of companies were achieving greater than the rule of 40. By early 2023 the median privately funded company had dropped below the rule of 40 as focus on cutting burn, extending runway and moderating growth were the top priorities being pushed by GPs. Since then, a more resilient US consumer and rising hopes of rate cuts have propelled the number of companies surpassing the rule of 40 back to more normal levels. Particularly as the S&P500 has recovered, the pivot back to a growth focus in private markets has become clear. However, just as private markets are rebounding on rule of 40, public companies surpassing this level have fallen back to pre-Covid levels.

Where is Stoxx50 Rule of 40 versus history? The STOXX50 has generally underperformed the Rule of 40 across the index on average. 2024 is expected to be at the lower end of revenue growth versus history, and EBITDA margin more aligned with the recent past. However, Europe nonetheless has a handful of companies that do routinely surpass the Rule of 40, and that can be seen clearly by taking the Upper Quartile growth stocks in any given year. Although, even here 2024 falls just short of the criteria, according to consensus figures.

How does 2024 look compared to prior years? Despite the Rule of 40 being formed of a combination of a revenue growth and an EBITDA margin component, through time it is the former that largely dictates the outcome of the overall score. In 2021 – the peak year of "growth at all costs" – 17 companies in Europe's leading index managed to surpass the Rule of 40. This compares to consensus estimates showing only 9 companies – a near halving – likely to surpass the Rule of 40 in the STOXX50 by the end of 2024. Revenue growth is the largest factor of this change.

Strong outperformance: Unlike the US Rule of 40 stocks where the last 5 years performance has been weaker relative to the headline index (given the AI trade and market cap weights of the index), Europe has been more promising on a shorterterm horizon Rule of 40 return versus the index. US small cap Rule of 40 stocks underperformed the broad index but for Europe, Small cap stocks have done almost as well as STOXX50. More importantly, Large Cap Rule of 40 has performed very well, outpacing the index c103% since 2019. Much of this – given this larger market cap skew – has come from the handful of AI winners Europe has but predominantly driven by the Obesity and Smart Chemo sub-themes within the pharma sector.

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.
