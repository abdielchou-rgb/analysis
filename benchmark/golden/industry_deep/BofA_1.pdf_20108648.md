# Chemical Metal Watch

# Petrochemicals, electronic materials, oil

Industry Overview

## Petrochemicals: US PVC prices trending upward

According to Independent Commodity Intelligence Services (ICIS), US PVC prices were flat MoM in May. Four US PVC makers announced they would hike their prices by 2¢/lb in June. The fall in supply by Orbia due to the effects of drought continues. High freight costs on Asian routes continue to affect PVC prices: in the week starting 3 June, US PVC export prices increased US\$20/ton WoW to US\$820/ton. According to OilChem, freight costs from China to India have increased to US\$130-160/ton, while freight costs from the US to India have remained at US\$60-80/ton. This is a tailwind for US PVC. Also, India is scheduled to introduce BIS certification (Indian quality standards) for PVC in late August, and currently only around 70% of PVC imported into India can comply with the standards. OilChem indicated this could lead to product shortages in India. This is a positive data point for Shin-Etsu Chemical. MDI prices in China have increased. Supply constraints likely had an impact, as the US plants of Dow, Covestro, and BASF claimed force majeure in April-May. This is positive for Tosoh.

## Asahi Kasei housing orders: May orders favorable

Asahi Kasei's housing orders (value basis) continued to grow strongly in May, increasing 37% YoY. This followed growth of 40%+ in April.

## Elect. materials: copper-clad laminate, copper foil - strong

May electronics materials data shows strong sales of copper-clad laminate and copper foil. Sales of ceramic capacitors and leadframes improved. Brisk copper-clad laminate sales is positive for Nitto Boseki and Resonac. Asia Vital Component' sales continued to grow, positive news for Furukawa Electric. There was no major change in silicon wafer sales compared with April. Smartphone lens sales slowed slightly, which is negative for Mitsubishi Gas Chemical and Mitsui Chemicals.

## Oil refining: supply constraints, refining margins stable

According to Platts, Cosmo Energy will suspend shipments of petrochemicals products from its Chiba plant for one week (6-12 June) due to power outages accompanying scheduled maintenance, causing supply disruptions. According to RIM, Showa Yokkaichi Sekiyu experienced a gas leak on 7 June but the impact was minor. In a meeting with Idemitsu Kosan on 7 June, the company said that domestic oil product supply/demand was stable, and margins remained favorable. While there is concern that margins will narrow once problems at oil refineries are resolved, Idemitsu Kosan does not expect any major fluctuation in supply/demand.

## Price objective basis & risk

## Nittobo (3110 / NBCLF)

We derive our ¥ 8,000 price objective using a P/E of 24.5x our FY3/26 forecasts. Given significant growth potential in AI servers, we apply the midpoint (24.5x) of the 12-37x P/E range since 2020.

Downside risks to the share price are (1) a decline in sales of Nittobo's glass fiber products due to slowdowns in the electronics industry, such as for smartphones, base stations, or servers, (2) sharp yen appreciation (each ¥ 1 appreciation against the dollar erodes annual OP by an estimated ¥100mn), and (3) sharp rises in crude oil prices (each \$1/bbl increase in the crude price erodes annual OP by an estimated ¥ 80mn). Upside risks are (1) stronger-than-expected momentum in the electronics industry, (2) sharp yen depreciation, and (3) sharp declines in crude oil prices.

## Asahi Kasei (3407 / AHKSF)

Our PO for Asahi Kasei is ¥1,320, based on sum-of-the-parts method with EV/EBITDA multiples on our FY3/26E implying EV/EBITDA multiple of 7.5x. Our EV/EBITDA multiples by segment are: 6.5x for materials (we refer to the median value for rivals with similar business structures), 7.0x for homes (we use a multiple close to the average for rivals), and 7.5x for healthcare (we refer to 7.0x, the valuation at the low-end for Japan pharmaceutical companies in our coverage, and 8.0x, the multiple for major overseas rivals in AEDs).

Upside risks are (1) brisk demand for petrochemicals, automobiles, electronics, and homes, (2) rapid growth in pharmaceuticals/medical device markets relevant to the company, (3) major restructuring in the petrochemicals business, and (4) substantial yen depreciation (each ¥1 depreciation boosts annual OP by ¥1.1bn against the dollar and by ¥0.4bn against the euro). Downside risks are (1) depressed petrochemicals prices, (2) deterioration of automotive/electronics markets, (3) declines in housing orders, (4) slower growth of pharmaceuticals/medical device markets relevant to the company, (5) substantial yen appreciation, (6) the company being found liable for large sums of compensation in lawsuits related to pile construction performed in 2015, and (7) change of subsidy system for electric vehicle.

## Resonac Holdings (4004 / SHWDF)

Our PO of ¥2,780 is based on a P/B of 0.9x on our end-FY12/24 BPS estimate. Since valuation based on P/E is difficult amidst EPS deterioration, we base our PO on P/B. We use the level near to the median of Resonac's P/B range (0.6-1.3x) since it acquired Hitachi Chemical (2020).

Upside risks to our PO are (1) sharp yen depreciation (we estimate every ¥1 decrease vs. the US dollar increases annual OP by ¥0.6bn), (2) improvement in artificial graphite electrode supply/demand due to recovery in steel production, and (3) rapid growth in demand for the company's electronic materials on rising demand for electronics. Downside risks are (1) sharp yen appreciation, (2) a steep decline in steel production, (3) a rapid drop in electronics demand, (4) equity financing, and (5) significant write-downs related to the Hitachi Chemical acquisition.

## Tosoh Corporation (4042 / TOSCF)

Our PO is ¥2,420. We base our PO on a sum-of-the-parts model using FY3/25 estimates in light of the need to gauge the value of business segments that vary in nature. Our PO corresponds to a EV/EBITDA of 5.7x on our FY3/25 estimate, close to the average multiple marked by the stock in past cycles. We assign an EV/EBITDA multiple of 6x to the functional products segment, which is the mid-point of the multiples of bioscience business competitors and zirconia ceramic competitor. For the petrochemicals segment, we use 5x. For the chlor-alkali segments, we use 5x. For the engineering segment, we use the 7x multiple of consolidated subsidiary, Organo.

Upside risks to our PO are: (1) yen depreciation, (2) decline in raw material and fuel prices, and (3) rise in global construction demand. Downside risks are: (1) sharp yen appreciation, (2) sharp increase in raw material and fuel prices, (3) a slump in global construction demand, and (4) regulatory changes that result in the loss of cheap coalfired power or require large investments in coal-fired plants.

We estimate an impact on annual OP for each ¥1 appreciation at ¥540mn against the dollar. We estimate annual costs will increase ¥1.8bn for each ¥1,000/kl increase in the naphtha price and ¥3bn for each US\$10/ton increase in the thermal coal price (Tosoh consumes 2.1-2.2mn tons annually). A slowdown in construction demand would have a severe adverse impact.

## Shin-Etsu Chem (4063 / SHECF)

Our price objective is ¥7,000. This is derived by multiplying our FY3/26 EPS estimate by a P/E of 20.0x, above the median of last 10 years range, 11-28x. We think this level is appropriate in light of stronger growth potential in earnings. Downside risks are a sudden decline in demand for semiconductors, a sudden decline in global demand for PVC resin, and a rapid appreciation of the yen (each ¥1/USD stronger JPY depresses annual OP by ¥4.4bn). Upside risks are a sharp rise in semiconductor demand, sharp increase in global PVC resin demand and weaker JPY.

## Mitsubishi Gas Chemical (4182 / MBGCF)

Our PO for Mitsubishi Gas Chemical is ¥2,250, based on a P/E of 11.0x on our FY3/26 EPS estimate. The stock has traded in a P/E range of 5.5-13x since 2014, and we apply higher than the average P/E in that range based on the outlook for earnings to improve in FY3/26.

Downside risks to our PO are: (1) sharp declines in methanol prices (each \$10/ton decline erodes annual NP by around ¥0.9bn), (2) Saudi Arabia country risk, and (3) sharp yen appreciation (we estimate each ¥1 appreciation against the dollar erodes annual RP by ¥0.6bn). Upside risks are: (1) sharp increases in methanol prices, and (2) yen depreciation.

## Mitsui Chemicals (4183 / MITUF)

Our PO for Mitsui Chemicals of ¥3,950 is based on P/E of 12.0x using our FY3/25 estimates, which is the average of Japanese diversified chemical companies. Upside risks to our PO are (1) sharp decline in raw material prices, and (2) brisker petrochemical demand in Japan and elsewhere in Asia. Downside risks are (1) a sharp rise in raw material prices, and (2) deterioration in domestic and Asian petrochemical demand. ¥1/USD depreciation may push up its annual OP by ¥1bn.

## Idemitsu Kosan (5019 / IDKOF)

We derive our 12-month PO of ¥1,230 by applying a P/E of 9.5x to our FY3/25 EPS estimate (ex-inventory impact). We set our fair P/E at 8.5x slightly below the average of 11x since April 2020, to reflect risks related to Nghi Son Refinery. Idemitsu's total shareholder payout ratio target is 50% (based on net profit ex-inventory impact) and a stable DPS of ¥32. We expect high shareholder returns to support share price.

Upside risks: (1) the normalization of Nghi Son Refinery's operations, contributing to earnings and significantly reducing the risk of losses, (2) a steep increase in thermal coal prices, and (3) a steep increase in petrochemical and oil prices.

Downside risks: (1) a deterioration in Nghi Son Refinery's operations due to a funding shortfall, leading to large losses, (2) a steep decline in thermal coal prices, and (3) a steep decline in petrochemical and oil prices.

## Furukawa Electric (5801 / FUWAF)

Our price objective for Furukawa Electric is ¥2,350, based on a P/E of 15x applied to our FY3/25 forecasts. We use a level close to the mid-point in the P/E range (6-22x) after 2017.

Upside risks to our price objective are (1) stronger-than-expected communications, automotive component and electronic materials demand, and (2) rapid yen depreciation (¥1 fall vs. US\$ adds ¥300mn to annual OP). Downside risks are steeply falling communications, automotive component and electronic materials demand and (2) rapid yen appreciation.
