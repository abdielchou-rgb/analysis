## SOE VSCC revisit – Positive signs reappearing with recent SOE policy updates

## Renewed SOE VSCC initiatives one year after our original report

VSCC first introduced in late 2022: "Valuation System with Chinese Characteristics" (VSCC) was first introduced by CSRC in November 2022 with a focus on ways to drive a re-rating for state-owned enterprise (SOEs). We have been making the point that such initiatives greatly align the interests of and benefits for government/authorities and capital market investors, as both would benefit from a valuation re-rating and market cap. improvement of listed SOEs. For more detailed rationale analysis on the background and initiatives, please see our insight report from last year China Equity Strategy: China SOE Reforms: A Multi-Year Theme with Stock Opportunities (28 May 2023). We have been tracking and documenting new and incremental updates from the policymakers on the SOE front since then. Throughout 2023 and January 2024, a series of SOE reform policy initiatives and documents have been issued at both central and local government levels, with the primary focus on the following areas:

• equity ownership and independent board regulations;

• M&A, restructuring and listing requirements;

• internal governance and compensation reform;

• asset assessment;

• technological innovation.

These policies were instituted by different regulatory agencies, e.g. CSRC and SASAC (State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission), to further regulate, support and guide state-owned enterprises to improve their governance, reorganization, supervision and asset value retention.

Initiatives reaccelerated since 2024: In 2023, the Ministry of Finance released board management policy which further specified financial sector SOE's performance responsibilities and a proposal review process for boards of directors, which clarified and improved on the previous regulations for board directors and paved the way for better fulfilment of directors' responsibilities. This was followed by CSRC's “Measures for the Administration of Listed Companies Major Asset Restructuring” released in Feb 2023 to ensure a healthy environment for restructuring and reorganization activities of both listed and non-listed SOEs, as the activities had become more intensified and frequent. In June 2023, SASAC issued SOEs shared participation guidelines through "Interim Measures for the Management of Equity Participation in SOEs" to deepen the expansion of mixed ownership reform. Two months later, CSRC issued “Measures for the Management of Independent Directors of Listed Companies”, to further promote a better matching of rights and responsibilities and optimization of internal functions, while ensuring more effective supervision and selection of management. Furthermore, innovative capabilities were also emphasized through a series of policies, along with "Scientific Reform Action"

and "Double Hundred Enterprise Action".

Focus on efficiency, capital allocation and governance: With the purpose of strengthening the equity participation by state-owned enterprises, improving the efficiency of allocating state-owned capital, and optimizing governance and the supervisory system, SASAC has further outlined the reform of SOEs in early 2024. On January 24 and 29, SASAC officials spoke during the Information Office of the State Council conference and SASAC work conference to initiate and comprehensively implement market capitalization assessment into COEs (central government owned SOEs) assessment. On January 30, SASAC released the "Optimization of COEs' Asset Assessment and Management Notice" to further ensure that the assets of state-owned enterprises are rationally utilized to improve the efficiency of asset operations, and to ensure the safety and value add of state-owned assets. Policies such as "One Enterprise, One Policy" and "One Industry, One Policy" were also emphasized to enhance accuracy and consistency of internal policies, and coordinated supervision of state-owned assets.

Aside from the multiple SOE reform policy announcements by SASAC since the beginning of this year, we have observed that this subject has continued to gain focus at the senior policy making level; the Central Economic Work Conference (CEWC) hosted in Dec 2023 further emphasized to deepen SOE reform, with the goal to promote a better state capital efficiency rate, quality of growth and better resource allocation (such as in the field of new energy, mineral resources, and infrastructure network). Based on what we see so far, we continue to believe that SOE reform remains a multi-year theme that is worth revisiting for long-term positioning, with a selective focus on high quality stocks.

## Singposts to gauge further momentum/focus on SOE rerating initiatives

We suggest the following signposts to watch: 1) Any updates to COE/SOE KPIs set by SASAC: i.e. "one profit, five ratios": with synergistic growth in return on assets, operating cash ratios, labor productivity, R&D %, and asset liabilities ratio, and closely monitor SOE's progress on "one increase, one stability and four improvement", which was brought up in early 2023 and the same target remains; 2) Further rollout of a broader range SOE policies from both central and local authorities: We continue to see increased participation and active implementation from local governments. In January 2024, Chongqing, Henan, Shanxi, Liaoning and other cities have successively held meetings to promote the implementation of SOE reform. 3) Reform-related policy events and detailed action plans: Specifically, we look for more detailed action plans that may be rolled out during upcoming policy meetings, i.e. the Third Plenary Session of CPC Central Committee.

\*\*Note: one increase, one stability and four improvements": 1) one increase: the growth rate of total profits is higher than the growth rate of national economy; 2) one stability: asset liability ratio stabilizes around 65%; 3) four improvements: improving on return on assets, R&D %, labor productivity and operating cash ratio.

## Recap of 2023 SOE opportunity list screening methodology

• We used listed Chinese SOEs as the starting universe and screen for stocks that meet qualifying criteria for 6 out of a total 9 metrics (7 categories of metrics and 10 metrics if counting the sub-metrics as shown in Exhibit 10 ). For a stock to pass the screening - 1) it needs to fulfill the threshold for 4 years out of 5, if the threshold is defined by an absolute level; 2) or fulfill for 3 years out of 5, if the threshold is defined by YoY change.

• We focused on seven categories of metrics: The first five are part of the KPIs set by SASAC in 2023 for central SOEs ( Exhibit 9 ). Operating profit and shareholder return policy are supplementary metrics we have chosen to add. See details on how 2023 KPIs and targets evolved from 2022 in Exhibit 14 .

• We set the threshold for each of the metrics in order to facilitate the screening. We also focus on the metrics' YoY trend instead of their absolute levels, which is to acknowledge the limitations in undertaking cross-sector comparisons, including SOE-dominated sectors.

• Our screening uses 5-year annual data based on fiscal years of 2018 to 2022.

• We further refined the screened results by keeping those that were Overweight-rated by Morgan Stanley Research.

Japan: Looking into other APAC countries, we have seen similar corporate reform themes ongoing within Japan and Korea. TSE Council of experts promoted the two new catalysts in order to focus on capital efficiency

improvement, and to ensure constructive dialogues between listed companies and institutional investors with the minimum tradable share ratio criteria.

Korea: On the other hand, we have recently seen the Korea Financial Services Commission (FSC) announce a focus on corporate reforms as well.
