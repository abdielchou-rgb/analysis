Meanwhile, core CPI inflation remained soft, rising 0.4%oya in July (or 0.0% m.m sa), compared to 0.6%oya in June. Similarly, non-food CPI stayed soft, rising 0.7%oya in July (or 0.0% m.m sa), compared to 0.8%oya in June.

● Overall, regarding major factors driving recent CPI trends, food prices appear to be stabilizing (with recent uptick in pork prices), energy prices have moved along with global commodity prices, with volatility in travel and transport costs along with holiday season (prices for recreational and cultural services picked up 0.4% m/m sa in July amid the summer travel season). Aside from these factors, general pricing power for goods and services seems to have remained soft amid sluggish domestic demand growth and weak private sector confidence.

Regarding PPI deflation trend, headline PPI fell 0.8%oya in July (unchanged from -0.8%oya in June). Details suggest easing trend in prices of industrial metals and construction-related materials, with a modest increase in energy-related PPI.

## Uptick in July headline CPI inflation led by food prices; general price trends for consumer goods and services still remained soft

The uptick in July headline CPI seems to be largely led by food prices, while non-food and core CPI remained rather soft, both staying flat in %m/m sa terms, reflecting ongoing softness in consumer price trends. In further details of the July CPI report, food prices rose 0.2% m/m sa, as pork prices recovered further, increasing 2.0% m/m nsa (or up 20.4%oya), while vegetable prices jumped 9.3% m/m nsa (or up 3.3%oya) on weather effect. As domestic travel picked up during the summer holiday period, the NBS noted rising prices of air tickets (+22.1% m/m nsa in July), tourism fees (+9.4%m/m nsa) and hotel costs (+5.8% m/m nsa).

Further breakdown of the CPI basket shows, other than rising food prices and travel-related costs, price trends across various CPI items remained rather soft. Prices of traffic and communication appliances and services fell 0.2% m/m sa in July, reflecting further decline in auto prices (despite moderate increase in fuel costs). Besides, the CPI component for household facilities and articles fell 0.1% m/m sa, and prices of medicine and medical care stayed flat (0.0% m/m sa). In addition, residence costs stayed flat (0.0% m/m sa) in July, and prices of clothing edged up 0.1% m/m sa. Meanwhile, prices of recreational, educational and cultural services rose 0.4% m/m sa, reflecting a pick-up in domestic travel.

## PPI deflation pressure eased modestly, prices of industrial metals and construction-related materials still weak

PPI stayed in deflationary territory for the 22<sup>nd</sup> consecutive month in July, falling 0.8%oya (unchanged from -0.8%oya in June). Meanwhile, in sequential terms, PPI seems to have stabilized somewhat lately, as overall PPI edged up 0.1% m/m sa in July, registering the third consecutive monthly increase in sequential terms. Details in the July PPI data reflects modest increase in energy-related PPI, while prices of industrial metals and construction-related materials remained weak.

Regarding the latest trend in input costs, the July PPI report reflects: 1) modest uptick in energy-related PPI, as PPI for petroleum and natural gas mining rose 3.0%m/m nsa in July, while PPI for coal mining and processing stayed flat at 0.0% m/m nsa; 2) regarding upstream industrial materials prices (partly reflecting domestic industrial demand conditions), PPI for ferrous metal (including steel) processing fell further by 1.7% m/m nsa in July, while PPI for non-ferrous metal processing (including copper) fell 0.4% m/m nsa; 3) regarding the construction sector, PPI for construction materials (including cement) fell 0.1% m/m nsa in July; 4) regarding the auto sector, PPI for overall auto production rose 0.4% m/m nsa in July, while PPI for NEV production fell 0.1% m/m nsa; 5) regarding downstream consumer sectors, PPI for consumer goods stayed flat at 0.0% m/m nsa in July (falling 1.0%oya).

## Low inflation environment to continue in 2H24

Regarding the underlying trend in CPI inflation, headline CPI rose marginally by 0.2%oya in the first seven months of 2024,while core CPI showed a modest increase of 0.6%oya. On the positive side, following four consecutive months of annual CPI deflation through January this year, headline CPI inflation has returned to modest positive zone since February, with uptick in 0.5%oya in July. While PPI remained stuck in deflation in over-year-ago terms (falling by average 2.0%oya in the first seven months), in level terms, PPI has started to turn up modestly since May, with the pace of annual deflation easing to 0.8%oya in July.

We maintain the forecast for low, positive headline CPI inflation in 2H24. Regarding food prices, domestic pork prices have gradually stabilized and recovered – as mentioned above, pork prices have picked up lately, rising 20.4%oya in July. In addition, a low-base effect will remain supportive of annual CPI inflation rate in the coming months (recall that CPI inflation softened through 2H23).

Meanwhile, in the broader picture, the general pricing power for consumer goods and services remains soft, reflecting weak momentum in domestic demand (consumer spending and private investment), ongoing weakness in domestic confidence (across households and the corporate sector), and the drag from the property market downturn, all of which will unlikely change materially in the near term. As suggested above, while the uptick in July headline CPI was boosted by higher food prices, non-food CPI and core CPI remained weak. In particular, amid lingering housing market softness, weakness in house prices and rental costs continues to drag overall CPI – note that the rental cost component of the CPI basket remains soft, falling 0.3%oya in July. In addition, consumer confidence remains generally soft amid lingering concerns of job/income uncertainty, dragging domestic demand and general pricing conditions for consumer goods and services. While policymakers have indicated measures to boost consumer demand lately, policy implementation and fiscal support are important, which remain to be watched. In all, the demand-supply imbalance and excess capacity concerns remain notable.

Overall, our forecast for full-year 2024 CPI inflation stands at a modest 0.4%. Meanwhile, PPI will likely stay in deflation zone (in %oya terms) through yearend.

## Monetary policy outlook

On monetary policy, it appears the external environment has turned more benign lately. Since the beginning of the year, the PBOC has faced the policy dilemma between price stability and currency stability, and between growth and financial stability concerns. In particular, domestic demand weakness and deflation pressure have pointed to policy rate cuts, but CNY depreciation pressure amid elevated USD-CNY interest rate differentials call for stable interest rates in China. Similarly, boosting growth calls for monetary easing, but continuous NIM compression pressure at the banking sector limits the room for rate cuts and loan growth.

The trade-offs have eased off recently, in part due to regulatory efforts to lower deposit rates and stabilize bank NIM. More importantly, the latest repricing of Fed rate cut outlook and the softening of the broad USD trend implied that currency stability concern will be less of a binding constraint (our year-end USD/CNY forecast has been revised from 7.30 to 7.15). In the past two weeks, CNY has appreciated against the USD, and the PBOC took the opportunity to restart the rate cuts in late July, including 10bp cut in 7-day reverse repo rate and LPR, 20bp cut in 1-year MLF and simultaneous reduction in deposit rates. Based on the latest Fed rate cut outlook of our U.S. team’s forecast, we expect the PBOC will continue to cut rates in 4Q and 1Q25 (10bp each), aiming to lift the growth momentum and address the deflation pressure in the economy. In addition, our forecasts look for another 25bp RRR cut in 3Q, though TSF growth will remain soft at 8.1% by the year-end (vs. 9.5% last year), with bank loan growth at 8.4% (vs. 10.6% last year).
