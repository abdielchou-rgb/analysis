# Biweekly Perspectives: Quant Takes Amid Acceleration of Global Momentum Reversal

Risk-off sentiment is on the rise. Low Volatility and Defensive stocks in APxJ have led outperformance MTD, while Japan was all about Momentum reversal. We reiterate our low volatility strategy as our model expects global Momentum reversal to accelerate.

QUANTWISE[]

## Key Takeaways

■ Sentiment and technicals are playing a bigger role than macro data, like PMI and UST yields, in driving factor performance in Asia/EM, according to our model.

While Momentum reversal is over in APxJ and China, Japan Momentum reversal is on-going, with factor volatility reaching its highest point in a year.

Our model recognizes rising signs of Global Momentum reversal as risk-off sentiment grows amid geopolitical and monetary policy uncertainty.

■ We expect market volatility to increase, and suggest being defensive via Quality and Earnings Revisions in APxJ/EM, and short Momentum in Japan.

Market dynamics: DXY above 106, UST 10Y yield above 4.6%, gold at record highs, and bond futures repricing toward a higher-for-longer US Fed rate scenario; these signals are driving our model to classify the current market dynamics to be in a riskoff mode. Momentum reversal has evolved sequentially from APxJ/EM in February, to Japan in March, and now to the US in April. Given Japan Momentum stocks are the most correlated with US Momentum stocks in Asia, we believe Japan Momentum might continue to face headwinds even after the April seasonal short covering dominated trading dynamics. Thus, we suggest continuing to short Price Momentum in Japan to achieve alpha.

In APxJ/EM, Momentum factor volatility has been moderating since mid-March, and is now below its long-term average. Our model estimates that its reversal risk is over, and the current risk-off market dynamics could also see its Momentum stocks to resume outperformance because of their correlation with various defensive characteristics, including Balance Sheet Quality, High Dividend. Our factor monitor shows that: (i) the market cares about dividends as yield and payout ratio are among the most predictive factors; (ii) negative earnings revisions are being punished now, in contrast to the last two months; (iii) cash on the balance sheet is being rewarded as it offers defensiveness amid market volatility.

## Macro & Market Dynamics That Matter

The charts and paragraphs below illustrate our latest views on market dynamics and style recommendations.

i) The reversal risk of Momentum trade is over in China and AxJ, but still elevated in Japan. China and AxJ equities resumed Momentum driven on the broader market level in April ( Exhibit 6 ), as the factor volatility has moderated back to the long-term average. Thus, the demand to de-leverage for factor risk management purposes is over. The reversal risk now is mainly at the individual stock level, e.g. TSMC.

On the other hand, Japan equities are still seeing the highest reversal risk within the region, as its Momentum factor volatility remains elevated ( Exhibit 7 ). The recent US Momentum reversal is likely to keep dragging the performance of its momentum stocks. We suggest continuing to short the Japan Momentum factor until signs of Japan Momentum factor volatility moderation emerge.

ii) Value only outperformed Growth marginally in April MTD despite a surge in UST

10Y Yield ( Exhibit 8 ): Bond futures markets have been pricing out Fed rate cut expectations YTD as inflation data appears to be stickier than expected. A higher-forlonger rate outlook has been re-emerging. The market has been pricing in the Fed policy rate at end 2024 to be up by 125bps compared to the beginning of the year ( Exhibit 9 ).

US Treasury bond yields have trended higher, while bond price volatility also surged ( Exhibit 10 ). These are all tightening signals for financial conditions, which should favor Value outperforming Growth significantly. However, so far the Value index has outperformed Growth in APxJ/EM by less than 1% YTD, this is because current market volatility is high and risk-sentiment has played a bigger role than macro data in driving the style rotation.

Still, Value vs. Growth in Japan is moving in line with the UST 10Y yield because its Value stocks, on average, have a lower market Beta exposure, so their defensiveness helped them outperform in a down market amid rising UST bond yields as the JPY is weakening.

## Factor Monitor – Biweekly Update April 19, 2024

## Methodology

Factor return calculation: To evaluate historical factor performance, we rank stocks as at month-end within defined universes, and form quintile portfolios based on the underlying factor values. We go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile (i.e., long the top 20th percentile of stocks within the defined universe and short those in the bottom 20th percentile), rebalanced monthly. We then calculate the performance of the time series of this L/S market-neutral portfolio over various time horizons.

Composite formation: To calculate the composite factors, we first z-score the individual factor values at each monthly observation within the defined universe. Individual factor zscores are then averaged into composite scores for each stock on an equal-weighted basis.
