## Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 4

USTs rally after weak job openings; media reports on BoJ purchases send JGB futures cheaper; BoJ’s Himino warns about weak JPY; USD/CAD finds resistance ahead of BoC; EM FX weakens amid elections; European risk sentiment deteriorates; DXY at 104.1 (-0.0%); US 10y at 4.326% (-6.3bp).

• UST yields fall for the fourth straight session as data continue to support a slowing economy, with job openings falling more than expected in April.

• JGB futures pare their gains after media reports suggest that, at its next policy meeting in June, the BoJ will begin discussing reducing bond purchases.

• USD/JPY (-0.8%) accelerates decline after BoJ Deputy Governor Himino says monetary policy should not target levels of currencies, but warns that JPY weakness could affect the broader economy.

• USD/CAD finds resistance at 1.370 ahead of the BoC meeting on Wednesday; market-implied pricing indicates over 80% chance of a 25bp cut.

• EM currencies exhibit volatility and weaken against the USD amid global elections (MXN: -1.0%; INR: -0.5%; ZAR: -1.1%).

• Deterioration in risk sentiment amid media reports sends European equities lower (Euro Stoxx: -1.0%) and widens peripheral spreads (10y BTP/Bund: +3.5bp) as European duration continues to richen alongside USTs.

In the US, attention this week is focused on the labor market ahead of Friday’s May employment report. Data released on Tuesday began to signal some softening. The JOLTs job openings data unexpectedly fell to the lowest level in over three years, with the prior month revised down, and UST yields tumbled lower in response. The ratio of vacancies to unemployed persons fell to 1.24 (P: 1.30), which is roughly in line with pre-pandemic levels. The data signaled softening labor demand and may translate into softening wage pressure.

While UST yields fell \~4bp across the curve immediately following the data release, yields quickly retraced the knee-jerk reaction. However, into the NY afternoon, yields continued to fall and USTs ended lower for the fourth straight session. Some market participants are beginning to fear that the slowing economic data might eventually cause the Fed to need to cut rates to help boost the economy. The weak data continued to slightly pull forward market-implied pricing of Fed rate cuts, and the first 25bp cut priced remained November. ## Japan:

Following the UST rally overnight, JGBs traded stronger across the curve during the Tokyo morning. Ahead of the 10y JGB auction, the belly of the JGB curve outperformed as market participants squared short positions. The 10y auction was stronger than the markets expected by 2c, which accelerated the rally in JGBs across the curve. Even the long and super-long sectors, which had been exhibiting weakness recently, saw better demand and rallied after the auction. 5y JGB rallied 3.0bp d/d to 0.60%, 10y JGB rallied 3.0bp d/d to 1.03%, and 40y JGB rallied 2.5bp d/ d to 2.385% on a simple yield basis.

The TONA-OIS curve bull-flattened and outperformed JGBs particularly on the long end. XCCY basis widened across the curve, led by the long end. There will be a 30y auction on Thursday. Market participants are wondering whether lifers will start to increase their buying in the super long-end sectors given the recent rally in the overseas bond market.

After the Tokyo market close, a Bloomberg report that the “BoJ Is Said to Mull Reducing Bond Buys as Early as June Meeting” surprised the market.

Overnight, USD/JPY fell on the back of lower UST yields, triggered by weak US ISM Manufacturing data. During the Tokyo morning, USD/JPY traded choppily, and the pair climbed toward 156.49 from 156.10, supported by demand from domestic real money market participants ahead of the publication of the TTM rate. Finance Minister Suzuki mentioned that Japan conducted intervention “to counter speculative moves” and that intervention had an “effect to some extent." He also said the government is watching moves closely, but the impact in the FX market seemed to be limited.

In the Tokyo afternoon, USD/JPY started to decline toward 154.85. BoJ Deputy Governor Himino remarked that the recently weaker currency could affect the broader economy, but monetary policy should not target the levels of currencies. The comments accelerated the USD/JPY decrease to reach \~154.85.

Other CCY/JPY pairs moved in tandem with USD/JPY. During the London morning, Germany’s unemployment rate for May was higher than expected, and EUR/JPY declined to 168.25.

## Europe:

Risk-sensitive currencies underperformed on Tuesday, while safe-haven currencies gained against the US dollar as global equities remained under pressure. NOK weakened the most (USD/NOK: +1%; EUR/NOK: +0.8%).

European duration’s bullish momentum continued as oil ground lower (-1%). Price action suggested a deterioration in risk sentiment as equities sold off and peripheral spreads widened (10y BTP/Bund: +3.5bp). In the London afternoon, weak US JOLTs job openings data accelerated the rally, and 10y Bund yields closed \~5bp lower at 2.53%. Curve-wise, EUR 2s10s was little changed while EUR 10s30s bull-steepened 0.5bp. Core ASW was supported by price action on duration.

## Dollar Bloc:

DXY USD Index (-0.1%) continued its decline as UST yields fell amid soft economic data. JPY gained as much as 0.9% against USD, and USD/JPY fell below 155.00 as US rates extended their decline. USD/CAD found resistance at 1.3700 ahead of the June BoC meeting on Wednesday. Market pricing is currently consistent with \~75-80% chance of a first rate cut from the BoC.

## Emerging Markets

Asia:

AxJ FX was mixed and most currencies remained largely stable against USD, despite the softer-than-expected US ISM Manufacturing data. INR was the main underperformer in the region, as the preliminary election results pointed to a narrower majority for the National Democratic Alliance than suggested by the exit polls. USD/INR rallied 0.5% to 83.5, erasing all the losses from Monday’s session, alongside a \~6% fall in local equities and sell-off in bonds. USD/CNH remained largely stable at \~7.2520 amid a stable USD/CNY fixing at 7.1083 (P: 7.1088). THB and MYR outperformed following the fall in UST yields; USD/THB fell 0.2% to 36.54, and USD/MYR edged down 0.1% to 4.70. USD/KRW opened 0.3% lower before it ground higher over the course of the Tokyo session as KOSPI weakened, and eventually finished unchanged at 1,376.

AxJ rates bull-flattened following the Monday overnight move in US rates. India rates were the exception; 10y G-secs sold off as much as 12bp intraday to 7.06%, before it finished the Tokyo session at 7.03%. 5y INR NDOIS yields rose 7bp, and the 1s5s curve bear-steepened 4bp. China rates remained stable across the CGB and NDIRS curves. Korea, Hong Kong, and Singapore rates outperformed due to their higher beta to US rates; 10y swap yields fell 9bp, 8bp, and 4bp, respectively. The rally in Korea rates was aided by slightly softer-than-expected May headline inflation. Thailand and Malaysia rates also gained modestly; 10y THAIGB yields fell 1bp while 10y MGS yields fell 2bp.

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
