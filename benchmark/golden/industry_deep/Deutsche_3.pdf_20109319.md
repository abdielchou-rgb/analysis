Date 20 July 2024

# What you need to know: 22 - 26 July

## Week ahead

We expect the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) to cut the policy rate by 50bps next week. The central bank delivered the last rate cut in March 2024 by 50bps and has already delivered 700bps of cuts thus far, but we expect another 50bps, as real rates remain positive by over 500bps on a forward looking basis, with inflation having fallen sharply since 2H 2023. Sri Lanka's CPI inflation came at 1.7%yoy in June, after falling to 0.9% in May. Inflation was also low in April at 1.5% and March at 0.9%, led by the reduction in electricity tariff, fuel and LPG price. The authorities have cut power prices once again this week by 22.5% and this will likely keep inflation in low-single-digits for now. The next monetary policy is scheduled in end-September, which is close to the Presidential elections, likely to be held sometime between September and October. Therefore, timing wise, next week looks like an opportune moment for the CBSL to cut the policy rate by 50bps, particularly with inflation remaining low at this stage.

We expect South Korea's GDP growth to slow to 2.4% YoY in Q2 from 3.1% in Q1, weighed down by weakness in domestic demand. We had earlier assessed that while the economy is on track to expand 2.5% in 2024, its growth recovery should be divergent, led by the export-oriented and large businesses, while domesticfacing and SMEs have a less sanguine outlook (see our latest note on South Korea and BoK).

Taiwan industrial production is expected to further improve to 18% YoY in June from 16% in May, driven by strong external demand. On a seasonally adjusted basis, exports surged by 9% MoM in June.

Hong Kong CPI is expected to stay low at 1.2% YoY in June, unchanged from the previous month. Inflation has been on a downtrend since the start of the year and local retail sales have also declined. Trade activities are likely to further improve, supported by rising mainland exports. We expect exports to grow by 15% YoY in June, slightly exceeding 14.8% in May. Imports are likely to rise by 2%, resulting in a trade deficit of HKD13.8bn.

In Singapore, we expect CPI inflation to fall to 2.6% YoY in June from 3.1% in May, as base effects fade from transport prices. Malaysia CPI is expected to edge up to 2.2%yoy in June, from 2.0% in May. CPI inflation has averaged only 1.8% in the first five months of the year. We are forecasting Malaysia’s inflation at 2.4% in 2024, while BNM expects headline and core inflation to come within the earlier projected ranges of 2.0-3.5%. This implies a higher average for the rest of the year, particularly as the impact of the recent subsidy rationalisation feeds into the inflation trajectory.

## Financial condition indicators (FCIs)

Advanced Asian economies' financial conditions have eased across the board, thanks to improvements in both the domestic market and external markets, as expectations of Fed rate cut increased.

## Week in review

China's real GDP growth decelerated to 4.7% YoY in Q2, missing market expectations by 0.4ppt. The drag mainly came from the services sector, where growth slowed to 4.2% in Q2 on the back of softer domestic demand and an underperforming property sector. Meanwhile, the industrial sector maintained 5.6% growth in Q2, in part thanks to strong exports. Sequential GDP growth halved to 0.7% QoQ in Q2 from 1.5% in Q1. This has led us to revise down our 2024 GDP growth forecast to 4.9% (from 5.0%), and we expect growth to stay at just below 5% in the next two quarters. As we have discussed in our China H2 Outlook, policymakers may need to rethink their near-term policy mix to achieve the 5% annual growth target. In particular, a budget revision is maybe necessary to keep up the pace of spending against a continued decline in local government revenues. The Politburo meeting in late-July will be a key event for the signaling of additional policy support. The Third Plenum of the 20th CPC Central Committee concluded on Thursday and approved China's reform blueprint for the upcoming years. We see fiscal reforms, urbanization, private sector development, attracting foreign investment, and property sector, among others, as the key economic policy issues. Interestingly, the third plenum also discussed achieving this year's growth goals; this likely raises the hope of more support measures being announced at the Politburo meeting in late July.

Bank Indonesia (BI) kept its policy rate at 6.25% as widely expected, while also retaining its 2024 macroeconomic targets for GDP at 4.7-5.5% and inflation between 1.5-3.5%. CPI inflation sits comfortably within the target range, and BI made clear that “the focus of monetary policy in the short term is directed at strengthening the effectiveness of rupiah exchange rate stabilization”, and Governor Warjiyo said that he sees “some room for rate cuts” in 4Q. In other words, BI could begin easing as soon as October if the Fed starts to cut in September, as our US colleagues now expect. Meanwhile, Thomas Djiwandono, who is President-elect Prabowo’s nephew, has been appointed as deputy finance minister. Djiwandono had pledged fiscal continuity and discipline for the incoming administration amid market concerns and has been a key part of Prabowo’s transition team on economic and fiscal matters.

Malaysia’s Q2 GDP growth accelerated to 5.8% YoY, compared to 4.2% in Q1, beating market expectations and was the highest since Q4 2022. The growth pickup was broad-based across all sectors including services, manufacturing, agriculture and construction.
