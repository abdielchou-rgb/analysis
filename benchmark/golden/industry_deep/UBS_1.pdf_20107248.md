# APAC Economic Perspectives Korea: Recalibrating macro projections

## Extended export recovery cycle in Mar and 1Q24

High frequency data suggests that overall export growth improved further in the most recent weeks into Mar. Korea's first 20-day working-day daily average exports growth further accelerated to 10.9% y/y in Mar (vs. 9.8% in Feb). Year-to-20th Mar, total exports grew by 11.2%y/y vs. 5.7%y/y in 4Q23. Semiconductor export is a key driver behind the export recovery, its shipment jumped by 46%y/y in the first 20 days of Mar after 60.1%y/y growth in Jan-Feb. Non-tech exports also brought positive contribution to overall exports though to a much lesser extent compared to tech exports. On the other hand, auto exports seemed to stay on the weakening side.

## Upward revision of GDP growth projection

We revise up full year GDP growth projection to 2.3%, against our previous projection of 2.0%, BoK’s projection (Bank of Korea) of 2.1% and the Bloomberg consensus expectation of 2.2%. This is also the first time we launch higher annual growth projection vs. the central bank in the past two years. The upswing of the tech cycle, especially the new trend of Artificial Intelligence technology, should continue to provide strong support for export and production in the coming quarters given Korea’s leading position in memory technology. UBS US economics recently revised its GDP growth projection, the team no longer projects a recession in the US economy ahead in 2024 or 2025. The absence of a US recession should imply much less external growth uncertainty for Korea in the coming quarters, promoting continuity and enhancing the spread of current export and production recovery.

## Moderating inflation, increasing real rate

Core inflation moderated further to 2.5%y/y for Jan-Feb vs. 2.9% in 4Q23. Headline inflation bounced slightly in Feb due to base effect but remained on a moderating trend at 3.0% for Jan-Feb period vs. 3.4%y/y in 4Q23. The moderating trend of core inflation so far suggests that the earlier rebound of headline CPI during 4Q23 does not appear to have had a large spillover to core inflation. The positive gap between policy interest rate and core inflation therefore widened to nearly 100bps, the widest since 3Q19. Furthermore, headline CPI also dropped below policy rate in recent months. We continue to project inflation to stay on a moderating trend in the coming quarters.

## Rate cuts in 2024 still, modest KRW appreciation

We continue to project the BoK to cut interest rate in 2024. Despite export turning into a robust growth driver, a series of other elements of the economy still appear to be sluggish. For example, the latest Mar consumer sentiment survey softened. Despite the upward revision, our 2.3% GDP growth projection is still well below the average growth of 2.9% Korea achieved in the pre-COVID 2015-2019 period. In our assessment, as the output continues to drift below potential, the BoK could be motivated to start lowering policy interest rate, against the backdrop of moderating inflation and higher real interest rate. That said, strong export recovery would allow the BoK to take a slower pace, considering of other factors such as the US Fed’s rate cuts. We expect the BoK to commence rate cut in July, with two 25bps cuts to take policy rate to 3.0% by year-end 2024 (vs. prior projection of 2.25%). The KRW experienced a period of volatility recently amidst strong USD DXY index. Assuming strong exports, a recovery in overall growth, and 75bps rate cuts from the US Fed during the year; we continue to project an appreciation of KRW to 1280 by year-end 2024 (vs. prior projection of 1240).
