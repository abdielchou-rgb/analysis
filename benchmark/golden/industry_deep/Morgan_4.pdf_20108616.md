# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 7

Strong NFP data support double-digit sell-off in USTs; ECB members push back on rate cuts; JGBs bear-flatten amid weak BoJ purchase operations; RBI leaves rates on hold; CAD outperforms given weak jobs data; USD strengthens; DXY at 104.94 (0.8%); US 10y at 4.434% (14.7bp).

• A significant upside surprise in May US nonfarm payrolls at 272k (C: 180k) catalyzes a double-digit sell-off across the UST curve; pricing for the first full 25bp Fed rate cut is pushed out to December.

• ECB members push back on further cuts, with ECB Governing Council Member Makhlouf noting policymakers do not know “how fast we’re going to carry on, or if at all”; market pricing implies 35bp and 90bp of cumulative cuts for December '24 and December '25, respectively.

• Ahead of next week’s BoJ meeting, weakness in the BoJ purchase operations, particularly in the 5-10y bucket, supports a bearflattening in JGBs.

• RBI leaves rates on hold, as expected, and INR (-0.1%) falls as Governor Das confirms the RBI is “there in forward market.”

• CAD (-0.7%) outperforms certain G10 FX as weak Canadian jobs data for May paint a very different picture than the US labor market.

• DXY USD Index (+0.8%) climbs the most since January, after unexpected strength in US employment data for May.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

After a week of data signaling economic slowdown, an upside surprise in Friday’s US nonfarm payrolls added volatility to the market and pushed back against the global duration rally from earlier in the week.

Overnight, global markets waited with anticipation ahead of the US May employment data released in the NY morning. Nonfarm payrolls shot up to 272k in May (C: 180k; P: 165k), well above consensus expectations. Other hawkish elements of the report included an increase to average hourly earnings (AHE), coupled with an upward revision to the prior month, which brought the annual rate to 4.1% (C: 3.9%; P: 4.0%).

Despite the stark headline data, the underlying details were not as strong. There was clear divergence between the establishment and the household surveys. The household survey was significantly weaker and actually showed a decline of 408k for the month. As a result, the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.0% (P: 3.9%), despite a drop in the labor force participation rate to 62.5% (P: 62.7%).

Even though some of underlying details pushed back on the strong report, market participants latched on to the robust headline figure and increase in AHE, which could signal further wage inflation. Treasuries immediately sold off over 10bp across the curve and were unable to retrace the moves into the NY afternoon. USTs ended the day with 2y yields up 16bp and 30y yields up 12bp. In STIR, marketimplied pricing pushed out the first full Fed rate cut to December.

The sell-off on Friday reversed a significant portion of the global duration rally. USTs ended the week with 2y yields largely unchanged and 10y yields down 7bp. Next week will continue to have large catalysts for market pricing, with May US CPI data and the June FOMC meeting, where updated projections will be provided. Depending on the signals from the inflation data, different underlying details from the May employment report can be used to support the policy rate narrative.

The unexpected beat in the employment data and AHE, which supported a higherfor-longer policy rate narrative, strengthened the USD, which was up 0.8%. It also sent gold prices (-3.5%) cheaper to below \$2,300 an ounce, in one of the largest daily declines in over three years.

Equities initially fell alongside the strength in the nonfarm payrolls figure, but a strong labor report sends mixed signals to equities. A strong employment figure can imply increased consumer spending, which supports corporate earnings; on the other hand, higher-for-longer fed rates can weaken profits. Ultimately, the S&P 500 (-0.1%) fell slightly but ended the week up 1.3%.

## Japan:

JGBs initially rallied, particularly in the super-long sector, but quickly sold off ahead of the scheduled JGB purchase operations for 1-3y, 5-10y, and 10-25y. The BoJ did not change the purchase sizes across the maturity buckets, in line with market expectations.

However, the results were weaker than expected across the maturity baskets, with particular weakness in the 5-10y bucket. It seems to us that market participants may rush to sell their JGB holdings ahead of the June MPM, as they broadly anticipate that the BoJ may officially announce the reduction of JGB purchase sizes.

The weak result for the 5-10y bucket also suggested that some investors might rush to sell the newly issued GX bond (JBX2) on the fear of it becoming ineligible for the BoJ’s purchase operations, similar to the JBX1 issue. This fear weighed on the 10y JGB in the Tokyo afternoon.

JGB curves bear-flattened on Friday: 2y JGB rallied 1.5bp to 0.345%, 10y JGB rallied 1.5bp to 0.97%, and 30y JGB rallied 0.5bp to 2.09%. TONA-OIS curve bear-flattened and underperformed JGBs on broad tenors. XCCY basis tightened across the curve mainly driven by the belly.

USD/JPY climbed higher in the morning to \~155.7 on the back of weak Japan household consumption data for April and remarks from Finance Minister Suzuki that struck an unchanged cautious tone. The pair traded in a narrow range during Tokyo trading hours, but started to decline into the London morning, which might have been driven by some unwinding of JPY carry trades ahead of the US nonfarm payrolls data. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY.

## Europe:

G10 FX price action was dominated by the US after a much stronger-than-expected NFP number sent US yields higher. The DXY USD Index posted a gain of +0.7% by the end of London trading. The most sensitive G10 currencies to this move can be found in the Nordics, with SEK -1.5% and NOK -1.4%; part of the sensitivity was likely a giveback following their recent strength. GBP only posted a loss of -0.5% despite a lack of local catalysts. EUR and the traditional funders, CHF and JPY, had similar performances and fell -0.8%, -0.8% and -0.7%, respectively, as investors re-engaged in positive carry trades.

European duration sold off on Friday amid strong US NFP data; 10y Bund yields rose 7bp to 2.62% and outperformed Treasuries by 5bp during the London session. Money market curves bear-steepened, and market pricing implied 35bp and 90bp of cumulative cuts for Dec 24 and Dec 25, respectively. Curve-wise, 2s5s10s cheapened over 3bp in swaps alongside the whites/reds, which steepened, while 10s30s bearflattened 1.5bp. Core ASW edged cheaper with the move lower in duration, and was led by the Schatz (-2bp). In EGBs, 10y BTP/Bund spreads widened slightly.

## Dollar Bloc:

The dollar bloc was also dominated by the move in USD following the upside surprise in the US May employment data. The DXY USD Index rose 0.8%. NZD dropped -1.4% against the USD, while its antipodean neighbor, AUD, “only” gave up - 1.2%. One of the smallest losses across G10 can be found in CAD, which fell -0.7%, despite weak labor data that painted quite a contrasting picture to the US.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies gained ahead of the US NFP release, but moves were mostly modest. CNH appreciated 0.1% to \~7.2520, aided by China’s upbeat May exports and trade balance data. USD/CNY held steady at \~7.2425, in line with a stable USD/CNY fixing (7.1106). USD/INR erased most of its gains on Thursday, down 0.1% to \~83.42, as the RBI said it is participating in the forward market. India equities rallied 2% and reversed the weakness seen after the election results. TWD, SGD, MYR, and PHP also gained \~0.1-0.2%. IDR led the rally in the region, up 0.4% to 16,195.5, possibly aided by the weakness in USD during the Tokyo session and talk of FX intervention in the past few sessions. THB was the main underperformer in the region, down 0.2% to 36.5 as gold prices fell 1.8% after China’s FX reserves data revealed it did not buy any gold in May, for the first time since October 2022. USD/KRW was flat at 1,366 despite nearly \$450mn of foreign equity inflows as KOSPI rallied 1.2% after being closed on Thursday.

Most AxJ rates rallied. In China, 5y NDIRS yields and the CGB curve shifted slightly lower by 1bp amid the combination of strong exports and weak imports, indicating sluggish domestic demand. In India, NDOIS yields initially rose during the RBI decision announcement, but fell afterwards and finished the session 1bp lower on the 1y point and 3bp lower on the 5y point. 10y G-sec yields were steady at 7.01%, even though the amount of government bond buyback on Thursday (INR 73bn) was much smaller than planned (INR 300bn). Korea rates outperformed, with 5y and 10y NDIRS yields [both?] falling 4bp and the 2s10s curve bull-flattening 1bp. In Indonesia, 10y INDOGB rallied slightly by 1bp. Thailand and Hong Kong rates were the outliers to the broader rally. 1y THB NDOIS yields rose 2bp as Thailand’s May headline CPI rose more than market participants anticipated, back into the BoT’s target range. 2y and 5y HKD IRS yields also rose 1bp following a similar magnitude

of rise in 1m HIBOR.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• RBA Deputy Governor Hauser reiterated the central bank’s resolve to be able to bring, and to succeed in bringing, inflation “back to a stable and low level”

• ECB Governing Council Member Muller said “the central bank needs to make its decisions rather cautiously” and should not “rush too much with cutting the interest rate”

• ECB Governing Council Member Holzmann described the ECB cut as a “hawkish” one and thinks it means the Governing Council will be “somewhat more cautious in the future.” He added, “we’re now confident that the disinflation process is working…it doesn’t mean, incidentally, that we know how fast we’re going to carry on or if at all because — this is the phrase we’ve been using — ‘the road is bumpy’”

• ECB Governing Council Member Villeroy said “we will adapt the precise rhythm of our next rate cuts without hurrying or procrastinating as the outlook for disinflation is confirmed,” and “maintaining optionality is key to successfully steering inflation to its medium-term targeted level”

## Emerging Markets

• The RBI maintained the policy rate at 6.50%, with an unchanged stance as widely expected. Relative to the last meeting, there was an additional member who voted for a 25bp rate cut and changed from a neutral stance. The FY2025 GDP growth projection was raised to 7.2% y/y (P: 7.0%), but the CPI inflation forecast was maintained at 4.5% y/y (for more, read here)

• RBI Governor Das stated that with growth being firm, monetary policy has greater room to pursue price stability to ensure inflation aligns to target durably. He highlighted policymakers should remain vigilant on inflation risks, particularly from food prices. He also noted the RBI’s monetary policy actions are determined by domestic growth and inflation conditions, signaling that RBI may be willing to move before the Fed. Regarding FX and liquidity, Das said the “RBI is there in forward market,” highlighting that its intervention in the NDF market has undergone a change. He said the central bank will deploy an appropriate mix of instruments to manage frictional and durable liquidity in all segments of the market, noting currency swaps was another approach used previously. RBI Deputy Governor Patra reiterated the RBI will continue to refine its toolkit and intervene as and when necessary

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
