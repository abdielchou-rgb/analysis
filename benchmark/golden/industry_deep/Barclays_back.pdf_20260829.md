## The world at a glance

• Political risk rears its head – this time in Europe

• An excellent week for US inflation, but one swallow does not a summer make

• Major global equity indices fall, but US equities keep forging ahead

• Bond yields rally, on both inflation data and a flight to quality

• Making sense of it all

## Rising political risk is a serious problem for markets

• President Macron’s call for snap parliamentary elections has been poorly received by markets <sup>1</sup>

o Both Le Pen and Melenchon (far right and far left) favour tax cuts, wage increases and a lower retirement age

o Both sides would reverse Macron’s reforms, at a time of stretched public finances

• Most polls show a hung Parliament, with National Rally in front followed by the Left coalition <sup>1</sup>

o The political centrists seem to be a distant third, which will make governing very difficult for Macron

o There is also a chance that National Rally can get to a majority with a few small parties

• A Macron/ National Rally government would be more poorly received than a hung Parliament <sup>1</sup>

o The President would be a lame duck on domestic issues; European integration initiatives would likely stagnate

o The government would likely not pass any further fiscal reform; France’s fiscal profile could well worsen further

• The President has ruled out resigning no matter what, but investors should consider it a tail risk <sup>1</sup>

o In such a scenario, National Rally might end up in charge of both Parliament and the Elysee

o 10y Bund-OAT spreads are at the widest levels in over five years; CAC40 is down over 6% last week

o Markets should be on edge through the second round of elections in early July, and possibly for some months after

## The Fed is unlikely to over-react to one good inflation print

• There was a lot of good news in the US inflation data last week <sup>1</sup>

o Core services ex-shelter fell to -0.05% m/m; the disinflationary bias was broad-based

o We now expect core PCE m/m of just 12bp, a welcome drop from 25bp in April

• But 11 of the 13bp drop in core CPI (from the prior month) came from transportation services <sup>1</sup>

o We think the extent of weakening is unsustainable; core PCE m/m should be 0.2% (rounded) in June

o Our forecast is for core PCE y/y at 2.8% at the end of 2024, close to where it ended 2023

• The Fed also played down the May inflation data, taking the dot-plot from three to just one cut <sup>1</sup>

o The statement acknowledged only ‘modest’ progress in inflation, and noted that expansion was ‘solid

o Our view is similar to the Fed’s; our call for one cut in 2024 didn’t change last week

• Meanwhile, we are sticking to our call for a July BoJ hike <sup>1</sup>

o We had expected the BoJ not to lay out a schedule for QT at this meeting

o But Governor Ueda also played down the impact of JPY weakness on underlying inflation

• Last week shows the Fed and BoJ are both willing to be patient – if in opposite directions <sup>1</sup>

## Making sense of it all

• The US inflation data was clearly risk-positive last week, but it was just one print

o And it doesn’t seem to have changed the Fed’s baseline for 2024 in a dovish direction

• In FX and fixed income markets, liquidity is getting squeezed and RV trades are having to unwind

o EMFX carry trades, which started unwinding after the Mexico elections, remain under pressure <sup>1</sup>

o Off the runs cheapened significantly this week, in the US, UK and euro area <sup>2</sup>

• Our Rates strategists now feel that with 10s below 4.25%, the US bond rally is now overdone <sup>2</sup>

o They feel markets are pricing in much lower real rates than short-run r\*; this should reverse

o Admittedly, until 7 July (French second round), flight to quality could still provide bonds a near-term bid

o But with a somewhat longer horizon, duration looks too expensive at current levels

• Most Asian and European indices dropped last week, and European credit spreads are widening

o The S&P500 and Nasdaq have been the exception, continuing to make new highs despite rising risks

o The narrowness of the rally has continued to worsen, with a handful of stocks carrying the broad index <sup>3</sup>

• We don’t see political risks easing immediately, and poor liquidity in FX and bonds is worrying

o We turn negative on risk assets after having been positive or neutral for the past several weeks
