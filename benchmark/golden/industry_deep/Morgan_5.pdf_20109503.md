## United States:

The risk-event-filled week, with multiple central bank meetings and critical economic indicators, started with a quiet session with little economic data or commentary from policymakers. Duration across the globe largely maintained the bullish momentum but traded in a narrow range as market participants awaited further guidance from monetary policy meetings, as well as economic indicators such as inflation data in the euro area and the July employment report in the US.

Market participants continue to expect the Fed to begin cutting rates in September, with market pricing indicating over 25bp of cuts at that meeting. Soft economic data continued to support this expectation on Monday. The Dallas Fed Manufacturing survey unexpectedly worsened and declined further to -17.5 (P: -15.1), driven by declines in shipments and orders, but was offset by an increase in the employment index. The soft data continued to support the rally in USTs, albeit in a small scale, as USTs traded in a narrow range.

In the NY afternoon, the US Treasury released its privately held net marketable borrowing estimates for the next two quarters. The 3Q24 estimate was revised down to \$740bn (P: \$847bn), largely due to lower Fed SOMA redemptions and a higher beginning-of-quarter TGA balance. The estimate was slightly above our strategists’ expectation of \$649bn, and the delta was driven by a lower December 2024 TGA estimate in the refunding estimate due to Treasury's expectations of fiveday outflows. Overall, the estimate did not change our strategists’ expectations for coupon auction sizes on Wednesday, and they expect the lower TGA target to result in less bill supply.

Following the Treasury announcement , the flattening of the curve accelerated slightly as the long end continued to rally, whereas 2y yields modestly edged higher. The USD strengthened 0.2% on Tuesday ahead of the FOMC meeting, supported by month-end fundamentals.

Equities were mixed on Monday ahead of earnings in the technology sector and upcoming risk events in the week ahead, but ultimately the S&P 500 rose 0.1%. The gains were led by increases in the consumer discretionary sector. Technical factors helped push oil prices lower, and Brent crude oil futures fell -1.6% to below \$80/bbl.

## Japan:

JGBs rallied across the curve in the Tokyo morning following the rally in USTs last Friday. On Friday, the MoF reported that it prefers a gradual pace of reduction in the BoJ’s JGB purchases, and there were no other critical reports that emerged over the weekend that gave further clarity on the upcoming BoJ meeting. As a result, demand to square positions likely supported the JGB rally to some extent. The BoJ did not change the scheduled JGB purchase sizes for the 3-5y, 5-10y, 10-25y, and 25y+ sectors, and the results were a bit weaker than the at-the-money levels. That said, the rally continued into the Tokyo afternoon, and the JGB curve bull-flattened; 2y JGB rallied 2.0bp to 0.39%, 10y JGB rallied 3.0bp to 1.025%, and 30y JGB rallied 3.0bp to 2.115% on a simple yield basis. The TONA-OIS curve also bull-flattened, but underperformed JGBs. XCCY basis continued to widen on broad sectors.

USD/JPY was volatile in the Tokyo morning. The pair initially climbed higher to \~154.35 ahead of the setting of the TTM rate. As month-end approached, there seemingly was demand from real money market participants to buy USD, which may have supported the increase. USD/JPY began to decline into the Tokyo afternoon and fell to \~153.05. Absent major catalysts or further clarity around the July BoJ meeting, the pair traded in a range \~153.5 into Tokyo close. Other CCY/JPY generally traded in tandem with USD/JPY, while GBP/JPY trended lower in the London morning.

## Europe:

European FX traded in tight ranges amid little data on the day and a busy schedule for the week ahead. EUR was one of the bigger movers, declining -0.3% against the USD, as market participants begin to anticipate eurozone price data, with Spain and Germany CPI data on Tuesday. Similarly, CHF weakened -0.3% ahead of Swiss CPI, although the data will not be released until Friday. GBP was down -0.1%. SEK fell - 0.1% after softer-than-expected 2Q24 Swedish GDP, while NOK gained +0.2% despite sustained weakness in oil.

## Dollar Bloc:

The USD rose broadly as USTs held up relatively well compared to government bonds in the rest of G10. AUD relatively outperformed and declined just -0.1% as compared to NZD, which fell -0.3%, with Australian inflation data expected on Wednesday as markets gauge whether or not the RBA will hike next week. CAD lost -0.1% on the day, and USD/CAD pushed past the 1.3850 mark to YTD highs. Ultimately, the DXY USD Index gained +0.2%.

## Asia:

AxJ FX exhibited mixed moves ahead of the upcoming FOMC and BoJ meetings later in the week. USD/CNH gained modestly by 0.1% to \~7.27 alongside a higher USD/ CNY fixing of 7.1316 (P: 7.1270). This supported the relative underperformance of low-yielders in the region. KRW was an exception and held steady as KOSPI rallied 1.2%. PHP and TWD were the main underperformers. USD/PHP gained 0.3% to 58.515 and approached its 50-day moving average of 58.525 alongside underperformance of Philippines equities, which fell -1.4%. USD/TWD gained 0.2% to 32.88 ahead of large dividend payments on July 31. MYR was the main outperformer in the region; USD/MYR fell 0.5% to a six-month low of 4.6363 as the local equity market recovered from Friday’s weakness and gained 0.7%. IDR also outperformed and gained 0.1%.

Unlike FX, AxJ rates rallied broadly following the move in global rates. India rates underperformed on a relative basis; 10y G-sec yields fell just 1bp to a two-year low of 6.95% even amid some expectations that G-sec demand could increase with the RBI’s new LCR requirements. In contrast to FX, Malaysia rates moved modestly, and 10y MGS fell 1bp to 3.76%, the lowest since early January. China and Indonesia rates both rallied 3bp on the 10y point of their government bond curves, and the CGB curve twist-flattened as the 1y yield rose 1bp. Singapore, Hong Kong, and Korea rates outperformed in line with their higher beta to US rates; 5y swap yields were down 7bp in Singapore and Hong Kong and 6bp in Korea, and the 1s5s curve bull-

flattened 3bp in all three markets. Thailand markets were closed amid a local holiday.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Emerging Markets

• Minutes from the June CBC Monetary Policy Meeting revealed that two members were supportive of a policy rate hike at the meeting, but ultimately backed the CBC’s decision to keep the policy rate on hold as the RRR hike and housing measures would have a tightening effect. One member noted that the April electricity tariff had a limited effect on the domestic inflation rate and that exports had been strong given growth in AI exports. The member flagged that another 12.5bp policy rate hike could add to the interest burden of firms in the industrial sectors as well as small and medium-sized enterprises, and hence supported a rate hold

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
