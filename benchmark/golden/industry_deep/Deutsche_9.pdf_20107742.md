When various sectors in the economy look at the sports M&A boom, it is easy to dismiss it as a niche ‘trending asset. We see that as a mistake. In fact, there are signals about the thematic drivers of M&A that we can pull from the sports dealmaking boom and then apply across many sectors, even as broader M&A remains supressed.

In this chartbook, we build on our recent report Sports M&A: Coming for your team (see the chartbook here). We highlight five themes that apply to all sectors but are emblematic of why sports M&A has continued to grow against the backdrop of aggressive central bank rate hikes. These include:

• Investors prioritising uncorrelated assets

• Longer time horizons are back in vogue

How the widening gap between ‘good’ and ‘bad’ operational efficiency offers synergy opportunities

• A lot of money is looking for a home

• How the in-demand 18-34 age group prioritises spending

It has been hard to adjust to the emergence of these five themes during the severe volatility of the last four years. Many businesses and investors have also ‘chosen’ not to adjust. They still hold onto the bias of “what worked last decade will eventually work again”.

When an M&A sector is ‘hot’ (such as sports right now) a bidding war can push up entry valuations. For non-sports companies, this indicates that the best returns from M&A over the coming five years will likely be generated by those firms and funds that act before the inevitable catalyst that triggers a reopening of the broader M&A market.

Given the record amount of money waiting for M&A opportunities in the wider market, we believe that once a catalyst does reopen the market, deal flow will resume more rapidly than after prior M&A downturns. In the meantime, the companies and financial sponsors that embrace the new themes that investors desire will likely generate the best returns.
