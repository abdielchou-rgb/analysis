# China and HK Equity Strategy Potential impact from withholding tax waiver

## Dividend names with higher southbound participation likely to benefit most

On 9 May, Bloomberg reported that the Chinese authorities are considering removing the 20% withholding tax currently charged on mainland investors for dividends paid by their HK listed stock holdings. While only 'individual investors' is mentioned in the news, it is likely that 'securities investment funds' would also be included in the tax exemption. Should the exemption be granted, we believe it could potentially lift HSI by up to 7%, particularly as the A-H premium currently still stands at 38%, higher than the historical average of 33%. Stocks with high dividend yield would obviously benefit, in addition we believe those with higher southbound participation, high A-H premium and higher trading volume stand to benefit more. With recent policies seemingly tilting in favour of the HK equity market and with H-shares trading at a 1.1 std below historical average (and A-shares at c.0.5 std below), we continue to prefer H-shares over A-shares.

## What's the potential upside to market?

For high dividend names (i.e. those with div yield over 6%), if we assume a 25% rerating in these stocks as a result of the taxation change, this implies a 7% upside to the HSI. The taxation changes could also bring about additional liquidity and fund inflows - our insurance team estimates that the lift to HK ADT (excluding the market impact) would be c.4% (see note). That said these positive impacts could be somewhat tempered by: 1) some southbound investors already receive the tax exemptions, 2) southbound participation may remain low for certain stocks and therefore the full benefit of the tax change may not be reflected in the share prices, 3) changes in investment thesis for the HK stocks - we note that due to investor's preference for high dividend names, dual listed high yield HK names are currently trading at a narrower discount vs their A-share counterpart than low yield names, and 4) there will always be an A-H premium - for certain stocks with higher trading volumes, such as CMB and BYD, the A-H premium is c.10%.

## Stocks that could benefit the most under this thematic

We run the following screens in respect of stocks that could benefit: 1) dual listed HK names at a significant discount to the A-share, 2) higher southbound participation as the investor base are more likely to pay for such tax change, 3) relatively higher trading volumes which typically would accommodate a lower A-H premium. Figure 5 shows the selection of stocks based on this screen while Figure 6 and 7 show the top picks from our analysts under this thematic. China Mobile, Sinopec, CPIC and BOC seemed to be the standout names from the screens as well as analysts' fundamental top picks.

## Increasingly favourable policies towards the HK equity market

Recently we have seen some favourable policy development towards the HK equity market including: 1) the five measures recently announced, e.g. the inclusion of the RMB counter for southbound investors and relaxing the eligibility of ETFs, 2) potential removal of the withholding tax, and 3) potential to reduce the eligibility criteria for southbound investors from HKD500k to HKD100k. While option 3 would be the most significant but least likely, it seems nevertheless that the policy environment is tilting more favourably towards the HK equity market.

## Valuation Method and Risk Statement

For various stocks across the industries we cover in the Hong Kong and mainland China stock markets, we use a variety of valuation approaches, including DCF models, Gordon growth model analysis and relative valuation analysis using various multiples such as PE, EV/EBITDA and P/BV.

We think the risks facing China's equities include a hard landing for the property market, a capital exodus associated with currency depreciation and slow structural reform progress. In our view, any government policies that do not adequately address these risks could result in a shock to the market. For example, an excess of stimulus policies could pose a risk to the transition from an investment-driven to a consumption-driven economy and increase the debt of government and state-owned enterprises.
