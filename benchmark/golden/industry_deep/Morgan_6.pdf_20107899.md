# Policy shift has paid off earlier than expected

Despite the ongoing market debate, we see more evidence of policy focus shift paying off earlier than expected, including moderating financial system risks and healthy export growth despite headwinds. This could continue to support the rebound in financial stocks.

Policy focus on prioritizing financial risk containment and industrial sector upgrades is paying off despite the market concerns about negative PPI: We acknowledged that this is not a easy process, given risks accumulated during years of credit-driven stimulus policies. However, we believe prioritizing risk containment – particularly in property and LGFV – while pushing industrial upgrades and tolerating some negative PPI to cushion economic downside is the better policy combo from a long-term perspective, albeit less friendly to the capital markets in the near term. Since implementation in 2021, we are seeing more and more evidence that the policy shift is gradually paying off after a couple of years of initial pain. This includes gradually moderating property and LGFV risks, continued industrial upgrades, and healthy export growth despite headwinds, which cushioned pain from financial risk containment efforts.

LGFV solution is not just about debt control but rather an effort to improve SOE asset efficiency, which is also on the right track: Our visits to six cities in China with a focus on LGFV solutions also show good progress on risk containment despite some pressure on local infra investments. Again, as we have highlighted in a series of reports, this is not only an effort to control debt but also part of the SOE efficiency improvement efforts. After years of investments with creation of tens of trillions of Rmb in assets, it is time to take a pause to review the investment returns and improve the efficiency of the assets created. This has become the priority at the local government level, with good progress already seen. Most credits in China are internal credits within the nation; thus, we believe the allocation of credit, rather than the ratio of total debt to GDP, plays a more important role in system risks and efficiency. Hence, LGFV solution, effectively focusing on rebuilding and enhancing the governance process for local government investments and SOE asset management, is more important than short-term growth and total debt control.

The process is not over yet, and it has supported a broad-based rebound in financial stocks: We see near-term pressure from risk containment lasting another 12 months. Yet meanwhile, reduced long-term risks, gradual stabilization of the property market, and continued healthy income and financial asset growth in households from industrial upgrades have reduced credit costs and supported insurance sales. This in turn has supported bank and insurer stocks. Also, once the process is completed, we see natural industrial cycles rather than the one-way deterioration feared by the market now. That could further support financial stocks.

## Policy shift paying off earlier than expected – but how did it get here?

In light of the risks accumulated during years of credit-driven stimulus policies, we believe prioritizing risk containment – particularly in property and LGFV – while pushing industrial upgrades and tolerating some negative PPI to cushion economic downside is the better policy combo from a long-term perspective, albeit less friendly to the capital markets in the near term.

After a couple of years of initial pain, we see more and more evidence that the policy shift is gradually paying off, including gradually moderating property and LGFV risks. We think the benefit of the transition will gradually increase market confidence and continue to support financial stocks.

## Pivoting back to industrial sectors has supported export growth and cushioned income growth during the LGFV and property risk containment process

Since the so-called regulatory reset in late 2020, we have been seeing a series of changes to reorient all kinds of resources, including capital and talent, and policies towards manufacturing.

• China Financials: Regulatory Reset + Industrial Policy Support – Implications for Financials (19 May 2022)

The goal, in our view, is to preserve and improve China's highly competitive decentralized industrial supply chain, as well as to push for continued upgrades and innovation, and global market share gains. This is because at the current stage of China's economic development, at slightly above US\$12,000 per capita GDP, the industrial sector could still support quicker productivity gains, and drive more broad-based income growth covering broader geographic areas in a more even and sustainable manner.

This also happened in the context of the fall in industrial contribution to GDP from 32% in 2012 to 26% in 2020.

Therefore, to achieve that end, we saw policies including:

• Encouraging bank lending support to manufacturing sectors;

• Capital market reform to drive more credit and capital to hard tech, and shift fintech development to industrial and trade finance by both government and banks;

• A reset on certain Internet and service sectors for more rational growth;

• Crackdown on after-school tutoring and education reform to support more vocational education;

• Continued fiscal support via tax cuts, in particular VAT cuts and deductions for R&D expenditure;

• Containing housing price bubbles and reducing speculative financial activities to decrease cost volatility for industrial sectors;

• Containing local government leverage to reduce the squeeze on industrial credits;

The shift of financial resources to industrial sectors is notable: we estimate that the percentage of TSF going to industrial sectors has risen from low single digits before 2020 to close to 20% in 2021 and 2022. On the other hand, the percentage of PE/VC investments and IPOs in industrial sectors has continued to rise – to 60-70% since 2020, compared to 30-50% before. Specifically, industrial medium- to long-term loan growth accelerated from mid-single-digits (%) between 2012 and 2019 to 20-30% per year between 2021 and 2023.

## Evidenced in expanding export market share...

Despite the ongoing market debate, we believe there is more and more evidence that these efforts are paying off earlier than expected. This can be seen in the rising export market share and continued upgrades in China's industrial supply chain in the past 2-3 years. Notably, of a total of 1,200+ product categories in which China exports, based on HS 1988/92 four-digit code products, China held >25% market share in 393 products in 2022, compared to 334 in 2019 and just 103 in 2001. ## ...and resilient household income and financial asset growth

As opposed to the market concerns that China may fall into a Japan-style balance sheet recession because of the reset on property and local debt, we see the progress made in industrial development as a more sustainable way to support income growth. This is evidenced in still solid household financial assets growth, at 8-9% per annum, during 2022 and 2023, despite concerns of falling property prices.

Importantly, as we first pointed out in Asset/Wealth Management: Where China's Wealth Will Go (22 Sep 2021), we estimate that unlisted equities account for roughly one-third of total household wealth. This includes over 100 million small and micro businesses, which iare a key source of income for a large number of households in China (also evidenced in <3% effective personal income tax rate because revenue from these businesses is not subject to tax). We believe that policies friendly to business formation, focus on strengthening industrial supply chains, and containing property price bubbles all served to support the millions of small business and people employed by them, and hence income generation.

In addition, according to our channel checks with payment firms, retail payment is stronger compared to official retail sales – more evidence of underestimated income resilience.

## Despite initial pain, property risk is on track to be digested

While the containment of property and LGFV risks is helping more resources to gravitate to industrial upgrades, the industrial progress made in recent years, such as the rising competitiveness of EV, batteries, and solar equipment, is helping to cushion the pain from risk digestion in property and local debt issue.

Since the property risks began to unfold in late 2021, we've argued that several factors will help keep the risks more manageable from a system perspective relative to most market concerns:

• Most of the banks' exposure remains project-based, and the incremental support encouraged by policymakers has not deviated from this principle. Therefore the focus on project completion with strict management of escrow accounts should cushion banks' losses.

• We believe banks have accumulated ample provision buffer, as extensive NPL digestion in manufacturing areas between 2016 and 2021 left room for increased digestion of property NPLs.

The peak of bank property NPL formation may be behind: Consequently, despite a rise in property NPL ratios to 4-5% and banks cumulatively digesting \~8% of property loans as NPLs in 2022 and 2023, per our estimate, the banking sector has maintained steady support to credit growth with double-digit annual loan growth, while generating stable, albeit slower, profit and dividend growth.

With progress achieved in project completion in the past two years, our field trips to different cities in early 2024 suggest that completion is no longer a major drag on property sales: In our view, this is creating a helpful environment for property supply and demand to strike a new equilibrium. From the banks' perspective, we think the industry is more than halfway through the digestion of property risk, considering cumulatively >8% of loans digested as NPLs during the current cycle. We see signs that incremental property policy, such as removing most purchase limits, is designed to help the sector bottom out to a new norm, which will also reduce market concerns about banks' asset quality.

More policies could help with the remainder of the soft landing process to avoid major NPL risks: With both property sales and property prices in many regions falling back to more sustainable levels, we see the potential for more policies to cushion the pace of sales and property price decline. These could include:

• Removal of more sales restrictions,

• A degree of property tax reduction, and

• Local government purchases n the form of social housing.

## We also see progress on improving SOE asset efficiency – key to the LGFV solution

On LGFV debt, we estimate that overall government interest burden is still manageable at 2.3% in 2023. We also see limited risks to financial institutions, because principal could still be rolled over. Effectively, our analysis shows that half of the interest payments from infrastructure projects can be covered by their own cash flows, and the rest could be better serviced in a variety of ways: improving efficiency, raising prices, swaps into government bonds, and/or lowering interest rates through negotiations or by new borrowings. Importantly, since most debt in China is domestic, we believe the allocation of credit plays a more important role in risks and efficiency rather than the ratio of total debt to GDP.

Consequently, it has become increasingly clear that LGFV solution is not just about debt control, but rather an effort to improve SOE asset efficiency. After years of investments with tens of trillions of RMB worth of assets created, we think it makes sense to take a pause to review the investment returns and improve the efficiency of the assets created.

This has been echoed in our visits to six cities in China in 2024: while we are witnessing continued slowdown in overall government-led infrastructure investments, we are also seeing more efforts from different local governments to coordinate and consolidate SOE assets with an aim to improve efficiency and utilization.

## Financial cleanup has notably strengthened the financial system, which was tested during Covid

We owe much of the financial system's resilience to the dedicated cleanup efforts that policymakers have put forth since 2016. We believe it has increased policymakers' confidence when they designed the policy shift to contain risk and support industrial upgrades in recent years.

China's financial cleanup, which started in, 2016 has addressed risks on multiple fronts in the financial system, including capital markets, industrial NPLs, shadow banking channels, large and highly levered companies, mid-sized to small banks, etc. We believe these efforts have reduced tail risks and have led to a comprehensive system to manage financial risks. The resilience of the financial system has been well tested, as seen in both our hypothetical stress test and the actual test posed by Covid, when China's GDP growth fell to \~2% twice during the pandemic.

# We believe financial stocks will continue to benefit from the success of the policy transition

In 2024, China is still in the process of digesting the property and local debt risks, which are to some extent the consequences of previous credit-driven stimulus during Covid. We believe this process will still take 12-18 months.

In the meantime, policymakers continue to push for accelerating manufacturing investments despite negative PPI. In our view, the strategy is effectively sacrificing corporate margin temporarily in exchange for upgrades in industrial capacity and potential further market share gains globally. The industrial upgrade would help ease the pain from risk digestion and benefit China in the longer term, although it will hurt corporate margins and capital market returns in the short term, and some risks from negative PPI will need to be digested later on. Once the process is completed, we see natural industrial cycles, which could be easier for the market to understand, rather than the one-way deterioration feared by the market now.

## Broad-based rebound in financial stocks driven by reduced long-term risks, still healthy credit demand, and resilient growth in household financial assets

We believe the recent rebound in financial stocks, including banks and insurers, suggests that there's more recognition of the progress in risk control and industrial upgrades. In addition, the PBOC also addressed the market debate about the fast decline in long-term treasury bond yields recently, suggesting that it expects long-term yields to rebound with economic growth and inflation. We think this should alleviate concerns of an overly low rate environment, which hads weighed on financial stocks previously.

## SOE banks to remain stable dividend plays at defensive valuations

We think SOE banks will remain attractive dividend plays as financial risk control efforts are showing better results than the market has expected: That should in turn support stable profit and dividend growth for these companies. Within SOE banks, CCB has indicated that it is adopting a more balanced approach between volume growth and margin, and will not overly sacrifice profitability in exchange for balance sheet expansion, which will support dividends in a more sustainable way in our view. This has been evidenced in the more measured loan growth of CCB in 2023 relative to other SOE banks.

## Some shareholding banks should benefit from more property downside support

We think potentially more policies to cushion the pace of property sales and price decline could help the sector to complete the soft landing process faster and avoid major NPL risks. This should reduce asset quality concerns, of which property-related assets are a major part, for banks like Minsheng, Industrial, and PAB, especially because these banks have been actively digesting property NPLs in the past 2-3 years.

In addition, we believe Industrial, Minsheng and Ningbo could continue to show an edge in serving different corporate and SME clients in industrial supply chains, helped by comprehensive service and products, a client-centric mindset which could support better pricing power. Notably, we're seeing more progress (please see here and here) on the transition in Minsheng's "operating DNA" lately. It has focused on meeting different client needs with continued improvement in financing and other product solutions, such as the loans specifically designed for cotton farmers in Northwest China. This is helping the bank to strengthen its client base and expand relationships to more mid-sized and small companies along the strategic accounts' supply chains. On the other hand, Misnheng's extensive risk digestion since 2020 should also help the bank to build more buffer to absorb future cyclicality associated with industrial credits.

## Wealth management franchise such as CMB and East Money will benefit from healthy household wealth growth over time despite near-term fee rate pressure

Despite cyclical headwinds in its wealth management franchise since 2022, we think continued growth in financial assets supported by income growth will still lead to expanding TAM for CMB over time. Higher cyclicality from the industrial sector-focused capital market, along with policymakers' encouragement for dividends and stable income streams, means that asset allocation could add more value for households in China. We see CMB leading in building this capability.

Among the brokers, we view East Money as a potentially bigger beneficiary when the equity market rebounds following industrial upturns and continued progress achieved by policy shifts. We believe East Money could accelerate market share gains in brokerage and margin finance when market conditions improve, while facing less impact than peers from tightened oversight on institutional businesses.

## Insurance

We think insurer stocks will also gain more market attention, helped by still solid business performance and a better interest rate environment. Both regional and Chinese insurers should be beneficiaries.

Regional player AIA: We believe the correction and concerns (including its buyback program, weak sentiment toward HK and China, ongoing y-y margin declines, concerns about growth in key financial metrics, and further clampdown on HK MCV business, etc.) over the past month are overdone. AIA reported a set of respectable 1Q24 results on 29 April: a strong beat with better-than-expected VNB growth, increased margin in HK and China, as well as enhanced capital management policy.

For its Chinese market, we see margin lift for both the agency and banca channels, and protection products also achieved double-digit growth in 1Q.

For its HK market, MCV business maintained strong growth momentum, 1Q24 showed further increase vs. 4Q23, and the majority of VNB came from its disciplined agency channel.

We believe AIA's key financial metrics will be back on a growth trajectory in 2024. It remains one of the most attractive stocks in our coverage.

Chinese life insurers: We continue to see improved business quality (substantial margin lift) in 1Q24 and overall insurance demand has remained strong. As for the market's biggest concern, the asset side, we believe:

• Interest rates have limited downside. The PBOC's recent comment on long-term rates being too low and gradual shift to de-emphasizing loan volume growth could also support government bond yields. Also, insurers have invested more in ultralong-term debt, sold more lower-priced interest rate products, and rearranged their commission and expense structure to reduce their overall liability costs. We believe the disturbance from low rates will gradually fade.

• Insurers have also accelerated their de-risking process to solidify their balance sheets.

We believe quality companies, including Ping An, CPIC, and China Life, will continue to benefit from these trends.
