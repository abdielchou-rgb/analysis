## Developed Markets

## United States:

Led by stocks that benefit from the adoption of artificial intelligence, the S&P 500 (+1.23%) rallied in the latter half of the week to a new record high. Amid this more conducive backdrop for risk assets, the US dollar (DXY USD Index: -0.3%) waned into the NY afternoon, while risk-sensitive G10 currencies outpaced peers. Resoundingly strong preliminary January University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment at 78.8 (C: 70.1), coupled with a decline in 1y inflation expectations to 2.9% (C: 3.1%) – the lowest since December 2020 – dovetailed with the risk asset optimism. Hawkish interpretations of comments from Chicago Fed President Goolsbee sent front-end USTs (2y: 4.39%, +3.2bp) into the red, while a long-end (30y: 4.33%, -3.7bp) rally twist flattened the curve. Curve shape lacked a distinct trend over the week: bear-steepener (Tuesday), bear-flattener (Wednesday), twiststeepener (Thursday), and twist flattener (Friday).

Remarks from Fed Governor Waller were in the spotlight this week after he referenced prospective rate cuts in 2024 but cautioned the sustainability of recent trends in data require the path of policy “to be carefully calibrated and not rushed.” He compared the Fed’s current disposition on rate cuts to prior cycles and said economic shocks which either threatened or caused a recession led the Fed to cut rates reactively, quickly, and often by large amounts. Waller believes a different starting point in this cycle – economic activity and labor markets in good shape and a gradual return of inflation to 2% – provide “no reason to move as quickly or cut as rapidly as in the past.”

This speech helped drive a distinct repricing higher in the market-implied Fed policy rate path this week. Consider at the end of last week, markets had priced in an 80% probability for the first 25bp Fed rate cut in March and almost seven full 25bp cuts this year. The probability for a March rate cut has now fallen below 50% with a little over five cuts priced in for this year.

We remain tactically neutral on UST duration, but are mindful as downside risks to inflation and the labor market arise – as possibly evident from big drop in the BLS new tenant repeat rent index (-4.74% y/y) and lack of breadth in the labor market’s resilience.

## Japan:

Despite the sell-off in USTs overnight on Thursday, JGBs initially rallied led by JGB futures after nationwide CPI for Japan continued to slow down. Although, as UST yields continued to grind higher, JGBs gradually retraced their initial strength and the curve bear-steepened. Despite the massive sell-off in the long-end, there appeared to be little demand to buy the dip; 30y JGBs sold off by 5.5bp d/d to 1.77% on a simple yield basis. Sectors inside 10y were relatively supported by reasonable demand to buy the dip. TONA OIS twist-steepened and this steepening pressure was particularly strong beyond the 10y+ sector alongside the JGB curve. XCCY basis curve tightened, led by the long-end, which retraced the recent flattening move.

From early in the Tokyo morning, CCY/JPY pairs continued to rally as the continued slowdown in nationwide CPI seemingly discouraged an earlier BoJ rate hike path. USD/JPY approached 148.80 as UST yields continued to grind higher. Other CCY/JPY pairs also rallied on a broad basis as AUD/JPY approached 97.80 amid the improved risk sentiment. However, CCY/JPY pairs reversed from upward trends, as USTs rallied from the London morning and USD/JPY subsequently fell below 148.00.

## Europe:

The London morning opened with strength in equities, which provided support to risk-sensitive currencies. NOK outperformed in the London morning, boosted by a spike in the price of Brent crude oil. The rest of G10 currencies, bar EUR, faired well against the US dollar before risk-on momentum began to falter. The notable underperformer was GBP, which displayed persistent weakness through London trading after a significant downside miss to December retail sales.

A pick-up in UST yields, seemingly spurred by comments from Chicago Fed President Goolsbee, subsequently drove G10 (ex-USD) gains off intraday highs. Risk-sensitive currencies ended the London trading with small gains against the US dollar; NOK (+0.3%) and SEK (+0.2%).

European duration rallied in the London morning amid weak UK sales and a monthly decline in German PPI before the open. Yields then sold off, in sympathy with US Treasuries likely due to comments from Chicago Fed President Goolsbee that were perceived hawkishly. Reds led the sell-off (\~7bp in OIS) and markets priced in less than 130bp of rate cuts in 2024 at the London close. In Germany, curves twist flattened on 2s10s (-5bp) while 10s30s bull-flattened \~1.5bp; the 5s10s30s fly richened. Core ASW widened (against 6m), led by Bobl ASW which richened 1bp to 44.5bp (DU/OE/RX ASW: +0.5bp). Semi-core and peripheral outperformance against core continued; the 10y BTP-Bund spread tightened by 3.5bp while the 10y OAT-Bund spread tightened by 0.5bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

G10 FX performance was mixed across the board and across the session. The DXY USD Index (-0.1%) closed the London session lower after choppy price action. In the

dollar bloc, AUD (+0.1%) posted mild gains.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies gained against USD. TWD outperformed, as USD/TWD fell 0.6%, back to its 200-day moving average of 31.4. The local equity market rallied 2.6% after positive earnings in the semiconductor space. Although the equity market strength spilled over to KOSPI (+1.3%), USD/KRW ended the session flat at 1,339. USD/CNH weakened 0.2%, back to the 7.20 level even though China equity markets resumed their underperformance. PHP was the outlier, as USD/PHP rose 0.2% to 55.9 despite the Philippines' December balance of payments at a surplus. USD/IDR was stable at 15,615 despite outperformance of IndoGBs (10y: -8bp).

AxJ rates were mixed. Besides Indonesia, Malaysia, and China rates also rallied. CNY NDIRS yields fell 1-2bp across the curve as the local equity market underperformed. Singapore, Hong Kong, and Korea rates sold off, particularly in the belly to long-end of the curve, following the overnight move in USTs. 10y swap yields rose 5-6bp in the respective three markets. Outside of these markets, which are highly correlated with USTs, India was the underperformer in the region, as 5y NDOIS yields rose 2bp.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session higher, helped by USD weakness. MXN (+0.4%) strengthened and COP (+0.8%) gained despite S&P changing its outlook on the dollar bonds to negative. CLP (+0.8%) outperformed peers, boosted by media reports the government will sell 3USDbn in 1Q24. LatAm rates ended mixed, as the TIIE curve bear-steepened (2s10s: -5bp). The IBR curve ended largely unchanged, while DIs rallied with gains concentrated in the belly despite no clear local driver (January 2031s: -7bp).

## Developed Markets

• In Japan, Nationwide CPI for December reported Japan-style Core CPI (exfresh food) eased to 2.3% y/y (C: 2.3%; P: 2.5%), which mainly reflected a decline in the contribution from energy-related items. Food inflation, which previously lifted prices, has begun to gradually settle down while nondurable consumption goods prices eased. Other items, particularly privatesector services did not show notable changes

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
