# FOMC Preview: Preaching Patience

Announcement of tapering QT is here. Lack of inflation progress this year and balance sheet details get heightened focus in the Q&A. Powell emphasizes patience amid bumpy inflation data. We continue to see a first cut in July. Our strategists stay neutral on duration.

## Key expectations

• The FOMC remains on hold at 5.375%. We expect little change to the FOMC statement but see the risk of a more hawkish press conference given Chair Powell's recent commentary on the lack of progress on inflation. Emphasis will be on remaining patient as the Fed awaits more convincing data.

• In the Q&A, Powell points to an expectation of rate cuts "at some point," but the Fed may be waiting "longer than expected" to cut rates. We expect signaling on tapering QT to come at this meeting with an announcement of the parameters and start date to begin in June.

• Our rates strategists do not expect markets to price more hawkish outcomes than current levels. Yet, they do not see a case for the Fed to move the dial towards lower yields either, until the data turn in a different direction. They stay neutral on the curve and duration.

• Our FX strategists expect USD to gain over the coming months as it offers attractive carry and US growth remains supported. However in the very short term they see USD risks skewed to the downside from the May FOMC meeting.

• On the agency MBS side, our strategists remain neutral on the mortgage basis.

## May meeting

We expect the Federal Reserve to maintain its patient stance in both the statement and Q&A. Chair Jerome Powell made it clear ahead of the blackout period that progress on inflation had stalled. In light of this, we see risks of a hawkish tilt in how the statement describes inflation progress and what would give the Fed "greater confidence" that inflation is moving towards target in the presser.

## FOMC policy statement

## Current conditions – we look for little change, such as follows:

"Recent indicators suggest that economic activity has been expanding has continued to expand at a solid pace. Job gains have remained strong, and the unemployment rate has remained low. Inflation has eased over the past year but remains elevated."

GDP continues to grow at a "solid pace." We published a fresh outlook on April 14 in which we raised our GDP growth forecast by 0.8pp to 2.4% 4Q/4Q this year and 0.7pp to 2.1% in 2025, relative to our outlook five months ago. 1Q GDP growth came in solid, with private final domestic demand ticking down only marginally from 3.3% Q/Q annualized growth in 4Q23 to 3.1% in 1Q24.

Job gains "remained strong." As of March, the three-month average of monthly net new jobs was 276k versus the six-month average of 244k. Taking on board new population data, we raised our forecast for average monthly job gains to 225k this year and 186k in 2025. We are preliminarily forecasting 250k total nonfarm payrolls in April (released May 3). Since the March meeting, the unemployment rate has ticked down to 3.8% and is likely to remain there in April (though in our forecast it is on the verge of rounding up).

Core PCE inflation has eased over the past year, but recent progress has stalled. Inflation has been volatile in the past few months, and progress has clearly slowed from the encouraging pace of late last year. The release for 1Q24 core PCE inflation came in higher than expectations at 3.73%Q (versus our 3.4%Q forecast). Assuming no revisions in

January and February, this quarterly print implies a monthly core PCE inflation rate for March of 0.48%M, well above our prior expectations (0.25%M). This raises the probability that the statement does acknowledge that recent progress has stalled.

This lack of progress has stripped away Powell’s appetite to get started as early as June, and alongside our new set of economic forecasts, we have pushed out the timing of the first cut to July and now expect a total of three cuts this year (July, November, December). Our conviction that inflation will head towards target remains high and was helped last week by fresh data on new tenant rents, which is a leading indicator for rental inflation in our models. It has slowed again and suggests an even faster pace of deceleration ahead.

Risk to the statement: Powell was very clear ahead of the blackout period that progress on inflation had stalled. As such, the "current conditions" paragraph may adopt a more hawkish tilt to match and explicitly acknowledge the lack of progress in recent months.

## Forward guidance – we see no need to alter

We think that there is no need to alter any of the language around the stance on rates. For example, we expect the FOMC to remain "highly attentive" to inflation risks, and that it will not adjust the policy rate until it "has gained greater confidence" that inflation is moving toward target.

Signal on tapering QT comes at this meeting. The FOMC held an in-depth discussion at the March meeting and was looking to slow the pace of runoff "fairly soon." We see the Fed announcing the parameters of a taper to QT in May and initiating the taper in June. QT is expected to end around February 2025, but could go on for longer. We expect the Fed to cut the pace of QT in half for Treasuries to \$30 billion a month, but leave MBS to run off with prepayments.

## Press conference

We think Chair Powell will stress that it is appropriate to give restrictive policy further time to work. He is fresh off his appearance at the IMF-WB Spring Meetings, where he expressed his latest views on monetary policy; it is unlikely that his message deviates far from the views he expressed.

In his appearance, Powell said that recent data have not increased confidence in disinflation and have in fact suggested that it will take longer to achieve that confidence. However, he said that policy is well positioned to handle the risks: if higher inflation does persist, the Fed can maintain current levels of restriction for as long as needed. However, if the labor market weakens unexpectedly, the Fed can ease.

Powell's remarks on April 16 stated that inflation data from January to March lacked convincing evidence that inflation was still making progress. As such, there has been more talk around letting policy be restrictive for longer to have enough time to cool inflationary pressures.

## The wait for additional confidence

Market context into the meeting: The May FOMC meeting comes amid a backdrop of significantly higher yields across the curve, precipitated by a unusually strong streak of data with three upside surprises in payrolls as well as core CPI prints, and upside surprise in retail sales, feeding a narrative of "re-acceleration" of the US economy. Markets have dialed down rate cuts significantly – with an 80% probability that the Fed will deliver two cuts or lower by the December FOMC meeting ( Exhibit 3 ). Importantly, the bulk of the price action has been around the CPI prints which were upside surprises ( Exhibit 4 ).

With the market pricing less than two cuts in 2024, the implied term premium with the Fed's last projection for three median cuts is now near the highest level in the last two years ( Exhibit 5 ). While there is no "dot plot" or SEP update at this meeting, markets have already gotten a lot of hawkish Fedspeak with sentiment of pushing out rate cuts, with some Fed speakers even suggesting the possibility of no rate cuts in 2024. With markets already having heard the FOMC's views after the recent uptick in core CPI, scope for further hawkish surprises at the May FOMC remains low.

On the other hand, the May FOMC meeting will come on the back of the core PCE update, and while normally core PCE doesn't add much to core CPI, the differences between those two have been more meaningful in recent months, helped by widely differing series for vehicle insurance inflation. Ultimately the core services ex-shelter series in PCE looks much more benign versus in CPI ( Exhibit 6 ), and it will be important to understand whether the Fed thinks of this as a CPI-PCE wedge issue, or a true inflation acceleration issue. Market have reacted to this as an inflation acceleration issue.

At the May FOMC, we do not expect markets to price more hawkish outcomes than current levels. Yet, we do not see a case for the FOMC to move the dial towards lower yields either until the data turn in a different direction. We head into the May FOMC neutral on the curve and duration.

## USD outlook

We continue to expect USD to gain over the coming months as it offers attractive carry and US growth remains supported. Our economists' above-market 2024 growth forecasts (2.9% versus 2.4% median Bloomberg forecast) imply that US growth momentum is likely to surprise to the upside over the coming months.

The supply-driven nature of US growth is likely to limit resulting price pressures. Risk demand is likely to remain elevated in line with regime 2 of our real yield FX framework. Regime 2 is a positive environment for trades with a positive carry profile. In particular, we recommend short EUR/USD via options, long NOK/SEK, short EUR/COP, and short CNH/ INR 3m NDF.

However, in the very short term, we think that USD risks skew to downside from the May FOMC meeting.

Market pricing for the Fed policy path has moved swiftly so far this year to remove cuts priced. Current pricing implies fewer than three cuts in the year following the first cut, down from around 150bp at the start of the year ( Exhibit 7 ).

The magnitude and pace of this Fed policy path repricing likely reflect a degree of extrapolation from recent faster-than-expected CPI prints. Particularly since 1Q core PCE rose faster than forecast, investors have focused increasingly on the possibility that

inflation re-accelerates into the end of 2025 – and that the Fed changes its tone dramatically as a result.

As a result, we see the balance of risks for USD around Chair Powell's tone as to the downside. These downside risks are reinforced by short-term USD positioning that appears stretched in a long direction ( Exhibit 8 ).

We expect little material market reaction if Powell reiterates his comments (made on April 16) indicating that recent inflation data did not represent convincing evidence of inflation deceleration.

With our economists expecting little to no change from this FOMC meeting and the market pricing in higher-for-longer rates, supply-demand dynamics remain in focus for mortgage investors.

While we recently adjusted our net issuance forecast lower for the year, Fed runoff is a source of mortgage supply that we think will continue. Our economists expect the Fed to reduce UST runoff in half to \$30 billion a month, and leave mortgages to run off beginning in June. Slower prepays have resulted in slow runoff ( Exhibit 9 ), but we would expect a slight pick-up into summer seasonals.

In addition to a lack of impact on mortgage supply, we also do not believe that UST tapering will have a direct impact on mortgage demand. There are existing beliefs around QE creating reserves, increasing deposits for banks and thus resulting in more bank demand for securities, or vice versa. However, when we compare total SOMA holdings to bank deposits over time, we do not see that relationship playing out ( Exhibit 10 ). We think that rather than UST tapering, realized rate cuts and curve steepening will have more of an impact on mortgage demand – and the outlook for those factors has shifted towards later in the year.

## Disclosure Section

Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)

Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any premium paid
