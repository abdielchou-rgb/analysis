# China Economics | Asia Pacific

## Bull vs. Bear: Summer Remains Indecisive

Growth imbalance worsened despite stable headlines: strong shipping prices on exports, but June ecommerce sales appear lackluster. PBoC improves communications while downplaying hope of rate cuts.

To bring you a variety of perspectives, we have picked several charts that consider both bull and bear arguments for China's economy in our biweekly chartbook:

• Bull arguments: Cyclical tailwinds to exports appear to be sustained in June, and we see capital goods as a medium-term structural tailwind amid supply-chain derisking. Supply chain competitiveness no longer hinges on cost advantage; instead, technological progress amid rising R&D has become the bedrock. There has also been a subtle and slightly positive development in resolving LGFV risks.

• Bear arguments: Consumption was subdued in Apr-May and could soften further amid accelerating housing price decline. Early data for "618 sales promotion" is disappointing.

Bull Arguments

## Export Strength Is Likely Sustained in June

The recent sharp rise in container shipping price, while partly reflecting geopolitical disruptions and front-loaded shipping (ahead of higher future prices), is also backed by resilient demand. Load factors remain very robust on US routes and Europe routes, per shipping companies. Nominal exports may further improve in June to 8-9%Y from 7.6% in May, partly on a low base.

![](images/ec1b0748c70742bcbd63c335f652296542c238ab466eed340a7bd1350ff15ee2.jpg)

## Capital Goods Could Support Exports amid Supply Chain De-risking

Besides renewable energy products, another somewhat under-appreciated structural tailwind to exports lies in the capital goods sector, with demand created by global supply chain de-risking and China’s own outbound investment to other emerging markets.

## Rising R&D Has Become the Bedrock of Supply Chain Competitiveness

China has developed a relatively complete industrial ecosystem on the back of a large end-market, learning-by-doing, FDI spillovers, and rising R&D. This has enabled China to climb up the value chain and sustains its market share in global trade, overcoming rising labor costs. The chart below shows China has ranked no.1 in international patent application since 2019.

## Subtle Development on Resolving LGFV Risks

Henan province will reportedly sell Rmb52bn in new LGSB to repay “existing debt” (may include LGFV debt), according to an earlier credit rating report and confirmed by local officials according to Caixin. Although the (possibly) updated report state that the new bond will fund new projects, project details are severely lacking compared with earlier batch of special bond issuance (see below).

While we are unable to confirm the usage of latest LGSB from Henan province, the latest development and the subtle details reveal more flexible use of formal government bond to swap existing debt, alleviating interest and repayment burden. This is in line with our view that central government balance sheet is critical in preserving financial stability as local governments (and housing sector) deleverage.

Bear Arguments

## Consumption Looks Sluggish in June

While May retail growth improved (3.7%Y vs. 2.3% in Apr), it likely reflects the impact of Labor Day Gold Week timing shift (May 1-5 in 2024 vs. Apr 29 – May 3 in 2023), as well as earlier month-long “618 shopping promotion”. The rebound in retail sales growth is likely transitory. Echoing this, according to Syntun (a big data service provider), GMV of 618 sales among major platforms declined by 7% versus 2023, the first YoY decline ever.

## Headline CPI Worse than Meets the Eye

While headline CPI has remained largely stable in lowflation territory in the past three months, the underlying mix is somewhat less healthy. Incremental support has been driven by supply: pork prices amid supply consolidation; vegetable prices on adverse weather (per NBS). On the other hand, core CPI has been on a downward trend since summer of 2023, except for the one-off jump due to revenge spending during Lunar New Year holiday in February.

## Home Price Headwinds Strengthened

Home price decline is not only accelerating in pace but also broadening in scope. In May, all 70 cities in NBS reported MoM price decline for existing homes, while the breadth for new home price decline was the widest since 2014. These suggest faster “market clearing” following relaxations of transaction restrictions. Declining housing prices may weigh on consumer sentiment in the near term. At a more fundamental level, while such price adjustment may be inevitable, the risk of “overshooting” – price and volume fall below reasonable levels for extended periods – needs to be reined by policy backstop, which appears insufficient for now.
