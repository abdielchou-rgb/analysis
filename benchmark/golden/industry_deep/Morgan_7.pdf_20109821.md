# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## August 12

JPY weakens in a partial reversal of prior volatility; USTs rally amid risk-off sentiment; Fed's Bowman flags upside risks to inflation; CGBs sell off after PBoC's warning; gilts and GBP remain stable; oil prices rise amid geopolitical tensions; DXY at 103.23 (-0.0%); US 10y at 3.904% (-3.6bp).

• Despite a local holiday, JPY weakens as much as 1.0% intraday in a partial reversal of the volatile carry trade unwinds from last week; USD/JPY ultimately gains 0.4%.

• USTs rally led by the belly of the curve in a data-sparse session amid a risk-off sentiment given heightened geopolitical tensions.

• Fed Governor Bowman flags upside risks to inflation as a result of geopolitical factors and persistent housing services due to increased immigration; she cautions the Fed should not overreact to a single data point, adding the rise in unemployment may be “exaggerating” labor market weakness.

• China rates underperform with 10y CGBs rising 4bp after the PBoC highlights that 10y CGBs, which have fallen to a 20-year low, have deviated from a reasonable level and contribute to accumulating financial stability risks.

• Gilts and GBP trade in a narrow range ahead of key UK data releases including employment, inflation, and growth indicators.

• A reduction in OPEC’s oil growth demand forecast due to softness in China is overshadowed by geopolitical developments in the Middle East; Brent crude oil futures rise 3.3%.

It was a similar story for US rates. While the calendar ahead includes critical data releases such as PPI, CPI, and retail sales as well as further indicators on the labor market, with jobless claims and state payroll data, Monday’s session was absent of first-tier economic releases.

In the NY morning, the July New York Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations showed that inflation expectations for the short-term (1y) and longer-term (5y) horizons were stable, but fell sharply for the 3y expectations. The 3y inflation expectation fell to 2.3% in July (P: 2.9%), the lowest level since the survey’s inception in June 2013. On the labor market, the perceived probability that the US unemployment rate will be higher one year from now, as well as the perceived probability of losing one’s job in the next 12 months, decreased. USTs largely traded in a narrow range absent major market-moving catalysts. Increasing tensions in the Middle East weighed on the market, and USTs rallied amid the geopolitical developments. Robust corporate issuance with 16 borrowers raising \$18.1bn on Monday had little impact on UST yields, and ultimately USTs rallied \~2- 5bp across the curve.

The risk-off sentiment given geopolitical developments across the globe, with heightened tensions in the Middle East as well as surrounding the Russia/Ukraine war, weighed on equities. Stocks were skittish on Monday, and eventually the S&P 500 ended the day relatively unchanged, with gains in energy and utilities offsetting some of the other losses.

OPEC lowered its forecast for growth in oil demand due to “softening expectations for China's oil demand growth in 2024.” However, the lower forecast was overshadowed by geopolitical developments as heightened tensions in the Middle East could threaten crude oil flows. Brent crude oil futures rose 3.3% to over USD 82/bbl.

## Europe:

It was a relatively quiet session in European FX amid sparse data releases. Staying true to the direction implied by yield differentials, EUR gained 0.1% against the USD during the London session. GBP remained unchanged. NOK was also unchanged despite the rally in oil prices, potentially counterbalanced by the weakness in gas. SEK, on the other hand, fell by 0.2%. CHF followed the path of JPY and fell -0.1%.

## Dollar Bloc:

AUD gained +0.2%, in line with the direction implied by yield differentials. NZD gained +0.3% despite the upcoming central bank meeting, which market pricing suggests is more likely to result in a cut than a hold. CAD and the DXY USD Index were relatively stable amid a light economic calendar.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly amid a rebound in risk appetite. USD/CNH was up 0.1% to \~7.1820 alongside the underperformance of the local equity market and a higher USD/CNY fixing of 7.1458 (P: 7.1449). Most other AxJ currencies (THB, PHP, IDR, and TWD) were also down by \~0.1-0.2% amid limited major local news. MYR and KRW were the main underperformers after outperforming in Friday’s session. USD/MYR finished the Tokyo session higher by 0.8% to 4.4577, though it still held on to most of the losses made during the carry trade unwinds. USD/KRW rose 0.5% to 1,371 as foreign equity flows were muted at -\$56mn despite the 1.2% rally in KOSPI. INR and SGD outperformed on a relative basis and stayed largely stable.

Most AxJ rates sold off. China rates underperformed the most in the region; 10y CGBs sold off 4bp, the most since late April, as the PBoC highlighted in its quarterly monetary policy report that 10y CGBs have deviated from a reasonable level and contributed to accumulating financial stability risks. Media reports also suggested several Chinese brokerages have reduced trading of CGBs since last week. 5y CNY NDIRS yield rose 4bp, with the 1s5s curve bear-steepening 1bp. In India, 10y G-secs remained steady at 6.88% while 5y INR NDOIS yield rose 2bp ahead of the July CPI data release. Singapore rates underperformed, particularly in the front end, and 2y IRS yields rose 6bp. 10y SGD IRS yield also rose 2bp, unlike in Hong Kong and Korea where 10y swap yields fell 2bp. Korea rates relatively outperformed, with front-end NDIRS yields up just 1-2bp, despite a continued increase in household loans in July, which is a concern for the BoK.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• RBA Deputy Governor Hauser acknowledged price pressures in Australia had been somewhat "stickier than expected" in prepared remarks

• Fed Governor Bowman noted that she still sees some “upside risks to inflation” due to geopolitical developments; increased immigration, which could lead to persistently high housing services inflation; and the fact that supply chains have largely normalized and improvements are unlike to continue to lower inflation moving forward. She added that she will “continue to watch the data closely…to assess the appropriate path of monetary policy.” On the labor market, she noted that the Fed should not overreact to any single data point and that the recent rise in unemployment may be “exaggerating the degree of cooling in labor markets”

## Emerging Markets

• In its 2Q24 monetary policy report, the PBoC noted its monetary policy stance is supportive of continued economic recovery. It revealed it is studying a plan to narrow the range in which short-term interest rates can fluctuate. It highlighted that the 10y CGB yield making a 20-year low near

2.2% is a clear deviation from reasonable levels and contributes to accumulating financial stability risks. It also highlighted investors should carefully assess the risk and reward of wealth management products, as some bond products are able to offer higher returns due to leverage but there can be a significant pullback in their value if market rates rise in the future

• BoK and CBT renewed their KRW 2.3tr/TRY 56bn bilateral currency swap arrangement for another three years

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
