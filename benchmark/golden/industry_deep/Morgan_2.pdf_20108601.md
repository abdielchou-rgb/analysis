ECB became the second G7 central bank to cut rates this week. The 25bp cut was widely expected, leaving market participants to react to forward guidance and updated forecasts. Inflation and growth forecasts for 2024 were revised higher than expected. Additionally, the ECB differed from the BoC in that there was a lack of forward guidance on future cuts. The hawkish elements of the meeting sent European yields higher.

Initially, USTs followed European duration, and yields climbed higher, but mixed data in the US resulted in some choppy moves in the NY morning. Both initial and continuing jobless claims were higher than expected, and at the higher end of their recent ranges without a clear driver. Final 1Q24 productivity data were mixed; nonfarm productivity was revised slightly lower, but higher than consensus expectations, whereas unit labor costs were revised lower than expected. Ultimately, the data supported solid output growth with minimal inflation pressure.

UST yields bounced around as market participants digested the data and ECB meeting, but ended the NY morning fairly unchanged. In the NY afternoon, USTs traded in a narrow range and eventually ended the day largely stable as market participants awaited Friday’s May employment situation data.

Usage of the Standing Repo Facility returned to near zero on Thursday, which confirmed the \$100mn pickup on Wednesday was likely related to a small value exercise.

Equities followed alongside UST yields and traded within tight ranges on Thursday. The S&P 500 (-0.0%) ended the day unchanged ahead of first-tier economic data that will be released on Friday.

Brent crude oil futures (+1.9%) continued to rebound after the bearish reaction to Sunday’s OPEC+ decision, as OPEC+ ministers reiterated that the group can pause or reverse production cuts, if necessary.

## Japan:

JGBs bull-flattened and the move accelerated after a strong 30y JGB auction. From the Tokyo morning, JGBs began to rally, led by the belly of the curve alongside the rally in USTs. There appeared to be decent demand to square short positions on the back of continued strength in overseas yields. Ahead of the 30y JGB auction, market participants were wondering if real money market participants such as lifers would start to accelerate their purchases of super-long JGBs, now that global yields appeared to pivot following continued weakness in US economic data. The result of the 30y JGB auction turned out to be surprisingly strong, with a price of 93.45 (C: 93.3; P: 96.1) supported by decent direct bidding. After the auction, the JGB curve strongly bull-flattened, led by the super-long sectors. By Tokyo close, 5y JGB rallied 2.5bp d/d to 0.540%, 10y JGB rallied 4.5bp d/d to 0.955%, and 30y JGB rallied 9.0bp d/d to 2.085% on a simple yield basis.

The TONA-OIS curve also bull-flattened but underperformed JGBs on the long end. XCCY basis tightened across the curve. On Friday, there will be JGB purchase operations in the 1-3y, 5-10y, and 10-25y sectors.

USD/JPY traded choppily throughout the Tokyo session. Early in the Tokyo morning,

USD/JPY steadily declined to 155.37 from 156.10 along with Wednesday’s lower UST yields, but it climbed higher after BoJ Board Member Nakamura’s speech at 10:30am JST. Nakamura’s prior remarks had leaned dovish, and he said it is appropriate to keep the policy rate unchanged. He highlighted the further room for improvement of wage growth, especially in SMEs from the aspect of disposable income growth. In the Tokyo afternoon, remarks from BoJ Governor Ueda and Nakamura sent USD/JPY higher. Ueda noted that it might be appropriate to reduce bond buying in the process of exiting large-scale monetary easing. Nakamura, on the other hand, remained neutral on cutting BoJ bond purchases, but noted that a weak JPY “is becoming a big issue.” By the London morning, USD/JPY reached \~156.35, and it seemed to us that carry-focused trades had also reemerged amid lower expectations for the BoJ’s early normalization. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY.

## Europe:

G10 FX traded in relatively tight ranges. EUR (+0.2%) rose slightly after the ECB's latest forecasts showed higher inflation and growth projections, coupled with a widely anticipated rate cut. Expected wage growth and output prices decelerated significantly in the UK decision-maker panel. EUR/GBP closed up 0.2%, and GBP lagged the rest of G10 in their gains against the dollar amid a rally in gilts.

All eyes were on the ECB on Thursday, which delivered a 25bp cut as expected. Revisions to inflation forecasts were higher than what our economists expected, and the lack of forward guidance likely weighed on European duration (for more on the ECB, read here). However, inflation fixings were up between 2bp and 4bp, which highlighted that the inflation forecast revision was partly expected by some market participants. The sell-off was limited, and the 10y Bund retraced some of the move after the press conference but closed over 3bp higher at 2.55%. In money markets, market-implied pricing indicated 4bp fewer cuts by the end of 2024, and whites/reds and reds/greens steepened. Curve-wise, 2s5s10s swap cheapened 0.5bp while 10s30s bear-flattened 0.5bp. Core ASW traded sideways; the complex closed mixed and the DU/RX box flattened. In EGBs, spreads were little changed following the French and Spanish auctions.

## Dollar Bloc:

Ahead of the US May nonfarm payrolls report on Friday, the US dollar weakened broadly. AUD (+0.3%) outperformed as global risk sentiment continued to remained positive. The DXY USD Index traded slightly above 104.00 in the NY afternoon.

## Asia:

AxJ currencies mostly rallied amid strong risk sentiment and USD weakness after Wednesday’s lower-than-expected US ADP and ISM Services data. CNH and INR were the exceptions. USD/CNH remained stable at \~7.26 as Chinese equities underperformed. USD/INR rose 0.1% to 83.48, even as equities continued to rebound (NIFTY: +0.9%) following the confirmation of Prime Minister Modi returning for a third term with support from key parties in the National Democratic Alliance. USD/IDR held stable for most of the Tokyo session and ended the session

0.1% weaker amid media reports of BI intervention in the spot and DNDF markets. High-beta currencies such as KRW and THB gained the most and rallied 0.5% and 0.3%, respectively, amid a local holiday in Korea. THB also tracked the gains in gold prices. PHP was also up by 0.3%. TWD appreciated 0.2% amid a nearly 2% surge in TAIEX and \~\$570mn of foreign equity inflows.

Similar to FX, AxJ rates rallied broadly following the Wednesday overnight rally in USTs. China and Indonesia rates underperformed on a relative basis, and remained stable both in local bonds and the CNY NDIRS curve. India G-secs gained after failing to participate in the recovery seen in INR FX and local equities on Wednesday; 10y G-secs rallied 2bp to 7.02% ahead of the planned INR 300bn government bond buyback, aided by foreign market participants who continued to buy FAR-listed G-Secs even after the election results. 5y INR NDOIS yields fell 4bp, and the 1s5s curve bull-flattened by 1bp. Hong Kong and Singapore rates outperformed given their higher correlation with USTs; for both markets, the 5y and 10y swap yields fell \~6bp and the 2s10s curves bull-flattened \~2bp. In Thailand, 1y NDOIS fell by another 2bp, and the 1s5s curve bull-flattened 1bp.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• BoJ Board Member Nakamura spoke at the regional leader meeting in Hokkaido prefecture. Nakamura’s prior remarks had leaned dovish, and he said it is appropriate to keep the policy unchanged for the time being. He cited concerns around Japanese household's consumption due to the sluggish trend in disposable income growth, and emphasized the importance of wage growth in SMEs and higher job-grade workers. At the press conference, he mentioned that at this stage, he is neutral on whether to cut the BoJ's bond purchases

• The ECB delivered the first 25bp rate cut on Thursday. As a consequence, some changes were introduced to the monetary policy statement. The rate cut, and hence moderation of the monetary policy restriction, was seen as warranted by the significant progress in disinflation. This was underpinned by easing underlying inflation measures, signs of price pressures weakening, and declining inflation expectations. Still, in continuation from the April statement, domestic inflation was highlighted as remaining high, warranting caution. The ECB revised up its forecast for GDP growth in 2024 to 0.9% y/y (P: 0.6%). 2025 was revised slightly down to 1.4%y/y (P: 1.5%), but still robust and above potential, and 2026 was left unchanged at 1.6% y/y. On inflation, ECB projections were revised upwards, even more than our economists expected. For 2024, headline was revised to 2.5% y/y (P: 2.3%) and core was revised to 2.8% y/y (P: 2.6%), higher than what data through April (the latest data available before the cut-off of the projections) suggested (for more on the ECB, read here)

## Emerging Markets

• CBC Governor Yang stated the JPY depreciation has a limited impact on Taiwan’s economy as Taiwan’s exports are mainly affected by global demand, and the similarity between Taiwan’s and Japan’s exports is low. He noted that TWD has been relatively stable compared to peers, and highlighted the importance of maintaining that stability. He attributed the TWD weakness to the USD strength. He highlighted that the prices of important consumer goods, commonly purchased items, CPI, and core CPI have all been declining since last year. He emphasized policy rate decisions are driven mainly by inflation instead of home prices, and that it is not good to surprise the markets

• BI Governor Warjiyo noted that global economic conditions are uncertain and will impact Indonesia's economy in 2024 and 2025, such as slowing global growth potentially affecting export growth. He highlighted the importance of maintaining capital inflows into Indonesia to preserve economic stability. Warjiyo also outlined five main challenges affecting growth, the exchange rate, and inflation: a slowdown in global growth, volatile commodity prices, high US interest rate policy, the strength of USD, and persistent sluggishness in global inflation rates

Our economists note the lower productivity and unit labor cost figures reflect the composition of 1Q24 GDP: strong demand but slow output growth, with a lot of the demand satisfied through inventories and imports. Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
