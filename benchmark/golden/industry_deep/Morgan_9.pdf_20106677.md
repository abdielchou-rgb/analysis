## Developed Markets

## United States:

A quiet NY session culminated with a notable bull-flattening of the US Treasury curve, in a continuation of its recent flattening momentum. Long-end strength certainly lacked a clear catalyst, considering the magnitude of the rally (30y: 4.37%, -8.6bp), but demand likely now emerged with the deluge of long-end investment grade corporate issuance in the rearview and ahead of expected large month-end rebalancing activity. Although one clear dynamic has been the incessant pain of curve steepener trades with the 2s10s, 2s30s, and 5s30s curves all at their lowest levels since December.

USD ended mixed against G10 peers, with the choppiness of the DXY USD Index (+0.0%) a microcosm of price action this week. Even though rates markets continued to imply fewer Fed rate cuts this year, strong data across the euro area and in the UK as well as equity ebullience kept the DXY USD Index in a tight range. US equities (S&P 500: +0.03%) naturally took a breather as risk was likely reduced after the intraweek surge. Both investment grade and high yield CDX spreads backed up a bit off 52-week tights, with the former likely digesting this week’s issuance that surpassed dealer estimates.

Given the degree of optimism around the artificial intelligence ecosystem and a limited economic data calendar, the micro stepped to the forefront this week. This allowed for broad gains in global equities. The S&P 500 reached a new record high and had its largest single-session gain since January 2023 on Thursday. Both investment grade and high yield CDX spreads tightened to new lows since 1Q22, prior to when the Fed commenced its current tightening cycle.

Macro implications, in this environment of strong risk appetite, translated into a further push out of implied Fed rate cuts. Forward pricing of the policy rate by the end of this year has all but converged with the December Summary of Economic Projections, which showed a median projection of three 25bp rate cuts. This helped apply pronounced flattening pressure on curves this week, with the 2s10s, 2s30s, and 5s30s curves all at their lowest levels since December.

Minutes from the January FOMC meeting memorialized the unease on the Committee around premature rate cuts. “Most participants” noted the risks of moving too quickly to ease the stance of policy, while only “a couple of participants” pointed to downside risks to the economy associated with maintaining an overly restrictive stance for too long. However, the Committee introduced new “upside risk” to both inflation and economic activity, should momentum in aggregate demand be stronger than currently assessed.

## Europe:

It was another choppy session for G10 currencies. EUR closed the London session flat after multiple rally and sell-off attempts were cut short by comments from central bank members. NOK (-0.3%) lagged alongside a sell-off in crude oil. SEK (+0.1%) gains were supported by a rally in equities. Despite deterioration in UK consumer confidence, GBP (+0.2%) managed to gain. CHF was not impacted by comments from SNB Vice President Schlegel and ended the London session little changed.

European duration continued to trade heavily early in the London session before price action reversed, likely supported by comments from ECB President Lagarde. She characterized the negotiated euro area pay data this week as “obviously encouraging numbers.” When asked about whether the ECB would cut rates before the Fed, she said, “the ECB is independent…We are determined to continue to be data dependent and independent in the assessment that we make and in the policy decision that we approve.” This comes in a context where most of the duration selloff and pricing out of ECB cuts has occurred alongside the US; ERZ4 rallied 8.5bp.

The cumulative amount of rate cuts priced in OIS for 2024 rose back to 100bp while Fed pricing was unchanged at \~80bp. In the London session, 10y Bunds rallied by \~8bp, to 2.36%. It was another volatile session for curves; 10s30s bear-flattened while 5s10s30s cheapened in the London morning. In the front-end-led rally, longend curves steepened; EUR 2s10s swap (against 6m) finished the London session - 0.7bp while 10s30s steepened by 1.5bp. Risk assets continued to perform well, which supported semi-core and peripheral spreads; the 10y BTP-Bund spread tightened \~4bp, to 144bp, a new local low.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar also displayed choppy price action, with the DXY USD Index (-0.1%) slightly lower by London close. CAD (-0.2%) weakened alongside lower crude oil prices, while AUD (+0.1%) was supported by the rally in equities. After a sell-off overnight, JPY (+0.1%) ultimately gained alongside the rally in USTs.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies broadly weakened against the US dollar but traded in a narrow range. THB (-0.8%) led the weakness, through the 36.00 level, weighed down by a largerthan-expected Thailand trade deficit. PHP (-0.3%) was also weaker, and USD/PHP

rose to 55.92. TWD (-0.2%) weakness continued to 31.56, although USD\$976.8mn in equity inflows helped erase some losses. CNH (-0.1%) weakened to 7.21 after seven consecutive sessions of gains. MYR, IDR, and INR remained relatively steady.

Most AxJ rates sold off following the rise in UST yields. Korea and Singapore underperformed the most. In Korea, the 2s10s KRW NDIRS curve bear-steepened by 2bp, while 5y KTB yields rose 5bp, to 3.44%. In Singapore, the 2s10s curve also bearsteepened by 2bp, while the swap curve shifted 2bp higher. In China, the NDIRS curve shifted 1bp higher; 2y CGB yields rose 1bp, while long-end yields remained unchanged. In Hong Kong, the swap curve bear-flattened 5bp, even though the HKMA injected HKD20mn to banks via the discount window.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session weaker; COP (-0.9%) led losses as oil prices fell more than 2%. BRL (-0.6%) sold off throughout the session amid few local developments while CLP (-0.08%) ended mostly unchanged after a retracement from session lows of 988. Rates in the region were mixed. Andean rates underperformed; the IBR curve had losses concentrated in the front end (2y: +6bp). DIs sold off marginally and ended the session \~2bp higher across the curve, while the long end of the TIIE curve outperformed (10y: -3bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• SNB Vice President Schlegel said the Swiss franc had depreciated in nominal and in real terms over the “last couple weeks.” However, he acknowledged the statement was not intended as a comment on whether CHF was over- or undervalued

• The ECB Consumer Expectations Survey for January showed inflation expectations at the 3y ahead horizon remained at 2.5% (C: 2.4%)

• ECB Governing Council Member Nagel argued “even though it may be very tempting, it is too early to cut interest rates” and “we will only receive a more detailed picture of how domestic price pressures are unfolding during the second quarter. Then we can contemplate a cut in interest rates”

• ECB Executive Board Member Schnabel said the ECB may be able to achieve a “soft landing” and “this is something we’re working on.” She added, “we need to be cautious and there are reasons for the last mile to be more difficult”

• ECB President Lagarde characterized the negotiated 4Q23 euro area wage data as “obviously encouraging;” however, “the Governing Council needs to be more confident that the disinflation process that we are observing will be sustainable and will take us to the 2% medium term target.” She explained “there are many sectors and employees that are covered by negotiations that will be completed in the course of the first quarter of 2024...I think that those numbers will — especially if they continue to be encouraging — will be important for us to assess going forward in order to reach confidence.” Asked about the possibility if the ECB would cut rates before the Fed, she replied “The ECB is independent […] We are determined to continue to be data dependent and independent in the assessment that we make and in the policy decision that we approve”

• New York Fed President Williams reiterated that rate cuts are likely "later this year” but should inflation progress stall, he would have to rethink his outlook. He added that rate hikes are not in his base case

## Emerging Markets

• BSP Deputy Governor Dakila said the central bank is still prepared to adjust interest rates as necessary amid persistent upside risks to the inflation outlook, despite inflation easing to a three-year low in January. He also noted the BSP trimmed its baseline forecast for this year to 3.6% (P: 3.7%) but kept its projection for 2025 at 3.2%. The forecast path is driven by the lower-thanexpected inflation in December and January, due to appreciation of the peso and lower global crude oil prices

• BCB Member Guillen noted the Board has kept an eye on possible wage pressures in labor markets and their effect through to services inflation. He also said the board is still committed to anchoring inflation expectations at their 3% goal. This comes ahead of next week’s IPCA-15 where the BCB hopes higher-than-expected inflation in January will not be a pattern. Markets have priced in 50bp cuts as most likely through the May meeting and then have

priced in a 68% chance for a deceleration to 25bp in June (-33bp)

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. ## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
