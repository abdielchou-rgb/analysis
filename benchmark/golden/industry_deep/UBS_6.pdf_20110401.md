# US Equity Derivatives Strategy Say that five times fast: The five P's driving volatility – Policy, Politics, Probability, Profitability, Positioning

We introduce a conceptual framework to determine the key drivers of equity volatility: The five P's. We weigh the trade-offs between each factor, and determine what could contribute more or less to volatility on a go-forward basis –

## Policy: 25 or 50? The debate may rage on, but sensitivity to said debate could decline

The Fed cut 50bps – November/December FOMC remain live in terms of whether we will see another 50bp cut in either, but we think uncertainty around this will decline relative to levels we witnessed heading into the September meeting. Monetary policy has unsurprisingly been one of the key drivers of equity volatility recently, but we think this could subside relative to the below factors with our US Economists forecasting 25bp cuts per meeting through year-end.

## Politics: Deviating candidate odds reducing election uncertainty, but implied moves suggest otherwise

We expect US election-related sensitivities to increase post-September FOMC – November FOMC is on 07-Nov; US elections are on 05-Nov, thus the latter could naturally take precedence from an event risk premia standpoint. Unless the data deteriorates considerably, we think US elections will start to be more at the forefront. Rising S&P 500 one-day implied move for US elections, along with risk premia still bubbling in election-sensitive sectors, suggest the same.

## Probability: Goldilocks rising again, but so is risk of overheating

Shifting macroeconomic regime probabilities has been one of the largest drivers of market volatility YTD, per our framework. Goldilocks probability has since risen again. We previously highlighted sectors that we felt were underpriced for a 'good is good' Goldilocks regime coming to fruition – we are starting to see this play out. The risk that we think may be underappreciated is the potential return of economic overheating, which could catch market participants offsides with many focused on scenarios involving a growth slowdown.

## Profitability: Earnings momentum has started to decline in aggregate, but Tech + and Defensives holding up

Earnings momentum contribution to equity volatility is at YTD highs. This jives with AIrelated fears starting to brew through Q2 reporting season, a key driver of rising intraindex correlations, in our view. We expect sensitivities around the AI narrative and Defensives rotation to increase heading into Q3 reporting, particularly with Tech+ and Defensive sectors carrying the earnings load.

## Positioning: Discretionary + systematic flows a secondary driver

Our framework suggests positioning has had more of a 'second-order' effect on volatility YTD – dog wagging the tail, so to speak. Both discretionary and systematic flows have contributed to higher/lower volatility at certain points this year (e.g. post-May/June FOMC), but typically because other 'P's shifted notably. Both our US composite positioning model and aggregate measure of CFTC, Volatility Control, and CTA suggest positioning is currently closer to neutral/underweight, with upside risks if the market rallies further and volatility continues to normalize.
