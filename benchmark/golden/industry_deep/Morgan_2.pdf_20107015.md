## Revisiting Our Inflation/Central Bank Path

• We think policy is restrictive: Slower growth, tighter credit and falling inflation should give the Fed/ECB confidence that policy is sufficiently restrictive. Real policy rates stay positive. Uncertainty around the ‘lag of tightening strengthens the case to cut before inflation hits target.

Tapering QT: We think the Fed likely announces parameters in May and starts tapering QT in June. We see a ‘slow taper’, with QT ending in early 2025 with reserves \~\$3.2 trillion.

## IG Curves – Expect Twist Steepening – Front End Tighter, Long End Wider

The demand-supply at the long end is normalizing as 30Y supply returns, M&A picks up, and yield-buyer demand stabilizes. We think long-end spreads are too rich and expect steepening out the curve. On the other hand, shorter tenors are still well off the post-Covid tights and have further room to compress. Carry and roll-down drive our preference for the 3-5Y IG bucket.

## Key Takeaways

• We think credit fundamentals and technicals are ‘better than average’, supporting ‘richer than average’ spreads (for now): We’ve taken our global end-2024 spread targets tighter to reflect this, and better align with yearto-date developments and our current recommendations.

• Richness varies: European IG, US 3-5yr, CCCs, and leveraged loans are all ‘major’ sub-sectors where spreads are not particularly expensive relative to the last 20 years. We continue to like them all. In the US, we expect a twistflattening (3-5yr tighter, 10yr+ wider).

• Loans could tactically benefit from ‘stickier’ inflation: CPI has been ‘sticky’ year to date. While we expect the Fed and ECB to cut in June, the high current yield on leveraged loans (\~9.3%) could attract flows if data remain stronger, and central banks cut later or less.

• The return of M&A: We’re forecasting a ‘return of M&A’, with expectations that volumes will rise 50% versus 2023. Low bond prices make this story especially interesting in credit.

• Retail credit: The consumer and corporate action: Retail credit sits at the intersection of consumer health and corporate activity. We discuss our latest views and best ideas on both.
