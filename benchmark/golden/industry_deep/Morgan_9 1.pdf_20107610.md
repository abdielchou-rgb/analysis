# Global Macro Commentary | Global

## April 15

Renewed escalation risks bear-steepen Tsy curve; USD gains as equities pivot lower; European rates bear-steepen; AUD/NZD rises on RBNZ neutral OCR estimate; ECB's Simkus talks pace of cuts; BRL lags peers on lower spending target; DXY at 106.21 (+0.2%); US 10y at 4.601% (+8.0bp).

• USTs open cheaper after the geopolitical events this weekend, losses deepen on robust US retail sales. The front-end begins to outperform and the curve bear-steepens on risks of further escalation.

• Late session US dollar strength occurs alongside a deterioration in risk sentiment, as US equities confront geopolitical risks, earnings volatility, and seasonal headwinds around tax payments.

• European duration bear-steepens, peripheral spreads widen as riskoff sentiment unfolds.

• AUD/NZD (+0.2%) rises as New Zealand government bonds bullsteepen after the RBNZ outlines neutral Official Cash Rate of 3.9%.

• ECB Governing Council Member Simkus believes “there is a more than 50% probability that there will be more than three rate cuts this year” and “three rate cuts is a conservative estimate.”

• BRL (-1.3%) underperforms peers, in part due to a change in the government’s official primary budget target for 2025 to 0% (P: +0.5%).

## United States:

An about-face in perceptions of geopolitical risks in the Middle East was a clear point of demarcation across macro markets. Provided the perception that events in the Middle East over the weekend had a plausible path toward de-escalation, macro markets initially retraced the acute flight-to-quality moves to end the prior week.

Brent crude oil futures moved lower in the overnight session and made a clear break below \$90/bbl during the London morning. S&P 500 E-Minis opened higher overnight and sustained gains throughout the London session, just shy of 5,200. Risk-sensitive currencies staged slight gains against safe-haven currencies, with the US dollar and JPY weaker after outperformance on Friday. Gold prices opened modestly above Friday’s closing level, that was only reached after a precipitous drop in the NY afternoon. Treasuries opened cheaper across the curve as the geopolitical risk premium which drove the rally on Friday receded.

The sell-off in Treasuries extended to session lows early in the NY morning alongside robust US retail sales data. Around 10:30am ET, the curve began to diverge and bear-steepen more distinctly alongside media reports which suggested risks of further escalation in geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

The front-end of the Treasury curve began to relatively outperform as risk of geopolitical escalation pulled back some of the rate cuts that had been priced out earlier; 5s30s steepened \~7bp intraday. The US dollar turned stronger swiftly, and progressively gained alongside the deterioration in risk sentiment. Broad US equity (S&P 500: -1.20%) losses came amidst geopolitical risks, earnings volatility, and seasonal headwinds around tax payments. Brent crude oil futures (-0.4%) pared prior declines to close at \$90/bbl while gold prices rallied late in NY trading to close near session highs at \$2,383/ozt.

Funding markets were in focus as cash has begun to move in earnest for mid-April tax payments (for more on the implications to key Fed balance sheet liabilities, read here). Outflows from money market funds (MMFs) have accelerated for both institutional (-\$77bn WoW) and retail (-\$19bn WoW) funds, with total MMF AUM (\$6.407tn) now only \$10bn higher quarter-to-date, per Crane Data.

This has led to the anticipated lower balances at the Federal Reserve’s ON RRP facility, given MMFs comprise over 90% of total usage of the facility. Balances at the ON RRP fell by \$48.0bn on Friday and fell a further \$80.3bn on Monday to \$327.1bn – the lowest level since May 2021. As of April 12, the Treasury General Account (TGA) rose to \$725.03bn, up \$57.31bn from April 11. Corporate income tax receipts (\$26.7bn) as well as non-withheld individual electronic tax receipts (\$30.3bn) were the primary drivers behind the increase in the TGA.

## Japan:

JGBs bull-steepened from the Tokyo open amidst the risk-off move, following heightened geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. Although, JGBs sold off into the 3:00pm Tokyo close, as USD/JPY ground higher, seemingly without further escalation in geopolitical risks. While the belly of the curve traded relatively firmly, long-end JGBs lagged but there appeared to be some demand to buy the dip into the 3:00pm Tokyo close. 5y JGBs rallied by 1.0bp d/d to 0.48% on a simple yield basis while 30y JGBs were unchanged at 1.91%.

TONA OIS rallied on a broad basis, especially in tenors beyond 5y. There seemed to be decent demand to square short positions given the uncertainty around geopolitical developments. As a result, ASW spreads continued to cheapen across the curve. XCCY basis widened across the curve, due to the risk-off sentiment.

Seemingly without further escalation in geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, risk markets gradually retraced losses from Friday. Accordingly, JPY sold off broadly while risk-sensitive currencies such as SEK were well supported; USD/JPY ground higher toward 154.00. Japanese Ministry of Finance officials said they are monitoring FX moves closely and had contacted FX officials abroad recently. While USD/JPY failed to break 154.00 at the time of writing, a media report that suggested the BoJ will focus more on incoming data rather than the inflation outlook was seemingly interpreted dovishly and prevented USD/JPY from meaningfully correcting. Into the London morning, USD/JPY hovered \~153.80.

## Europe:

It was a choppy session in G10 FX, with more strong US data and further geopolitical risks in the Middle East. Many currencies settled close to their opening levels, with muted performance among risk-sensitive currencies. JPY was the biggest mover, 0.7% weaker against the US dollar as USD/JPY rallied comfortably through 153 and 154 to end the London session at 154.24.

NOK (-0.5%) weakened alongside the decline in Brent crude oil prices. GBP and SEK were flat for the London session after a prior drift higher in US equities reversed in the London afternoon. EUR (-0.1%) weakened against the US dollar, with the DXY USD Index +0.1%) little changed.

European duration opened lower and reversed the move from Friday. The sell-off accelerated, led by the US after US retail sales were stronger than expected, with upward revisions to prior months (10y Bunds: +8bp to 2.44%). Curves bearsteepened and left 2s10s 3bp steeper in swaps while 10s30s closed unchanged. In money markets, greens led the sell-off (+10bp) and reds/greens steepened. Initially, BTPs were little changed while ASW tightened from the open, a sign risk sentiment improved. In the London afternoon, a renewed escalation in geopolitical tensions in the Middle East widened peripheral spreads and richened core ASW. Overall, the 10y BTP-Bund spread closed \~4bp wider and the core ASW complex \~0.5bp cheaper.

## Dollar Bloc:

CAD (+0.1%) gained, despite a sell-off in Brent crude oil prices. AUD/NZD ground higher, as AUD (-0.1%) relatively outperformed NZD (-0.4%). Front-end rates in New Zealand repriced down toward the RBNZ’s communicated 3.9% neutral rate.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly amid risk-off sentiment due to the escalation in geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. CNH was the exception; USD/CNH fell 0.1% to 7.2580, aided by a continued stable USD/CNY fixing, outperformance of the local equity market (CSI 300: +2.1%), and tight offshore liquidity; 1m CNH HIBOR rose 4bp to 4.63%, the highest level since October 2023. The PBoC also net drained CNY70bn in medium-term onshore liquidity via the MLF. KRW led regional currencies lower, down 0.6% to 1,384 despite verbal intervention from BoK. PHP declined 0.5% to 56.82 alongside the slide in local equities (-1.5%). TWD was

weighed down by underperformance of local equities (TAIEX: -1.4%) and US\$827mn in foreign equity outflows; USD/TWD rose 0.3% to 32.38. THB weakened 0.3%, with the weakness cushioned by the rise in gold prices. MYR fell 0.2% to 4.78 as BNM stated it stands ready to maintain orderly financial markets. Indonesia markets were closed.

AxJ rates exhibited mixed moves. Thailand rates led losses, with 3y and 10y ThaiGB yields 14bp and 10bp higher, respectively. Korea rates also underperformed and sold off 2-3bp across the curve. The 2s10s KRW NDIRS curve bear-flattened by 1bp. In China, 1y CNY NDIRS yield rose 2bp and the 1s5s NDIRS curve bear-flattened 2bp with the liquidity withdrawal from the PBoC. 2y CGB yields rose 2bp to 1.87%, although long-term CGB yields were largely steady ahead of multiple economic data releases on Tuesday. Singapore and India outperformed peers. Singapore yields fell 1-2bp across the IRS curve; the 2s10s curve bull-flattened 1bp, after the decline in UST yields on Friday. The INR NDOIS curve shifted \~1bp lower while G-sec yields were stable.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies weakened as risk-on sentiment prevailed following higher than expected US retail sales. CLP (-1.0%) and COP (-1.4%) were weaker with few local drivers while BRL (-1.3%) performed worse than most peers, in part driven by a change in the government’s official primary budget target for 2025 to 0% (P: +0.5%), most participants expect a 2025 realized primary balance of -0.6%. Rates in the region ended higher, largely because of higher UST yields. TIIEs had losses concentrated in the belly (5y: +17bp) and the IBR curve was \~18bp higher along the belly and long-end. DIs underperformed peers, with the January 2030 contracts +19bp.

## Central Bank Monitor

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
