## Bond News

[Euronext Dublin, London Stock Exchange, Bloomberg Finance L.P.] ABS

GTR 2020-1: The Issuer has encountered challenges in delivering correspondence to the Servicer, as outlined in the notice dated 12 December 2023, despite multiple attempts. This delivery failure is a source of concern for the Issuer and in light of this and considering the portfolio<sup>’</sup>s performance deterioration and media coverage, the Issuer plans to persist in seeking dialogue with the Servicer and will closely monitor the situation. This monitoring will focus on servicing arrangements and the Issuer<sup>’</sup>s rights under the Servicing Agreement.

PVLLN 2021-1: The Servicer has identified that between 31 January 2022 and 18 December 2023 in respect of 19,519 Purchased Receivables, it has written off balances owed by the relevant Customer in an aggregate amount of c.£139.3K. Such write-offs are related to final repayments due by Customers of £1 or more and less than or equal to £10 each. The Servicer intends to remedy these write-offs by making a payment to the Issuer in an amount equal to the aggregate Write-Off Amount. Payment of the Write-Off Amount will be identified in the Servicer Report delivered in respect of the January 2024 Collection Period.

## CMBS

EURO 33: The Senior Borrower has requested a 12-month extension to the Senior Loan Maturity Date until 20 January 2025. In light of this, the Senior Borrower and Servicer have agreed several amendments, including increasing the Senior Loan Margin by 0.35% per annum for the extension period (20 January 2024 to 20 January 2025); implementing a one-time senior loan maturity fee of 0.25% of the outstanding principal amount of the Senior Loan; purchasing hedging for 100% of the Senior Loan at a strike rate of 2.5% per annum for the extension period; prohibiting the Senior Borrower from making equity distributions, repaying subordinated debt, or voluntarily prepaying the Mezzanine Facility from funds available in its General Account; extending Cash Trap covenants; disapplying the Senior Borrower<sup>’</sup>s ability for a Permitted Change of Control; and enhancing reporting obligations. These amendments, collectively known as the “Senior Loan Amendments<sup>”</sup>, align with the Senior Obligors<sup>’</sup> plan to recapitalize the Property by sale or refinancing before the extended maturity date. The Servicer, deeming this consistent with the Servicing Standard, directed the Senior Loan Facility Agent to execute an amendment deed effecting the Amendments, as of 15 December 2023. Modification and legal fees will be covered by the Senior Obligors or a related entity outside the Senior Loan structure.

EURO 35: Following the sale of the Ravenswade 3 and Wijde Wade 3 property (release price - c.€2.1mm) and the Bijster property (release price - c.€2.8mm), the Securitised Senior Loan was fully redeemed on 5 December 2023, by an amount equal to c.€4.2mm. The Senior Loan Security has been released, and the amount of the prepayment will be applied against the Notes which will be fully repaid at the Note Payment Date on 23 January 2024.

FROSN 2018-1: On 13 December 2023, a series of Extraordinary Resolutions were passed by Noteholders of each Class of Notes via Written Resolution, approving several Basic Terms Modifications. These modifications involve extending the Senior Loan Maturity Date by an initial two years, with one additional year of possible extension upon meeting specific conditions, through to 15 February 2027. In addition, the Final Note Maturity date has also been extended to 21 May 2031. Subsequently, on 15 December 2023, various agreements and deeds, including the SFA Deed of Amendment, ICA Deed of Amendment, Margin Letter, Guarantee, Obligor Novation Agreement, CMBS Deed of Amendment, and Supplemental Trust Deed, were executed. Additionally, Sponda Mezza Holding C Oy, a parent entity of the Senior Borrower, granted certain contingent value rights to Noteholders in connection with these Extensions and Amendments via the CVR Deed .

HWAYS 2021-1: On 15 September 2023, Freehold Bidco Limited submitted a written request to CBRE Loan Services Limited (the Servicer) seeking an extension of the Loan Initial Repayment Date under the Senior Facility Agreement to the Loan First Extended Repayment Date. The Servicer, upon receiving hedging documentation, confirmed the renewal of the Hedging Agreement until 16 December 2024 (the Loan First Extended Repayment Date). With no existing default and no anticipated default resulting from the extension, the Loan Initial Repayment Date has been successfully extended to the Loan First Extended

Repayment Date.

WINDM X: The Issuer has requested that the Notes continue to be listed for a further period of 12 months from and including the date of expiry of the previous listing extension (being 8 April 2023) or until such earlier time as Euronext Dublin is notified by the Issuer that the Notes have been finally redeemed and/or otherwise discharged and an application is made by the Issuer to delist the Notes.

## Negative rating actions

## ABS

Moody<sup>’</sup>s downgraded Classes E and F of the UK Auto ABS, DWSON 2022-1, to B1 and Caa3, respectively, from Ba2 and B3, previously, reflecting worse-thanexpected performance of the transaction.

## CMBS

DBRS placed Classes A, B, C and D of the UK CMBS, EURO 33, under review with negative implications, reflecting the reduction in the tail period to four years from five years following the senior loan amendments that came into effect on 15 December 2023. The senior borrower had requested an additional 12 months maturity extension to 20 January 2025, to which the servicer agreed in December 2023, including related additional amendments. The final maturity date of the notes remains unchanged at 23 January 2029, thus reducing the tail period between the loan maturity and the final note maturity to four years from five years. A shorter tail period may not be sufficient time to work out the loan, if necessary, and repay the bondholders. The rating agency will undertake a thorough review of the loan performance and amendments that were put into effect, along with the extension , in order to resolve the negative watch placement of the notes.

## Explanation of Credit Research Valuation Methodology, Ratings and Risk to Ratings:

J.P. Morgan uses a bond-level rating system that incorporates valuations (relative value) and our fundamental view on the security. Our fundamental credit view of an issuer is based on the company's underlying credit trends, overall creditworthiness and our opinion on whether the issuer will be able to service its debt obligations when they become due and payable. We analyze, among other things, the company's cash flow capacity and trends and standard credit ratios, such as gross and net leverage, interest coverage and liquidity ratios. We also analyze profitability, capitalization and asset quality, among other variables, when assessing financials. Analysts also rate the issuer, based on the rating of the benchmark or representative security. Unless we specify a different recommendation for the company<sup>’</sup>s individual securities, an issuer recommendation applies to all of the bonds at the same level of the issuer<sup>’</sup>s capital structure. We may also rate certain loans and preferred securities, as applicable. This report also sets out within it the material underlying assumptions used. We use the following ratings for bonds (issues), issuers, loans, and preferred securities: Overweight (over the next three months, the recommended risk position is expected to outperform the relevant index, sector, or benchmark); Neutral (over the next three months, the recommended risk position is expected to perform in line with the relevant index, sector, or benchmark); and Underweight (over the next three months, the recommended risk position is expected to underperform the relevant index, sector, or benchmark). J.P. Morgan Emerging Markets Sovereign Research uses Marketweight, which is equivalent to Neutral. NR is Not Rated. In this case, J.P. Morgan has removed the rating for this particular security or issuer because of either a lack of a sufficient fundamental basis or for legal, regulatory or policy reasons. The previous rating no longer should be relied upon. An NR designation is not a recommendation or a rating. NC is Not Covered. An NC designation is not a rating or a recommendation. For CDS, we use the following rating system: Long Risk (over the next three months, the credit return on the recommended position is expected to exceed the relevant index, sector or benchmark); Neutral (over the next three months, the credit return on the recommended position is expected to match the relevant index, sector or benchmark); and Short Risk (over the next three months, the credit return on the recommended position is expected to underperform the relevant index, sector or benchmark).
