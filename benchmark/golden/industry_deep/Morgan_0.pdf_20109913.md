## 1Q FY25 Earnings Dissector: Upgrade Staples, downgrade Autos

1Q results were lackluster, with a moderate uptick in revenue growth and margin expansion. Lower input cost benefit is largely getting over as we anticipated in the previous quarter (link). Factors such as limited government expenditure, a relatively higher base in the previous year, demand fluctuations and rising competition were also a key drag. Domestic cyclical segments fared well compared to global cyclicals. For majority of the sectors, the focus is now shifting towards business prospects, top line guidance, balance sheet strength and other operational efficiencies. Near term, the market will remain range-bound due to rising domestic headwinds and global market volatility, in our view. We recommend a barbell strategy, playing defensive story through consumer staples and healthcare from tactical standpoint. Then on the other side of the barbell, we continue to prefer longer-term capex story via Industrials, Real Estate and Financials sector.

Nifty 50’s ex-financials revenue/PAT came in at +6%/+4% YoY and EBITDA margin expansion was +72bps. Nifty 50’s Beat/Miss came at 20%/24% (using Bloomberg consensus estimates for the non-covered companies). MSCI India ex financials companies’ revenue/PAT grew at +7%/-4% YoY with EBITDA margins expanding by 52bps, leading to an overall operating margin of 22%. MSCI India’s Beat/Miss came at 32%/27%. Within MSCI India universe, Real Estate, Communication Services and Industrials had the most beats (>+5% of estimates), whereas Energy had the most misses (<-5% of estimates). See pages 4-5 for detailed sector earnings review.

● Over the last one month, MSCI India consensus earnings expectations for 2024/25 were marginally revised down by 0.5% each. A higher number of companies in Communication Services, Financials and Real Estate had earnings revised upwards. For FY25, Lupin, Godrej Properties and Adani Green witnessed double-digit positive earnings revisions (Table 2). Overall for 2024 consensus earnings estimates, Communication Services, Real Estate, Insurance and Industrials had >1% upward revisions, while Energy and Materials had negative revisions of <-1% (Table 1). MSCI India consensus earnings growth estimate for CY24/25 is 10%/17% respectively.

## 1QFY25 Earnings Review

● Oil & Gas companies. Q1 was weak for most OMCs due to weaker GRM and also qoq lower auto-fuel marketing margins. OMCs booked under-recovery on the sale of domestic cooking gas LPG at government-controlled rates. While RIL’s 1Q EBITDA was slightly lower than expected, though the bottom-line fared worse on higher interest/lower other income. A sharp decline in refining margins was to blame (along with lower E&P).

● 1Q earnings across Indian Financials were marked by EPS downgrades across lenders, driven by upward normalization of credit costs (lower recoveries, unsecured credit delinquencies) and deposit growth challenges. NIM degradation continued, though it was factored into earnings. Deposit cost increases are notably moderating. Banks have started pushing on the opex lever to maintain target ROAs and we expect this trend to sustain through FY25. On loan book, a notable pick-up is seen in mortgage and corporate loans with slowdown in unsecured. CASA growth continues to be a challenge on incremental spreads. Looking ahead, NIM challenge and credit normalization upward will imply pressure on ROA which can be partly offset against opex. Impact of new investment norms (AFS reserves) and positive accretion to book was largely washed out by the market. With ROAs having peaked, growth differentiation is likely to drive valuations. ICICI/KMB/SBIN across large banks have done relatively better on growth. We continue to stick with our two thematics “capex/housing over consumption” and “saver to investor”. Top picks are ICICI/KMB/SBIN/SHFL/CIFC/HDFCAMC (link).

Overall IT companies‘ earnings were a mixed bag. While the discretionary spending still remains weak, the management commentary indicate a stable (if not a deteriorating) demand environment. Although we saw some positive cues from a few companies like Infosys raising guidance and TCS expecting lesser deal cancellations. Discretionary spending still remains weak but some greenshoots were seen in BFSI. The Health Care sector showed resilient earnings growth aided by improvement in EBITDA margins by 338bps. There was a decent YoY revenue growth(+11%) and a solid PAT growth (+21% YoY). For Pharma, Q1 is a seasonally soft quarter due to the lower incidence of medical cases and elective procedures (due to holidays). Margins are also comparatively lower due to negative operating leverage and impact of annual salary increments.

● Autos posted strong earnings results as we had expected, driven by a combination of revenue growth of and margin expansion. Operating leverage benefits, better product mix, healthy volume growth across most segments and lower raw material cost largely drove the growth for the Auto sector in 1QFY25. Margins going ahead are expected to be under pressure as this was probably the last quarter of a favourable base. Consumer Staples’ aggregated 1QFY25 revenue in MSCI India rose by +9%/+7% YoY with EBITDA margin of 23%. FMCG companies witnessed a recovery in volumes, partly due to higher demand for summer products owing to heatwave in many parts of the country. There was an optimistic management tone on a rural demand recovery in FY25 owing to good monsoon expectation and contained inflation.

● Strong profits growth in capex-heavy sectors show that India’s large industrial companies are in good shape (MSCI India revenue/PAT growth +15%/+25% YoY). Companies are optimistic about the near to medium-term business outlook with the enquiry level strong across industries. Order inflows for the quarter were strong. This offers hope for private capex to regain momentum as and when capacity

utilization peaks. Hindalco and Vedanta (base metals) reported strong performance in Aluminum segments driven by the LME price rally and EBITDA came in higher than JPMe. The 1Q prints from steelmakers was mixed with TATA reporting a slight beat, JSTL EBITDA largely in line with JPMe and SAIL reporting a miss on EBITDA due to lower than expected volumes. Going forward in 2QFY25, outlook remains a bit soft as both LME prices and domestic steel prices have seen a QoQ decline.

India’s life insurers reported a new business value (NBV) growth of 15% Y/Y in 1Q25 on average. This was largely on the back of 25% Y/Y APE growth, which was partially offset by decline in NBV margins (-2.2%p Y/Y on average). As the equity markets remained strong, the APE growth was largely supported by strong ULIP sales. For non-lifers, the underwriting performance was mixed as elevated claims level were offset by lowered expense ratio.

Nifty Midcap 100 ex-financials revenue/PAT came in at +10%/-2% YoY, and EBITDA margin expansion was +59bps. Nifty Smallcap 100 ex-financials revenue/ PAT came in at +10%/+14% YoY, and EBITDA margin expansion was +42bps.

## Upgrade Consumer Staples from Neutral to Overweight

From a near term and tactical perspective, we believe Consumer Staples offer defensive exposure amidst rising headwinds. Consumer Staples sector rallied 9% over the last 3 month but continue to underperform MSCI India by 12% YTD. FMCG companies witnessed a recovery in volumes, partly due to higher demand for summer products owing to heatwave in many parts of the country.

Note, rural FMCG volume growth outpaced urban growth for the first time in Q1CY24 (per Nielsen) and we came across the ‘green shoots emerging in rural’ narrative across companies. There was an optimistic management tone on a rural demand recovery in FY25 owing to good monsoon expectation and contained inflation. India recorded 9- 10% above normal rainfall in July, offsetting June’s deficit and Kharif crop sowing (+3% y/y) data has been steady further supporting agri income growth expectations.

This bodes well for the sector, coupled with upcoming festive phase of the year. Of course a weak base is also helping – more so for HPC vs. Foods. Volume growth is expected to gradually improve from low single digit to mid-high single digit across companies. Also government’s focus on employment generation in the Union Budget as well as tweaking of tax slabs (in the New Tax regime) augurs well for people in the lower income group – this supports consumption on the margin.

Despite recent gains, companies in consumer staples offers pocket of value in an increasingly expensive Indian equity markets. However, FMCG sector continue to remain under-owned, both by foreigners as well as the domestic investors. Our preferred picks include HUVR, ITC, Nestle India, Marico and UBBL.

## Downgrade Autos from Overweight to Neutral

We are booking profits in our OW Auto sector call, post significant outperformance over the last 2-3 years vs. the broader market (Figure 21). Near term, we believe sector could witness consolidation given the higher base of the previous year and expensive valuation. 1QFY25 was probably the last quarter with a favourable base and while commodities have begun to rise, the impact is likely to be felt with a lag in 2Q (assuming no pricing actions).

Also auto sales enter slow lane. Auto wholesales for July 2024 were a mixed bag with 2Ws (+c15% YoY) and Tractors (+5% YoY) growing faster than PVs (-c.2% YoY) and CVs (-c.13% YoY). Retails, on the other hand were better for most categories barring tractors (-13% YoY). For the first time after many months, Maruti Suzuki India Limited's SUV segment has experienced a decline of 9.3%. This is coupled with continued poor performance in the compact segment, which is down by 12.3%. We are seeing moderation of demand outlook from PV and CV, while the 2W continues to show positive demand outlook (Figure 18Domestic 2Walgrwh v.PV ). Decline in sales growth of passenger vehicles has dampened demand for auto loans (-15%oya) in June 2024.

Domestic wholesale growth for Bajaj Auto (+19% YoY) beat the industry while retails underperformed (+10% YoY) in July. Honda remained the standout yet again with strong wholesales (+41% YoY) as well as retails (+23% YoY). There has already been an upswing in domestic two-wheeler sales over the past few months and increase in rural spending in the run-up to the elections is expected to key driver of demand for scooters and motorcycles. Sustenance of the recovery in rural demand, higher minimum support prices for agricultural produce, removal of export banks on agricultural commodities, a good festive and the wedding season, new model launches, and rising consumer confidence auger well for two-wheeler demand. Hence, it will be prudent to have stock specific approach and creating alpha well within the sector. Our preferred picks are Bajaj Auto, Tata Motors and Samvardhana Motherson.
