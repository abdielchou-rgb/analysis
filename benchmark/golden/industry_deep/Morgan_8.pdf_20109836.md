# July credit data continue to show impact of reduced window guidance

July TSF growth recovered slightly to 8.2%, with more support from government bonds. Household loans and short-term corporate loans remained weak amid reduced window guidance on SME and industrial loan growth. M1 and corporate deposits fell further, by 6.6% and 3.3% YoY, respectively, in July.

July credit numbers supported mainly by government bond issuance amid a seasonally low month: Headline TSF growth recovered slightly to 8.2% in July vs. 8.1% in June from a low base - new TSF was Rmb771bn, up RMB234bn YoY but down Rmb2.5tn QoQ. Government bond issuance acted as the main support, adding Rmb691bn, up Rmb 280bn YoY, which in our view could continue to support TSF growth in the rest of 2024. Notably, YTD government bond issuance, at Rmb4tn, already exceeded the amount in the same period of last year. On the other hand, new corporate bonds of Rmb203bn remained somewhat low although up Rmb74bn YoY, as LGFV tightening had already kicked in last year. RMB loan growth slowed further in July as banks continued to rationalize loan growth amid reduced window guidance and continued efforts to reduce self-circulating loans.

Household loans and corporate loans continued to decline with reduced window guidance on SME and industrial loans: Household loans recorded a net decline of Rmb210bn in July, dragged by weak short-term HH loans. We think this resulted from: 1) less household operating loans, mainly SME loans, with reduced targets on SME loan growth; 2) conservative lending practices at banks as much lower consumer credit loan yields do not allow banks to cover higher credit costs; and 3) cautious attitudes from households on higher leverage despite low interest rates. Corporate loans added Rmb130bn, down 45% YoY, despite more support from bills (up 55% YoY). Notably, short-term corporate loans recorded a net drop of Rmb550bn, which we think also resulted from the ongoing reduction in selfcirculating loans and more rational support for industrial and SME loans. This could also help drive up SME and industrial loan yields with risk-based pricing, in our view.

M1 growth further moderated to -6.6% in July from the reclassification of deposits: Corporate deposits declined 3.3% YoY in July, while deposits from nonbank financial firms growth picked up to 12.5%, reflecting continued impact from the requirement that large SOEs need to centralize funding and deposits at banks via their group finance companies. On the other hand, M2 growth remained relatively stable, at 6.3% YoY in July, despite a sequentially lower amount, down Rmb1.7tn qoq. Although TSF data has remained weak in the near term, we believe it will help to restore a more market-oriented financial system, which would be positive for banks.
