# Venture Vision: Back to Neverland?

On the cusp of US rate cuts, according to our Economists, anticipation is high that venture/growth markets will step in to support a growing cohort of struggling start-ups. History tells us c3.6% rates would still be sufficiently restrictive to keep the record \$250bn of dry powder sidelined.

## TLDR:

• Unicorn funding is 80% below peak. However, unicorns continue to be minted largely thanks to the AI trade.

• 42% of the 40 new unicorns in 2024 are Generative AI focused start-ups, they represent 54% of the \$79bn unicorn value minted in 2024 YTD; both are record levels for a single funding vertical.

• The speed to reach unicorn status is over a year faster for AI companies than non-AI (6.8 vs 5.4 yrs from founding) - xAI 1.3yrs; Cognition 1.3yrs; Typeface 1.5yrs; ElevenLabs 2.1yrs; Perplexity 2.2yrs.

• As rate begin to fall - starting next month according to our Economists - the market expects \$250bn of dry powder to start dripping into markets, broadening out from a concentrated AI market.

• The proportion of US unicorns that are both unprofitable and seeing declining YoY sales has risen to 30% from 5% in 2021. Despite their valuations, most of these companies will need further funding to avoid becoming victims of rising bankruptcies across the venture ecosystem.

• However, if rates were to hover in 2025 as our Economists forecast between 3-4%, this has been the worst fundraising environment of all back to 2000.

• Only 3% of all venture funding has been deployed during this 3-4% window of interest rates. Of course, one could retort that US rates have only stayed in this 3-4% channel for 14 months of the 312 months back to 2000.

• In our view, rates would have to breach 2% to see material dry powder deployment, something our Economists do not foresee in their US soft landing scenario.

## THE FINTECH RADAR (18 AUG 2024)

• #1 PayZen raises \$232m in funding: This week (14th August), US-based fintech start-up PayZen secured \$232m in funding. The \$232m capital included \$32m equity raised in a Series B round led by NEA with participation from existing investors 7WireVentures, Signal Fire, and Viola Ventures. The remaining \$200m was in the form of a credit facility from Viola Credit and a syndicate of insurance companies. The fintech company aims to increase accessibility and affordability to healthcare by providing medical loans that are integrated into patients’ medical record portals. The additional capital will be used to expand the start-up's 'Care Now, Pay Later' product and to support its further market expansion.

• #2 Skydo secures \$5m in a pre-Series A funding: This week (13th August), the Bengaluru-based cross-border fintech company raised \$5m in pre-Series A funding round, led by existing investors Elevation Capital. The injected funds will be put towards expanding the company's operation and improving its risk monitoring and compliance systems. The fintech previously raised \$5.2m in a Seed funding round back in 2022, which was led by Elevation Capital. Skydos' platform allows exporters to link their local Indian bank accounts and receive funds through NEFT/ RTGS. The company has processed payments for more than 6,000 Indian SMB exporters since its inception (equivalent to \$100m) and aims to process \$750m by 2025. Skydo has also submitted an application for the Cross-Border Payment Aggregator.

• #3 Open Access Energy secures \$750m: This week (15th August), South African start-up Open Access Energy raised \$750m in Seed funding from Factor E Ventures. The funding round marks the first tranche of a total of \$1.5m seed round. The capital is expected to facilitate the development of new features, expansion of operations, and further solidify its position in the energy sector. The company develops software solutions that aim to address South Africa's chronic energy challenges. To date, the start-up has developed 2 software solutions to support real-time energy transactions and facilitates energy management for both municipalities and private generation.

Unless otherwise indicated, the news reports referenced above have not been confirmed.

## Real-time valuation monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
