# June data: More evidence of a shift away from quantitative monetary target

TSF growth further slowed in June, from a high base, with less focus on quantitative targets from policymakers. Loan growth further rationalized on continued efforts to reduce selfcirculating loans and matching loan growth with real demand. M1 growth further declined amid lower corporate deposits.

Headline TSF growth moderated to 8.1% in June with more rational loan extension for quality of credit growth: Despite support from government bond issuance (at Rmb849bn), new TSF declined Rmb928bn YoY, to Rmb3.3tn in June, from a high base. RMB loans added Rmb2.2tn in June, down Rmb1.05tn YoY but up Rmb1.38tn MoM, with more rational loan extension. We believe the moderating loan growth could have resulted from: 1) a high base last year; 2) optimized credit demand with projects with better returns; 3) continued efforts to reduce selfcirculating loans; and 4) the revitalization of existing loan resources rather than aggressive new loan extension. We think this is in-line with recent actions from policymakers to shift away from quantitative targets for more sustainable credit growth in the long run, especially given an already sizable TSF balance at Rmb395tn in June. We believe that more rational loan growth should lead to higher loan yields and help banks to rehabilitate NIM from a very low level and support banks' ROE.

All types of loans moderated in June as banks focus on quality, particularly after loan front loading in 1Q24: Total new RMB loans further moderated in June as we expected – new household and corporate loans declined 41% and 29% yoy, respectively. Notably, YTD new short-term household loans declined 79% yoy, likely due to still less consumption credit demand and higher standards for new consumption loans by banks. Long-term household loans added Rmb320bn, down 31% yoy, amid a new norm in property sales and reduced window guidance on personal business loan growth. In addition, net new medium- to long-term corporate loan declined 39%, or Rmb623bn, yoy, from a high base last year. We believe the trend will continue in the rest of 2024 with more rational loan extension amid less focus on quantitative growth.

Further negative M1 and corporate deposits growth, suggesting continued cleanup of self-circulating loans and moderation in business activities: M1 growth recorded consecutive decline since April – at -5% yoy in June, led by a 2.9% decline in corporate deposits. Total deposit growth also slowed, to 6.1% in June from 6.7% in May, following slower credit growth. Household deposits growth slightly slowed but remained resilient at 10.6% in June, down from 11.2% in May.
