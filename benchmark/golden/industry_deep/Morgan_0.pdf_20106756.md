To look at a more holistic measure, we proxy the internal rate of return or equity yield via our long-term fair value framework for the S&P500 index for which we have historical data since the late 1950s (see “A fair value model for US Bonds, Credit and Equities<sup>”</sup>, Panigirtzoglou and Loeys, Jun 2005 and F&L, Nov 2013). The starting point of this valuation model is the Dividend Discount Model (DDM). Equity valuation is more complicated than bond valuation because of the difficulty in measuring expectations of future cash flows, but ultimately equities also need to be analysed in a discounted cash flow model. That is, the equity price is equal to the expected future cash flows (i.e. dividends) discounted to present value using an Equity Discount Rate (EDR). The equation below illustrates the discounted cash flow model:

$$
P = \sum _ { i } \frac { k \cdot E _ { 0 } ( 1 + g ) ^ { i } } { ( 1 + E D R ) ^ { i } }
$$

where P denotes the equity price, E0 is current earnings, k is the payout ratio, g is the growth rate of dividends, and EDR is the Equity Discount Rate. In brief, we take as the starting point for current earnings the four-quarter rolling sum of earnings per share. For the growth rate of dividends, we assume a two-stage DDM, where the first five years are modelled in a recursive model that depends on lagged 5-year earnings and the previous period<sup>’</sup>s P/E ratio. And the EDR is then derived as the residual by inserting the current price of the S&P500 in the above equation.

On our estimates, the real yield of the S&P500 index stands at 4.7% currently. This is similar to the EDR at the 2007 peak before the Lehman crisis of around 4.7%. In other words, the current equity yield in our fair value model is equal to the level we saw in 2007, which is a very different picture from what we get in Figure 1 where if we proxy the equity yield with the reciprocal of the PE multiple the 2007 yield looked much higher. And in the 2000 market peak the EDR reached around 4.3% on our model, only 0.4% lower from where we are currently.

If we focus instead on the implied equity risk premium (ERP) , given the EDR of 4.7% currently and the 10y real IST yield of 2.1% (proxied by the difference between the nominal 10y UST yield of 4.3% minus the most recent Philly Fed survey of professional forecasters<sup>’</sup> 10y inflation expectation of 2.2%) this implied ERP currently stands at 2.6%, which is some way below its long-term historical average of 3.2%. However, the fact that the ERP is still modestly higher than 2007 and well above 2000 levels is largely the result of real bond yields, which stood at 4% in early 2000. ## Potential equity selling by quarter-end due to rebalancing by US defined benefit pension funds and Norges Bank/GPIF/SNB could be up to \$120bn with a similar amount of bond buying

The question of month-end and quarter-end rebalancing flows from multi-asset investors has resurfaced as equity markets rallied while bond markets sold off. We update below our analysis of rebalancing flow from multi-asset investors such as balanced mutual funds, US defined benefit pension funds, the Norwegian oil fund, the SNB and the Japanese GPIF.

Starting with mutual funds with strict monthly rebalancing such as 60:40 funds, we estimate the size of this universe at around \$3.3tr currently. This is much narrower that the AUM of the broader hybrid fund category, which at the global level stood at \$6.8tr in 3Q23 according to ICI (\$7.5tr if extrapolated to current asset prices). However the total universe is larger than this estimate and perhaps closer to \$10tr if one includes target date and lifestyle funds. Because target date and lifestyle funds are fund of funds, their flows or AUM are excluded from ICI to avoid double counting. But they do matter for rebalancing. With month-end rebalancing largely behind us, the focus shifts to quarter-end rebalancing by pension funds and sovereign wealth funds. US defined benefit pension plans is a large universe with AUM of around \$9tr. They tend to rebalance more slowly over 1-2 quarters or so. If we assume they were fully rebalanced at the end of December and taking into account the QTD performance of US equities and bonds, we estimate that the pending equity rebalancing flow by US defined benefit pension fund plans into quarter-end could see net selling of equities of \$180bn and similar net buying of bonds. The caveat with this calculation is that pension funds overall are less strict in terms of rebalancing than either balanced mutual funds or other pension fund related entities such as Norges Bank/GPIF/SNB. Therefore, the above \$180bn of estimated equity selling is unlikely to take place by this quarterend. Given the previous years<sup>’</sup> US pension fund behaviour, we expect around a third of that rebalancing flow or \$60bn of equity selling/bond buying to materialise in the current quarter-end.

By doing the same calculation for Norges Bank, a \$1.5tr AUM entity as at end-Dec 2023, we estimate that the pending equity rebalancing flow by Norges Bank into the current quarter-end could see net sales of equities of around \$21bn. This assumes that its equity weight remains broadly unchanged from its end-year levels. However, taking into account the government<sup>’</sup>s projected oil revenues less the expected non-oil budget deficit (Norway 2024 budget, Oct 6th), and assume net revenues are invested roughly equally across quarters and by their end-2023 asset weights, this would reduce the net sales of equities to around \$10bn.

In addition to the Norges Bank, the SNB also invests a considerable portion of the foreign currency reserves it has accumulated over the course of its FX interventions from 2008 onward in equities. Its equity allocation had remained relatively stable at 20% from 2016 to end-2020 its equity allocation, then increased to 23% during 2021 and to 25% in 1Q22, after which it has stabilised at that level. To estimate its pending rebalancing flow, we estimate returns on its equity portfolio using the SNB<sup>’</sup>s Form 13F filings with the SEC to estimate the split between US and non-US equities, and approximate portfolio returns using the MSCI US and MSCI World ex-US non-financial equities since in order to avoid conflicts of interest the SNB does not buy bank equities. We then estimate the pending rebalancing flow based on its reported reserves as of end-December and assume its target equity allocation remains unchanged. If we assume its balance sheet remained unchanged, this proxy would suggest that the SNB would need to sell around \$11bn. Against a backdrop of a somewhat dovish tone from SNB speakers and a modest rise in sight deposits since the start of the year suggests if anything some risk of modest reserve accumulation which could point at somewhat reduced equity selling pressure, though monthly data for January on other key liabilities such as SNB bills have not been released at the time of writing.

For the GPIF, the Japanese government pension plan with around \$1.6tr of AUM as at end-December 2023, to estimate a pending rebalancing flow we assume that it would rebalance to its end-December allocations. Based on quarter-to-date returns on the GPIF<sup>’</sup>s benchmark indices for domestic and foreign bonds and equities in domestic currency terms, we estimate that it would need to sell around \$40bn of equities and buy and equivalent amount of bonds to rebalance its portfolio allocations.

In all, we estimate the potential equity selling by quarterend due to rebalancing by US defined benefit pension funds and Norges Bank/GPIF/SNB could be up to \$120bn with a similar amount of bond buying.

## Bitcoin mining concentration likely to increase post halving with a higher share by publicly listed bitcoin miners

The upcoming bitcoin halving event is the most anticipated event in the crypto space expected to happen around the end of April. The halving event will reduce the issuance rewards for bitcoin miners securing the bitcoin network from current 6.25 BTC per block to 3.125. This will have a negative impact on miners<sup>’</sup> profitability given reduced rewards and a higher bitcoin production cost.

The bitcoin production cost has empirically acted as a lower bound for bitcoin prices. Figure 5 hows the bitcoin price against its production cost range over time. The central point of our estimated production cost range stands at \$26.5k currently, which would mechanically double post halving event to \$53k. However, as discussed previously in our publication the bitcoin network could experience around a 20% drop in its hashrate immediate post halving event, mainly due to less efficient rigs exiting mining as they become unprofitable. This 20% drop would bring the hashrate closer to its historical trend as shown in Figure 6. This would effectively cut the central point of our estimated production cost range to \$42k. This \$42k estimate is also the level we envisage bitcoin prices drifting towards once bitcoin-halving-induced euphoria subsides after April.

This \$42k central point of our estimated production cost range is based on average electricity cost of 0.05 \$/kWh. Some miners could have lower or higher electricity costs than this average depending on their location and their scale of mining activity. Bitcoin miners with below average electricity costs and more efficient rigs are likely to survive while those with high production costs would struggle.

We believe in this fight for survival, larger publicly listed bitcoin miners are better placed to survive. First, their larger scale mining operations help them to reduce overhead costs via economies of scale. Second, the larger publicly listed miners tend to have more attractive fixed price agreements with power companies which along with a more sustainable energy mix help them to lower electricity costs, a major component of the overall bitcoin production cost. Third, publicly listed miners have access to equity markets. In fact they have been raising larger amounts of equity capital in recent quarters as shown in Figure 7 to fuel their operations via purchases of more efficient mining rigs.

Figure 8 shows the share of publicly listed bitcoin miners in the overall bitcoin network hashrate at the end of each calendar year. The public miners share had increased sharply by the end of 2022 as the bitcoin price declines at the time made it more difficult for less efficient private miners to compete. In a reversal, the public miners share saw a decline during 2023 as less efficient mining rigs from smaller and private mining firms were incentivized to increase their mining activity due to the large bitcoin price appreciation. In a similar fashion to 2022, we expect the share of publicly listed miners to increase post halving, especially if our call of bitcoin prices drifting lower towards \$42k after April materializes.

## Definitions:

Low: Lowest closing level in the last 1y

## Chart A11b: Option skew monitor

Skew is the difference between the implied volatility of out-of-the-money (OTM) call options and put options. A positive skew implies more demand for calls than puts and a negative skew, higher demand for puts than calls. It can therefore be seen as an indicator of risk perception in that a highly negative skew inequities is indicative of a bearish view. The chart shows z-score of the skew, i.e. the skew minus a rolling 2- year avg skew divided by a rolling two-year standard deviation of the skew. A negative skew on iTraxx Main means investors favour buying protection, i.e. a short risk position. A positive skew for the Bund reflects a long duration view, also a short risk position.

## Chart A11c: Equity-Bond metric map

Explanation of Equity - Bond metric map: Each of the five axes corresponds to a key indicator for markets. The position of the blue line on each axis shows how far the current observation is from the extremes at either end of the scale. For example, a reading at the centre for value would mean that risky assets are the most expensive they have ever been while a reading at the other end of the axis would mean they are the cheapest they have ever been. Overall, the larger the blue area within the pentagon, the better for the risky markets. All variables are expressed as the percentile of the distribution that the observation falls into. I.e. a reading in the middle of the axis means that the observation falls exactly at the median of all historical observations.
