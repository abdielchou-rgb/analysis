Investors appear more confident in the future of the ECB's and BoJ's next monetary policy moves, but less confident in the future of the Fed's. This reflects different degrees of uncertainty — higher for the US, lower for the eurozone and Japan — about what recent economic data mean.

## The Importance of Fed Policy Pricing

10y Treasury yields rose 32bp in the first quarter of the year, and another 30bp in April month-to-date. Wild swings have defined the up-trend so far. Ordinarily, such swings would make it difficult for investors to hold underweight, or short, duration positions – especially for those whose fiscal years began on January 1.

However, it seems that macro hedge funds weathered the various duration rally storms, according to the Bloomberg Macro Hedge Fund Return Indexes (see Exhibit 1 ). After two years of lackluster returns, macro hedge funds regained their swagger from 2020-21.

Indeed, macro hedge fund 3-month annualized returns (1Q24) almost treble their 2023 performance and sextuple their 3-year compound annual performance (see Exhibit 2 ). Discretionary macro hedge funds have outperformed multi-strategy funds over the past few years, but both began the year on a strong note.

## Strong USD vs. Low Yielders

In macro markets, the year has been dominated by two themes: 1) The outperformance of the US economy and 2) Low volatility, leading to carry trades ( Exhibit 10 ). This has led to a split performance of USD – outperformance vs low yielders ( Exhibit 11 ), but total return losses vs high yielders and some commodity FX. Recent conversations with clients have revealed some scepticism about the durability of these themes. We disagree.

Divergence exists: One of the more puzzling trends in markets recently has been the strong co-movement of European and US front-end interest rates, despite the clear divergence in the recent cycles of the Eurozone and US economies. This co-movement led market participants to question whether the ECB can or is willing to start an easing cycle ahead of the Fed.

This debate has always been a bit puzzling as there are numerous examples of central banks starting their easing cycles ahead of the Fed this year, so it shouldn't be a great surprise to learn that the ECB is willing to do the same. As always, it simply depends on the data. There might be a case that any US data suggesting a delay – if so, then perhaps the same would be the case for the Eurozone.

But data have clearly diverged, and as we heard from the ECB, June cuts are the base case and our economics team is anticipating 100bp in rate cuts from the ECB this year vs around 75bp priced by the market, though risks are tilted toward fewer cuts.

Divergence between the US and European economic and monetary policy cycles is now starting to be reflected in markets ( Exhibit 12 and Exhibit 13 ), quite rightly, and points to continued EUR/USD downside.

Our year-end target remains 1.04, though recent momentum may put this target in play considerably sooner. We would also argue that GBP fits into the same category, where the risks of a more substantial easing cycle are higher vs the US.

Asia ex-Japan is another region where desired monetary policy divergence is likely to lead to currency weakness. Regional inflation is falling and the bias of monetary policy for many central banks in Asia is for easing, with any risks of a delayed cycle largely in response to currency weakness.

• In other words, as regional inflation stays under control, it is no surprise that regional currencies are weakening vs USD as rate differentials widen further in favour of USD.

Carry to outperform: The other reason USD is likely to stay strong vs low yielders is simply that volatility should stay low.

• One of the most significant drivers of low volatility and strong risk appetite is the view in markets of the growing evidence of a supply-side driven expansion of the US economy that is simultaneously keeping growth robust and wages muted. While recent CPI data may cause some to start questioning that benign narrative, we believe the drivers of it are powerful.

In any case, the combined CPI and PPI, when translated to PCE, does not look overly worrying for the Fed (see here). Provided growth remains robust, we doubt risk appetite will be affected significantly, even if US rates rise further.

Indeed, UST yields have been on an upward trend all year, and risk appetite and carry currencies have outperformed. Carry trades would be undermined by a pickup in volatility. # Interest Rate Strategy

## United States

Not the "insurance" you were looking for: With cooling inflation in 2H23, US rates investors had been thinking about when and how fast could the Fed deliver "insurance cuts" in 2024. Instead, the back-to- back strength in labor market data, and especially CPI data, has surprised markets. The strength in CPI, owing a lot to the price of "vehicle insurance", has upended the "insurance cuts" investors were hoping for.

Inflation prints driving yields, but should they? In the last three months, nearly 100% of the recent 80bp rise in 10y yields has been from the +/-3 days window around CPI. It is notable that while we have had three consecutive strong prints, the strength has not been from consistent sectors. In particular, vehicle insurance has been nearly 40% of the value of the widely-watched core services ex-shelter category.

PCE on a different page vs. CPI: The vehicle insurance category that drove coreservices ex-shelter CPI higher does not contribute to PCE as PCE uses data from another source. Therefore core-services ex-shelter PCE looks set to keep cooling, a trend further reinforced by the fact that wages in transportation services sector have been cooling as well.

Stay neutral, even as yields overreact: We think the market reaction in yields is overdone given 1) the concentration of CPI strength in vehicle insurance, 2) a much more benign trajectory of core PCE, and 3) alongside cooling wages. We stay neutral on duration and curves for now, given the streak of strong optical data and the lack of an immediate catalyst that we think can change the perception of a strong U.S. economy and inflation risks.

## Euro area

Market dynamics remain unaltered – the US market is still creating unfavorable spillover effects to the rest of DM markets, with the Bund continuing to lack directionality. The upside surprise on US CPI provided had an adverse external impact on European duration, although the ECB language at the April meeting provided hints of an imminent cut at the June meeting. However, as President Lagarde indicated a lack of pre-commitment on the rate path after June, the extent of the stronger US inflation print has been translated in Europe into a front-end repricing, coupled with a higher fixings profile to Dec 24.

We tend to think that with 3x25bp cuts priced in to Dec 24, the market, especially in Europe, is not factoring in any potential premium. So, at the current juncture, the base case of quarterly cuts implied by the market is creating a “cheap optionality” for the pricing of an additional cut by Dec 24. We enter a long ERZ4 97.125 call.

In EGBs, these past weeks, France's fiscal position has been in the spotlight. The country will be reviewed by Fitch and Moody's on the 26 April and by S&P on 31 May. We only see a clear risk of downgrade by S&P, which we believe is already priced by markets.

In that sense, risks around the S&P review seem asymmetric to us. While we see little potential for a further cheapening in case of a downgrade, should S&P leave their rating unchanged, OATs would likely richen back relative to peers. We recommend buying OATs on the 10y Bund/OAT/Bono barbell (weights: 0.65-1- 0.35), i.e., long OAT 3.5% 2033 against Bund 2.2% 2034 and Bono 3.25% 2034.

## United Kingdom

Macro domestic data will take center stage over the upcoming week, with wage growth and inflation data the main focus. ## Currency & Foreign Exchange

## G10

G10 | Does the CNY central parity fixing matter for G10 currencies? Yes… and no. While the data does not point to direct currency intervention when USD/CNH rises 2% above the day's Central Parity Rate (a.k.a the fixing), it is informative. USD/CNH breaching this limit appears to be indicative of a global riskoff or US outperformance environment, both of which engender dollar strength and weakness in the rest of G10.

Nevertheless, there does appear to be a mechanism in place which limits further depreciation of CNH. A possible explanation of this mechanism is found in CNH funding markets. When USD/CNH rises too far past the fixing, CNH funding becomes expensive, deterring speculators from weakening the off-shore RMB further. The first round effects of this do not appear to influence G10 currencies directly.

## Yes... and No.

Investors are undoubtedly well versed in the significance of the Chinese economy for global macro. Likewise, that a colloquially called "USD/CNY fixing" exists is not unknown.

However, how said fixing works and the impact that it has on G10 currencies directly, may not be as well understood.

In simplified terms, the China Foreign Exchange Trade System (CFETS) gathers quotes from market makers, filters them, weighs them and then, according to the PBOC, introduces a "counter-cyclical factor".

This fixing, also known as the Central Parity Rate, is then published (every trading day at 9:15 Beijing time) and serves as a benchmark for the day's trading. Specifically, USD/CNY is allowed to deviate, at most, 2% from the fixing.

Exhibit 52: The dollar is the best intra-day G10 performer when USD/CNH exceeds the fixing limit
![](images/678c3d2c883fda4af0982afbbe33ba703b68bcbbc5fb6d75b666578f8321f9b5.jpg)
Source: Morgan Stanley Research, Bloomberg
Exhibit 53: G10 currencies account for a large portion of China trade upon which the CFETS RMB basket is based

![](images/fe6feb6a3edefeaded1221dfaab34dbe8bfbf4f446b32d29bd905e773c3e9616.jpg)
Source: Morgan Stanley Research, BIS, CFETS

What happens when USD/CNY approaches this 2% limit? Or better yet, what happens when USD/CNH (the offshore RMB market) exceeds the 2% limit?

That is the question that matters for G10 and one that is difficult to answer. Details about the mechanism used to defend this limit are scarce.

As a result, we look at historical price action in an attempt to decipher what the likely consequences for G10 currencies might be in such a scenario.

Over the past 10 years, USD/CNH has traded 2% above the day's fixing 61 times, on 20 separate continuous occasions, 7 of which were separated by a month or more.

On these 61 days, G10 currencies, on average, sold-off versus the dollar ( Exhibit 52 ). A potentially counterintuitive result given that in these scenarios either the RMB needs to strengthen, or, the dollar needs to weaken, to bring USD/CNH back towards the fixing.

This data can be interpreted to suggest that the PBOC does not employ (at least as a primary tool) direct currency intervention. Particularly given the size of G10 trade with China ( Exhibit 53 ).

## So why are G10 currencies selling off against the dollar when USD/CNH trades more than 2% above the fix?

The answer, according to Exhibit 54 appears to be about coincidence as opposed causality. Not coincidence in the sense of serendipity, but rather that one common driver is causing both G10 ex USD to sell off, and USD/CNH rate to rise higher than desired: risk.

Whilst not exclusive, USD/CNH appears to breach the 2% threshold more often in risk-off environments, which are often characterized by a sell-off in equities and a rally of the dollar ( Exhibit 54 ).

When USD/CNH breaches the 2% threshold in risk-on environments, there appears to be a concentration of instances in equity rallies greater than 1%. Perhaps the other side of the dollar smile where US outperformance drives a dollar bid.

Exhibit 54: Both G10 (ex dollar) and S&P500 intraday returns are skewed negative when USD/CNH exceeds the fixing's 2% limit

![](images/f45c34eaeecde77f12be22724af79289dde08e170550ca1951cf8a2ef76eacff.jpg)
Source: Morgan Stanley Research, Bloomberg

Exhibit 55: On average, the fixing yields to USD/CNH when the 2% is first exceeded, but ultimately sees USD/CNH correcting

![](images/d9d631fff2f897aedaa0e629cd31265beea9d263a2339dcb42f579b581cc5bb3.jpg)
Source: Morgan Stanley Research, Bloomberg

The dollar strength and rest-of-G10 weakness that occurs when the threshold is exceeded, can therefore plausibly be linked to the broader macroeconomic backdrop, as opposed to direct currency intervention.

## What does happen in that case, when USD/CNH exceeds the fixing's limit?

By looking at average price action following the initial push above the 2% barrier ( Exhibit 55 ), it appears that despite the fixing adjusting first, there is a process in place capable of halting CNH depreciation.

Not only this, but over time, CNH appears to be the one that converges towards the fixing as opposed to vice versa.

The average initial reaction of G10 currencies, on the other hand, is very mixed ( Exhibit 56 ). If there were to be widespread direct currency intervention, it would not be unreasonable to expect more uniform price action of G10 (ex dollar) strength.

Looking at Exhibit 57 , it appears that the mechanism used to defend USD/CNH from drifting further from its fixing may actually be with regards to funding. As CNH devalues, pushing 1% above and then ultimately 2% above the fix, funding in CNH appears to get more expensive.

The more expensive funding costs make short CNH positions less attractive for speculative short sellers, thus limiting the CNH sell-off.

However, it is important to note, that this analysis only looks at primary effects of exceeding the fixing limit. Secondary effects from RMB strength or weakness and the consequential impact on trade, macro economic conditions, and ultimately the currency again, may differ.

Nevertheless, from this data it seems that USD/CNH rising more than 2% past the fixing is significant for G10 currencies in what it indicates as opposed to what it causes. It indicates a macro economic environment, where on average, G10 currencies sell-off.

## "Roughly Half"

The minutes from March FOMC meeting were released this week and revealed considerations amongst the Committee around slowing the pace of its balance sheet runoff. At this meeting, the Fed staff reviewed the 2017-19 balance sheet run-off episode and the lessons learned.

Participants viewed the current level of reserves as “abundant” and observed balance sheet run-off was proceeding "smoothly." The Committee agreed on a “cautious approach” to further run-off and decided an in-depth discussion would help inform future decisions about how and when to slow the pace of run-off. There were three main components to the discussion.

RRP Is the Key: A slower pace of decline in ON RRP balances this year was highlighted. Risk of more-limited further declines in ON RRP balances and subsequent potentially rapid declines in reserve balances was the driving factor for the discussion to slow the pace of run-off. Furthermore, we do not believe a stabilization in ON RRP balances will push the taper decision out.

Taper Size Still Undecided: There was agreement on reducing the monthly run-off pace by “roughly half” via adjusting the Treasury redemption cap but leaving MBS unchanged, but the size was not determined. The term “roughly half” could mean reducing caps on Treasury securities from \$60bn, to \$30bn (our base case). Or lowering the Treasury runoff cap to \$20bn to achieve “roughly half” the current run-off pace of \~\$80bn, given MBS paydowns average \~\$20bn per month (compared to the \$35bn cap).

Longer-Run Aspects: There is a clear intent on the Committee for primarily Treasury holdings on the balance sheet. There was initial discussion on the maturity composition of SOMA but no details with respect to a potential desired maturity, nor agreement on a preferred composition. This "longer-term" decision was also clearly separated from the “more immediate issue” of slowing the pace of run-off.

The two main benefits from slowing the pace of run-off "sooner rather than later" would help:

1. Facilitate a smooth transition from abundant to ample reserves;

2. Provide more time to assess market conditions, allowing banks and short-term funding markets to adjust to a lower level of reserves.

Both factors reduce risk of a premature end to run-off from undue stress in money markets.

## And Now, for Something Completely Different

Playing for curve steepening has been a consensus theme this year, with history indicating that ahead of cutting cycles markets ex-ante underprice the ex-post depth of Fed policy easing. Even after this week's reduction in Fed cut pricing, swap markets continue to price steepening over coming months (see Exhibit 95 ).

The past year has served as a stark reminder that the curve can experience "anomalous" periods of bull flattening and bear steepening. As recently as 4Q23, yield curve moves were driven \~70% of the time by the backend of the yield curve — as opposed to \~30% of the time in normal bull-steepening/bear-flattening regimes (see Exhibit 96 ).

In a world where curve steepening remains consensus, but anomalous periods abound, could investors seek value in deviating from the beaten path? While not our core view, we think there is a case for arguing that bull-flattening provides an attractive risk/reward profile.

Indeed, we think the market should be pricing the probability of bull-flattening as we near the cutting cycle start. The likely catalyst would be real money investors locking in elevated bond yields. This community has largely remained sidelined amid the uncertain policy outlook painted by incoming economic data. However, as more certainty is ascertained, a real money bid could lead the 30y to outperform in a rally.

Tail term structures for upper expiries are inverted, implying that conditional bullflatteners have a vol-pickup. As shown in Exhibit 97 , 6m2y and 6m5y vols are trading \~1.30x and \~1.25x against 6m30y vol, respectively. ## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Definition of terms

Buy/Long: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be positive over the relevant time period.

Sell/Short: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be negative over the relevant time period.

Selling protection or Buying Risk: The analyst expects that the price of protection against the event occurring will decrease over the relevant time period.

Buying protection or Selling Risk: The analyst expects the price of protection against the

event occurring will increase over the relevant time period.

Pay: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will increase.

Receive: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will decrease.

Unless otherwise specified, the time frame for recommendations included in the Morgan Stanley Fixed Income Research reports is 1 - 3 months and the price of financial instruments mentioned in the recommendation is as at the date and time of publication of the recommendation.

When more than one issuer or instrument is included in a recommendation, analyst expects one part of the trade to outperform the other trade or combination of other trades included in the recommendation on a relative basis.
