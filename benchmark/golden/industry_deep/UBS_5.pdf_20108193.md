# US Equity Derivatives Strategy Jack of vol trades: Defensives rotation raising eyebrows with VIX back to lows

## Trade ideas to consider (pricing indicative a/o 10 May):

 How low can we go?: VIX Jun 13 put vs. 16/19 call spread, collect \$0.025 (40% net delta; 14.38 UXM4 ref); Alternatively use VIX Oct 14.5 puts to fund

 Playing defense: XLU Jul 75 call, pay \$0.65 (0.9% premium; 25% delta; 71.27 XLU ref)

## In limbo: How low can vol really go? [Figures 1-8]

Equity volatility has retraced sharply, in line with our view – We would look to take profits in our VIX 1x3 put backspread idea ahead of May VIX expiry. With index realized volatility and correlation having not kept pace with implied, risk premia has fully eroded as reflation and geopolitical concerns have waned. So if it's not geopolitics, it's earnings; If it's not earnings, it's macro; If it's not macro, then VIX at 12-13 seems fair, ordoes it? The S&P 500 has not had a -2% day in >300 trading days since the likes of pre-Volmageddon and pre-GFC. We have written extensively on the structural reasons as to why. VIX should normally trade at a 2-3pt premium to SPX 1m realized volatility, but realized has actually been sneakily on the rise. We think vol (VIX) and vol-of-vol (VVIX) have now found a lower bound in the near-term.We favor selling puts to fund upside call spreads to position for a moderate retracement higher in volatility risk premia ahead of key CPI and Retail Sales data this week (>1% implied move for the former).

## Last line of defense: Defensives rotation worth monitoring ahead of key CPI data [Figures 9-12]

Macro back in the driver's seat again with Q1 earnings season largely in the rearview. Alan Detmeister (US Economist) sees a wider range of uncertainty on Core CPI, with the skew slightly to the upside this month. Two things that stick out to us ahead of the print: 1) Option-implied moves ahead of major macro events continue to rise even with implied volatility back to lows, and these moves are realizing and then some (especially post-CPI); and 2) The swift rotation into Defensives with the market reapproaching all time highs is worth monitoring. We recently noted that whilst stagflation risks have crept back into the narrative, the market is not priced for them. Our Market Cookbook suggested a strong Defensives > Cyclicals bias – see More than a feeling.

## With great power...: Utilities (XLU) calls to position for further momentum in Defensives [Figures 13-20]

Utilities in particular have started to outperform recently. We see three potential reasons: 1) Electrification companies are trading as second derivative beneficiaries of the AI tailwind, as previously noted; 2) Utilities are more prone to a squeeze having been relatively underowned; and 3) Bond-proxy sectors are benefitting from rates retracing lower post-FOMC/NFP. We think the space could potentially benefit in both scenarios: 1) Rate cuts getting priced back in further on a cooling CPI print, or 2) Stagflation fears continuing to brew if inflation remains sticky. William Appicelli (Head of NA Power & Utilities Research) is more selective on the space as he sees pockets of upside, but not necessarily a 'meat and potatoes' broad sector trade. He suggests Constellation Energy (CEG) and Vistra Corp (VST) on the power demand theme and NextEra Energy (NEE), Entergy (ETR), and NiSource (NI) as attractive Utility names, with Southern Company (SO) being the highest quality Defensive option.
