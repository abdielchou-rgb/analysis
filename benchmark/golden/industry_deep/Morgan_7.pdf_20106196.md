## What is needed for Eurozone to start outperforming again? We stay OW Growth vs Value

The year started with the US equity market ahead of Eurozone. Can this change? The last positive spell for Eurozone was between Sept <sup>’</sup>22 and May <sup>’</sup>23, when it outperformed S&P500 by as much as 32%, in USD terms - see top chart. That run was helped by the easing energy pressures and bottoming growth momentum. We cut Eurozone to UW in early May of last year, and believe that it is right for the region to have lagged by 15% since. The question is what can help Eurozone equities to mount another leg of outperformance.

• For sure, Eurozone certainly screens cheap vs the US, at a 15% greater discount than typical on a sector neutral P/E metric - see middle chart. Further, the region is likely underowned, as seen in 40 weeks of outflows over the last year. Also, the relative growth momentum could be bottoming out, given a series of disappointments in Eurozone over the past year, and the positive surprises in the US, the bar for this to continue is getting more difficult.

A more constructive China backdrop would definitely be a help for Eurozone to take the lead, in our view. As MSCI China is down as much as 30% vs a year ago, trading cheap and is likely a fully consensus UW everywhere, the current short squeeze could continue for a bit longer. Having said that, from a fundamental standpoint, we remain bearish on the region, for now, and cautious in global equity strategy allocation.

Another factor is the style leadership. We stay OW Growth vs Value, a position we held through 2023, and opposite to our Value preference that we held in 2021 and 2022. As long as the market stays narrow, heavily concentrated and Tech driven, the US is likely to have the upper hand vs Eurozone. We keep our preference for Quality Growth over Cyclical Value, which translates into a continued preference for the US over Eurozone.

Earnings momentum is a consideration, too. We are cautious on earnings globally, and note that EPS revisions in Europe remain worse than in the US. As noted above, the growth surprise differential might be peaking, but our economists again project stronger GDP growth in US vs Eurozone this year.

Finally, USD direction matters. The Q4 USD downmove might be finishing, as relative interest rate differential bottoms, and as the Fed cuts might have been overdiscounted in the near term. If USD is bottoming out, it would be hard for Eurozone to work. Backtested, Eurozone was actually the worst performing region in times of USD strength, and Japan was the best international market - Japan remains our key regional OW for 2024.

What to buy/sell in Europe? From the negative side, we highlight the following 4: still UW Chemicals on no clear turn in activity momentum. We also remain UW Autos on concerns over pricing, and as of Q4 UW Hotels and Airlines, given tough comps. We have in November turned bearish on Banks, believing that their earnings momentum is peaking. On the buy side, we have OW Utilities, on resilient EPS momentum, OW Healthcare, upgraded in November, taking advantage of their poor run. We double upgraded Real Estate to OW last October. Finally, we are still bullish Aerospace & Defense.

## ...but that may not sustain

We accept that the China bounce could have some legs given how far it has fallen, but are not clear that this will lead to a fundamental turn.

## Equity Strategy Key Calls and Drivers

As of end October ‘23, the average stock in S&P500 was down on the year, with SPW at -5%. A lot has changed, courtesy of the November-December rally, and equity markets are now showing overbought conditions, with sentiment moving into complacent territory. This can be seen in high RSIs, elevated Bull-Bear, VIX near lows, tight credit spreads, as well as in the current S&P500 P/E at 20x. Importantly, while a year ago risky assets were fully pricing in a recession, and economists unanimously agreed with that, now the picture is quite different, recession probabilities are currently near the lows of the range, and most macro forecasts are hopeful. This might be too optimistic. Our key call was to go long duration, we advised last October to position for the rollover in bond yields, and while we see this call having legs in 2024, there is likely to be some payback given the sharpness of the move over the past 3 months. Yields could be consolidating near term, and have a next leg lower only when activity dataflow shows clearer deterioration. Crucially, while the market interpreted falling bond yields since Oct as solely a positive development, we do not think that this will sustain through the year Lower yields could end up signaling weaker EPS delivery ahead, on softening pricing, sequential activity slowdown and profit margin compression. Bottom line, the risky assets have started to fully embrace the macro combination of central banks easing on lower inflation, but at the same time resilient growth and continued record profitability – this might end up contradictory. All this suggests a much less attractive risk-reward than what would at face value lower bond yields/central banks easing and up to now resilient growth suggest. Healthcare, Telcos, Energy and Utilities have started the year on a positive note, and we think this may continue.
