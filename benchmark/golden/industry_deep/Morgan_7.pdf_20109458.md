## US Fixed Income Overview

## Off to the races

● Economics: Real GDP rose a firmer-than-expected 2.8% q/q saar in 2Q, boosted in part by strong export and consumption growth. Core PCE rose 0.18% m/m in June, leaving the year-ago rate unchanged at 2.6%. We expect non-farm payrolls rose 150k in July as the unemployment rate held steady at 4.1%. We look for the FOMC to hold rates steady at next week’s meeting

Treasuries: We see limited room for the FOMC decision or the August refunding announcement to drive significant Treasury volatility next week. We wait for better entry levels to engage in duration longs, given curve inversion and dovish Fed pricing, but continue to hold 5s/30s steepeners, hedged with beta-weighted 2-year shorts. We preview the August refunding process: we expect Treasury to leave nominal auction sizes unchanged and think Treasury and TBAC might revisit the T-bill market. Breakevens remain tightly rangebound and we stay neutral on TIPS. IOTAs have narrowed further despite the focus on funding pressures

Interest Rate Derivatives: We favor a ‘synthetic’ 3M forward 5s/30s swap curve steepener constructed by replacing the 5-year leg with a 3Mx3M / 3Mx2Y curve flattener and reducing risk on the 30-year leg to 65%. We think the recent narrowing in swap spreads is likely due to funding pressures which we think are behind us and cautiously remain positioned in front-end wideners. We maintain our neutral stance on short expiry volatility due to what we see as balanced risks

● Short Duration: Recent volatility/elevated levels of SOFR is a product of market frictions and inefficiencies in the repo markets. Despite this, odds that the Fed will have to end QT prematurely remain low. The stickiness with ON RRP should fade in 2H24 as net T-bill issuance turns positive, and we get further clarity on the Fed’s easing cycle

● Securitized Products: Mortgages have bounced off their post-CPI tights and higher coupons now look closer to fair. Remain neutral on mortgages, and we prefer generic higher coupons and seasoned discounts. We think home prices are poised to slow down meaningfully in the second half of the year. AAA ABS spreads have room to tighten further

● Corporates: The JULI spreads closed at its widest level in a month, amid a 4% drop in the S&P500 and 7% in the NASDAQ. Mixed earnings releases and higher supply were offset by rising yields, limiting the widening so far. High yield spreads were able to outperform equity weakness amid strong fun inflows and light capital markets activity

● Near-term catalysts: August refunding (7/29, 7/31), May JOLTS (7/30), 2Q employment cost index (7/31), FOMC meeting (7/31), Jul employment (8/2)

As we look ahead to next week, we face a calendar replete with key monetary policy meetings and labor market releases. We and consensus expect no policy changes from the FOMC meeting, with a lack of signalling from the more dovish wing of the Committee leaving little chance of a surprise cut next week even if there may be a case. Instead we look for changes to the statement which signal the Committee has gained further confidence in achieving its inflation target while the Chair is likely to avoid providing guidance on the timing and pace of cuts in the press conference (see FOMC preview, Michael Feroli, 7/26/24). Outside the US, we expect the BoJ to raise its policy rate by 15bp to 0.25%, an outcome that is largely priced in TONAR markets, alongside a significant reduction in the future path of JGB purchases (see BoJ Preview: A touchstone, Ayako Fujita, 7/24/24). Finally, the July employment report will be released Friday: we forecast a 150k increase in nonfarm payrolls alongside no change in the unemployment rate (see Economics).

While FOMC decision days have been consistently bullish for Treasuries over the last year, money markets are already pricing in a faster than quarterly pace of cuts through 1H25, and this leaves the bar high for Treasuries to rally on Wednesday. Though we continue to think even shallow easing cycles are supportive of lower Treasury yields, given the market’s current Fed pricing and ongoing inversion of the yield curve, we do not find value in adding duration at current levels. Instead, we continue to favor long-end steepeners as a more strategic position, with a better carry profile than outright longs. However the 5s/30s curve near it steepest levels of the last two years and looks locally too steep relative to the level of front-end yields (Figure 1The curvastpnd gily o m). Thus we think the curve is unlikely to resume its steepening trend until we get closer to the first cut and prefer to hedge our structural steepener position via beta-weighted short in 2-year Treasuries (see Treasuries). Similarly, in derivatives space, a ‘synthetic’ 3M forward 5s/30s swap curve steepener constructed by replacing the 5-year leg with a 3Mx3M / 3Mx2Y curve flattener offers superior carry, and we recommend reducing risk on the 30-year leg to 65% given the beta of the long-end to 3Mx5Y yields.

Adding to an already event-filled week, Treasury will deliver its quarterly refunding announcement on Wednesday. Despite the deficit tracking somewhat larger this year, we continue to believe that Treasury remains well financed through FY25 and that the strong guidance Treasury provided last quarter that it does not expect to make further nominal coupon auction size increases over the next several quarters remains appropriate. This is largely consensus, and the policy announcement is unlikely to move markets. However, we acknowledge that markets could react if Treasury revisits guidance around the optimal T-bill share of debt outstanding. We argue that guiding towards a higher share of T-bills would be reasonable for a host of reasons, and not reflective of an “activist” debt management strategy (see US Treasury Market Daily: August refunding review, Jay Barry, 7/24/24).

Meanwhile, the persistence of SOFR setting at or above EFFR for the better part of this month has sparked concerns about broader funding stress (Figure 2). However, we continue to think recent SOFR volatility is a product of market frictions and inefficiencies in repo markets. Importantly, the rapid growth of repo markets and concentration within the MMF space have likely left many MMFs running up against counterparty limits. When combined with changing liquidity regulations, this has incentivized MMFs to hold more RRP, regardless of economics (see What’s going on with MMFs and their dealer/Fed RRP exposure?, Teresa Ho, 7/18/24). However, we think RRP balances are biased lower in 2H as T-bill issuance turns positive and further clarity around the Fed’s easing cycle allows MMFs to extend duration, and this in turn should enhance liquidity provisioning. Importantly, GSEs, banks, and dealers are likely also to police repo rates on the upside, limiting the likelihood SOFR persistently trades outside of the policy rate corridor. While SOFR volatility outside month/ quarter-ends is worth monitoring going forward, we think the recent shift is not indicative of burgeoning funding pressures and still expect QT to continue through YE24 with ON RRP balances ending slightly below \$300bn and reserves at \$3.1tn (see Short-Term Fixed Income).

Meanwhile matched maturity swap spreads are \~1.5-2bp narrower across much of the curve, and 3bp narrower in the long end. We think spreads look too narrow to fair value in the front-end, and note that this cheapening vis-a-vis our model is likely a result of increased funding costs stemming from elevated GC repo rates. However while we are mindful of repo market dynamics that have pressured funding levels higher to where they are, we believe this rise has mostly run its course at this point. Therefore we cautiously maintain our widening bias in swap spreads, particularly at the front-end, especially given the considerable attractiveness of USTs on an asset-swapped basis for investors such as banks. We maintain our neutral stance on short expiry volatility due to what we see as a balance of risk. On one hand we continue to be in a state of significantly better policy clarity which is helpful in moderating jump risk alongside lowering delivered volatility, and the recent bounce higher in implied vols this week provides a good opportunity to get short volatility. However on the other hand we face significant event risk next week and, despite our consensus view, the condensed schedule of events poses significant enough short term risk to warrant caution (see Interest Rate Derivatives).

Benchmark breakevens are unchanged to 1bp narrower over the last two weeks, outperforming slightly versus our fair value framework and remaining squarely in the range they have held in recent months. With valuations fair and breakevens near the middle of their range, we remain neutral on TIPS. Notably, breakevens have continued to outperform versus inflation swaps, with 3- to 5-year IOTAs trading at their tightest levels since 2021, despite a renewed focus on funding pressures Though we don’t think the drift higher in SOFR relative to FF is symptomatic of broader funding scarcity, we think it should be difficult for IOTAs to tighten further from current levels, particularly around the 5-year sector, and we think inflation swaps offer better value versus breakevens (see TIPS).

Mortgage spreads bounced off their post-CPI tights from two weeks ago as the exuberance following the soft reading appears to have faded to some degree. On balance, current coupons are closer to ‘fair’ and offer a somewhat wider risk premium as of late, with our models indicating a defensible 25-35bp range (Figure 3Curent copslk fai wh25-3b g). On that front, the ‘upper belly’ in conventional 4.5s and 5s continues to outperform as investors are willing to give up some spread for out of the money bonds which should appreciate more into a rally. They offer more ZV than deep discounts while avoiding some of the modeling risk of premium loan balance, could benefit from a significant decline in vol, and stand to see more demand when the Fed cuts and carry improves. These considerations seem to be in the price (see Agency MBS).

In the mortgage credit space, we think jumbo 2.0 in the 70/80s OAS range should be a good resting place for spreads over the near term. Non-QM in our new prepayment model shows OAS north of 100bp, reflecting better presumed convexity of investor and bank statement loans, with some added optionality. Down the capital structure we prefer B1/B2 ‘21/‘22 originations over new issue, as investors searching for hard enhancement and supply/ demand considerations have driven primary spreads tighter. Looking ahead, we have seen 3.8% (nsa) home price growth YTD and expect it to slow over 2H24 for full year growth rate of 5.5%. The months of supply for existing homes reached 4.1 months, the highest since May 2020, due to low demand matching limited supply. Meanwhile new home inventory is increasing, with nearly 10 months of supply, a level not seen since September 2022 and prior to that, April 2009. This rise in inventory comes as affordability for potential buyers is at its worst (Figure 4New hominvtry scg ak fdbl ). With more than 100% ARM deals being issued for the first time in a couple years,we see OAS in AAA ARMs comparable to deep discount non-QM AAAs (see RMBS).

ABS spreads have remained firm to slightly tighter recently due to strong oversubscription on new issues and reduced dealer inventory. While stable technicals and fundamentals were key in the first half of 2024, election impacts are now a major focus as investors grapple with how the outcome might affect consumers, industries, and regulations. Despite the eventual election results, consumer credit trends will largely depend on the labor market, and sponsor risks will remain idiosyncratic. Meanwhile, Fed rate cuts could lead to tighter ABS spreads in the second half of 2024 despite lower yields, especially for AAA ABS, which still have room to narrow (Figure 5). Short, plain vanilla prime auto ABS from benchmark issuers offers excellent liquidity, a great place to park cash ahead of the election results, a safe haven during market volatility, and are relatively cheap (see ABS).

Our CRE strategists report back from a recent trip to San Francisco, where they note client sentiment has largely improved from last year; fears that regional bank stress would accelerate the mark-to-market of CRE and catalyze distressed loan and bond sales have abated as we have instead seen heightened modifications including extensions. With rate cuts expected soon and the economy holding up, sentiment has improved, spreads have rallied, and new issue volumes have surged (Figure 6Sentim hCMBark spovd cly  b g u ). Across the property types, we think office remains fundamentally challenged but we are likely seeing the bottoming out among other property types like multifamily. Indeed, most property types outside office that tend to be more consumer-oriented have basis problems stemming from higher rates but cashflow growth has held up reasonably well. With valuations tight, CMBS investors are split searching for value in new issue 5yr fixed rate conduit, looking among seasoned profiles, or remaining neutral (see CMBS).

HG credit spreads came under pressure this week, as JULI spreads moved towards the upper end of their recent 103-109bp range, but notably this underperformance was not more pronounced given the weakness in equity markets and elevated supply. Indeed the S&P500 is down 4% from its recent peak and the NASDAQ down 7%. Meanwhile EPS estimates for the S&P500 for the quarter are 8%, a high bar and there have been notable misses so far in the earnings season. However the muted passthrough into the credit space makes more sense considering tech spreads started this period wide to the index, there are compositional differences between tech equity and credit indices, and those tech companies on both indices which are under pressure in the equity market demonstrate low leverage fundamentals. Meanwhile supply MTD has reached \$103bn, well above the typical \$90bn average for the month. Looking ahead, August supply has historically been only slightly below that of July and usually front-loaded (Figure 7). Offsetting these headwinds for spreads has been the move higher in rates, with the JULI yield closing 7bp higher at 5.42%, and this continues to be the primary driver of spreads. Notably, spreads have shown a muted reaction to the recent steepening of the UST curve, but we think over time lower yields and a steeper rates curve should translate into a steeper spread curve (see Corporates).

High-yield bonds similarly outperformed the declines in equities over the past week, with spreads rising a modest 3bp over the week to 350bp, as the asset class continues to benefit from sound balance sheets, its largest stretch of inflows in eight months, and the lightest capital markets activity of 2024. Lower-quality bonds outperformed, with CCC spreads 4bp tighter while BB spreads were 2bp wider. Overall the HY index is returning +1.56% in July, the strongest monthly performance YTD, and +4.47% over the year thus far. Turning to high yield money managers, we note that cash balances increased 51bp q/q in 2Q to a 5-quarter high of 3.54%, the largest quarterly increase since March 2020, on the back of strong inflows and modest net new-issuance. Meanwhile, HY funds’ allocation to loans increased 35bp q/q to a 10-quarter high of 4.04%. Moreover, HY funds modestly reduced their historically conservative positioning by decreasing BB exposure by 41bp and increasing exposure to CCCs by 30bp over the quarter (see High Yield).

Our CLO strategists review their recent investor survey, with three key takeaways. First, investors on average agreed with our YE24 T1 AAA spread forecast of 130bp, leaving spreads relatively flat over the course of 2H24 from their current 135bp. Second, most participants see relative value in CLO AAAs, but were divided across manager and duration tiers; overall more investors see value in longer new issue than shorter refi/reset and look for better value in T2/3 managers than T1. Third, investors seemed most interested in putting cash to work within the AAA to IG Mezz US Primary BSL market, but notably many funds reported low cash balances and this makes sense given the record pace of new issuance creation recently (see CLOs).
