# Assessing Trends in Domestic & Global High-frequency Prices

Global commodity prices have risen sequentially over the last 3 months. Favourable starting point of macro stability, prudent policy mix & nimble supply-side response have lowered vulnerability to such volatilities. We remain watchful of the evolving global & domestic price trends.

Trend in global commodity prices: Global commodity prices have risen on a sequential/absolute basis over the last three months, thereby reducing disinflation for some categories, whilst others have grown positively on a YoY basis. Indeed, the CRB commodity index has risen sequentially over the last three months after contracting in the previous six months. Similarly, the CRB food index has risen over the last three months, the CRB metal index over the last two months and Brent prices over the last four months. To be clear, on a YoY basis the CRB commodity index, CRB metal index, and CRB food index continued to decline at a slower pace, while Brent prices rose on a YoY basis over the last two months. These global commodity prices constitute \~13.2% of India's CPI and 33.8% of the WPI basket.

Domestic high-frequency food prices inching up: Food constitutes 45.86% of the CPI basket, & has an important bearing on headline inflation. At an aggregate level, high-frequency food prices have reflected a gradual uptick since Jan-24 in YoY terms, due to elevated prices of vegetables, pulses and cereals. Among others, while milk, sugar and spices reflect a moderating trend, oils & fats are declining at a slower pace. The positive prospects of the winter (rabi) crop harvest, coupled with a normal southwest monsoon and pro-active supply-side measures by the government, augur well to keep food price inflation in check. Thus far, the World Metereological Department (WMO) suggests an 80% chance of neutral conditions in April-June (as El Nino recedes) and a possibility of La Nina in 2H24 ( WMO, Mar-24). As such, we closely monitor any risks from idiosyncratic conditions on domestic prices.

Assessing sensitivity of higher oil prices on macro stability indicators: Global crude oil prices have crossed the US\$90/bbl mark from the trough of US\$74/bbl in Dec-23. Per our global commodities strategist Martijn Rats, OPEC supply constraints, downside to production in Russia, geopolitical tensions and a seasonal upswing in demand ahead, notwithstanding an equal pace of growth in global demand and non-OPEC supply, put upward pressure on forecasts, averaging US\$89/ bbl (vs US\$85/bbl earlier). See The Oil Manual: Incorporating Geopolitical Risk. Oil remains a key source of energy for India, with \~85% of India's oil consumption needs being met through imports. However, oil intensity has been declining in the last 10 years and is now tracking at 0.12, lower than the long-term average of 0.15. A prolonged increase in oil prices can, however, adversely impact inflation and the current account deficit with implications to monetary policy. Our View

Our view – Firming up domestic food prices and global fuel prices; watchful of further upside risks: In our base case we expect CPI inflation to track at 4.4% in 1HF25 and 4.7% in 2HF25, averaging 4.5% in F25. The disproportionately large share of food (45.86%) in the CPI basket, makes headline inflation susceptible to food price volatilities, as we witnessed in 2HF24. The government's preemptive measures to improve supply-side response have helped in allaying the unprecedented increase in food prices. Going forward, we expect a normal south-west monsoon, which should help tame food price inflation. Global oil prices, on the other hand, have risen by \~19.8% YTD and are likely to sustain between US \$92-94/bbl in 2Q24 and 3Q24. In our base case we estimate the current account deficit to track around 1.3% of GDP, assuming an oil price average of US\$89/bbl in F25e. While the starting point on macro stability remains favourable due to a benign trend in current account deficit and inflation, if oil prices were to sustain at US\$95bbl for longer, or further inch up, there could be a further delay to the start of a rate cut cycle (base case for 3QCY24).
