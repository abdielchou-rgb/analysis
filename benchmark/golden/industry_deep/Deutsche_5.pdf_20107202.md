# Copper mine supply vs demand signals. Yunnan restarts. ANTO. Valuations

## Global Copper: mine production stalling, demand signals subdued

Combined output across Chile and Peru, the world's two largest copper producing countries, was flat YoY in January, a sharp deceleration from 7% YoY growth in July last year, reflecting declining Codelco output and major projects reaching steady state in Peru. Following material downgrades to 2024 production in recent months, we forecast 2024 mine supply growth of only \~1%, leaving the market in a \~300kt deficit. On the demand side, reports of potential smelter cuts in China and excitement over medium term demand growth from data centres and electricity growth more broadly drove a rally in the copper price last week, however, near-term indicators in China remain subdued. The next few weeks will provide further evidence of the demand picture in China, with the risk of disappointment if smelter cuts are only limited and inventory draws are lower than expected. Nonetheless, we expect the concentrate market deficit to translate late into a tighter refined market in H2 as ex-China demand recovers (DB \$10,000/t copper in 2025) (link).

## Boliden CMD: still waiting for the (capex) peak

We upgraded the shares in September as a value and cash flow recovery call following steep underperformance in the summer of 2023. While we have waited patiently for the rebuild plan at Ronnskar, the expected FCF turnaround and deleveraging continues to be pushed out to well beyond H224. What has changed since September? (1) Capex for 2025 is now guided at >SEK12bn and could end up at >SEK13bn (consensus in September was 8.5bn and currently sits at SEK 11bn). (2) The Ronnskar tank house rebuild, whilst an essential, high return project, is costing >20% more than expected. (3) The backdrop for copper and zinc smelting margins has deteriorated and this should become more evident in the Q1 and Q2 results. (4) In the near term, Q1 is shaping up to be a challenging quarter due to weather and strike-related disruptions (link).

## Aluminium: Yunnan smelter restarts, inventories peaking

Multiple sources reported that smelters in Yunnan have received the green light to restart \~0.5mt of smelting capacity (total of 1.16mt offline). The restarts are underway, although the pace of the ramp up remains to be seen in the coming weeks. The remaining curtailed smelting capacity in Yunnan (0.6-0.7mt) could potentially return from May onwards (rainy season). With the restarts yet to flow through, aluminium inventories in China are showing signs of peaking. Inventories are now at 851 kt, up 360 kt from just before CNY (491 kt). So far, the inventory build compared to prior years is low and from the lowest starting point on record. Typically, inventories build by 400-500 kt and peak \~40 days after CNY (around now); a peak below 900 kt would send a bullish signal in our view.

## Week ahead

Multiple datapoints are expected over the course of next week including China solar and wind installed power generation capacity, China state grid investment, China CISA Daily steel production, L&M steel mills inventory, China Industrial Profits and China PMI index.

## Key datapoints & newsflow

ANTO has agreed to sell the water supply assets for Centinela to an unidentified consortium for \$600m (in-line with expectations). The consortium will continue to provide water to existing operations at Centinela and expand the system as required by the Second Concentrator expansion. The \$600m payment is expected to be received by ANTO later this year. China BF capacity utilisation rate for the week ending 14 Mar stood at 82.6%, down 0.5ppt WoW (peak of \~93% in late September), marking four weeks of consecutive decline. Steel margins increased WoW and are close to neutral levels. Iron ore port inventories increased by 1% WoW (142.8mt) and are up 4% YoY. China copper concentrate spot TCs increased 7% WoW (US\$ 12.2/t on 15th March), after hitting a new multi-year low last week. TCs are down 61% MoM and 84% YoY. China property sales increased 19% WoW and are down 58% YoY over the past four weeks.

## Commodity price and FX trends (Fig 9-16)

Base metals were mixed over the past week with ongoing uncertainty over the demand outlook in China post CNY holidays; aluminium (+2%) led the move higher alongside copper (+1%), while nickel (-3%) and zinc (-1%) decreased. From the bulks, iron ore increased (+1%) and coking coal (-4%) declined. Within Energy, European natural gas (+1%) and oil (marginally) increased and thermal coal (-3%) decreased. From the precious metals, gold (+1%) increased although palladium (-5%), platinum (-2%) and silver (-1%) decreased. Mining currencies broadly depreciated against the USD.

## MTM earnings and valuations (Fig 1-8)

2024 MtM downgrade risk for the majors has moderated with Anglo, BHP and GLEN close to neutral (previously -5-10%), although RIO (-10%) and Vale (-35%) are still facing significant downgrade risk following the downtrend in iron ore prices and higher freight rates. The base metal names have moved to positive upgrade territory (5-10%) following the copper price rally. Spot FCF yields (2024) are highest for GLEN (8%), followed by BHP (6%), Rio (5%), Anglo (4%) and Vale (4%). Spot EV/ EBITDA is lowest for Anglo and Rio (4.9x) followed by Glen (5.0x), Vale (5.2x) and BHP (6.3x). Our Mining Valuation Composite is at a 15% discount to trend levels.
