## The world at a glance

• A massive US rotation from Big Tech to small cap, for the second straight week…

• …and a massive unwind of carry trades that pushed the USD lower, especially against JPY

Meanwhile, US data continues to show more resilience than either China or the Euro area

A big week for US tech earnings, but also for the US macro outlook

• Making sense of it all

## An unprecedented rotation, that should stop very soon

Higher rate cut hopes in the US have sparked an unprecedented rotation into small cap firms <sup>1</sup>

This move has included a Big Tech unwind, NDX to RTY flows, momentum reversal, and SPW/SPX broadening

o This is a 3.5X weekly standard deviation rally in small caps, beating all other indices

US small caps have more levered balance sheets (than large caps) and more floating rate debt <sup>1</sup>

o This makes them more sensitive to rates than at most times during the last decade

o Small cap vol to S&P vol is near a 20-year peak, and far and away at the 10-year peak

We believe this is primarily thanks to a positioning-driven technical squeeze <sup>1</sup>

o The strongest spot up/ vol up dynamic in decades and much higher call to put volumes support this argument

o Similarly, flattening skew and short futures / CTA positioning going into the rally also imply technicals are at fault

• The record run-up means small caps have mostly closed their record discount to large caps <sup>1</sup>

o Their fundamentals are weaker, with deteriorating leverage profiles and lower forward EPS expectations

o We are skeptical of street forecasts that call for strong H2 margin pick-up in small caps relative to large caps

o We think this rotation has largely run its course, and do not expect it to continue this week

## US data resilience continues, despite the USD move last week

The US economy continues to be ‘steady as she goes’, despite fears of a sharp slowdown <sup>1</sup>

o Q2 GDP came in at 2.8% q/q saar (consensus was 2.0%) in its initial reading

o Domestic final sales contributed 2.7%, a bit stronger than the 2.5% seen in Q1

Personal consumption grew 2.3%, over consensus, and June’s strong retail sales suggests Q3 momentum <sup>1</sup>

o Stronger than expected core PCE inflation means June’s core PCE inflation could be as much as 28bps

o Q1 and Q2 core PCE inflation rates of 3.8% and 2.9% respectively are both well above target

• On the other hand, Euro area PMIs in July were below our and consensus forecasts <sup>1</sup>

o The consensus PMI only narrowly missed slipping into contraction (-0.8pt to 50.1pt)

Services and manufacturing surveys eased across sectors, and geographies (especially Germany and smaller countries)

o This week, we expect Q2 GDP to print a below-trend 0.2% q/q, and core inflation to decelerate slightly, rounding to 2.8% y/y

o China’s Q2 data has now missed consensus forecasts by a wide margin; we expect weaker manufacturing PMIs this week

Despite US resilience, USDJPY continued its sell-off last week; the JPY has now rallied 5% from its early July levels <sup>2</sup>

o We think there are limits to JPY strength; this weak USD move is largely technical, helped by money fleeing US Big Tech

o The resilience of US data, especially relative to Europe and China, suggests that USD weakness is unlikely to persist

## Making sense of it all

This is a critical week for US tech earnings, especially with the Nasdaq 100 close to its first correction in many months

o Microsoft reports this Tuesday, followed by Meta, Apple and Amazon over the next two days <sup>1</sup>

o Big Tech earnings will be closely scrutinized to see if the massive AI spend by these firms is delivering near-term returns

Last week was one of the busiest of the earnings season, with a third of market cap reporting <sup>2</sup>

o EPS beats in Europe in particular have declined, with companies sounding more cautious on the economy

o Q2 EPS growth y/y is at 2% in Europe, and 5% in the US; beats and margins have been better in the US than Europe

o In sum, Q2 earnings have not been very disappointing, but have definitely failed to impress so far

Our NFP payroll forecast for this Friday is 175k; this implies a 3mma of 200k per month <sup>3</sup>

o We expect the jobless rate to stay at 4.1%, and average hourly earnings to rise 0.3% m/m

o In sum, we expect the US payroll report to reinforce the narrative of steady US growth

o As a result, we expect the FOMC to do no more than hint at a September rate cut this week

Shorter US rates rallied after Dudley suggested a July cut; 5s rallied almost 10bp on the week <sup>4</sup>

o We think this makes no sense; nominal GDP grew 5.2% in Q2 and has averaged 5.8% over four quarters

o Treasury’s QRA announcement should have few surprises, but should remind investors of large US funding needs

o We have been short 5s from 4.13% a few weeks ago, and remain so

• The ‘quick US rate cuts are coming’ narrative has driven the recent equity rotation and USD move

o We believe this sentiment is now overdone, and expect the moves of the last two weeks to fade
