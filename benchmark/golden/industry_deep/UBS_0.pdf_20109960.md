# Hong Kong Strategy Positioning for a rate cut cycle

## Kick-start of rate cycle could be positive to risk assets in Hong Kong

UBS expects the US rate cut cycle to start in the upcoming Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) in September, with the Fed fund rate ending the year at 450-475bps and reaching 300-325bps at end-2025E. For Hong Kong, we expect a lower interest rate environment to be fundamentally positive as it would alleviate the debt service burden for households and non-financial corporates, injecting the overall economy with positive growth momentum. It should also drive fund inflows into risk assets in Hong Kong, potentially leading to multiples re-rating as dividend yields compress. Nonetheless, kick starting an interest rate cycle does not necessarily herald a complete resolution of the debt serviceability challenge facing highly geared corporates, especially for small- to mid-cap HK property developers. We maintain our cautious stance on owners of commercial real estate assets while the downgrade of these loans could increase the Hong Kong banks' credit costs. We remain selective and highlight our top six rate-cut beneficiaries (see Figure 3 for our screening criteria), namely PCCW (Buy), HKT (Buy), Cathay Pacific (Buy), Swire Properties (Buy), HKBN (Neutral) and Wharf REIC (Neutral).

## Lower interest rate presents potential earnings upside to HK companies

While the debt to GDP ratio of Hong Kong's non-financial sector has declined from its peak, it remains close to its highest historical level. As of Q224, the debt to GDP ratio of the non-financial sector (including households, corporates and the government) stood at around 3.6x, versus 3.5x in Q419. We estimate the total interest burden on the nonfinancial sector could be close to 10% of GDP in Q224, up 8% from end-2019. A lower interest rate environment should at least lower the finance cost borne by the nonfinancial sector, potentially driving better appetite for private consumption and corporate investment. A lower interest rate could also translate into immediate earnings upside, in our view. Out of the 31 Hong Kong non-financial companies we cover, 42% of their total debt is floating rate, with the earnings of the telecoms, airlines and property sectors most sensitive to a rate cut.

## Equity Strategy

## Top beneficiaries of a rate cut

While an interest rate cut would favour most HK companies, we remain cautious towards highly geared companies as challenges relating to debt serviceability and paths to deleveraging remain. We prefer companies with positive earnings sensitivity to interest rate cuts, above-median shareholder returns and modestly high gearing ratios (excluding the highest gearing quartile). On that basis, in our list of most preferred HK stocks we replace Sino Land with Swire Properties and remove Samsonite on its deteriorating growth outlook and absence of positive catalysts in the near term. In our least preferred HK stock list, we replace Link REIT with CKA, given its low gearing.
