# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## August 9

USTs twist-steepen in a quiet end to a hectic week; China bonds sell-off after strong local headline inflation; CAD bonds rally following a miss in jobs data; Fed's Collins says cuts are "appropriate soon"; Norway inflation eases; S&P 500 rebounds; DXY at 103.15 (-0.1%); US 10y at 3.940% (-4.8bp).

• USTs twist-steepen and partially unwind the jobless claim sell-off driven in a quiet end to a hectic week with no US data or Fedspeak.

• China yields rise and USD/CNH weakens 0.2% following an upside surprise in China headline inflation in July, yet Core CPI slows and inflation remains low amid the continual weak demand.

• Canada bonds rally following a miss in the headline employment data which shows the second straight month of job losses; underlying details of the release, including a stable unemployment rate, offer a less bleak sentiment.

• Boston Fed President Collins says that if data continue as expected, it will be “appropriate soon to begin adjusting policy and easing how restrictive the policy is” adding that she is optimistic around growth; market pricing currently implies \~40bp of cuts in September.

• NOK weakens intraday after Norway’s core inflation eased to its lowest level in over two years, but a rise in brent crude oil prices ultimately support the currency (EUR/NOK: -0.3%; USD/NOK: -0.2%).

• After tumbling earlier in the week, equities continue to rebound and the S&P 500 (+0.5%) posts its best two-day run of the year, pushing the index to finish the week fairly stable.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

A hectic week ended with a fairly lackluster session across the globe. A bleak risksentiment had overcome markets on Monday as market participants began to fear that an imminent recession was a real possibility given the potential labor market deterioration that the US July employment report signaled. However, stronger-thanexpected data in the US including a beat in ISM services and better-than-feared initial jobless claims calmed the anxiety, and a relief rally primarily drove price action throughout the middle of the week.

After tumbling earlier in the week, global equities recovered a good amount of the losses. In FX, carry trade unwinds subsided and the USD ended the week relatively stable. And despite the flight to safe-haven securities at the start of the week, USTs ended the week cheaper as the curve bear-flattened with 2y yields up 17bp and 30y yields up 11bp.

On Friday, it was a quiet NY session as there were no major macro catalysts given the absence of economic data releases or fedspeak. USTs rallied in the NY morning alongside a temporary sell-off in the equity market. While equities recovered into the NY afternoon, USTs largely managed to maintain their gains. The front-end, however, was under pressure with a lack of clear drivers other than market fundamentals and technical factors. Light liquidity may have exaggerated the moves, and ultimately the 2s30s curve flattened over 7bp on the day.

Equities sold-off in the NY morning and were further weighed down by a large corporation announcing future layoffs. However, equities were able to rebound and nearly all sectors gained on the day. This pushed the S&P 500 to gain 0.5% on Friday and have its largest two-day advance of the year. Brent crude oil futures continued their gains and ended the day 0.6% higher which resulted in its first weekly gain in over a month.

While the tumultuous week ended on a more quiet note, next week is poised to be another eventful week with US CPI and Retail Sales on the horizon, in addition to a plethora of fedspeak, all of which may continue to give further guidance on the policy rate path ahead.

## Japan:

Following the bear-flattening pressure in USTs on the back of the smaller-thanexpected initial jobless claims on Thursday, short to medium term sector of the JGB curve began to sell-off in the Tokyo morning. Although the belly of the curve continued to sell-off throughout the Tokyo trading hours, real money market participants seemingly began to purchase the super-long-end given its attractive absolute yield level despite the looming risk of US economic slowdown.

Ultimately the JGB curve twist flattened. The 30y JGB rallied 3.0bp to 2.055%, even though it had traded at 2.23% before the 30y JGB auction on Thursday. By Tokyo close, 5y JGB sold off 3.5bp to 0.435%, and 10y JGB sold off 2.5bp to 0.855% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve bear-flattened and outperformed JGBs except for the 30y+ sector. XCCY basis widened especially in the belly of the curve. Next week, there will be the 5y JGB auction on Wednesday and the linker auction on Friday.

Market participants continued to feel relief around the conditions of the US labor market on the back of the benign jobless claim on Thursday, and USD/JPY jumped up +0.4% toward 147.82 in the early Tokyo morning. USD/JPY traded in range of 146.75 to 147.75 alongside the resilient Japan equity performance during Tokyo trading hours. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally traded in tandem with USD/JPY.

## Europe:

European FX trading was relatively muted given limited macro catalysts on Friday. CHF outperformed and strengthened 0.2% against the USD. NOK strengthened (EUR/NOK: -0.3%; USD/NOK: -0.2%) despite a downside surprise in core inflation, as Brent crude oil prices supported NOK. GBP was little changed ahead of a busy macro week in the UK (EUR/GBP -0.1%; GBP/USD +0.1%).

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar traded slightly weaker in the NY afternoon and the USD DXY Index fell -0.1%, as the JPY and CHF outperformed. The Antipodeans traded on the back foot, and underperformed in the G10 complex; AUD fell 0.3% while NZD fell 0.2%.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

The usual inverse relationship between USD/AxJ and equities returned for the first time in August, and AxJ FX gained as equities recovered amid lower-than-expected US initial jobless claims. USD/CNH weakened 0.2% to \~7.1735, aided by a lower USD/CNY fixing of 7.1449 (P: 7.1460) and an upside surprise in China July inflation data. MYR was the major outperformer in the region; USD/MYR was down 1.1% and returned to the August 5 low as local equity markets gained 0.4%. KRW also outperformed and rallied 1% to 1,364 as KOSPI rallied 1.2%, even though foreign equity inflows were muted at \$37mn. IDR and THB were underperformers, down 0.2% and 0.1%, respectively, though USD/IDR remained below 16,000. USD/INR

## remained largely stable at 83.94.

Most AxJ rates sold off in line with the Thursday overnight move in USTs. In China, yields rose \~3bp in 10y and 30y CGBs, and \~1bp across the CNY NDIRS curve, aided by higher-than-expected July inflation data and media reports of regulators asking state banks to record details of long-end government bond buyers on a daily basis. Hong Kong rates underperformed the most in the region; 2y swap yields rose 6bp and the 2s10s curve bear-flattened 1bp, in line with its correlation with US rates. Korea front-end rates also underperformed, with 1y KRW NDIRS yield up 3bp, likely a reflection of positioning. India G-Secs remained stable ahead of a bond auction; 10y yield edged up by 1bp. Indonesia rates outperformed and 10y IndoGB rallied 1bp.

## Economic Release Notes

## Developed Markets

• In Norway, CPI accelerated to 2.8% y/y in July (C: 2.8%; P: 2.6%) on the back of energy base effects. CPI-ATE fell to 3.3% y/y in July (C: 3.4%; P: 3.4%). Our economists anticipated core inflation to weaken in July, driven down by goods inflation, which has been the case. Both domestic goods (2.7% m/m) and imported goods (0.2% m/m) grew below seasonality. On the other hand, services inflation moved sideways (4.0% y/y) and remain fairly persistent, as prices grew above seasonality in July (0.6% m/m). The data comes 60bp below headline and 40bp below core Norges Bank projections, which in our economists' view speaks in favor of the cut coming a touch earlier than indicated in June (i.e., in December 2024 rather than 1Q25)

• In Canada, Employment fell by 2.8k in July (C: 25k; P: -1.4k) and the Unemployment Rate remained at 6.4% in July (C: 6.45) as the participation rate fell from 65.3% to 65%. Hourly wage rate declined to 5.2% (C: 4.8%)

## Emerging Markets

• In China, Headline CPI rose 0.5% y/y in July (MSe: 0.4%; C: 0.3%; P: 0.2%). This was mainly driven by food amid bad weather; egg, vegetables, and fruits prices rose meaningfully. Pork prices continued to climb on low supply. Core CPI rose 0.4% y/y in July (MSe: 0.3%; P: 0.6%), primarily driven by summer travel. Meanwhile, PPI remained soft at -0.8% y/y in July (MSe: -0.9%; C: - 0.9%; P: -0.8%) amid weak construction demand. Our economists maintain their GDP deflator forecast at -0.5% as strength in the July inflation data was largely transitory and narrow in scope (for more, read here)

• In Malaysia, Industrial Production grew by 5.0% y/y in June (C: 4.2%; P: 2.4%)

• In Thailand, FX Reserves rose to \$232.1bn as of August 2 (P: \$228.7bn)

• In Turkey, Industrial Production fell to -4.7% y/y in June (P: -0.1%)

• In Ukraine, CPI accelerated to 5.4% y/y in July (C: 5.3%; P: 4.8%)

• In Brazil, IPCA accelerated to 0.38% m/m in July (C: 0.35%; P: 0.21%) driven by food inflation and the impact from gasoline and electricity tariff hikes. On core, core services were softer than expected at 0.63% m/m (MSe: 0.70%). Our economists note this data endorses their call for unchanged rates at 10.50% until the end of the year

• In Mexico, Industrial Production fell to -0.7% y/y in June (C: -0.2%; P: 1.1%)

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
