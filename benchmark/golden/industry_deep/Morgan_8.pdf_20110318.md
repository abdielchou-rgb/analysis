## Developed Markets

## United States:

It was a fairly quiet session ahead of a busy week filled with central bank monetary policy meetings across the globe including the FOMC, BoE, and BoJ meetings. Price action in Japan and Europe was relatively muted with limited data catalysts and market participants seemingly waiting on the sidelines ahead of next week’s central bank meetings.

Speculative articles rejuvenated the discussion of a 50bp cut at the September FOMC meeting. In the NY overnight session, an article cited former New York Fed President Dudley, who said “there’s a strong case for 50” in reference to the size of the Fed cut at next week’s meeting. This sentiment was reiterated by former Federal Reserve Board economist Sahm who cited inflation progress and a slowing labor market as reasons for a larger rate cut. These articles followed another report released on Thursday which stated that policymakers were weighing the case for whether to cut by 25bp or 50bp.

For a number of market participants, these reports seemingly revived the possibility of a 50bp cut whose probability had been lowered post the CPI beat. This sentiment was reflected in price action; STIR pricing reached a market implied probability of a 40% chance of a 50bp cut. Front-end USTs repriced lower accordingly and the curve bull-steepened. The move spread to global duration as well, albeit on a smaller scale, as market participants contemplated the prospect of a larger initial Fed cut.

Data in the US also supported the rally in front-end yields. Import prices were softer-than-expected, but did not change our economists' core PCE forecast which they maintained at 0.15% m/m in August. Preliminary September University of

Michigan consumer survey showed that 1y inflation expectations fell for the fourth consecutive month to 2.7% (P: 2.8%), the lowest level since 2020. This came alongside a larger-than-expected rise in consumer sentiment with increases in both the current conditions and expectations indices.

USTs bull-steepened into NY close, and despite the beat in CPI earlier on Wednesday, USTs ended the week with 2y yields 6bp lower and 30y yields 4bp lower from last Friday. The USD weakened alongside the repricing in the FOMC OIS curve with the DXY USD Index marginally down on the week.

The case for a larger cut in September was positive for risk assets. The S&P 500 was up 0.6% on the day resulting in a weekly gain of 4%. The gains on Friday were broad-based with over 80% of constituents in the green. Sectors that would benefit from lower policy rates outperformed.

## Japan:

Following the steady performance of long-end JGBs on Thursday, JGBs continued to rally led by the 20y sector from the Tokyo morning. While there were no notable catalysts, the position adjustment was seemingly conducted ahead of next week’s FOMC and BoJ meetings. The trend continued and the curve bull-flattened into Tokyo close as domestic market participants increasingly added long-end JGBs. 2y JGBs were unchanged at 0.385%, 20y JGBs rallied 3.5bp to 1.635%, and 30y JGBs rallied 3.5bp to 2.01% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve also bull-flattened, but underperformed JGBs across the curve especially on the longer end. XCCY basis widened in the front of the curve, while the super-long sectors tightened. Next week there will be JGB purchase operation in the 1-3y, 3-5y, 5-10y, and 10-25y sectors on Wednesday and the TAP auction for 5-15.5y on Thursday.

Market participants priced in the chance of a 50bp Fed rate cut once again on the back of speculative articles from media overnight. Accordingly, USD/JPY ground lower in early Tokyo morning alongside the rally in UST yields. Real money demand from exporters might also have contributed to the move lower heading into the setting of the TTM rate at 10am JST. USD/JPY continued to move lower toward 140.65 at 1pm JST alongside the sluggish Japanese equity performance. While it bounced back towards London morning, the pair resumed to sell-off towards 140.40 as it aligned with the lower UST yields. The other CCY/JPY pairs generally traded in tandem with USD/JPY. Dollar bloc currency pairs (AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, CAD/JPY) particularly underperformed other G10 pairs.

## Europe:

In a data-light session, price action in European FX largely followed through on the trend it set into Thursday’s close, albeit, with less fervor. This translated to gains for NOK: +0.5%, SEK: +0.7%, GBP: +0.1%, and EUR: +0.1%. Interestingly, safe havens were undeterred by the rally in equities, as CHF gained +0.3%.

## Dollar Bloc:

There was a lack of major catalysts aside from comments by former New York Fed President Dudley and former Federal Reserve Board economist Sahm, who both arguably leaned towards a 50bp Fed cut as opposed to 25bp. US yields fell as Fed pricing for next week’s meeting slid back towards the middle of the 25-50bp range, and equities rallied. As a result, the DXY USD Index fell -0.3% by the end of London trading. The antipodeans underperformed with AUD down -0.2% and NZD down - 0.3%. Price action in CAD was choppier than the rest of G10 FX, but ultimately CAD settled at -0.1%.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies advanced amid a rally in USTs and a higher market implied probability of a 50bp Fed rate cut. CNH gained 0.3%, and broke below 7.10 alongside a lowerthan-expected USD/CNY fixing at 7.1030. Most other currencies in the region (SGD, IDR, THB, PHP) also gained \~0.2-0.3%. THB rallied as much as 0.5% intraday as gold prices hit a record high, before it gave back some gains after the BoT said it will ensure THB does not move too excessively. KRW was the best performer in the region, and rallied 1% to move back below 1,340 due to its high beta to USD, unaffected by \$830mn of foreign equity outflows, which was the largest since mid-March. MYR and TWD also outperformed, up 0.7% and 0.5%, respectively. INR was the relative underperformer amid the weak USD environment; USD/INR retreated just 0.1% to 83.9.

AxJ rates also rallied broadly following the move in USTs. China rates were the main focus as 10y CGB yields fell below the widely watched 2.1% level, and reached a new low of 2.07% ahead of Saturday’s release of August activity data. The CGB curve twist-flattened as front-end CGB yields rose 1bp while 10y and 30y CGB yields fell 3-4bp to new lows. In India, the 2s10s G-sec curve bull-flattened 1bp. Catalysts to front-end rates pulled in opposing directions with front-end rates torn between less supply, given that the RBI cancelled the remaining T-bill auctions for September, and the RBI continuing to signal no rush to cut rates. Hong Kong, Singapore, and Korea rates outperformed given their high correlation with US rates; 2y swap yields fell 8bp, 6bp, and 5bp, respectively, and their 2s10s curves bull-steepened.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• ECB President Lagarde referenced that there is a lot of data to come before the next meeting and added that if there is a significant change relative to the ECB's baseline they will reassess the path forward

• ECB Governing Council Holzmann said there could be room for another quarter-point cut in December and added that “monetary policy is now on a good trajectory.” He argued that 2.5% is “probably close” to the neutral rate

## Emerging Markets

• RBI Governor Das reiterated the importance for the RBI to stay on course and not get carried away by some dips in inflation. Even though India’s inflation has been brought within the target band of 2-6%, he stated that the RBI’s target is 4%. He also urged global monetary authorities to remain prudent and agile as inflation continues to pose a risk even if it is stabilizing in many places. He expects India’s economy to be able to grow at \~7% in the next few years, which he said is him taking a more conservative approach on growth. Regarding FX, he noted that the RBI does not have a specific level of when they would take action, and the authorities intervene in order to have an orderly appreciation or depreciation of the currency

• MAS Managing Director Chia said Singapore’s inflation levels for this year and next year will likely come within the MAS’s expected ranges, while growth this year will likely come in on the stronger side of their expected range. He also noted the importance of supervising the culture and conduct of banks, and added that re-examining banks’ stress tests is important in light of rapid digitalization

• BoT Assistant Governor Chayawadee said the central bank is closely monitoring the local currency, which has been quite volatile mainly due to external factors and gold prices. He said the BoT will ensure THB does not move too excessively to the point that businesses are affected by FX volatility

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
