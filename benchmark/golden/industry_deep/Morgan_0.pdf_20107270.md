## Globo

Stay tuned for better valuations

Globo reported a good quarter, where, despite lower revenues, EBITDA increased and FCF was healthy. Revenues of BRL 4,043 million were stable sequentially but 10% lower yoy, mainly on lower advertising and content revenues due to the absence of the World Cup. The lack of big events also drove a significant decline in transmission and exhibition expenses, which in turn drove a -29% yoy decline in total costs and took EBITDA to BRL 304 million, down 22% qoq but up from negative territory in 4Q22. Margins stood at 7.5%, down from 9.6% in 3Q23 but significantly better than the negative margin in 4Q22. We calculate that FCF after dividends was BRL 246 million in 4Q23, taking 2023 cash generation to BRL 243 million. Cash flows continued to benefit from financial income related to the company<sup>’</sup>s large liquidity position, which as of 4Q23 covered total debt by 2.5x. On valuations, bonds have had decent performance in the last month, relatively in line with similarly rated TMT peers, but continue to trade among the widest in the BB universe. Despite being optically attractive, spreads are near the LTM tights, and the relationship with TMT peers is relatively in line with LTM averages. Compared to the sovereign, the pickup of \~180bp is below LTM averages of \~250bp. Even though we see good carry, we don<sup>’</sup>t see a catalyst for near-term tightening from current levels, and therefore we affirm our Neutral rating.

Pay-TV subscribers continued to decline in Brazil, but Globo’s audience share metrics remained relatively stable. According to the company and government agencies there were 69 million homes with TV in Brazil in 2023, stable yoy but below the peak of 71 million in 2021. However, the number of pay-TV subscribers did fall to 11.9 million, from 14 million in 2022. In terms of market share, Globo<sup>’</sup>s share of the average audience between 7:00 am and midnight was 35%, which has been stable over the past 6 quarters. Globo continues to have a significant gap to its closest competitors Record (12% audience share) and SBT (8% audience share). On pay-TV specifically, Globo reports an audience share in prime time of 46%, stable from 2022. On the non-traditional TV platforms, the company reported that Globoplay had revenue growth of 23% yoy in 4Q23, with subscribers and hours consumed growing 11% and 21%, respectively. We do highlight that disclosure on Globoplay remains very limited, and although there are no financial details on it, management has mentioned that it will not be breakeven for at least a couple years.

Revenues declined on tough comps with 4Q22 given the World Cup. Globo reported revenues of BRL 4,043 million in 4Q23, stable sequentially but down 10% from 4Q22. The decline in revenues was mainly driven by a BRL 315 million fall in advertising revenues due to the absence of big events in the quarter. Additionally, Globo reported lower content/ programming revenues due to a lower base of subscribers in linear channels, partially offset by a larger subscriber base in Globoplay and other digital channels. Revenues were split between advertising (68%), content/programming (31%) and other (1%).The concentration on advertising revenues increased in 4Q23, from 61% of the total in 3Q23 and 66% of the total in 4Q22. For the full year, revenues of BRL 15.1 billion were essentially flat yoy, as a decline in content/programming revenues were offset by higher advertising and other revenues (these include sublicensing of sports events).

EBITDA came back to positive territory in 2023. Globo<sup>’</sup>s 4Q23 EBITDA stood at BRL 304 million, down from BRL 389 million in 3Q23 but above the negative number in 4Q22. The yoy improvement was mainly due to lower transmission and exhibition rights given the absence of the World Cup in 4Q23 and the renegotiation of other sports contracts. Globo also reported a decline in third-party services due to lower journalistic, travel, linguistic and technology costs related to the World Cup. In all, total costs and expenses were 29% lower in 4Q23 compared to the previous year. For the full year, total costs and expenses declined 9% due to the reasons mentioned above and partially offset by higher costs at Globoplay. As a reminder, Globo<sup>’</sup>s largest cost item is their transmission and exhibition rights, which represent 44% of total costs in 2023, down from 48% in 2022. EBITDA margin stood at 7.5% in 4Q23 and 8.2% in 2023, from negative margins in the same periods of the previous year. We do expect to continue seeing volatility in profitability with lower margins in 2024 as the company incurs higher transmission costs for big events like the Paris Olympics.

FCF was positive in 4Q23 and 2023 supported by financial income. Despite the volatility in EBITDA seen in recent quarters, Globo has been able to maintain positive FCF generation mainly driven by the income generated from its large liquidity position. We calculate that the company generated positive operating cash flow of BRL 534 million in 4Q23, up from BRL 264 million in 3Q23 and BRL 256 million in 4Q22. In 2023, cash from operations stood at BRL 1,015 million, mainly driven by net financial income of BRL 1,119 million, which offset BRL 465 million of taxes, BRL 843 million of negative working capital and BRL 339 million of cash interest expense. In 2023 capex (including software) stood at BRL 300 million, which represents an increase of 3% from 2022; we note that capex is relatively small as a percentage of revenues at 2%. FCF was BRL 715 million, which, after subtracting dividend payments of BRL 472 million, declined to BRL 243 million. Globo has accelerated shareholder returns this year, as 2023 dividends were above 2022 and 2021 payments of BRL 274 million and BRL 172 million, respectively. We also note that Globo mentioned that if the tax regime on dividends were to change, they could anticipate future payments and upstream a larger amount upfront, although we expect them to remain prudent and maintain a strong liquidity position.

Liquidity improved sequentially and remains a key strength of the company. Globo<sup>’</sup>s total debt improved slightly in 4Q23 due to the positive cash generation and small payment of financial debt and leases. Gross debt stood at BRL 5.6 billion, and it was mainly composed of the USD bonds due 2030 and 2032, a stub of bonds due 2025 and 2027, leases and a small balance of bank debt. The company<sup>’</sup>s amortization schedule is comfortable, with 78% of debt maturing in 2030 or later. Additionally, Globo has maintained a very strong liquidity position in recent years to mitigate the swings in EBITDA and overall challenges of the industry. As of 4Q23 the company<sup>’</sup>s cash and equivalents stood at BRL 1,431 million, and they also reported marketeable securities of BRL 12,602 million. Including marketeable securities, Globo<sup>’</sup>s liquidity covered their total debt by 2.5x. Gross leverage in 2023 stood at 4.5x, while net leverage including securities was negative.

Globo<sup>’</sup>s investment portfolio is generally liquid and generates income consistently. The largest component of Globo<sup>’</sup>s marketeable securities are government bonds, which pay a floating rate tied to Selic, Brazil<sup>’</sup>s standard interest rate. The portfolio also includes Certificates of Bank Deposits, which are issued by the largest banks and are fairly liquid; CBDs accrued interest at an average floating rate of 103% of CDI in 2023. Globo also has investment funds that have a more varied investment strategy and risk profile, including fixed income instruments, FX, stocks, commodities and others. While investment funds can be more volatile, these represent only 15% of the portfolio. Finally, Globo owns Financial Notes that are issued by financial institutions, which are fixed income instruments combined with floating rates. These notes are traded in the secondary market, and in 2023 accrued interest at an average rate of 107% of CDI.

Risks to our recommendation include: i) a more adverse economic environment in Brazil that could impact advertising revenues, ii) higher costs related to content production that pressure profitability, iii) slower-than-expected growth at Globoplay or higher competition from streaming platforms, and iv) unexpected cash uses such as acquisitions or higher dividends. To the upside, risk include: i) faster growth at Globoplay that drives content revenues higher, ii) a better economic environment in Brazil which leads to higher advertising revenues, and iii) higher profitability which results in stronger cash generation.
