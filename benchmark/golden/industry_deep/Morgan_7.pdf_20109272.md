USTs began to retrace Monday’s move and bull-flattened overnight. The rally was also supported by soft inflation indicators across the globe. In the UK, May Kantar data showed grocery sales rose at the slowest pace since June 2022, and the soft grocery price inflation aided a rally in gilts; 2y yields fell 8b and broke below 4%. June UK CPI data will be released on Wednesday and will be the final inflation release ahead of the BoE’s August meeting, where market pricing currently implies a \~50% chance of a rate cut.

In the NY morning, soft inflation data continued and Canadian June CPI surprised to the downside, decelerating -0.1% m/m (C: 0.1%; 0.6%) largely driven by a decline in gas prices. The trimmed mean and median CPI, the BoC’s preferred core measures, remained within its target range. This supported market participants' expectations of additional BoC rate cuts, and the probability for a July rate cut repriced higher to over 90% (P: 80%). Canadian rates rallied accordingly, spilling over into global duration as well.

However, data in the US pushed back against the decline in yields. June retail sales were strong across the board alongside upward revisions to May data. Headline retail sales were flat in June despite consensus expectations for a decline, and the control group retail sales grew 0.9% m/m (C: 0.2%; P: 0.4%). The strength was broad-based, with growth in e-commerce, home furnishing, electronics and appliances, personal care, apparel, and general merchandise. The data raised our economists’ real consumption forecasts and consequently raised their 2Q24 GDP, tracking to 2.2% (P: 1.8%). The Atlanta Fed GDPNow estimate for 2Q24 also rose to 2.45% (P: 2.02%). The retail sales data alleviated some fears of weakening in the consumer. June import data in the US was also above expectations, which resulted in a modest increase to our economists’ PCE forecasts.

USTs sold off following the robust data and largely erased overnight gains. However, into the NY afternoon, the curve bull-steepened, supported by market fundamentals. This also came alongside sizable block trades that positioned for increased and quicker Fed rate cuts. By NY close, 2s30s flattened by \~5bp in a reversal of Tuesday’s steepening.

The S&P 500 rose 0.6% amid expectations for Fed rate cuts, retail sales data signaling strength in the consumer, and better-than-expected bank earnings. The rotation to small-caps continued, likely led by foreign investors, and the Russell 200 Index rose 3.5%, with sizable action in overnight futures. Gold prices climbed alongside expectations of Fed rate cuts and rose 1.9% to record highs.

## Japan:

Despite the sell-off in USTs on Monday, JGBs rallied broadly in the Tokyo morning ahead of the liquidity tap auction for the 5y to 15.5y+ sectors. The rally in the belly of the curve seemed to be driven by a fall in market participants' expectations for a BoJ rate hike in July after USD/JPY dropped from its recent high, led by potential FX intervention. Given that there are no major JGB auctions for the rest of this week aside from the 5y GX bond auction on Thursday, the MoF’s tap auction had solid results supported by the demand to square positions even after the rally in the morning. Short and medium sectors continued to rally in the Tokyo afternoon, while super-long sectors slightly retraced their initial strength into Tokyo close. 5y JGB

rallied 2.0bp to 0.56%, 10y JGB rallied 2.5bp to 1.02%, and 30y JGB rallied 1.0bp to 2.16% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve bull-flattened but underperformed JGBs in sub-10y sectors. XCCY basis continued to tighten, led by the long end.

USD/JPY rose slightly after Japan’s national holiday on Monday, when USD/JPY had traded in a narrow range. The USD was supported by market participants' increased expectation of inflationary pressures related to the US election, as well as the potential demand from Japanese importers. USD/JPY rose to 158.7 in the London morning from \~158.0. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/ JPY. In the London morning, the BoJ’s daily current account data for July 12 was released, and it implied that the potential MoF intervention for that day was estimated to be JPY \~2.1tr. USD/JPY slightly declined after the report was released.

## Europe:

In European FX, CHF outperformed and strengthened 0.2% against the EUR and USD. Trading in GBP was muted ahead of the last UK CPI data release before the BoE’s August meeting. GBP remained close to its YTD high of 1.2995. EUR/USD was relatively flat on the day.

## Dollar Bloc:

The DXY USD Index ended the day roughly flat and reversed the gains it posted after an upside surprise in US retail sales. The antipodeans underperformed on Tuesday. AUD/NZD held steady ahead of New Zealand’s 2Q24 CPI report, while NZD (-0.4%) hovered around \~0.605. Market pricing is currently consistent with \~50% chance of a first RBNZ cut as early as August, after the July meeting showed a weaker tightening bias than some market participants had expected. A downside surprise in inflation in Canada failed to weigh on CAD, and USD/CAD traded almost unchanged in the NY afternoon.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

In a quiet session without much local data or major news flow, most AxJ currencies edged modestly lower amid continued USD strength. CNH was the underperformer in the region and weakened 0.2% to \~7.2850, as the USD/CNY fixing moved up for the first time since July 10, to 7.1328 (+15pips). TWD weakened 0.1% to 32.6, as local equities continued to see foreign outflows (US\$279mn). MYR and IDR also depreciated 0.1% to 4.68 and 16,180, respectively. THB and PHP were outliers. Supported by gains in gold prices (+0.8%), USD/THB declined 0.2% to 36.1. PHP also gained 0.2% against the USD amid limited local catalysts. INR, SGD, and KRW were relatively stable.

Unlike the FX market, AxJ rates rallied meaningfully following Fed Chair Powell’s comments overnight on Monday. China rates were the outlier, possibly aided by liquidity pressure from tax payments. Yields were unchanged across the CNY NDIRS curve as the PBoC net injected RMB 674bn of liquidity via open market operations, the largest amount since January. In the bond market, front-end (1-2y) CGBs rallied 1bp, while 10y and 30y CGBs sold off \~1bp. Indonesia was also a relative underperformer; 10y IndoGB rallied just 1bp ahead of Wednesday’s BI meeting.

Singapore rates outperformed, and yields fell 6-7bp across the IRS curve. The 2s10s SGS curve bull-steepened by 1bp as 2y bond yield continued to slide and fell by 8bp to 3.1%, the lowest level since May 2023. Korea and Hong Kong yields fell more modestly, by 4bp and 3bp, respectively, across the swaps curve. India rates also outperformed, and 5y NDOIS yield declined sharply by 7bp to 6.29% and the 1s5s curve bull-flattened 4bp, while 10y G-sec yield fell by a more modest 1bp to 6.97%.

## Developed Markets

• In the eurozone, the ECB Bank Lending Survey for 2Q24 was released. Credit standards for households and non-financial corporations were largely unchanged. Firms expect credit standards to ease slightly for non-financial corporations and to remain virtually unchanged for households over the next quarter. Households' loan demand rose for the first time since the July 2022 survey, while non-financial corporations' loan demand fell, but at a much slower pace than the previous quarter. Banks expect a further rise in household demand over the next two quarters. Our economists note that the survey results do not include much information that could materially influence the Governing Council's decision at this week’s meeting

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
