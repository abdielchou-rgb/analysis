# Commodity Market Positioning & Flows

Crude leads first weekly aggregate outflow of the year

• The estimated value of open interest across global commodity markets declined by 1.7% WOW to \$1.21 trillion, with declines across crude oil and agri markets more than offsetting gains across base metals (as of 16 Feb, Table 1). Notably, global commodity markets experienced net aggregate outflows for the first time since the start of this year (calculated across all trader types), totaling -\$21 billion over the week (Figure 1 204YTDgloba cmdites fwrp \$65.7 n x hky u ). The majority of the outflows were concentrated in crude oil markets at -\$19.4 billion, the highest since March 2022. Meanwhile, metals and softs markets registered modest outflows as well (16 Feb, Table 1).

The estimated value of the net investor position aggregated across commodity markets declined by -8.5% WOW, as shortening in notional net length across metals and environment markets outweighed net lengthening in energy and agri markets (13 Feb, Figure 3The valuoft ngisr pc bmdy k  -8.5%WO,     ). Investors lengthened their net long notional position across energy markets by \$7 billion over the week to a 14-week high of \$17 billion. Precious metals notional net investor length declined by 23% WOW (-\$7.7 billion) to the lowest level in four months while the aggregate notional net investor position across major base metals flipped from net long (+2.9 billion) in the previous week to a small net short (-\$2 billion) last week.

The estimated value of open interest across energy markets decreased by \$16 billion (-3% WOW) (16 Feb, Figure 6). The decline was primarily driven by net outflows of \$19 billion across crude oil markets, which was only partially offset by stronger price action during the week. Petroleum products saw an inflow of \$3.6 billion, however the estimated open interest decreased by \$0.8 billion, driven by weaker prices. Natural gas markets saw a relatively sizeable net inflow of \$2.7 billion, however this was more than offset by weaker prices as US Natural Gas prices continue a downward trajectory below 2 USD/MMBtu. Our strategists flag increasing storage congestion risks.

The estimated value of open interest in precious metals stabilized over the week at \~\$142 billion, remaining relatively unchanged from the prior week (16 Feb, Figure 8). Across all trader types, contract-based outflows totalled \$1.9 billion, driven mainly by gold. After a hotter than expected US CPI reading for January, the US rates market pushed out the timing and reduced the magnitude of expected Fed cuts, driving some short lived weakness in gold prices last week and contributing to a 50% reduction in Managed Money net length in COMEX Gold futures (as of 13 Feb) to just over 34k contract net long . Despite this, gold has remained more resilient than expected over the recent Fed repricing, leading our strategists to mark their 1H24 forecasts modestly higher while maintaining a bullish outlook with an eventual upside target of \$2,300/oz.

The estimated open interest value of base metals markets increased by 3% WOW to \~\$154 billion, the highest level YTD (16 Feb, Figure 9Thestimad vluofpn rc bk y 3%WO). Contract-based inflows (for all trader types) were modest at \$440 million, mostly from copper. Supply cuts are changing the narrative in base metals, with copper being the most impacted in the near term as further cuts to mine supply now result in a forecasted deficit refined market this year. While our strategists expect Chinese base metals demand growth to slow off an extremely strong 2023, they are not expecting a significant hangover in 2024.

The estimated value of open interest across environmental markets edged lower by -1% WOW to \~\$41.7 billion (16 Feb, Figure 7Thestimad vluofpn rc  k by-1%WO w J20). Contract-based inflows were \$796 million WOW, mainly absorbed by EUAs. Investments funds further shortened their net short position by 5.7k lots across EUAs over the week to -38.2k lots (as of 09 Feb).

Global agricultural market estimated open interest declined by -2.5% over the week to \$300 billion (16 Feb, Figure 10). Contract-based outflows were \$4.2 billion over the week, mainly from softs markets. Non-commercial investors’ net position across US-traded soybean markets remains seasonally weak at -162k lots, compared to the five-year average of 58k lots (as of Feb 13). Short covering risks lie ahead amid potential Brazilian production cuts, with the Brazilian Ag Ministry projecting 2023/24 production at 145 million tonnes, significantly below the USDA’s estimate of 156 million tonnes, as yields fail to recover as anticipated.

Price momentum generally increased across oil, petroleum products and metals markets but declined for natural gas, and agriculture market over the week (16 Feb, Table 2). Notably, the short-term lookback trading signals on COMEX Silver and LME Copper switched from sell-to-buy. The short-term lookback trading signals on ICE Brent and NYMEX WTI are approaching a “buy” switch.

Estimated open interest value is calculated as the number of outstanding contracts (OI) multiplied by the lot size and price of the commodity in USD terms. The estimated value of weekly flow is calculated as the weekly change in the estimated value of open interest – holding prices constant at the prior week’s level to strip out the price impact, and assign value to contract driven flows only.
