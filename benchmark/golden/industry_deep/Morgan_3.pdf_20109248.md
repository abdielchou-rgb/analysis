## Developed Markets

## United States:

Politics were at the forefront on Monday. As prediction markets increased the probability of a Trump presidency, trades that some market participants believe will benefit with this outcome gained ground. Long-term Treasuries sold off as higher term premium was priced in amid some market participants’ expectations for higher tariffs, looser regulations, and increased tax cuts. In addition, Bitcoin (+10%) and oilrelated companies gained as well. The Republican National Convention kicked off on Monday where former President Trump was formally named the Republican presidential candidate and Ohio Senator J.D. Vance was announced as his running mate.

Overnight, Treasuries twist-steepened alongside political events. Absent first-tier data, attention was focused on Fed Chair Powell’s interview that took place midday. While Powell was careful to not give any signal on the timing of future cuts, his overall tone was more confident than at the FOMC. He noted that the three most recent inflation readings add to the confidence that inflation is moving toward target. He also reiterated his view on labor markets coming into better balance. While UST yields initially rallied following his acknowledgement of the soft inflation data, they largely retraced the move given that the overall sentiment was fairly unchanged.

In STIR, additional rate cuts were priced in, particularly in the front end, as market participants weighed the rationale for a July rate cut. Market-implied pricing implied a \~9% probability of a July cut and 67bp of easing this year. Additionally, as market participants' expectations for rate cuts increased, demand dampened for bill auctions, with the bid-to-cover ratio for both the three-month and six-month auctions well below their six-auction average.

Equities in the US gained with the S&P 500 up 0.3%, led by energy companies driven by strength in oil-related companies, and by financials amid continued G-SIB bank earnings. US equities outperformed their European and Asian peers, which were dragged down by weaker-than-expected China GDP and retail sales. The weakness in Chinese economic data also weighed on oil, and Brent crude oil futures fell -0.2%.

## Europe:

Price action was choppy in European FX. EUR gapped lower overnight, only to end the day flat despite yield differentials moving in favor of EUR weakness. Big movers were found in Scandinavia, where NOK fell -1% and SEK fell -0.8% despite a lack of evident local catalysts. The large moves in the Scandies were likely exacerbated by poor liquidity. CHF was relatively stable and ended the London session down -0.1%. GBP followed yield differentials and also closed down -0.1%.

## Dollar Bloc:

The antipodeans made decent moves on Monday; AUD fell -0.3% despite strong equity performance, and NZD fell -0.6% as market participants awaited local CPI data. CAD weakened -0.4% against the US dollar as inflation expectations continued

to moderate and the Canadian labor market loosened. Despite some intraday volatility, the DXY USD Index gained 0.1%.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies weakened amid a rally in USD and weaker-than-expected China activity data. USD/CNH rallied as much as 0.2% intraday at the start of the Asia session, but retraced the gains and finished unchanged despite weaker-thanexpected China 2Q24 GDP data and soft retail sales. KRW was the main underperformer given its high beta to USD; USD/KRW rose 0.4% to 1,382 despite flat foreign equity flows. IDR ended eight days of consecutive gains and fell -0.2% to 16,170 amid weak June exports. THB and PHP also depreciated by -0.2% to 36.2 and 58.5, respectively. INR, MYR, SGD, and TWD outperformed relatively and weakened by ess than 0.1% against USD.

AxJ rates saw mixed moves. China 1y NDIRS yield rose slightly by 1bp after the PBoC held the MLF rate unchanged, as expected, and net withdrew CNY 3bn of liquidity. The CGB curve flattened amid the soft June economic data; 30y CGB yield edged lower by 1bp to 2.46%. Singapore and Hong Kong were the main outperformers following Friday’s rally in USTs. In Singapore, the 2s10s IRS curve bull-steepened 3bp; 2y swap yield fell 8bp while 10y was lower by 5bp. The Hong Kong swap curve also steepened as 2y swap yield declined 6bp while 10y held steady. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve twist-steepened 3bp, though the fall in front-end yield was more modest at 2bp. The move did not capture reports released after the market close saying Korea’s ruling People Power Party (PPP) had asked the BoK to be cautious about interest rate policy that could stimulate house prices. In India, NDOIS yields were largely stable, while 10y G-sec yield fell 1bp to 6.97% amid higher-thanexpected June CPI released on Friday and lower-than-expected June WPI. Indonesia rates underperformed, and 10y INDOGBs sold off 2bp to 6.95%. In Thailand, 10y ThaiGB yield held steady at 2.61% as the government stated it has no plans for a supplementary budget for FY2025, though it could be done if necessary.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Fed Chair Powell did not give any signal on the timing of interest rate cuts but sounded more confident than at the FOMC, noting that there have been "three better readings" on inflation that “do add to confidence” that inflation is headed toward 2%. He also reiterated that the labor market is better balanced, and that if policymakers were to see "unexpected weakening" in the labor market, they could take action. On the neutral rate, he noted that it has probably risen from where it was prior to the pandemic

• San Francisco Fed President Daly said “inflation is coming down and it is doing so in a way that confidence is growing that we’re getting nearer a sustainable pace of getting inflation back down to 2%.” She added that she sees a policy adjustment "over the coming term" while also noting that they continue to need "a lot more information...before we can make any real

## determination"

## Emerging Markets

• The PBoC kept the 1-year MLF rate unchanged at 2.50% in July (C: 2.50%) and net withdrew RMB 3.0bn of liquidity via its MLF operation (C: RMB 13bn)

• BoK Deputy Governor Ryoo said future monetary policy will stay sufficiently tight while the BoK also closely monitors the conflicting relationship between policy variables such as growth, financial stability, and inflation slowdown. He highlighted the need to examine how rate cuts will affect financial stability through the currency market, house prices and household debt. He also noted Korea’s inflation rate is continuing to slow, but the BoK needs to see more evidence of the trend continuing

## Developed Markets

• In the US, Empire Manufacturing fell to -6.6 in July (C: -7.6; P: -6.0). Underlying details were soft with declines in employment and orders

• In Canada, the BoC released its Business Outlook Survey and the Canadian Survey of Consumer Expectations for 2Q24. They showed that inflation expectations declined “significantly” and firms’ expectations for inflation are in the BoC’ inflation control-range. Businesses report that the sales outlook remains “more pessimistic than average" which was similar to 1Q24. The labor shortages measure reached survey lows, consistent with evidence from recent job reports that the Canadian labor market is loosening

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
