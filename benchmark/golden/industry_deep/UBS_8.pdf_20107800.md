## Around Emerging Markets in 10 minutes

## UBS’ strongest views across the 22 biggest EM economies

In this note we synthesise UBS's macro, FX, equity, rates and credit strategy views on the 22 largest EM economies. We outline the core themes per economy, highlight our preferred trades, and identify key signposts and dates to monitor over the next 6m. See page 3 for our latest trade recommendations and pages 5-7 for 10 key charts.

## 3 key themes for EM investors over the next 6m

1) A cheaper currency in all but name? China's cocktail of outperforming export volumes and contracting export prices may pose similar challenges to the rest of EM as a weaker RMB. 2) EM's 'great monetary divergence' to the US is slowing, but may yet go beyond what markets expect amid different inflation drivers. Sharp FX depreciation is the key constraint. 3) EM growth proving resilient but has struggled to keep pace with the US thus far. Our export volume lead indicator 'disagrees' with the improvement in global PMIs by the largest margin in \~20 years. Bottom line: EM FX to lag, equities to stabilise, front end local debt (FX neutral) to outperform.

## FX: Even if US rates settle, a narrow path for EM gains. Fade EM rallies

We have highlighted weak risk/reward in EM FX this year. While pressure from rising US yields could abate, we don't anticipate this providing sustained support to EM, for 3 reasons. 1. EM FX carry hasn’t improved despite the rates repricing, and will likely weaken ahead - different output gaps, margins, and scope for core goods disinflation in EM relative to the US suggest greater pressure on EM central banks to ease, in our view, barring FX depreciation. 2. Implied vol sits at just the 14th percentile of its historic distribution; from these tight levels of risk premia EM FX historically struggled. 3. Under the hood of China’s strong Q1 real GDP lies weakening credit flow, capacity utilisation and export prices, posing challenges to trade growth in the rest of EM. We prefer idiosyncratic stories such as EGP and TRY, and recommend fading potential COP, THB, CZK, HUF and CNH rallies.

## Equities: OW China; rest of EM to see more modest gains

We have upgraded China to OW within MSCI EM amid resilience in internet/consumer earnings, increasing focus on “investor-oriented” capital market policy, and favourable risk/reward stemming from national team support and cheap valuation. UBS' year end target for MSCI China: \$65. Against this we have downgraded Korea and Taiwan to neutral after a strong run; tech valuation premiums there are at a decade-high to the rest of EM. Outside of China, we prefer Brazil, Malaysia & Poland, underpinned by resilient consumption. Weak risk/reward in EM FX is likely to be a constraint elsewhere.

## FI: Improving risk/reward in selected EM local debt, FX hedged

Front end EM local debt, FX neutral has been our preferred asset class for over a year but in January we noted valuations were "increasingly demanding... and risk/reward in a few markets ha(d) turned outright negative". We think the selloff is recreating opportunities in the context of our below-consensus views on inflation in much of EM (Latam, Korea, India, Czech/Hungary). While resilient growth and services inflation in much of EM today is a risk, we see potential information in economies such as Korea and Europe that are making significant progress in this regard. We like front end local debt (FX hedged) in Brazil & Korea, and now add India; we're cautious on US duration and the front end in Poland. In credit, valuations look expensive (EMBI spreads screen \~40bps / 1.5 s.d. tight), led by IG. Await better levels to re-engage here.

US growth and inflation have surprised to the upside this year, and the big declines in Asia and Eastern Europe inflation generally appear to be behind us. So, is EM's "great monetary divergence" with the US over? We don't think so. We see EM experiencing different inflation drivers to the US: output gaps, margins, proximity to weak Chinese domestic demand and export prices, and greater scope for core goods disinflation. UBS economics remains below consensus on inflation in several markets for year-end e.g. Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Korea, India, and Czech. Services inflation is sticky in many parts of EM, but we note substantially stronger progress in Korea and the Euro area than the US on this front in recent months.

Our lead indicator for EM export volumes are "disagreeing" with the global manufacturing PMI by one of the largest margins over the last 20y. Our indicator could be signalling that the best of the global manufacturing PMI upswing has passed. It may also be signalling that the trade intensity of global growth is weaker than in other cycles, amid a consumptionfuelled recovery in growth (which are typically less trade intensive).

Despite the sharp global rates repricing this month, the carry available in several EM FX "high yielders" has remained close to cyclical lows e.g. BRL, ZAR, HUF, IDR, INR. Alongside low vol risk premia, subdued trade growth, and our below-consensus outlook for monetary policy in much of EM, we therefore remain cautious in EM FX. We are more constructive in parts of EM equity and front ends in EM local debt.
