## March 7

ECB stays on hold; Powell says Fed "not far" from confidence to cut rates, USTs twist-steepen; JPY gains defy risk-on tone; USD pushes weaker; MYR gains with BNM support; Fed's Mester considering raising long-term fed funds forecast; DXY at 102.82 (-0.5%); US 10y at 4.083% (-1.9bp).

• ECB maintains the depo rate at 4.00% (C: 4.00%) and reiterates it will "continue to follow a data-dependent approach to determining the appropriate level and duration of restriction."

• USTs twist-steepen, front-end gains extend after Fed Chair Powell remarks the Fed is "not far" from getting confidence that it will be appropriate to begin dialing back the level of restriction.

• JPY (+0.9%) strength defies marked risk-on sentiment as Japan's largest union group wage demands eclipse 5% for the first time since 1994; market pricing of BoJ normalization in March increases.

• USD remains under pressure from further gains in equities and lower US yields, the DXY USD Index breaks below 103 and posts its largest two-session decline since mid-December.

• MYR (+0.7%) gains against USD as BNM reiterates the currency is undervalued and the authorities have taken actions to encourage foreign income repatriation by government-linked companies.

• Cleveland Fed President Mester states "strong output and employment growth could be an indication that the neutral rate of interest, which rose during the pandemic, might remain high" and later adds she is considering raising her long-term fed funds rate forecast.

The decline in Treasury yields continued but the curve bull-flattening of two prior sessions transitioned into a twist-steepener. Underperformance of the long end served to steepen the 5s30s curve to 17bp (+4.6bp d/d). Front-end gains (5y: 4.073%, -4.4bp) extended after Fed Chair Powell remarked the Fed is "not far" from getting confidence that it will be appropriate to begin dialing back the level of restriction.

The US dollar was pressured by both the risk-on tone and the decline in US yields; the DXY USD Index broke below 103 and posted its largest two-session decline since mid-December. JPY (+0.9%) led G10 gains and defied strong demand for risk assets amid reports Japan's largest union group wage demands eclipsed 5% for the first time since 1994. Against the JPY strength, the Nikkei (-1.23%) posted the second largest single-session loss of the year.

For Friday’s February nonfarm payrolls report, our economists are not forecasting the same strength as January. They expect total nonfarm payrolls rose by 205k (C: 200k; P: 353k) – in line with a market-based survey (206k) – and flat m/m average hourly earnings (C: 0.2%; P: 0.6%).

In the weeks that followed January non-farm payrolls, regional Fed Presidents Kashkari, Mester, Collins, and Barkin all posed the question if the neutral rate of interest in the economy has risen post-pandemic. Should February nonfarm payrolls not convey a notable loosening of the labor market, more Fed participants may offer comments like Cleveland Fed President Mester in a speech this afternoon.

She stated, "strong output and employment growth could be an indication that the neutral rate of interest, which rose during the pandemic, might remain high" and later added she is considering raising her long-term fed funds rate forecast.

## Japan:

Despite the rally in USTs overnight on Wednesday, JGBs continued to sell-off as market participants increasingly priced in odds of a March BoJ lift-off. BoJ board member Nakagawa did not suggest possible normalization at the upcoming March meeting and maintained a relatively cautious tone. However, market participants seemingly focused more on a media report that suggested government officials had green-lit BoJ normalization. The belly of the JGB curve led the sell-off, ahead of the 30y JGB auction. The 30y auction was weak, with a 37c tail, as domestic market participants were seemingly still reluctant to add duration exposure before the March BoJ meeting. JGBs sold off further after the weak 30y auction but gradually recovered alongside demand to buy the dip in the long-end; 30y JGBs sold off by 1.0bp d/d to 1.76% on a simple yield basis. 20y JGBs lagged ahead of the 20y auction on Thursday; 20y JGBs sold off by 2.0bp d/d to 1.76%. TONA OIS sold off, led by the belly of the curve as market participants priced in a slower but longer BoJ normalization cycle. Long-end JGB ASW was finally supported due to demand to buy the dip in the long-end after the 30y auction. XCCY basis tightened, led by the longend.

JPY continued to rally on a broad basis. The catalyst was a media report that suggested some government officials were in support of a near-term BoJ lift-off. This followed a media report from Wednesday, which suggested the possibility of

NIRP removal at the March BoJ meeting. From the Tokyo morning, USD/JPY sold off and ground lower to 148.50 in the Tokyo afternoon. Rengo’s strong wage growth target of 5.85% (2023: 4.49%) also seemingly encouraged speculation for near-term BoJ normalization. During London hours, USD/JPY continued to trade lower and broke below the 148.00 level. Other CCY/JPY pairs also traded lower alongside USD/JPY, and EUR/JPY fell near the 161.00 level.

## Europe:

The US dollar sold off across the board and the DXY USD Index closed the London session 0.5% lower. Risk-on sentiment dominated G10 currencies and equity strength lent support to strong gains in risk currencies. Although, funding currencies were also stronger; JPY (+0.9%) outperformed peers as media reports pointed to policy normalization in Japan.

The Scandinavian currencies benefited from equity optimism; both NOK (+0.8%) and SEK (+0.6%) gained. NOK/SEK rose, with NOK also supported by a small gain in oil prices, while SEK expressed a bit of caution ahead of the release of January GDP data. GBP (+0.6%) strengthened following release of DMP survey data, which market participants interpreted as too slow progress regarding inflation. CHF (+0.4%) exhibited modest gains against the US dollar and was resilient to a rise in SNB FX reserves. EUR (+0.4%) gained, despite a balanced ECB press conference following weak inflation and growth forecasts.

## Dollar Bloc:

Dollar bloc currencies benefitted from the risk-on sentiment: AUD (+0.8%), NZD (+0.7%), and CAD (+0.4%) all gained.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies rallied across the region as USD continued to weaken. Against the weak USD backdrop, CNH and INR underperformed and remained stable. Currencies that have weakened meaningfully lately, namely MYR and IDR, outperformed. MYR gained the most, 0.7%, to 4.70, as BNM reiterated the currency is undervalued and the authorities have taken actions to encourage foreign income repatriation by government-linked companies. IDR rose 0.3%, aided by a 10bp rally in 10y INDOGB. Despite TAIEX outperformance, TWD only gained 0.1%. THB extended recent gains, by 0.1%, amid the rise in gold prices.

AxJ rates exhibited mixed performance after the broad rally on Wednesday. China rates underperformed; the NDIRS curve shifted 2bp higher, aided by China’s stronger-than-expected January-February exports and reports that Chinese regulators had scrutinized regional banks’ bond investments. Korea rates also underperformed; the NDIRS curve shifted 2bp higher amid limited domestic catalysts. Singapore and India rates outperformed, as 5y swap yields fell 3-4bp and the curve bull-flattened. Malaysia NDIRS yields were largely stable after BNM kept rates on hold, in line with consensus expectations.

## Latin America:

Latam currencies were broadly stronger for the session. CLP (+0.3%) led peers and retraced some of the prior session’s losses, while BRL (+0.2%) also gained despite few local developments. MXN (+0.1%) and COP (+0.1%) were mostly unchanged for the session. Rates ended the session mixed, front-end TIIEs ended flat, despite generally positive CPI data and a rally in USTs. The CAM curve had losses in the front end (1y: +5bp), while DIs sold off after cautious comments from BCB member Guillen. The IBR curve sold off in the front end ahead of Colombia’s CPI release.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• BoJ Board Member Nakagawa spoke at the regional leader meeting in the Shimane prefecture. While she conveyed an optimistic view of the Japanese economy and inflation, she did not provide any insight for policy normalization at the upcoming March meeting. She said the economy and inflation have developed in line with the 2% price stability goal, but she noted risks to the outlook, including: 1) deterioration of the overseas economy, 2) geopolitical risks and subsequent higher commodity prices, and 3) deterioration of consumer sentiment

• The ECB maintained the depo rate at 4.00% (C: 4.00%). Unchanged from previous meetings, the pause was deemed warranted as rates have reached a sufficiently restrictive level and will contribute "substantially" to inflation returning to target. Further progress on disinflation was confirmed in the statement, as underlying inflation has continued to fall. As a qualification, domestic price pressures were characterized as high, due to strong growth in wages. Forward guidance remained unchanged, and the data-dependent approach was maintained. No reference to the rate cuts was made (for more, read here)

• In testimony to the Senate Banking Committee, Fed Chair Powell reiterated if the economy evolves as expected, rate cuts can begin this year. He expects the Fed will carefully remove its restrictive stance when the time comes

• Fed Governor Bowman said the current stance of monetary policy is restrictive, and is appropriately calibrated to reduce inflationary pressures. She noted should the incoming data continue to indicate inflation is moving sustainably to the Fed’s 2% target, it will eventually be appropriate to gradually lower the Fed’s policy rate to prevent monetary policy from being overly restrictive

• Cleveland Fed President Mester said monetary policy is in a good place to respond to risks, and she expects rate cuts later this year if the economy slows as expected

• Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) maintained its policy rate at 3.00% (C: 3.00%). The central bank expects Malaysia’s growth to improve in 2024, driven by a recovery in exports and resilient domestic spending, while inflation remains moderate. It reiterated MYR is currently undervalued and it will work with the government to encourage government-linked companies to repatriate foreign income. It highlighted structural reforms will provide more enduring support to the currency over the medium term

• RBI and BI signed an MoU to establish a framework that would encourage cross-border transactions using local currencies. All current account transactions, authorized capital account transactions, and any other agreedupon economic and financial transactions between the two nations are covered by the MoU

• MAS Deputy Managing Director Robinson said the benefit of Singapore’s policy regime is the MAS can alter domestic monetary conditions, i.e., exchange rate expectations, independently of the Fed. He expects the current policy setting will put S\$NEER on an appropriately restrictive path to ensure core inflation falls to 2% by early 2025. In his view, the cyclical neutral path of S\$NEER is associated with a positive rate of appreciation. He also argued against the view that the S\$NEER appreciation has weighed on Singapore’s exports

• HKMA advised banks to put in place effective risk-management procedures and controls to manage the additional risks when lending to property speculators

• BCB Member Guillen discussed the direction of the BCB’s terminal policy rate and argued it will have to remain contractionary as longer-term inflation expectations remain at 3.5% (target: 3.0%). In the shorter term, he stated the heavy forward guidance the BCB has provided restricts the board's flexibility and the decision to maintain it is made meeting by meeting. Markets have priced the BCB will likely diverge from its current guided pace at the June meeting (-36bp)

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
