# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 25

Strong Canadian inflation data push out future BoC cuts; USTs slightly cheaper; hawkish remarks from Fed's Bowman support USD; CNH and CNY underperform; JGBs bear-steepen even with strong 20y auction; BRL underperforms despite hawkish BCB minutes; DXY at 105.63 (+0.2%); US 10y at 4.248% (+1.6bp).

• Canadian inflation data for May surprise to the upside, sending CGB yields higher (2y CGB: +7bp); probability for a July rate cut reprices to \~15% from over 60% prior to the release.

• USTs close modestly cheaper as the sell-off following the higherthan-expected Canada inflation data is partially offset by an on-thescrews 2y auction, which is met with solid demand.

• Hawkish remarks from Fed Governor Bowman, who notes that she remains willing to raise rates should “progress on inflation stall or even reverse,” adding she does not expect any rate cuts in 2024, support USD rally (DXY USD Index: +0.2%).

• CNH and CNY relatively underperform AxJ peers and remain largely stable after the PBoC allowed the USD/CNY fixing to move higher to 7.1225 (P: 7.1201), and alongside the continued decline in local equities (CSI 300: -0.5%).

• JGB curve bear-steepens amid supply and demand concerns for the super long-end regardless of a stronger-than-expected 20y JGB auction, with a cut-off price of 96.40 (C: 96.30) and a tail of 7 cents.

• BRL underperforms (USD/BRL: 1.1%) despite hawkish minutes from the June BCB meeting, indicating policymakers increased their estimates of the neutral rate and some viewed upside risks to inflation.

Despite staying in a relatively narrow range, USTs traded choppily on Tuesday. Overnight, Monday’s bull-flattening trend persisted alongside similar moves in European duration, and USTs opened modestly richer. However, the trend reversed in the NY morning following hawkish remarks from Fed Governor Bowman, who noted that we are not yet at the point where it is appropriate to lower rates. She added that she does not expect any rate cuts in 2024 and reiterated a willingness to hike rates further should “progress on inflation stall or even reverse.” The hawkish remarks also supported USD, which accelerated its rally in the NY morning.

An upside surprise in May Canadian inflation, which showed a reacceleration driven by elevated shelter and services prices, spurred a global duration sell-off. Canadian government bonds led the move higher, and 2y yields rose over 10bp immediately following the release. Further, market-implied pricing for the BoC policy rate pushed out cuts with the probability of a July hike down to \~15% from \~60% before the data release. The sell-off in Canadian bonds spilled over to European duration, which pared some of its earlier gains, as well as accelerated the sell-off in USTs, which rose into the NY afternoon.

In the NY afternoon, the \$69bn 2y auction was on the screws with a cut-off yield of 4.706% following the rise in yields into the auction. The auction was met with solid demand, with a bid-to-cover ratio of 2.75x, above the last six auction average of 2.57x. Allotment to indirect participants rose to 65.6% (P: 57.9%), which was stronger than the 63.7% average of the previous six auctions. This was partially offset by a decline in the allotment of direct participants, which fell to 20.9% (P: 25.5%) but was in line with recent averages, leaving primary dealers with an allotment of 13.5% (P: 16.6%).

The solid demand from the 2y auction pushed UST yields lower in the NY afternoon and retraced earlier losses. However, into the 5pm close, yields reaccelerated their sell-off absent clear catalysts and ended the day \~1-2bp cheaper across the curve.

In a familiar trend as of late, technology stocks continued to drive S&P 500 (+0.4%) performance, and the gains in the technology sector outweighed the losses of over 75% of constituents. Brent crude oil futures (-1.3%) fell as the rally in USD and weakness in technical factors overshadowed rising geopolitical risks.

## Japan:

During the Tokyo morning session, JGBs began to sell off across the curve, led by the long end ahead of the 20y JGB auction. With USD/JPY remaining at elevated levels on the back of the higher-for-longer Fed narrative, it seems that market participants may also be concerned about further BoJ hawkishness.

The 20y JGB auction was stronger than expected, with a tail price at 96.40 (C: 96.30) driven by decent demand to square positions. However, the outperformance of the 20y sector was short-lived, and the long end gradually sold off into the Tokyo 3pm close. Supply and demand concerns, especially in the super long end, seemed to remain despite the MoF’s intention to reduce long-end JGB issuance. The JGB curve bear-steepened; 2y JGB was unchanged at 0.30%, 10y JGB sold off 1.0bp to 0.995%, and 30y JGB sold off 1.5bp to 2.190% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve twist-steepened and outperformed JGBs across the curve. XCCY basis widened

## in the front end of the curve.

In the Tokyo morning, USD/JPY moved lower from 159.70 to 159.20, aligning with the rally in UST yields overnight. Demand from exporters may have accelerated the move lower heading into the setting of the TTM rate. After the 10am JST TTM rates, USD/JPY gradually bounced back to \~159.45 and traded in a range of 159.30 to 159.50 during the Tokyo afternoon. AUD/JPY moved higher from 105.95 to 106.40 level into the London morning on the back of strong Australia June consumer confidence data. Other CCY/JPY pairs moved in tandem with USD/JPY. EUR/JPY moved lower from 171.40 to 171.00 into the London morning.

## Europe:

In G10 FX, the largest mover was NOK, which fell -0.7% following the worst credit growth numbers seen since the turn of century for Norway. Sweden, on the other hand, stayed true to its habitual US rates beta, and SEK fell -0.3%. USD/JPY continued to flirt with the 160 level and intraday had a couple of short-lived nosedives, but ultimately it closed the London session at 159.69, flat from the open. Sterling also ended the London session flat as it caught a bid as market participants continued to seek out short EUR/GBP positions. CHF gave up -0.2% to the US dollar.

Tuesday was another quiet trading session in London. Morning gains in European duration were partly retraced following the higher-than-expected Canadian inflation data. This provoked a roundtrip in duration as the 10y Bund closed relatively unchanged at 2.40% and outperformed USTs. Semi core spreads continued to drift tighter, and the 10y OAT/Bund spread outperformed peers and closed 1.2bp tighter to 75. The move had limited impact on the ASW complex, with the German complex marginally tighter.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar was broadly bid, and the DXY USD Index rose +0.2% during the London session on the back of hawkish-leaning Fedspeak, which pared rate cut expectations and sent UST yields upwards. AUD succumbed to the US dollar move and gave up -0.2% as investors await Wednesday’s CPI data, with NZD likewise down -0.2%. CAD pushed back on the US dollar strength following Canada's own hotter-than-expected CPI and closed the London session flat.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies rallied as risk appetite recovered. CNH was an outlier and ended the Tokyo session largely stable at \~7.2860 after the PBoC allowed the USD/CNY fixing to move higher to 7.1225 (P: 7.1201). USD/CNY also gained slightly by 0.04% to 7.2626 alongside a continued decline in the local equity market (CSI 300: -0.5%). This spilled over to a relative underperformance of the low-yielding currencies (THB, TWD, SGD), led by TWD, which weakened 0.2% to 32.45 as equities saw a third session of foreign outflows (\$370mn). KRW was an exception and gained 0.1%, similar to IDR and PHP which saw comparable gains as risk appetite recovered. MYR led the regional gains and advanced 0.2% to \~4.7050. USD/INR weakened slightly by 0.04% to 83.43 alongside continue foreign portfolio inflows.

AxJ rates saw mixed moves. China rates rallied given the weakness in the local equity markets; 10y CGB yields and the NDIRS curve moved lower by 1bp, while 30y CGBs rallied 2bp to 2.44%. Hong Kong rates rallied led by the 1y IRS, which fell 3bp even as 1m HIBOR continued to edge up to 4.71% (P: 4.64%). Singapore rates also rallied 3bp on the 5-10y part of the IRS curve, and the 2s10s curve bull-flattened 2bp. Indonesia rates gained for the second session, but at a slower pace compared to Monday; 10y IndoGB yield fell 1bp to 7.08%. India, Korea, and Thailand, rates sold off. In India, 10y G-sec yields rose 1bp to 6.98%, and 5y INR NDOIS yield rose 2bp. In Korea, front-end NDIRS yields rose by 1bp, while other parts of the NDIRS curve were more stable. In Thailand, NDOIS yields rose \~1bp.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Fed Governor Cook said that current monetary policy is restrictive and weighing on demand, and that it will be appropriate to reduce rates at some point. She expects three- and six-month inflation rates to continue to move lower on a “bumpy path.” She added that she would expect policy variation among central banks

• Fed Governor Bowman spoke on monetary policy and bank capital reform. On bank capital reform, she noted that in the absence of international coordination, there is a risk of a "regulatory 'race to the bottom,' as regulators compete to grow their banking and financial systems by lowering regulatory and supervisory standards below levels that are appropriate based on risk," and that in engaging with regulatory reform efforts, one must acknowledge the "benefits, costs, and trade-offs." On monetary policy, she expressed willingness to raise rates should “progress on inflation stall or even reverse,” and said she does not expect any rate cuts in 2024

## Emerging Markets

• BoT Assistant Governor Piti said the central bank is not in favor of raising the inflation target given that higher prices often hit the poor more than the middle-income group. He noted the BoT is “okay with the inflation developments so far” and does not want prices to go higher. He reiterated the current monetary policy stance is roughly neutral, and noted that if the digital wallet program can be implemented in 4Q24 as planned, Thailand’s GDP growth this year will likely reach 3% versus 2.6% predicted by BoT

• Minutes from the BCB June monetary policy meeting leaned more hawkish. The minutes noted policymakers “unanimously decided to interrupt” the easing cycle and showed that the board was united on “re-anchoring inflation expectations.” The BCB also raised its assumptions for the real neutral interest rate in its models to 4.75% (P: 4.5%). Moreover, the committee flagged the resilience of economic activity with stronger projections for this year, and uncertainties about the Rio Grande do Sul events' effects on inflation

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
