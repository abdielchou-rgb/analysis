## Heavyweight champions

Venu Krishna, CFA, BCI, US | Japinder Chawla, CFA, BCI, US | Rex Feng, BCI, US | Madhuresh Rai, CFA, BCI, US | Young Chen, BCI, US

Excerpted from Equity Strategy: Heavyweight Champions, published on March 14, 2024

Mega-cap Tech has driven a historically high degree of concentration in US equities, leading many to ask whether the rally is sustainable. A deep dive into our six-stock Big Tech group leads us to believe that, while not without risks, fundamentals remain supportive of Big Tech over the near to medium term.

Big Tech is driving a high degree of concentration in US equities. Whether by market cap or contribution to returns, the ascendancy of Big Tech (AAPL, AMZN, GOOGL, META, MSFT and NVDA) has boosted concentration within US large-cap equities well above the 90th percentile of long-term observations. For some investors, a fast and furious bull run driving an increasingly Tech-centric and top-heavy US stock market may be uncomfortably reminiscent of the Dotcom bubble. While it is true that the Dotcom bubble is one of the few comparable instances of the S&P 500 becoming so concentrated in terms of both returns and index weighting, we think US equities are much better supported in terms of fundamentals today, especially with regard to its largest Tech constituents.

Big Tech valuations reflect high-quality earnings growth, plus the scarcity of that growth elsewhere in the equity market. Yes, multiples are extended relative to other US equities, but they are nowhere near post-COVID highs, let alone the extremes of the Dotcom bubble. Meanwhile, Big Tech earnings grew 60%+ y/y in Q4 23, compared with ex-Tech's -2.4%; its margins are nearly double that of the overall S&P 500; and it is the only source of upward revisions to FY24 estimates. The AI boom is a real growth vector, but far from the only one. Large Tech companies have shifted from "growth at any cost" to a new phase of cost containment and focus on profitability, and Big Tech has scale advantage in a diverse set of end-markets leveraged to the US consumer, among the most durable segments of the global economy through the current cycle.

We view Big Tech as a six-stock microcosm of an increasingly Tech-centric global economy, rather than a monolith. The group is exposed to a diverse set of growth vectors and endmarkets. As AI, digital advertising and iPhone replacement cycles all have diferent drivers, we could see a modest reduction in concentration, driven solely by varying outcomes within Big Tech, even as the group's aggregate growth continues to outpace that of the broader equity market for the foreseeable future. Some of this is arguably taking place already, with Tech's Herfindahl-Hirschman index and Big Tech's pairwise correlations down moderately from the high levels seen in 2022-23. Also, concentration driven by six names is a far cry from past peaks, when one or two stocks accounted for 50%+ of a sector by weight. For example, GE represented more than half of the Industrials sector at the peak of its reign in 2000.

Risks to the group centre on out-year estimates, regulation and macro. Although unlikely to be a concern in the near term, there is less certainty in estimates as CY24 moves to CY25, particularly at the current run rate of earnings and capex growth. Regulatory risks are a current and potentially future overhang, regarding both AI and inquiries from competitive authorities, the latter of which GOOGL's TAC cases are perhaps the best current example. Finally, while not our base case, a major downturn in US consumption would likely be a significant drag on Big

Tech, given the degree to which it has underpinned the group's rally over the past year. Any inflection lower could be amplified by extended long positions among both active discretionary investors and systematic funds, as well as the historical tendency for correlations between Big Tech and the rest of the US equity market to increase sharply during major sellofs.

We expect value to continue accruing to Big Tech over the near term. Visibility into earnings growth for the next few quarters is good, and revisions for Big Tech continue to leave the rest of the S&P 500 in the dust. At the same time, correlations within Big Tech and between the group and the rest of the index are fairly low. While Big Tech shoulders its fair share of risks, we do not see anything to argue against the group's continued outperformance from a fundamental perspective over the near term.

Fed dovishness, a "soft landing" and strong risk asset performance continue to favour carry exposures. Structural US exceptionalism and the November election, however, point to upside dollar risks and a challenging year for open economies. We discuss the relevant channels of transmission and update our forecasts.

Goldilocks and low FX vol still favour long carry exposures: A dovish Fed and strong risk asset performance against a "soft landing" backdrop for the global economy continue to favour carry in select EMs (mainly LatAm). Further dollar strength versus G10s requires a dovish pivot from their central banks or an external vol generating shock. We judge both are likely in coming months.

## Our updated forecasts reflect upside dollar risks: The structural features of US

exceptionalism and faster disinflation outside the US entail larger dovish risks in the rest of the G10. A second Trump Presidency harbours bullish dollar risks via flagship policies, too. We envisage the rest of 2024 to pose challenges for the major open economies, with EURUSD in the 1.05-1.06 region in the coming 12 months. In EM, we expect LatAm to outperform in the next quarter. We have pencilled in EMFX weakness into the US election, and a rebound in LatAm and Asia after that.

Regional diferentiation creates RV opportunities: GBP remains the stand-out long in the G10 as we approach the UK's general election; and the CHF the better funder due to quick local disinflation. In EM, we expect central bank divergence to keep PLNCZK high and relative carry to support short TWDINR in Asia.

We are selective in EM rates: We are turning more cautious on the blind receiver; the last mile of disinflation will be challenging for EM central banks and margins of safety are smaller for FX stability. The key exceptions are cycles that are just starting or accelerating, owing to higher level of real rates or those that will prioritise slower growth. In some cases, however, we prefer to express this via short FX trades over rates.

## Europe

No cuts yet, but still a lot of liquidity

Emmanuel Cau, CFA, Barclays, UK | Matthew Joyce, CFA, BBI, Paris | Magesh Kumar Chandrasekaran, CFA, Barclays, UK | Arihanth Bohra Jain, Barclays, UK

Excerpted from No cuts yet, but still a lot of liquidity, published on March 15, 2024

Sticky inflation keeps rate cuts in the balance, and potential revision to the Fed's dot plot next week will be closely watched. Excess liquidity remains plentiful, however, which is supportive of asset prices. But given concerns about RRP depletion, Fed's communication about QT tapering will be important in coming months.

Fed cuts still in the balance. 'Why equities keep rallying?' is what many puzzled clients have been asking us this week. US CPI and PPI were once again hotter than expected, sending rates higher, while activity data was soft ish - not a great combo. However, equity markets don't seem bothered. It could be because the current data flow in fact maintains the 'not too hot, not too cold' status quo. With the Fed so far endorsing current market pricing of three cuts starting in June, investors continue to see the glass half full on the soft landing narrative. ## Relentless

Søren Willemann, Barclays, UK

Excerpted from European Credit Alpha, published on March 15, 2024

Credit spreads tightened relentlessly this week, with the rally in macro products stalled only by the hotter-than-expected US inflation data. We believe demand technicals will prevail, and are loath to turn negative on credit spreads in spite of the strong rally.

Credit spreads tightened relentlessly this week, with the €IG IEAC ETF spread 8bp tighter from Friday 8th to Thursday COB. The rally was not confined to credit, with the SX5E (at the time of writing) up 0.65% since Friday. But cash credit was the clear outperformer.

Only the US PPI data Thursday, following the CPI print earlier, caused the rally to stall, with CDS indices taking the brunt of the weakness: in contrast to the €IG ETF being 8bp tighter over the week, Main was slightly wider, reacting strongly to the PPI data.

US inflation data this week led our economists to estimate that core PCE – the FOMC's key inflation measure – accelerated 0.32% m/m in February. This highlights the risks of fewer rate cuts this year. We retain our call for the FOMC to cut three times this year (in line with current market pricing), with every-other-meeting cuts starting in June, but acknowledge that outcomes remain highly data dependent: the implied probability of a June hike is 67% now, vs 75% before this week's inflation data.

We interpret market strength in cash credit – past and future – as driven by a view that rates have peaked amid strong technicals: measured supply and strong yield-driven demand. The timing of cuts from the ECB, the Fed and the BoE is less relevant as long as they come, supporting the 'peak rates' narrative. Unless that changes (ie, concerns around no cuts are forthcoming), market strength could last. As such, even if the €IG spread at 113bp is inside our end-2024 upside forecast of 115bp, we do not turn short here.

The outperformance of IG vs HY in the rally (Figure 8) is also notable: in spite of essentially no supply in HY, IG credit has managed to outperform. This fits with our general view that €IG credit has better risk-reward, but could equally be a sign that, with refinancing risks lingering for HY, we can start to experience 'bull decompression' in the HY/IG cash space.

Looking more broadly at relative performance over the week (Figure 9), the underperformance of HY stands out further, with HY widening alongside AT1s. Xover has underperformed the most, not least thanks to increasing default risk concerns around INTRUM.

## Economic Outlook

## Connecting the dots

Christian Keller, Barclays, UK | Mark Cus Babic, Barclays, UK | Akash Utsav, Barclays, UK

Excerpted from Global Economics Weekly, published on March 15, 2024

Following robust Japanese wage growth and higher-than-expected US inflation prints, next week, we expect the BoJ to exit NIRP and the Fed to adjust its dot plot to show only two cuts in 2024. The BoE should hold steady with a cautious tone. On the data front, the focus will turn to China's activity releases, European PMIs, and UK inflation.

## From data time to decision time

## BoJ: A farewell to NIRP

In Japan this week, the first survey of shunto wage negotiations showed an average increase of c.5.3% (of which 3.7pp in base pay), the largest rise at this stage in over 30 years. The final result is likely to be smaller, as further rounds of surveys incorporate more smaller companies, but we still expect it to be 4.6%, sharply above last year's 3.6%.

We think these stronger-than-expected early shunto results are likely to push the BoJ to scrap negative interest rate policy (NIRP) at its meeting next week. This would make it the last central bank to exit negative rates, which it had introduced in 2016. As part of the exit, we expect the BoJ to shift the policy rate from the IOER to the O/N call rate, scrap its inflation-overshooting commitment (paving the way for gradual QT), and exit an already-diluted YCC while keeping its full range of JGB buying operations as a backstop.

## Fed: Connecting the dots

This week, US core CPI decelerated on the margin, but continued to round up to 0.4% m/m in February. This was driven by an upside surprise from core goods, which posted their first increase since last May. Although core services inflation moderated, price pressures remain elevated after smoothing through monthly data. Combined with stronger-than-expected PPI data later in the week, this implies core PCE probably rose 0.32% m/m in February, easing from the prior month, but notably firming from its pace at the end of last year.

At the same time, retail sales disappointed expectations, with the control group flat on the month. Although this should weigh on the strength of personal consumption expenditure in Q1, we note a number of particularities in this surprisingly weak print and find that most fundamentals continue to support a resumption of growth.

Although the Fed is all but assured to keep the federal funds target range unchanged, the recent price and activity data likely undermined the FOMC's confidence that inflation is on course for a sustained return to 2%, while the economy remains resilient. We expect the statement to be little changed, with the modifications focused on the recent evolution of the economy, but think that the median core PCE projection will be raised 0.2pp for 2024 and 0.1pp in 2025.

In line with this, we also believe at least three participants will see the need for tighter policy than previously expected, implying a 25bp move up in the 2024 and 2025 median dots. This would signal two cuts this year, followed by four next year.

Our baseline assumption remains the Fed will initiate an every-other-meeting cutting cycle in June, but with a growing risk of fewer cuts. Besides rates, the FOMC will also discuss the balance sheet, but we do not expect an announcement of immediate QT tapering.

## BoE: Still cautious

This week's UK January labour market data were soft across the board. Wage growth slowed especially in the private sector, in addition to loosening labour market quantities, which produced a further fall in the V/U ratio. Next week, we expect UK inflation to decelerate 0.5pp on both headline and core CPI, to 3.5% y/y and 4.6% y/y, respectively. Easing should be seen across services, core goods and food, all aided by base efects. The latter should contribute to a string of declines in UK CPI and core CPI over the next four months.

Based on these data (the MPC will have CPI at hand when they meet), we think the BoE will hold the Bank Rate at 5.25% in a three-way vote split and maintain a cautious tone. We do not expect any changes to key guidance from the MPC, though a particularly weak inflation print could lead it to soften its language further. Beyond next week, the evidence is building that disinflation is suficiently embedded for cuts to come in June.

## ECB: First hints of a new framework

In the euro area, the ECB presented high-level results of its operational framework review, with key decisions largely in line our expectations. The floor system was formalised, the MRR will remain at 1%, and the ECB will narrow the corridor between the deposit rate and the main refinancing rate to 15bp this September. Liquidity will continue to be provided through shortterm credit operations and, at some point in the future, structural longer-term refinancing operations and a structural portfolio of securities will be introduced. That said, details on the structural component, eg, the ultimate size of the bond portfolio and structural credit operations, remain scant. We do not expect this review to have implications for the ECB's easing cycle.

## In search of growth outside the US

As we highlight in our Global Outlook: The US, and then the rest, the growth dynamics between the US and most other advanced economies and China continue to difer significantly. As the US goes on growing above trend, Europe and the UK are only slowly escaping their shallow recession of H2 23, mostly still showing only stagnating activity. China's oficial growth rate reached just above 5% in 2023 and the target for 2024 is 5% again. However, many underlying activity data paint a more worrisome picture, as the downturn in the property sector continues to weigh on the economy (China Economics: The drag on the Dragon).

## China: All eyes on activity data

China's CPI returned to inflation in February, with a larger-than-expected print (0.7% y/y vs 0.3% y/y). While this was driven in part by the diferent timing of LNY, average CPI inflation across January and February suggests goods deflation persists and services inflation remains soft, despite some recovery. Importantly, the credit data disappointed, with total social financing growth easing to 9.0% y/y from 9.5% y/y, signaling continued softness in borrowing demand. Household loans outright declined, reflecting the deeper housing slump and weak sentiment. Slow progress on government bond issuance could further delay the efect of fiscal policy easing on the real economy.

Against this backdrop, next week's key data release will be the fresh set of China's activity data for January-February. We expect retail sales to ease to 6.0% y/y, due to base efects from last year's reopening, while sequential momentum could pick up modestly. We think industrial production could grow 5.8% y/y. We forecast FAI growth to remain at a low 3.0% y/y, in light of soft property investment amid falling home prices and sales. We think the 1y loan prime rate will stay at 3.45% following no change in the MLF.

## Europe and UK: Hoping for better PMIs

While not always tightly corresponding to economies' GDP growth rates, PMIs remain a key variable to monitor the momentum of activity. Next week, we will watch March euro area flash PMIs, which we expect to improve further, to 49.6 (+0.4pt), with gains in both sectors. We also expect the UK PMIs to edge slightly higher (53.1, +0.1pt).

Single Stock

American Healthcare REIT, Inc. (AHR; OW/Pos)

Initiating Coverage With Overweight; Attractive Entry Point Into Healthcare Real Estate 14 March 2024

Anthony F. Powell, Jason Wayne, CFA, Michael Stefany

U.S. REITs

We initiate coverage on AHR with an Overweight rating and an \$18 price target given its diferentiated operating platform, improving balance sheet, and favorable healthcare industry tailwinds. Trading at a discount to its diversified healthcare peers, AHR ofers investors an attractive entry point into one of the better positioned REIT subsectors. The company's Trilogy "integrated senior health campuses" provide medical services across diferent levels of acuity (from independent living to skilled nursing) at a single location, allowing residents to "age in place," and increasing retention. AHR builds new integrated campuses each year, and has achieved 11% yields on developments completed since 2021; we believe this approach ofers investors the opportunity to buy into a unique business model, which could ofer upside as new properties stabilize. On the external growth side, AHR also has an option to purchase a minority stake in Trilogy at an accretive price (we estimate up to 17% AFFO accretion) and can further consolidate senior housing, medical ofice and other sectors as its cost of capital improves.

IR day highlights TRI's transformation and approaching inflection point; upgrade to OW

14 March 2024

Manav Patnaik, Brendan Popson, Princy Thomas

U.S. Business & Professional Services

We have been constructive on the ongoing transformation at TRI, and IR day gave us a much anticipated look ‘under the hood’ to convince us that TRI’s content-enabled technology growth is still only in the early innings – and thus we are upgrading to OW with a new \$180 PT.

## Single Stock

Xcel Energy Inc. (XEL; OW/Pos)

Rationalizing Value Down South: Upgrade to Overweight

11 March 2024

Nicholas Campanella, Fei She, CFA, Nathan Richardson

U.S. Power & Utilities

We upgrade shares of XEL to OW from EW following sharp underperformance related to the Texas Smokehouse fire. Shares have underperformed XLU peers -18% YTD which provides an attractive entry point to own a high quality balance sheet, management team and decarbonization plan at a substantial discount to peers. While the Smokehouse event is likely to remain an overhang on the stock we believe shares more than discount the potential liabilities that could materialize from wildfire claims, and we have more comfort in stepping in now given the fire is 90% contained, with \~4-500 structures involved across the complex. Ultimately the liability from this event could potentially be fully absorbed by XEL’s current insurance of \$500mn for year 2024, in our view. We acknowledge an ongoing impact to XEL’s multiple from the wildfires and believe a P/E discount approach is now warranted. Even so, shares trade at a -11.5% discount to the large cap peer group which we see as attractive given 1) XEL's best-inclass balance sheet with 17-18% FFO/debt; 2) management; 3) new wildfire hardening programs to be introduced in Colorado & likely SPS; and 4) Catalyst path.
