# Moving to Neutral on the USD

The USD tried - and failed - to rally as falling rates and buoyant risk appetite drove Regime 1 in FX, generating universal USD weakness. We turn neutral (from bullish) on the USD but still see tactical upside risks given positioning, seasonality, and overextended momentum. Pivot short EUR/USD to short EUR/ JPY.

Homer Simpson was right: "Trying is the first step toward failure," said Homer. The USD tried to rally in early December but this proved short-lived. US data deceleration, coupled with the market interpreting a dovish pivot from the Fed, fueled a decline in US rates, in turn bolstering risk appetite. Falling real rates and higher equities is Regime 1 of our USD framework, which sees universal USD weakness.

USD strength is harder to envision: In our year-ahead outlook we said USD strength would continue, driven by growth outperformance, favorable rate differentials, and investor defensiveness. US data deceleration has compressed growth differentials, US rates have fallen further compared with peers, and investors appear far from defensive based on equity returns.

Turning neutral on the USD... We turn neutral on the USD as our conviction about USD strength has waned meaningfully. Investors appear to be adopting an 'early cycle' mentality where peak Fed hawkishness is sufficient to 'paper over' other risks. It's not so clear if previous USD-positive catalysts, such as upside surprises to US growth, downside surprises abroad, or an optical uptick in core PCE moving averages will be enough to weigh on risk and bolster the USD.

...but risks remain skewed toward USD strength: That said, we note three important (tactical) USD-positive risks. First, positioning has turned meaningfully USD-negative. Second, seasonality has turned from USD-negative in December to positive in the first quarter. Finally, the USD's selloff has overshot what historical relationships imply.

Pivot short EUR/USD to short EUR/JPY: The cloudier outlook for the USD doesn't change the fundamentals for the other G3 currencies, in our view. JPY should continue to gain as long as US rates are falling, regardless of the risk outlook. Meanwhile continued downside surprises in Eurozone data, particularly inflation, could lead EUR to re-emerge as a funding currency of choice given its lower carry versus the USD and risks from a dovish ECB pivot down the line.

"When the facts change, I change my mind." - John M. Keynes

"Trying is the first step toward failure." - Homer J. Simpson

When we published our year-ahead outlook in late November, we penciled in continued USD strength with the DXY peaking at 111 in the second quarter, which corresponded with EUR/USD falling to parity.

Since then, things haven't panned out as we expected. The USD tried to rally in early December but was overwhelmed by a shift into Regime 1 - falling real rates, positive risk appetite, and a universally weaker USD, particularly versus higher beta risk currencies like the Nordics and Antipodeans. As Homer Simpson once famously said, trying is the first step toward failure.

With the outlook for the USD getting cloudier, we turn neutral on the USD and close our recommendation to be short EUR/USD, though we still see risks asymmetrically skewed toward a (tactically) higher USD due to seasonality, positioning, and evidence that the USD may have overshot in its weakening trend.

Why did the USD weaken so much? With the benefit of hindsight, the fall in the USD is natural and easily explainable: US rates fell in real terms, in nominal terms, and relative to peers, while risk assets rebounded. A scenario where US real rates are falling and risk appetite is firming corresponds with Regime 1 in our USD framework, which sees widespread and meaningful USD weakness ( Exhibit 1 ).

The drop in US rates was meaningful and it fully eroded the 'term premium' shock seen over the summer ( Exhibit 2 ). With the US rate move outpacing those of its peers, aided by very strong 3Q economic data, the reversal has meant that US rates have fallen in excess of peers in both real and nominal terms ( Exhibit 3 ).

It is impressive how 'risky' the risk asset rally has been amid falling rates. For much of the year, investors have discussed the meaningful divergence between the S&P 500 (a valueweighted index) relative to its equal-weighted peer. The divergence was explained by the sizable outperformance of megacap stocks, while smaller index constituents, which carry much more weight in the equal-weighted index, failed to follow suit.

So the fact that the gains in November and December were largest in US small caps and non-tech stocks suggests that investors really have been taking a more optimistic view about risk, as opposed to simply buying a few securities for idiosyncratic, firm-specific reasons that move the index due to their size ( Exhibit 4 ).

Where were we wrong? Why? We pounded the table pretty hard that the USD would rally, particularly versus the EUR. We were wrong in recent weeks - mea culpa. A key element we thought that could catalyze EUR/USD selling was the risk that the ECB would sound more dovish versus market expectations whereas the Fed would push back on market pricing. This doesn't appear to have transpired.

We're still skeptical that the ECB will be able to maintain such a hawkish messaging in the face of such meaningful data weakness, particularly inflation. Its desire to see how wage negotiations play out before changing tack is understandable, but should wage pressures fail to materialize, market pricing could ultimately be validated by a faster pace of cuts beginning in the late spring.

But, until wage data becomes clearer, we may be too early. Similarly, the Fed may have pushed back on market pricing after the December FOMC meeting, but the deceleration in data, particularly inflation, may limit how much negative term premium can be removed from market pricing. That is, if investors assume the probability of any additional hike(s) is extremely low, then risks are skewed almost entirely toward rate cuts.

For the USD more broadly, in our year-ahead outlook we posited that strength would continue due to three factors:

1. US growth would outperform peers, particularly in Europe and China;

2. Rate differentials would remain favorable toward the USD; and

3. Asymmetric downside risks to growth, low absolute growth levels, and geopolitical risks would fuel investor demand for defensive assets.

So far it doesn't look like growth in Europe and China is improving much, but the deceleration in US data has been fairly pronounced ( Exhibit 5 ). This data compression has, as noted above, led US rates to fall faster than peers, reducing the USD's rate differential advantage.

Finally, demand for defense is much more limited than expected, as evident in the rally in various risk assets. This buoyant outlook is also reflected in financial conditions which, based on our US economics team's calculations, have eased the equivalent of about 150bp since November ( Exhibit 6 ). That level of implied-easing has likely reduced investor concerns of recessionary outcomes meaningfully.

We turn neutral on the USD...with a USD-bullish skew: With the factors we assumed would propel the USD failing to materialize, our conviction over USD has softened meaningfully. We turn neutral on the USD overall as we see the outlook for the greenback as less certain.

That said, we still see short-term, tactical risks skewed to the upside for the DXY for three reasons:

1. Positioning: Market positioning has turned short USD to fairly stretched levels

2. Seasonality: December seasonality for the USD is negative, but for the first quarter, particularly February and March, it turns positive

3. The USD has moved too much too fast: Based on historical relationships, the USD's response in recent weeks is overdone, suggesting room for a tactical correction

As seen in Exhibit 7 , market positioning based on our positioning tracker has moved from long to short and we are now near record-short levels. Such a quick and marked shift, particularly to near-record short levels, suggests that risks are increasingly asymmetric for a tactical USD rebound.

Seasonality also begins to favor the USD. Exhibit 8 shows the average monthly performance of the DXY and G10 crosses over the past 10 years by month. While November and especially December tend to be quite negative for the USD, the first quarter tends to see more USD gains (except for JPY). This suggests seasonality as a technical factor is turning from a headwind to a tailwind.

Finally, the USD appears to have overshot what fundamentals imply. The rapid shift in positioning likely explains this, but it also suggests there is scope for the USD to tactically correct higher ( Exhibit 9 ).

Exhibit 9 shows the actual DXY move over November and December compared to what would be implied based on the historical beta to the equal-weighted S&P 500, US nominal 10y, and DXY-weighted 10y rate differentials. The DXY has fallen almost double than what would be implied based on these univariate regression results.

Pivot short EUR/USD to short EUR/JPY: The cloudier outlook for the USD doesn't change the fundamentals for the other G3 currencies, in our view. JPY should continue to gain as long as US rates are falling, regardless of the risk outlook. Indeed JPY is the only currency to gain on average in both Regime 1 and Regime 4, both of which see real rates falling but have very different outcomes for risk.

Meanwhile continued downside surprises in Eurozone data, particularly inflation, could lead EUR to re-emerge as a funding currency of choice given its lower carry versus the USD and risks from a dovish ECB pivot down the line.. A key risk to this trade is that US and global data both rebound - supporting both EUR versus the USD and also the USD versus the JPY through rising rates in a risk-on environment.

• Trade idea: Exit short EUR/USD

• Trade idea: Enter short EUR/JPY at 156.10 with a target of 145 and a stop of 162

Given the cloudiness of the USD outlook, it may be too soon to formally revise our longterm forecasts. It's so early in the quarter and, as we observed last winter, market narratives can change quickly. A meaningful, sudden, and unexpected slowdown in US data could push us into Regime 4, our original expectation, which sees broad-based USD and JPY strength. Our call for EUR/USD falling to parity by end-March looks increasingly aggressive as time passes, and though we think it is too soon to formally revise the longterm USD call based on tactical price action, we will be monitoring markets carefully in the coming weeks.
