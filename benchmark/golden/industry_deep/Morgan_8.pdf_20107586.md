# Why slower loan growth and notably slower corporate deposit growth in March?

March TSF growth further slowed to 8.7% yoy, as expected, mainly due to slower RMB loans. Slower loan and corporate deposit growth could mean banks are reducing some self circulating loans, which could save capital and support bank NIMs. We expect TSF growth will slow towards 8% in 2024.

RMB loan growth further rationalized, which may not be a bad thing for banks. Headline TSF growth further moderated to 8.7%, in line with our expectations. New TSF, at Rmb4.87tn, is Rmb514bn lower yoy, mainly driven by Rmb656bn yoy lower new Rmb loans in March. New corporate loans added Rmb2.34tn in March, down 13% yoy, dragged by Rmb470bn lower mid- to long-term corporate loans yoy, itself likely driven by: 1) slower demand from local SOEs due to LGFV tightening; and 2) lower self circulating loans due to reduced window guidance to support headline TSF growth. We believe this could actually be good for bank NIMs as such loans lead to high-cost term deposits sitting at banks and negative spreads for banks. New retail credit was lower on a yoy basis vs. a high base in March 23, but both new short- and long-term personal loans seem normal on a monthly basis, indicating rational demand but less impact from window guidance.

Deposit growth trends show on-going effective stimulus in China, but further acceleration very unlikely. Corporate deposit growth moderated notably, partly due to lower deposits generated from self circulating loans. With the impact from self circulating loans moderating, the big gap between corporate deposit growth and household deposit as well as corporate loan growth effectively shows the impact of on-going China-style stimulus such as policy push for investments and related credit growth, which the market has taken for granted. While the market may be hoping for US-style QE, we believe China-style stimulus has been more effective, although now is running at a somewhat more modest pace from its historical peak and is being taken for granted. If China needs to fall back to USstyle QE, it could lead to effective tightening vs. the current model.

Support to credit growth from government bond issuance likely to last in 2024 but at a moderating pace. As we highlighted in our previous report, we expect government bond issuance to continue to serve as a major support to TSF growth in 2024, as indicated in the government budget plan. However, this support will likely be reduced with a slower growth rate, as evidenced in less government bond issuance YTD (new government bonds totaling Rmb464bn in March were Rmb137bn lower yoy; YTD government bond issuance of Rmb1.36tn is Rmb470.8bn lower yoy). We expect this trend will continue in 2024 if no additional bond issuance is announced.
