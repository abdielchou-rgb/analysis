## April 17

Belly leads Tsy rally, NY Fed OMO report puts portfolio composition mechanics in focus; TGA surpasses \$900bn; GBP gains after stronger UK CPI; BoE's Bailey instead looks to future CPI drop; Gilts bull-flatten; KRW gains on remarks from authorities; DXY at 105.95 (-0.3%); US 10y at 4.587% (-8.0bp).

• Treasury rally is led by the belly, a sharp widening in swap spreads follows release of the New York Fed's 2023 OMO report which suggests at the end of QT "MBS paydowns are reinvested into Treasury securities in the secondary market."

• The Treasury General Account (TGA) returns above \$900bn for the first time since May 2022 as inflows from individual and corporate tax receipts continue.

• GBP (+0.2%) strengthens after an equity sell-off tempers the early gains following stronger-than-expected UK inflation data.

• BoE Governor Bailey expects inflation data next month will show "quite a strong drop" due to the UK's particularly unique energy and household energy pricing system.

• Gilts bull-flatten and retrace early session losses as risk sentiment wanes into the London close.

• KRW (+0.6%) outperforms, as the Korean finance minister and BoK governor express “serious concerns” over the recent weakening and drastic volatility of the won.

## Japan:

As UST yields continued to grind higher, JGBs sold off across the curve. The BoJ conducted scheduled JGB purchase operations for the 1-3y, 3-5y, 5-10y, and 10-25y sectors with no changes to the purchase sizes. However, the result for the 10-25y sector was particularly weak. Thus, this sector particularly underperformed on the curve after the JGB purchase operations; 20y JGBs sold off by 2.0bp d/d to 1.665% on a simple yield basis. Front-end JGBs traded well, as market participants seemingly found it difficult to price in a sharper rate hike cycle; 5y JGBs sold off by 0.5bp d/d to 0.49%. TONA OIS bear-steepened with the belly of the curve better paid, likely a reflection of the elevated USD/JPY level. XCCY basis widened, led by the belly of the curve ahead of potential upcoming Samurai issuance.

USD/JPY traded in a range in the absence of major catalysts. As USD/JPY approached 155.00, market participants became increasingly cautious about risk of potential intervention by the Japanese Ministry of Finance and were reluctant to chase the pair above the 155.50 level. MoF officials remained quiet as they have now joined the G20 Summit held in Washington. If USD/JPY grinds higher, without major catalysts, we believe there is increased risk for MoF intervention even if MoF officials are at the G20 summit. From a practical perspective, the MoF can intervene in the FX market at any time.

## Europe:

The risk-sensitive, Scandinavian currencies, NOK (-0.4%) and SEK (-0.2%) were both weaker. GBP (+0.1%) closed the London session after an equity sell-off tempered the early boost from stronger-than-expected inflation data. EUR (+0.4%) and CHF (+0.3%) gained while the US dollar was weighed down by a faster drop in US yields than the rest of the G10. USD/JPY (-0.3%) fell after it traded unchanged for most of the session.

## Dollar Bloc:

Price action was mixed in the G10. The Antipodeans rallied; AUD (+0.3%) and NZD (+0.4%) strength came amidst wider yield differentials to USTs, despite a tough session for risk assets. Yield differentials appeared to dictate the price action. CAD (+0.2%) gained with the DXY USD Index (-0.2%) lower.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies were mixed after Fed Chair Powell signaled a delay to rate cuts but also limited the chance of a hike. USD/CNH declined 0.1% to \~7.2550 amid a largely stable USD/CNY fixing at 7.1025 and a media report that suggested the US had called for higher tariffs on Chinese steel and aluminum imports. CNH liquidity continued to tighten as 1m CNH HIBOR surged 22bp to 5.11%, a number near the highest since October 2018. KRW outperformed, up 0.6% to 1,387, as the Korean finance minister and BoK governor expressed “serious concerns” over the recent weakening and drastic volatility of the currency. TWD steadied at 32.5, aided by the recovery in local equities (TAIEX: +1.6%). THB underperformed peers, down 0.6% to 36.8, amid a decline in local equities (SET: -2.1%). USD/PHP rose 0.3% to 57.2, the highest level since November 2022, after the BSP governor signaled the recent PHP weakness is not large enough to derail monetary easing later this year. IDR continued to depreciate; USD/IDR rose 0.3% to 16,220. The India market was closed.

AxJ rates also exhibited mixed moves; most rates rallied across swap curves but sold off in bonds. Indonesia rates were the major underperformer; 2y INDOGB sold off by 18bp while 10y INDOGB sold off 6bp to 6.93%. In Thailand, 2y THB NDOIS yields fell 2bp, while the 2s10s ThaiGB yield curve bear-flattened by 4bp. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-steepened by 1bp while the KTB curve bear-steepened; 10y KTB yields rose 2bp. In Hong Kong, the swap curve bear-steepened 1bp given its high correlation to UST yields. China rates relatively outperformed; 30y CGB yields fell 1bp to 2.46% while other maturity bonds remained stable.

## Latin America:

LatAm FX strengthened on Wednesday as USD pared some of the week's gains. BRL (+0.95%) was strongest in the region despite a volatile session. MXN (+0.58%) and COP (+0.51%) also retraced some of Tuesday's move with few local drivers. Rates in the region were generally lower, led by the long end of the TIIE curve (10y: -20bp), while the CAM curve bull-flattened (1s5s: -7bp). Front end DIs underperformed, as positioning likely worsened Tuesday's move, while the curve twist flattened (January 2033s/2026s: -31bp).

## Emerging Markets

• BoK Governor Rhee said recent movement in the won is a bit excessive, and if necessary, the central bank will act to curb FX volatility as the BoK has the necessary tools to stabilize the market. Rhee attributed KRW weakness to geopolitical issues, USD strength, and weakness in other regional currencies like CNH and JPY

• BSP Governor Remolona stated recent PHP weakness is not large enough to impact monetary policy. The central bank's central scenario is policy will be eased in 4Q24, with a delay to 1Q25 if things are worse. He believes PHP is adjusting to some recent events, and the BSP tends to allow these adjustments to take place unless the moves are sharp. He highlighted the current policy rate is already tight. He noted there is a “good chance” the government will issue peso-denominated sukuk, with the issue potentially being made in small denominations

• Minutes from the April BCCh meeting were released and markets will likely interpret the tone as dovish. The board members discussed the eventually enacted 75bp cut as well as a more dovish 100bp cut given activity, in their opinion, is still weak. They also continued to sound unconcerned with possible FX pass-through to inflation. To be clear, these minutes are unlikely to move markets as the external situation is now in a quite different scenario (for more, read here)

• Banxico Board Member Heath sounded cautious about the continuation of easing from Banxico. He pointed towards sticky core services inflation and the government's expansionary fiscal policy as reasons to be cautious. Additionally, he reiterated Banxico's first cut was only an adjustment to keep real rates in their preferred range, not the start of a cycle, and if the Fed delays their first cut longer, this gives Banxico more time to act independently. Currently, have only priced in a full cut in for the August meeting (-25bp)

• BCB Governor Campos Neto came off as broadly balanced. He noted recent market moves, while unexpected, were mostly in line with the boards

expectations of greater uncertainty facing the factors that impact monetary policy. He mentioned while the changing of the fiscal target by the government is not ideal, there is no mechanical link between fiscal and monetary policy. Lastly, he continued to state the board still had some concerns on services inflation. Markets have currently priced in a deceleration to a 25bp cut in the May meeting (-24bp) despite the BCB guiding toward a 50bp cut in its last statement

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
