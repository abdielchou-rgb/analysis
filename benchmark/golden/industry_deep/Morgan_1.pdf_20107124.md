# Farewell, NIRP and YCC as Expected: March MPM Review and Market Implications

The BoJ has decided to remove NIRP, YCC, equity ETF purchases, and other unconventional policies as we expected. We discuss our BoJ outlook after Gov. Ueda's press conference and market implications on rates, FX, and equities.

## Key Takeaways

We maintain our existing call that the BoJ will raise the short-term policy rate again from 0.1% to 0.25% in July 2024.

We expect JGB to outperform OIS, and the curve to be under flattening pressure.

Easy monetary conditions would be supportive for JPY carry investors. Meaningful decline in USD/JPY is unlikely unless markets price in US hard landing.

■ We reiterate our overweight stance on Japan equities versus Asia & EM, with a base case target of 2,800 (+2%) and bull case of 3,100 (+13%) for TOPIX.

Favor ex-deflation plays for the longer term; names heavily owned by the BoJ may also receive attention with suspension of ETF purchases.

# Our BoJ Outlook after Gov. Ueda's Press Conference

## Takeshi Yamaguchi, Masayuki Inu

## Shifting from unconventional monetary policy to "normal" policy

Back to a "normal" policy framework: At the March Monetary Policy Meeting (MPM), the BoJ judged that achievement of the price stability target of 2% in a sustainable and stable manner had come into sight. It decided to remove its negative interest rate policy (NIRP), yield curve control (YCC), the inflation overshooting commitment on the monetary base (as it "fulfilled the conditions for its achievement"), and ETF and J-REIT purchases.

Purchases of CP and corporate bonds will be gradually reduced and discontinued in about one year. Gov. Ueda characterized the changes as a shift to a "normal" monetary policy framework in which the BoJ guides the short-term policy rate as a primary policy tool.

The content of the policy adjustment was mostly in line with what we predicted in our preview note (BoJ Watch: March MPM Preview: Farewell, NIRP and YCC(11 Mar 2024))( Exhibit 1 ). This was not the consensus view when we predicted a policy change at the March MPM as our base scenario back in January (BoJ Watch: Review of the January MPM and Outlook Report (23 Jan 2024)), but the markets have since fully discounted such action in light of the robust results of shunto wage negotiations and detailed media coverage ahead of the March Monetary Policy Meeting.

The BoJ included in the policy statement the sentence that "Given the current outlook for economic activity and prices, the Bank anticipates that accommodative financial conditions will be maintained for the time being", but we think that we need to pay attention to the expression "Given the current outlook　for economic activity and prices" and "for the time being". As Gov. Ueda implies in the press conference, this suggests that the future policy path would depend on developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions from the perspective of sustainable and stable achievement of the 2% price stability target.

## Changes in the Monetary Policy Framework (March 2024)

As recent data and anecdotal information have gradually shown that the virtuous cycle between wages and prices has become more solid, the Bank judged it came in sight that the price stability target of 2 percent would be achieved in a sustainable and stable manner toward the end of the projection period of the January 2024 Outlook Report. It considers that its large-scale monetary easing measures have fulfilled their roles, including the negative interest rate policy and the yield curve control.

With the price stability target, the Bank will conduct monetary policy as appropriate, guiding the short-term interest rate as a primary policy tool, in response to developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions from the perspective of sustainable and stable achievement of the target. Given the current outlook for economic activity and prices, it anticipates that accommodative financial conditions will be maintained for the time being.

Market mechanism to play a more important role in the formation of levels of longterm interest rates: The BoJ issued guidance stating that it will continue its JGB purchases with "broadly the same amount as before", which is about 6 trillion yen per month, as we expected. Judging from Gov. Ueda's press conference, we think the BoJ will likely maintain the gross purchasing pace for the time being, which would maintain the size of the balance sheet as a trend. Although the Financial Markets Department can potentially adjust the pace in narrow ranges specified in its quarterly schedule of outright purchases, it will likely continue its JGB purchases at a roughly constant pace unless there are sharp fluctuations in the market.

This implies that the BoJ will allow more fluctuations of JGB yields and that market mechanism will likely play a more important role in the formation of levels of long-term interest rates. For example, the BoJ will likely not prevent a rise in 10-yr JGB yields if it happens on the back of a rise in US Treasury yields as long as the move is not disruptive. The (almost) constant pace of JGB purchases also implies that the BoJ will allow fluctuations of the monthly balance sheet size, as monthly gross JGB redemption schedule is volatile.

In the policy statement, the BoJ has a backstop sentence that "In case of a rapid rise in long-term interest rates, it will make nimble responses by, for example, increasing the amount of JGB purchases and conducting fixed-rate purchase operations of JGBs -- both of which can be done so regardless of the monthly schedule of JGB purchases -- and the Funds-Supplying Operations against Pooled Collateral". That said, we think the BoJ maintaining the fixed-rate operations is more like deterrence, and we expect it to be rarely used ahead.

Uncollateralized overnight call rate to be slightly below 0.1%: As for the guideline for market operations, the BoJ decided to "encourage the uncollateralized overnight call rate to remain at around 0 to 0.1%". The BoJ abolished its three-tiered current account structure and will start to apply 0.1% to the whole excess reserves, which was also in line with our forecast. While the textbook view is that the interest rate on excess reserves should set the market’s lower bound of the interbank rate at +0.1%, in Japan’s case, it would become the upper bound due to arbitrage transactions between financial institutions that are subject to the reserve requirement and those that are not. Depending on banks, the payment of interest on the whole excess reserves could significantly boost interest earnings when rates are eventually raised ahead.

## Our BoJ outlook after Governor Ueda's press conference

Our impression is that Gov. Ueda does not see a need to raise the short-term policy rate in a rapid and aggressive manner. For example, he denied intent to raise rates aggressively by mechanically following the Taylor rule, although stable and sustainable achievement of the 2% inflation target has finally come in sight. He said that current financial conditions are accommodative because the current (ex ante) real short-term rates are lower than the real neutral rate, even assuming that Japan's inflation expectations are around 1% or above, unless the real neutral rate is deeply negative, which he thinks unlikely. He reiterated that such accommodative financial conditions will be maintained for the time being, given the BoJ's current outlook for the economy and inflation.

Gov. Ueda also clearly stated, however, that the future rate path depends on the outlook of the economy and inflation. Our impression is that he did not exclude the possibility of rate hikes at a gradual pace, depending on the underlying inflation trend, upside risks to BoJ's inflation outlook, and the degree of wage hikes spreading to smaller firms. Gov. Ueda also said that the BoJ would react to FX (i.e., a weaker yen) if it should affect the outlook of domestic economy and inflation, which was an unusual comment as BoJ governor, in our view.

As such, we would like to maintain our existing call that the BoJ will raise the shortterm policy rate again from 0.1% to 0.25% at the July MPM. As Gov. Ueda said, we think the path ahead for the short-term policy rate will be data-dependent, hinging especially on the BoJ's underlying inflation data and nationwide wage data including SMEs.

We expect Japan's inflation to remain elevated in Apr-Jun 2024 on solid wage growth and the resulting rise in service prices. In addition, the BoJ will likely revise up its inflation forecast and emphasize the upside risk to its inflation outlook in the April Outlook Report, as strong results of Shunto (spring wage negotiations) will be

## reflected for the first time.

In the press conference, Gov. Ueda mentioned the possibility of firms' price and wagesetting behavior significantly changing as the main upside risk, when he was asked about potential risks to the outlook. He said that "the risk is not high at this moment, but we need to take it into account as a future risk". We think the BoJ may also show new statistical evidence of a virtuous cycle between wages and prices, potentially in the April Outlook Report, as we showed in our recent empirical research note (Japan Economics: Has Japan's Inflation/Wage Growth Finally Become Sustainable? Revival of Wage-Price Pass-through (14 Mar 2024)).

We expect Japan's inflation to gradually stabilize towards 2% in 2H 2024 and thus do not anticipate another rate increase in our base scenario. However, if the yen should remain weak and inflation domestically remains elevated, we would not rule out a further hike of 25bp to +0.50% in 2H2024 or 1H2025 as our risk scenario.

Note that we currently expect the first rate hike to come ahead of the BoJ deciding to reduce the pace of the gross JGB purchasing. We doubt there is a consensus about the timing of reducing JGB purchases within the BoJ at this moment. The timing would also likely depend on market conditions ahead.

## Limited impact on the economy

Risk of BoJ policy mistake has declined: We think risk of the BoJ making a policy mistake which results in a return of the Japanese economy to deflation has substantially declined. The BoJ did not have an explicit inflation target before 2013 and only clarified its understanding of price stability as 1% inflation goal "for the time being". The lack of an appropriate inflation target led the Bank into hasty shifts to tightening when inflation marginally turned positive. Since adoption of the 2% inflation target (the price stability target) in 2013, such shifts disappeared. The inflation target still ties the hands of the BoJ　to maintain its accommodative bias. This time, the BoJ waited to remove the NIRP until it confirmed two straight years of strong wage hikes in the spring wage negotiations. As a result, the Japan-style core CPI YoY remained at 2% or above for 22 consecutive months.

Japan’s monetary policy still accommodative: We expect impact on the economy from NIRP removal to be small enough to ignore. According to the BoJ’s Outlook Report from January 2024, medium-term expected inflation rates are roughly 2% for firms and about 1% for households. The "ex-ante" real interest rate, defined as nominal interest rates minus inflation expectations, is thus still substantially negative. We estimate that Japan's nominal neutral interest rate is about 1%. A 10bp hike of the uncollateralized overnight call rate from a range of -0.1% to 0% to a range of 0% to +0.1% keeps monetary policy accommodative.

No immediate impact on home mortgage loans: In Japan’s case, there is a framework that keeps policy rate hikes from immediately affecting floating-rate mortgage loans, and we advise caution toward discussions that ignore this institutional aspect. The first point is that many floating-rate mortgage loans are linked to the short-term prime rate charged by banks. However, the short-term prime rate has not changed at all since 2009, including after an introduction of NIRP in 2016. We think the short-term prime rate is unlikely to

shift until after multiple hikes of the policy rate and hence expect minimal impact on floating-rate mortgage loans ( Exhibit 2 ).

Another point is that many floating-rate mortgage loans contractually apply the so-called five-year rule and 125% rule. The five-year rule stipulates that the monthly repayment amount stays the same for five years through use of a reduction of the principal portion payment by an amount that offsets any increase in the interest payment portion. The 125% rule, meanwhile, limits increase in the repayment from the sixth year to 125% of the original repayment amount, although we do not expect the pace of the BoJ's rate hikes to be fast enough to hit the 125% rule.

Overall positive interest income in the corporate sector: As explained in a speech delivered by BoJ Deputy Governor Himino in December 2023, we think short-term impact on the corporate sector should be minor on an aggregated basis.

“Next, looking at the corporate sector, it has reduced its debt, has accumulated funds on hand, and has come to register a net interest income surplus. Therefore, the impact of rising interest rates on the net interest income of the overall corporate sector may be more limited than would have been the case when the sector had more debt and less funds. Of course, as some firms carry high debt while others have a significant amount of financial assets, the impact would vary across individual firms.” (Dep. Gov. Himino, Dec 2023)

While there might be impact on some SMEs and companies with large debt levels if the Bank raises policy rate further, we do not anticipate the start of a rapid rate-hike cycle by the Bank.

# Macro Strategy: Turning point for ASW

## Koichi Sugisaki

## Tailwind for cash JGBs

In an outcome that came as no major surprise after having been so heavily telegraphed in the media, the BoJ terminated its negative interest rate policy (NIRP), “yield curve control” (YCC) framework, and so-called “inflation-overshooting commitment”.

The central bank did however signal that it will leave its pace of JGB purchases “broadly” unchanged for at least 2Q 2024, and also offered (conditional) forward guidance to the effect that “Given the current outlook for economic activity and prices, the Bank anticipates that accommodative financial conditions will be maintained for the time being”.

The market focus at the press conference was 1) how the BoJ communicates the future policy trajectory, and 2) the future JGB purchase schedule. BoJ Governor Ueda suggested that any monetary policy adjustment will be conducted by tweaking the short-term rate. He also suggested that the Bank will set the short-term rate as an appropriate level in light of the economic and price outlook.

Regarding the necessary conditions for additional hikes, he explained that the Bank still does not see the 2% price stability target being achieved with 100% degree of certainty, although it already has strong conviction. He said that increasing certainty for achieving this target or further increase in underlying inflation　towards 2% would become the reasons to deliver additional hikes.

However, he also emphasized that the Bank will need to maintain accommodative monetary conditions as medium-term inflation expectations still have not anchored around 2%.

Regarding the future JGB purchases, he replied that the Bank will consider the balance sheet size reduction at some point in the future, while continuing to maintain the current roughly JPY 6tn purchase per month on a gross basis for a while. The important thing is that Gov. Ueda didn’t communicate anything about the reaction function for the balance sheet reduction.

All in all, there was no specific guidance for the timing of additional hikes and balance sheet reduction. Hence, we believe that the market will attempt to gauge them from the incoming data (service inflation, wage development, and inflation expectation) as well as external developments.

## Near-term view for JPY rates

Given that any further policy tightening would be conducted by the short-term rate going forward, we believe that overseas investors are likely to price in additional rate hikes in anticipation for larger-than-expected preliminary spring wage negotiation results and an elevated USD/JPY level making inflation more sustainable.

Hence, we see the risk for the short to medium-term that OIS could underperform. Losing incentives to use the BoJ's funding support program (as discussed below) and the

subsequent decline in banks' ALM receiving demand could also become reasons for weak short to medium-term OIS pricing.

In near-term, all eyes will now be on the March FOMC meeting, and our US rates strategists see potential for a Treasury market rally if the updated “dot plots” are basically unchanged from December or Fed Chair Jerome Powell reiterates that still-robust growth might not necessarily be incompatible with a cooling of inflation now that the recent influx of immigrants has helped to alleviate supply-side pressures. A decline in UST yields should be supportive for super-long JGBs (in particular) via the term premium channel (see Exhibit 4 ). As such, we see the risk of curve flattening in the near term.

Moreover, market participants may believe that there is still a large distance until the BoJ officially begins QT, as it didn’t communicate anything regarding the reaction function about QT, which is in contrast with the communication for the future rate hike. If the BoJ keeps the current JGB purchase pace of roughly JPY 6tn/month, the BoJ's JGB holding amount will be maintained on net (see Exhibit 5 ).

Hence, domestic investors might in any case be more inclined to buy JGBs. The initial market reaction to the announcement was in fact a rally led by cash JGBs, suggesting that domestic investors may indeed have regained somewhat of an appetite for duration.

But it is not as though today’s news was entirely positive. It will now be more expensive to borrow via the Loan Support Program and other funds-supplying operations, loan terms have been shortened to just one year, and the Interest Scheme to Promote Lending has been abolished.

The net upshot will obviously be a diminished incentive to participate and a corresponding decline in demand for short- to medium-term JGBs for use as collateral. That said, we basically expect supply/demand to improve for the sub-10y sector as a whole given that the BoJ has promised to (“broadly”) maintain its current purchase amounts for (at least) 2Q 2024 and fresh issuance is meanwhile set to decline from April as FY2024 gets under way.

All in all, we see the risk for recent cheapening of ASW to be reversed if domestic investors do indeed start to rebuild their duration positions in the JGB space, and overseas investors start to initiate paying positions in the OIS space in anticipation for additional hikes.

## FX market

USD/JPY actually rose in reaction to today’s BoJ announcement, with the widely expected “dovish hike” seemingly helping to convince market participants that JPY carry trades are in no immediate danger. Recent downward pressure on the JPY actually appears to have been amplified by (1) the lack of any indication that the BoJ might be in a particular hurry to tighten its policy settings as well as (2) a renewed rally in Japanese equities that will have left overseas investors with additional (JPY-selling) FX hedging needs. We basically see little prospect of this overall picture changing until and unless a US “hard landing” starts to be viewed as more likely.

We would expect a weakening of the greenback to drive USD/JPY temporarily lower if—as envisaged by our economists—the Fed this week leaves its dot plots basically unchanged from December and is thus seen as sending a somewhat dovish signal.

That said, markets are unlikely to price in a rapid rate cut trajectory so long as they continue to assume a “soft landing”, in which case downside for USD/JPY should be limited by dip-buying demand amid a sense that US-Japan interest rate differentials will remain sufficiently lucrative to carry traders for quite some time to come. Our basic expectation as of now is thus for USD/JPY to remain in elevated territory.
