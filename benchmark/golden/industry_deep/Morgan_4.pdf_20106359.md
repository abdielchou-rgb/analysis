Powell reiterated it is “not likely that this committee will reach that level of confidence in time for the March meeting” to begin rate cuts. He focused on weighing the risk of moving too soon against the risk of moving too late. Given economic growth at a “solid pace,” a strong labor market, and continued declines in inflation, he said the Fed can “reduce interest rates carefully.”

The UST curve moved in a bear-flattening manner throughout the overnight session until the NY morning when high grade corporate issuance announcements materialized, with longer-dated tenors included in guidance (10 borrowers combined to price \$18.1bn). An op-ed from Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari, which alluded to the possibility that the neutral rate has increase, also weighed on the long end.

Kashkari cited two significant economic developments since his last publications: (1) a more rapid than expected decline in inflation; and (2) remarkably resilient economic growth. He suggested that instead of monetary policy doing the heavy lifting to bring down, it appears “supply-side increases are boosting output and bringing supply and demand into balance, thus reducing inflation.

He cited the resilience of data in more traditional interest-rate-sensitive sectors of the economy (i.e., housing) led him to question how much downward pressure monetary policy is currently placing on demand. In his view, the constellation of data suggests the current stance of monetary policy (using the 10y TIPS yield) may not be as tight as in the pre-pandemic low neutral rate environment. Kashkari then suggested it is possible, at least during the post-pandemic recovery that “neutral has increased.”

The prevailing moves across macro markets became more defined alongside the 10:00am ET release of the January ISM Services Index. Economic activity in the services sector expanded for the thirteenth consecutive month as the headline index rose to 53.4 (C: 52.0; P: 50.5).

Key drivers of the rise in the headline index were large rebounds in the employment and prices paid subindices. The employment index returned into expansion at 50.5 (C: 49.4; P: 43.8) and retraced the steep decline in the prior month. Respondents commented on “ramping up head count as projects come on line.”

The prices paid index rose to 64.0 (C: 56.7; P: 56.7), the highest reading since February 2023. ISM Chair Nieves attributed the largest m/m increase in prices since August 2012 to higher shipping costs and across-the-board increases in commodities and services prices. Respondents specifically noted the “transportation impacts of the Suez Canal, due to unrest in the Red Sea and the issues at the Panama Canal are impacting both costs and schedules for the transport of global goods.”

After additional data, which reduced odds for an “unexpected weakening” in the labor market and indications of a more persistent stall in the core goods deflation trend, STIR markets continued to reduce the number of rate cuts (-113bp) priced in this year. The belly (5y USTs: 4.12%, +13.6bp) underperformed in the double-digit sell-off across the UST curve as intermediate forwards repriced higher. The sell-off in SFRZ5 (+14bp) implied fed funds to be \~3.50% by December 2025.

Fewer implied Fed rate cuts and a sell-off in US real rates translated to widespread US dollar strength against peers in the G10. In fact, the DXY USD Index (+0.5%) closed at its highest level since mid-November. USD displayed the most pronounced gains against risk-sensitive currencies, in particular SEK (-1.0%) and NOK (-0.8%).

Once again, market capitalization weighted indices (S&P 500: -0.32%) masked the acute pain under the surface. Over 80% of S&P 500 constituents closed the session in the red, with particular pressure on pockets of the market (REITs) sensitive to interest rates from a credit perspective (Russell 2000: -1.30%).

The January 2024 edition of the Fed’s Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey (SLOOS) showed banks reported tighter standards and weaker demand for commercial and industrial (C&I) loans to firms of all sizes over the fourth quarter. Banks also reported tighter standards and weaker demand for all commercial real estate (CRE) loan categories. For credit card, auto, and other consumer loans, lending standards tightened and demand weakened on balance. However, a lower net share of banks reported tightening lending standards than in the third quarter across all loan categories, which suggests standards on the margin became less pervasive.

A special set of questions inquired about bank’s expectations for changes in lending standards, borrower demand, and loan performance over 2024. This outlook showed some divergence across loan categories as banks expected lending standards to tighten for CRE, credit card, and auto loans but expect lending standards to remain basically unchanged for C&I and RRE loans.

Yet banks expected loan demand to strengthen across all loan categories due to an expected decline in interest rates. A significant net share of banks reported higher customer spending or investment needs, an expected shift of customers from other banks and nonbanks, and an expected decrease in precautionary demand for cash and liquidity as reasons for stronger demand over 2024. Although banks reported expectations of a deterioration in credit quality – measured by delinquencies and charge-offs – across loan types during 2024.

## Japan:

Strong US nonfarm payrolls drove a significant sell-off in JGB futures during the overnight session on Friday, and JGBs accordingly gapped lower early in the Tokyo session on Monday. JGBs sold off further from the open, alongside a sell-off in USTs, as Fed Chair Powell reiterated his view that a rate cut in March is unlikely. The belly of the curve particularly underperformed, and 10y JGBs sold off by 6.0bp d/d to 0.715% on a simple yield basis. Long-end JGBs traded relatively firmly amid an appetite to buy the dip at \~1.80%; 30y JGBs sold off by 4.5bp d/d to 1.790%. The JGBi linker auction was weak, with a tail price at 104.50 (C: 105.50)m and JBI 28 breakevens sold off by 6.1bp d/d to 116.2bp. TONA OIS sold off, led by the belly of the curve, alongside JGBs. XCCY basis tightened, led by the front-end.

USD/JPY surged toward the mid-148.00 level after strong US nonfarm payrolls on Friday, but USD/JPY traded rangebound \~148.50 throughout Tokyo trading hours on Monday, given limited catalysts. European CCY/JPY pairs ground lower, particularly from London trading hours following weak German data. EUR/JPY broke below the 160.00 level and trended down toward 159.50.

## Europe:

SEK was a laggard in the G10, with losses of 1% against USD, a result of its high sensitivity to US rates. NOK weakened by 0.8% against USD but was shielded from further losses amid the rally in oil prices. Despite BoE Chief Economist Pill’s balanced rhetoric, GBP (-0.7%) also depreciated against USD. GBP losses likely came down to the comparison between his balanced rhetoric on Monday and his more remarks considered hawkish by the market last Friday. EUR closed the London session 0.4% weaker, without local catalysts.

European duration continued to trade heavily on Monday. In the London afternoon, a higher-than-expected rise in the ISM Services Index drove a deeper sell-off and took 10y Bunds to \~2.31% (+7.5bp d/d). In cash, curves bear-steepened; 2s10s steepened by 3bp and 10s30s by 0.5bp. In core ASW, boxes flattened as ASW cheapened, led by Buxl and Bund ASW, -2bp and -1bp, respectively. In money markets, reds underperformed (+7bp). OIS now priced \~125bp of cuts in 2025, 20bp less than prior to the January FOMC meeting. In the G4, Spain outperformed its peers, 1.5bp tighter against 10y Bunds (10y BTP-Bund spread: -0.5bp) ahead of the 30y syndication.

## Dollar Bloc:

In London trading hours, G10 FX price action was driven by the US dollar. The DXY USD Index (+0.5%) rose to 104.42, its highest level since November 2023. General USD positive momentum was supported by higher UST yields after better-thanexpected ISM data.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly due to a combination of strong US nonfarm payrolls (released Friday), Fed Chair Powell’s reiteration that a March rate cut is unlikely, and a lack of announced specific government support measures for smallcap China stocks. Although small-cap stocks are down as much as 6-9%, CNH outperformed in AxJ, steady at \~7.2183. This was aided in part by the PBoC fixing CNY stronger than expected, below 7.11. MYR was the main underperformer in AxJ, down 0.8%, weighed down by USD strength and weakness in risk sentiment around China. KRW, THB, and PHP were all down \~0.7%. USD/KRW opened 1.2% higher at \~1,338 before gradually easing lower throughout the Asia session following the recovery in KOSPI. USD/THB ground higher throughout the session and decisively broke above its 100-day moving average (35.588) as gold prices moved lower and Thailand had deeper deflation in January.

AxJ rates sold off broadly following the rise in UST yields. China was the exception, as 10y CGB yields fell 2bp to a new recent low of 2.40%, and the 1s5s NDIRS curve bull-flattened by 1bp. Thailand rates also relatively outperformed; swap yields rose just \~2-3bp across the curve as Thailand’s January inflation came in lower than expected. Hong Kong and Singapore rates underperformed the most in the region, given their higher beta to US rates. The underperformance was most acute in the belly of the curve, as 5y swap yields rose 20bp and 15bp, in Hong Kong and Singapore, respectively. The rise in Korean yields were more in line with the rest of the region; 5y NDIRS yields rose 9bp, like the 8bp rise in India 5y NDOIS yields.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies largely weakened in a session defined by risk-off sentiment. CLP (- 0.9%) underperformed peers and broke through 950, while COP (-0.5%) weakened, with few local drivers, despite higher oil prices. MXN (+0.2%) outperformed peers and ended the session stronger, despite reduced liquidity. LatAm rates mostly followed USTs higher; long-end IBR sold off the most in the region (10y: +19bp). The CAM curve had losses concentrated in the front end (1y: +16bp), while DIs outperformed peers, 1-2bp higher across the curve. Mexican assets had reduced liquidity as local markets were closed in observance of a holiday.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Fed Chair Powell reiterated in a television interview that it is “not likely that this committee will reach that level of confidence in time for the March meeting” to begin rate cuts. Powell focused on weighing the risk of moving too soon against the risk of moving too late. Given economic growth at a “solid pace,” a "strong" labor market, and continued declines in inflation, the Fed can “reduce interest rates carefully.” Host Scott Pelley commented that Powell told him off-air the “likely time for the first interest rate cut would be the middle of the year”

• Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari said he believes in the post-COVID recovery period, it is possible the neutral rate has increased. As a result, this gives the Fed time to assess upcoming economic data before starting to lower interest rates

• BoE Chief Economist Pill cited weak UK economic activity and expressed a desire for more confidence in inflation progress, in order to reduce rates

• The Federal Reserve’s January 2024 Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey (SLOOS) suggested bank credit conditions had become less of a drag on business sector growth at the start of 1Q24, as there was less tightening and less decline in demand. However, headwinds for the consumer sector did not relent much. In our economists’ view, the implication from this survey is that the effects of the Fed’s earlier tightening may be having diminishing effects (for the business sector), which would support the fed funds rate staying “higher-for-longer,” rather than lowered prematurely

## Emerging Markets

• BI Governor Warjiyo highlighted room to ease in 2H24 will depend on IDR; the key is for IDR to strengthen in a consistent manner until it stabilizes around a level that indicates inflation is no longer a concern. He noted BI’s policy tools are currently geared toward growth except for monetary policy, which needs to lean toward IDR stability, due to global risks weighing on it. Warjiyo expects the BI’s macroprudential policy stance will remain expansive through 2025 to support the economic recovery, and may only shift to neutral in 2026 or 2027. He said the magnitude of rate cuts will depend on whether economic growth is driven by the supply side – which would allow more easing – or the demand side – which would signal higher risks of overheating

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
