China equities (Hang Seng: -3.71%) kicked off the Wednesday session with sharp weakness after a chorus of disappointing economic data in China. Quarterly GDP growth softened, with notable weakness in industrial production, while home prices registered the largest decline since 2015. Our economists view the size of reactive easing (Rmb1trn of special bond issuance) as insufficient to reverse the fiscal austerity. Weak sentiment around China weighed on the Antipodean currencies, AUD (-0.5%) and NZD (-0.4%), which lagged G10 peers all G10 peers except JPY.

An upside surprise in UK CPI ahead of the London open assured there would be no reprieve for risk assets at the start of the London session. Strong core CPI at 5.1% y/ y in December (C: 4.9%) induced a sharp bear-flattening of the UK gilt curve as market participants reduced this year's implied BoE rate cuts; SFIZ3Z4 steepened by 27bp, to -113bp. Declines in UK equities (FTSE 100: -1.48%) exceeded those of European peers (SX5E: -0.98%), whilst the largest rise in 10y gilt yields (+18.8bp) since February 2023 boosted GBP (+0.3%) even against a generally stronger US dollar (DXY USD Index: +0.0%).

The pronounced sell-off in UK gilts weighed on both US and European duration. Front-end losses across Europe were sustained amid comments from ECB President Lagarde, who acknowledged it is likely the ECB will cut rates in the summer but stressed the aggressive pricing of rate cuts are not “helping our fight against inflation, if the anticipation is such that they are way too high compared with what’s likely to happen.” Amid this market participants pushed out the pricing of the first full 25bp ECB rate cut to June from April. In the sell-off across the European government bond complex, peripheral spreads widened as 10y BTPs underperformed 10y Bunds by 3bp.

Impacted by the move in cross-market rates, USTs entered the NY session distinctly cheaper with the curve flatter. Moves would then become more pronounced alongside the 8:30am ET release of robust December US retail sales. This reignited the duration sell-off and the UST curve now bear-flattened as the long end turned lower for the session. The Atlanta Fed’s latest estimate of 4Q23 real GDP growth was revised up to 2.41% q/q (P: 2.2%). Amid this, short-term interest rate markets reduced the odds for a full 25bp rate cut by the Fed in March to near 50%.

With the underperformance of the 20y on the 10s20s30s butterfly near the middle of the 40-50bp range, where it has spent most of the past year, the \$13bn 20y UST re-opening auction produced a small tail. The auction stopped-out at 4.423%, a 0.8bp tail, although auction internals underwhelmed. A drop in the allotment to indirect bidders to 62.2% (P: 66.4%) and an unchanged allotment to direct bidders of 20.5% (P: 20.7%) left primary dealers with 17.3% (P: 12.9%), their highest allotment since November 2021.

US equities fared better than global peers after a rally in the last 30 minutes of trading brought the S&P 500 (-0.56%) well off session lows. Rate-sensitive stocks from a credit perspective (Russell 2000: -0.73%) still lagged broader equities, as the market continued to discount increased balance sheet stress from higher financing costs.

A lone bright spot for rates and equity markets was release of the BLS and Cleveland Fed’s new tenant repeat rent index. The New Tenant Rent Index (R-CPI-NTR) measures prices renters would face if they changed housing units every period, a subset of the data included in the official CPI reports. The latest new tenant repeat rent index release for the quarter ended in December 2023 registered a decline of 4.74% y/y, which suggests weak rental inflation ahead in 2H24.

## Japan:

JGBs initially sold off on Wednesday, after further weakness in USTs overnight on Tuesday, but later recovered. The combination of higher UST yields and a higher USD/JPY typically increases market discussion about a hawkish BoJ path. However, the support for tenors less than 10y suggested market participants are already biased toward short expressions; 10y JGBs sold off by 1.5bp d/d, to 0.605%, on a simple yield basis.

Long-end JGBs lagged, without support from domestic market participants, and the curve bear-steepened ahead of the 20y JGB auction on Thursday; 30y JGBs sold off by 2.7bp d/d, to 1.62%. Like USTs, there is not a specific buyer base for the 20y sector. Market participants only participate in this sector if they have a strong bullish conviction toward duration because of cheap valuations. We will closely monitor the result of the auction to assess market sentiment. Given the size of the 20y JGB auction will be decreased to JPY 1.0tn/month (P: JPY 1.2tn/month), we expect reasonable demand from various market participants. TONA OIS bearsteepened alongside JGBs, 30y+ sector particularly underperformed. XCCY basis widened, led by the 10y sector.

Broad USD strength continued after Fed Governor Waller advocated against quick and rapid rate cuts. USD/JPY continued its upward momentum throughout Tokyo hours and approached 148.00 in the London morning. Demand for JPY carry trades remains intact despite weak risk market conditions, as fair value implied by US-Japan policy differentials is \~141.80. European currencies performed well due to a combination of strong UK CPI data and continued pushback against market pricing of rate cuts by ECB officials; EUR/JPY approached 161.00 toward the London morning. Antipodean currencies lagged due to weak economic data from China.

## Europe:

GBP outperformed: it was the only G10 currency that managed to strengthen against USD following an upside surprise in CPI. Yields on gilts rose sharply (2y: +22bp). However, our economists note the underlying details of the UK CPI report were weak, and the beat was mainly driven by airfare prices.

Comments from SNB President Jordan on recent CHF strength fueled expectations the central bank may increasingly be comfortable with currency weakness. His comments followed the SNB decision in December, where the statement removed language around FX interventions. Both EUR/CHF and USD/CHF closed 0.4% stronger.

European duration opened lower after strong UK CPI data and as ECB policymakers once again stressed the importance of wages data. ECB President Lagarde even said the aggressive pricing of rate cuts is not “helping our fight against inflation, if the anticipation is such that they are way too high compared with what’s likely to happen.” Although, she said a cut in the summer “is likely” but added “I have to be reserved, because we are also saying that we are data dependent, and that there is still a level of uncertainty and some indicators that are not anchored at the level where we would like to see them.” Duration traded sideways until the US data releases. Retail sales were strong in December, with holiday spending for 2023 above expectations. Gains were broad-based, with some softness in home furnishing and restaurant sales (for more, read here). December industrial production also surprised to the upside, but the November data were revised lower.

Fed and ECB meetings sold off sharply, led by the late 2024 and early 2025 meetings. In OIS, the ECB April meeting sold off 12bp; the strip now implied only 135bp of cuts in 2024 (compared to 165bp at the beginning of the year). Curves bear-flattened; the front-end led the move cheaper in Germany; 10s30s flattened 5bp while 2s10s flattened 4bp. In swaps, 10s30s flattened to -22bp, a one-month low and 5s10s3s cheapened \~1.5bp. Core ASW cheapened, led by Bund ASW (- 1.5bp). Semi-core and peripheral spreads widened (10y BTP-Bund spread: +3bp) alongside a sell-off in equities (SX5E: -1%) and widening of credit spreads (iTraxx Xover: +7bp).

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar extended its rally as the same currency drivers from Tuesday’s trading session continued to move G10 FX, namely, a deterioration in risk sentiment and market participants' repricing of the implied Fed policy path higher. A string of weak economic data from China weighed on AUD (-0.5%) in particular. This data included disappointing quarterly GDP growth, the GDP deflator remaining negative, and housing prices falling the most since 2015.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies continued to weaken against USD on Wednesday as, equities sold off sharply and Fed Governor Waller pushed back against immediate rate cuts overnight on Tuesday. KRW underperformed as a high-beta currency. USD/KRW rallied 1% to 1,344.6 as foreigners withdrew US\$804mn from the local equity market, the most since early March 2023. This prompted authorities to label recent KRW weakness as somewhat excessive. The CBC also released a statement which highlighted TWD’s relative stability compared to regional peers. TWD continued its decline, down 0.6% to 31.6, as the equity market had US\$1.5bn of foreign outflows, the most since June 2022. MYR also underperformed, down 0.5% to 4.72. USD/CNH stood out as it finished the Asia session unchanged despite USD strength, soft China data, and a 2% sell-off in Chinese equities.

AxJ rates sold off broadly and curves bear-steepened following the overnight move in USTs. Singapore and Hong Kong rates underperformed given their higher correlation with US rates, as 10y swap yields rose 8bp. Korea 10y NDIRS yields rose by a smaller 5bp, while 1y yields rose just 1bp as upside from KRW weakness was offset by a continued decline in 3m CD fixing. In Indonesia, 10y INDOGB sold off 2bp, to 6.68%, as BI kept rates on hold as widely expected. China was the outlier as rates rallied, particularly in the long end of the curve given soft data and weakness in local equities; 5y CNY NDIRS yields fell 2bp.

## Latin America:

Most of LatAm FX extended Tuesday's losses, driven again by risk-off sentiment. MXN ended the session roughly flat weaker after initial declines of \~1%, while COP (-0.6%) underperformed peers despite few local drivers. CLP (+0.2%) strengthened in the session, following the government's announcement of a USD bond issuance of USD1.7bn. LatAm rates largely sold off, likely driven by core rates. TIIEs ended higher, with losses concentrated in the front end (2y: +10bp). The CAM curve also sold off (5y: +6bp), while DIs ended slightly lower in the very front end (January 2025s: -2bp).

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
