# Will a Cut Cut it?

Rate cuts are coming, but are they coming fast enough and big enough? Data have weakened in the US but the Fed looks set to kick off with 25bp. This leaves the risk backdrop fragile and investors lacking confidence. We move to bearish on EM credit and expect recent Asia FX outperformance to reverse.

FX & EM Strategy: Downside risks to the US economy have been met with more dovish commentary from Fed officials. Yet a 25bp start to the easing cycle amid labour market weakness leaves a fragile risk backdrop. We see risk that recent Asia FX outperformance can reverse. Downside risks to DXY from lower US rates should offset headwinds to some extent for EM FX.

Sovereign Credit Strategy: We suggest reducing risk further, turning outright bearish on EM sovereign credit in the near term and favouring IG over HY. Lower UST yields have been supportive so far but any further fall will likely be risknegative. We also don't see a first cut as the decisive factor for inflows to EM funds to restart. Away from the Fed, spread valuations are far from cheap. EM growth keeps moderating, with August data out of China set to be weak. EM fiscal data keep deteriorating nearly across the board. We expect issuance to remain high and, while this in itself should not reprice markets, it leaves technicals more vulnerable to any shocks. Finally, the uniform sentiment across other key asset classes is that the soft landing is priced, leaving risk/reward unattractive, including in US credit and US equities. We suggest selling EM CDX and remove like stances on Nigeria, Argentina and Morocco, replacing them with like stances on Mexico and Romania.

LatAm Macro Strategy: Local rates should grind lower amid Fed cuts, with steepening pressures across the board bar Brazil. We see important differentiation among countries, given stages of their own cycles and external imbalances. We add receivers in 1y IBR, Jan 29 DIs and 1y TIIE vs. SOFR, as well as long BRL/COP. In Mexico, we keep 2y2y TIIE receivers and play for tighter 10y swap spreads.

Asia Macro Strategy: We see Asia FX exposed to a US slowdown, lower equity markets, possible US tariff risks and China's weak growth. Plus the short positioning in Asia FX is cleaner. We are cautious on Asia FX. In FX, we like short KRW/JPY, long SGD/IDR, short CNH/INR and long 6m USD/CNH 7.40/7.60 (November expiry). In rates, we like receive 1y CNY NDIRS, long 1y CGBs with onshore FX hedge, long 10y G-Secs without FX hedge, pay 1y HKD versus US and pay 5y SGD versus US.

CEEMEA Macro Strategy: We believe that the market is yet to buy fully into the South Africa story, and the recent moves in local rates were driven by markets pricing deeper rate cuts primarily from developed markets. The local story is yet to play out completely, and we expect the curve to twist flatten. We recommend a 1y1y versus 5y5y twist flattener with a target of 260bp and stop of 390bp, which has a 3m roll of +15bp. Going forward, the change in inflation target, improved fiscal story and probable lower SAGB issuance at the MTBPS should drive the local story.

## Fragile risk backdrop

EM risk assets may be in for a more rocky September, though the extent will likely depend heavily on incoming data and the speed with which the FOMC adjusts its communication.

Rate cuts are coming from across the global economy and this likely keeps fixed income bid and global FX markets more volatile and choppy. However, with risk sentiment at risk of turning more negative, we see some challenges ahead for EM FX. We continue to see EM rates outperforming EM FX as a source of returns in EM local markets. In EM credit, we downgrade our view to bearish – consistent with our concerns about risk assets.

Last Friday's US labour market report indicated greater downside risk for the US economy versus the baseline expectation of our US economics team (see Constructive Outlook Turning Hazy, December 8, 2024). Yet, the weakness is not enough to cause the Fed to quicken the pace of easing versus our current expectation of three 25bp rate cuts this year, starting in September, versus the 110bp of rate cuts priced.

FOMC communication last week was consistent with this, where Fed Governor Waller suggested that he would need to see "significant deterioration" in future labour market data to act "quickly and forcefully". This suggests that Waller would prefer to kick off the easing cycle with a 25bp cut on September 18.

In 1H24 the monthly payrolls trend was stable, helping to make the case that the labour market was stabilising and normalising at healthy levels. So far in 2H we have seen the NFP trend weaken notably, consistent with the indication from the JOLTS data of a steady trend towards reduced labour demand yet a rising number of people looking for a job.

Against this slowdown in the labour market, policy rates are still deep in restrictive territory, and so investors debate whether the Fed is behind the curve in adjusting policy.

Based on market pricing, investors already believe, based on current available data, that the Fed should be quickening the pace of easing. Yet, the Fed appears to see a need for more data before deciding whether that is necessary. This tension is likely to result in defensive price action in risk assets, in our view, particularly if we see continued weakness in US/global economic activity, until Fed communication catches up with market pricing.

Risk assets are more likely to respond negatively to weak US activity in part because large chunks of the remainder of the global economy show weakness too. Eurozone 2Q GDP was revised 0.1pp lower, with significant weakness in wage growth relative to expectations, according to our economics team (see here).

China continues to show deflationary trends with limited evidence of a shift away from the reactive easing approach from the authorities that has left sentiment weak and growth likely ending up below target at around 4.6-4.7%Y (see here and here).

## LatAm versus Asia relative value/rotation

We have seen a significant outperformance of Asia FX relative to LatAm in the past three months. Lower US yields, an expectation for a US soft-landing narrative, a stronger JPY and an repricing of US election risks have helped to consolidate Asia gains.

We now see valuations pointing towards a reversal of Asia outperformance, with some fundamental catalysts ahead.

Our FX risk premia model suggests that Asia FX is now more expensive relative to LatAm by an extent we haven't seen in around three years. This risk premia model flags Mexico and Brazil as the cheapest currencies within EM, with SGD and THB the most expensive.

Which catalysts could tip the scales towards Asia underperformance?

• Repricing of US slowdown risks: Export growth to the US has been a key support for economic growth in the Asia region. Should US labour weakness accelerate and US imports drop off, this will likely impact Asia FX negatively.

• Equity market volatility: A repricing of US growth risks, combined with market concerns about how quickly the Fed will cut rates, should lead to weaker risk asset performance, including equities. Outflows from Asian equity markets could weigh on regional FX.

• Repricing of US election risks: The next debate is on September 10. Should this and future polling data indicate improving chances for the Republican party then, as our past research demonstrates, we would expect USD to rise. Given the current pricing of Asian FX versus other currencies such as MXN that could be affected, we see more value in positioning for this risk via Asia FX.

The scenario that we are outlining strongly suggests that USD/JPY should continue to fall, given that currency pair's renewed correlation and relationship with US rates. JPY strength would help to anchor the Asia region, but not offset the factors above. It does suggest that JPY can continue to outperform the rest of the Asia region.

EUR/USD, much like USD/JPY currently, is also heavily driven by interest differentials, so in the absence of a substantially more dovish ECB, further concerns about the outlook for the US economy that push US rates lower could help to keep EUR/USD resilient, and DXY facing downside risk.

This downside DXY risk helps to offset some of the risk-appetite challenges facing EM currencies. Moreover, a resilient EUR mechanically helps the performance of CEE currencies versus USD, given that they primarily trade versus EUR. This helps to keep our broad EM FX stance neutral as opposed to outright bearish, despite our concerns about risk appetite.

## The Fed cutting is a necessary but not sufficient condition to be bullish

Historical performance analysis shows that the Fed cutting is supportive of bonds: EM sovereign total returns have been positive in the six months after the first Fed cut in all five recent cutting cycles where we have EM total return data ( Exhibit 9 ). So far the return path looks very similar to 2019, which is indeed the most comparable episode in addition to 1995 as they both involved soft-landing scenarios, or what our economists call mid-cycle adjustments ( Exhibit 8 ).

Yet the real turning points for market are earlier when US rates pricing peaks, meaning that the actual first cut is more of a confirmation than a first signal to buy: The performance of EM bonds really tends to turn when the US rates market peaks in terms of the pricing of monetary policy, as for instance evidenced by the 1y1y US rate ( Exhibit 7 ). Using when this peaks as opposed to the actual first cut does show returns as being higher over the coming six months ( Exhibit 10 ). Of course this can only be done in hindsight, a fact the current cycle has in particular demonstrated via the high volatility of the 1y1y rate.

Today, the issue is also that markets are already pricing the cuts anticipated in a softlanding scenario: As noted by our rates strategists, US rates markets currently price a soft landing as the average outcome with a trough policy rate at 2.75%. Any move that takes the trough rate below 2.75% will likely be risk-negative as it will suggest that, on average, the Fed needs to deliver an accommodative policy stance – something investors will associate with a hard landing.

Whether the first Fed cut will actually bring inflows to EM dedicated funds is also unclear: One of the biggest debates among EM investors is when inflows to EM debtdedicated funds will return more meaningfully. While the Fed cutting is a net positive, especially if it also keeps returns positive, we don’t see it as the significant catalyst that would finally see significant flows return to EM.

• History not conclusive that Fed cuts bring flows into EM, yet in part due to lack of data: Overall we don’t have much history when it comes to assessing the impact of Fed cuts on EM flows. Looking at EM portfolio flows since 2005 suggests that the start of hiking cycles does lower portfolio flows to EM ( Exhibit 11 ). On the other hand, the 2019 cutting cycle did see inflows accelerate initially before Covid cut it short. 2007 did not result in an acceleration of inflows. Looking at only EMdedicated fund flows also shows that 2019 was supportive, with the other instances less so ( Exhibit 13 ).

• So far, EM has already demonstrated an issue in attracting flows even as the US does: Inflows to US IG funds have been strong for a while. Even inflows to US HY funds picked up in late 2023. Meanwhile, flows into EM have remained very subdued ( Exhibit 12 ). Evidence so far is thus that there is an issue with EM attracting inflows.

• Additionally, cash is not expected to rush out of money market funds (MMF) into longer-duration assets in general: Work by our cross-asset and US rates team suggests that there have been no large outflows from MMFs yet. More importantly, they also don’t think that a rotation out of the US\$6 trillion of MMF into risky assets will happen until \~12 months after the first Fed cut (see Just Where Are Fund Flows Going? July 29, 2024). This matters as it means that any spillover flows into EM would in turn also be lower from global portfolios.

So what would make flows return? In our year-ahead outlook we argued that EM should eventually see some inflows in 2024 around the first Fed cut, yet only due to push factors, namely broad flows into fixed income, of which EM is a small part, while the pull factors are not in place (see In Search of Inflows, November 17, 2023). We think that this still holds.

The pull factors that could change include China and EM growth improving, geopolitical risk reducing, broader structural reforms and sovereign ratings. To be fair there have been some positive reform stories this year, including Argentina, Ecuador, Egypt and Turkey. Multiple sovereign debt restructurings completing is also a clear positive. Yet we suspect that cross-over investors would still want more evidence of it being sustainable, as is indeed a question in all the aforementioned countries.

## 2) EM growth moderates as fiscals worsen nearly across the board

EM growth has held up ok but is slowing on aggregate. China's growth is not picking up and our economists expect August data to be weak. Broader PMIs in Asia are also moderating, with weaker readings in seven out of ten economies. GDP prints have also in general been weaker in CEEMEA and LatAm.

Perhaps more importantly for credit, fiscal results are also coming through weaker nearly across the board ( Exhibit 20 ). The deterioration so far this year is mostly driven by higher spending, both primary and interest expenses. Revenue performance is a lot more mixed. However, if growth keeps slowing into the second half of the year, this presumably raises risks to revenues as well.

## 3) Issuance to persist as long as possible

Year-to-date sovereign issuance now stands at US\$150 billion, up US\$48 billion versus 2023 at this point in the year and US\$14 billion above full-year 2023 sovereign supply. We are currently 73.4% of the way through our 2024 issuance expectations. Clearly it's been a very active year. Yet if the market allows, we suspect that it will continue. Both yields and spreads should look attractive to issuers and they should still see a window of opportunity ahead of the US elections. So far there has also been strong demand for the issues.

We're not of the view that issuance in itself will necessarily reprice spreads wider, especially as most of the issuance in the near term is likely to be IG and BB rated. However, it does raise risks in case of a weaker risk backdrop.

Potential issuers include Mexico, Kazakhstan, Romania, Sharjah, Indonesia and the Philippines in the IG space, and Angola, Bahrain, Colombia, Morocco, Nigeria, Oman and Turkey in the HY space. In HY, the BBs are most likely and within the single Bs only Bahrain and Turkey have access at this point.

## 4) External drivers not supportive according to our colleagues

Uniform sentiment that a lot is priced, leaving risks to the downside: Nearly all of our colleagues agree that the soft landing is priced already. Any larger correction in either US equities or US credit would have a negative spillover impact on EM credit. In US credit, our colleagues advocate an up-in-quality stance, favouring IG over HY, and within HY they are cautious on HY single Bs and prefer a BB/CCC barbell. If the USD weakness was to accelerate with the DXY breaking well below 100, it would be a positive sign for EM credit. However, as our FX colleagues detail, this is not their expectation.

• US Credit Strategy: Safety First, September 5, 2024: In the near term, credit remains vulnerable to bad data surprises with spreads not far from the July tights. Seasonality in September is weak, while supply could stay higher than usual ahead of the election. We like owning hedges.

• US Equity Strategy: Weekly Warm-up, September 3, 2024: The challenge for US equity investors is that markets already appear to be pricing in a soft landing. At 21x NTM earnings, the S&P 500 is trading around the top decile of its historical valuation range

• G10 FX Strategy: Skew Gardens, September 3, 2024: A more dovish Fed reaction function skews USD risks from the upside to downside, but forces abroad, still present political risks and stretched positioning mean we're not yet sold that the DXY will break below 100.

## 5) US elections risks no longer as well priced

We reviewed the implications of the US elections on EM in a larger note last week (see US Election Implications for EM, September 3, 2024). It spans both macro and micro factors, most of them negative for EM under the scenario of a Republican presidency. However, given that the race has become much more balanced in recent weeks, this downside is no longer priced to any great extent, with CNH a good example. Looking ahead, this therefore raises risks, particularly into the first debate this week.

## Strategy implications

Turn more cautious – a bearish near-term stance and favour IG outright versus HY: We have been reducing risk gradually in recent months and continue to do so, this time moving to outright favour IG over HY and an overall bearish stance in the near term.

In our mid-year outlook we were more constructive, favouring HY over IG and seeing returns of 8% from mid-May until end-2024, although with nearly all the returns coming from lower core rates. So far returns are up 5% since then, with rates delivering an even larger share than anticipated. Risk/reward is thus worse and we think that spreads can easily reprice wider from here, especially as US rates may struggle to decline further in a riskpositive fashion.

# LatAm Macro Strategy: Trading US Growth Prospects

Since returning from a much-needed summer holiday, we have been asked for our thoughts on common threads for LatAm markets into year-end. There are of course two main global themes to incorporate into any views: the upcoming US easing cycle and the upcoming US election (see here for our thoughts on EM implications).

We think that trading the upcoming FOMC easing cycle in LatAm could be trickier than most assume.

## Our current stance on Asia FX and rates

As discussed in FX & EM Fixed Income Strategy: Bumpy Ride Ahead , we believe that EM FX is likely to underperform versus USD. For Asia, we see a number of risks:

1) Asia is more dependent on the US economy: Our economists did a deep dive into Asia's economic linkage versus the US and argued that Asia has become more reliant on the US, as evident in a rising trade surplus with the US and the US being the key driver of end global demand.

2) Asia FX is exposed to equity volatility: Korea and Taiwan's economies have benefitted from the tech cycle in the US. However, their currencies struggled to perform well because equity inflows are largely FX hedged and domestic investors are still buying foreign equities. Hence, a sell-off in the equity market isn't necessarily bad for KRW and TWD. However, we believe that investors could focus on the weak economy instead, which could weigh on KRW and TWD.

3) Asia is still sensitive to US election polls and tariff risks: We believe that Asia is vulnerable to possible tariff risks and a higher USD/CNH given the correlation. The market has largely ignored the US election so far because the polls shows both candidates level pegging. But when we get more information and the attention shifts towards the election, we believe that the market could price in more risk premium.

4) China is slowing down: This is seen in recent sets of data. As we discussed below, there is a change of view from the government about the recent slowdown but there isn't an urgency to address this as the government feels that the tools to support growth aren't used up yet. This could weigh on investor sentiment.

Overall, we would like to take a more cautious stance on Asia given the reasons above, plus the positioning in short Asia FX is much cleaner now (see more below). Here are the top trades in FX and rates:

• Short KRW/JPY (new): The risk backdrop is fragile with markets increasingly looking for a 50bp rate cut given the recent soft growth data. In a scenario where markets price in higher chances of a hard landing, KRW would come under pressure as risk sells off while JPY should benefit. We see potential for markets to price in a sharper BoJ rate-hiking path if they believe that the Fed can achieve a soft landing, while we expect the BoK to start cutting rates in October. We add long JPY/KRW with a target of 10.00 and stop of 9.00.

• Long SGD/IDR 3m NDF (new): IDR has performed well in the past few weeks thanks to the strong bond inflows. Foreign ownership of INDOGBs is back to IDR 850 trillion, the peak seen in the past two years. For foreign ownership to increase from here, it would require GBI-EM investors to reduce somewhere and add allocation in Indonesia or get new inflows to buy Indonesia. Neither will happen, in our opinion, especially as inflows to local currency funds are lacklustre. IDR could reverse its outperformance should Asia FX suffer. ## What do your peers think about Asia/China?

Throughout August, we have been travelling and marketing in Singapore, Shenzhen and Beijing, meeting over 30 clients across real money and hedge funds in both fixed income and equity space. ## 1. What drove the Asia FX rally?

Short Asia FX has been quite popular in the first seven months of 2024: We, along with many investors, argued that USD could stay strong versus Asia FX thanks to the US growth prospects, high US interest rates, relatively low yields in Asia and exporters reluctance to increase their FX conversion ratio. Indeed, Asia FX was the worst performer versus CEEMEA and LatAm FX.

This has caused short Asia FX positions to increase: Our GBI-EM position tracker shows that EM-dedicated investors have gradually increased their short Asia FX position from - 1% at the start of 2024 to -4.2% in July 2024. The short positioning has been kept since March 2024.

Our view on positioning is that it won't be problematic even if it is heavy, as long as it is supported by the fundamentals. However, it makes the market vulnerable to a correction should fundamentals change, whether internal or external driven.

This happened in August, when short JPY carry trades got unwound and softer US data prompted investors to expect a more dovish Fed.

We don’t believe that real money investors' short Asia FX will be fully unwound in August because 70% of that short position is driven by short CNY, a structural UW. But such short positioning probably reflects the broad sentiment on Asia FX, especially among the hedge fund community.

This is similar to the feedback we got on our trip as the majority of short Asia FX carry trades among hedge funds were unwound in August: Hence, the short Asia trades among the fast money community are quite clean. Real money might have covered some but they are unlikely to cut it to neutral as they need short positions in Asia to fund long high yielders in CEEMEA and LatAm.

Given the clean positions among asset managers, our conversations with clients has been focused on whether exporters in the region could start to increase their FX conversion ratio (see more details below).

## 3. What's US election impact on Asia?

There are many moving parts in the US election, e.g., potential changes in trade policy, immigration policy, fiscal policy, foreign policy, climate and energy policy and overseas investment and aid policy. We have elaborated on the potential impact of each of these policies in EM Fixed Income Strategy & Global Economics: US Election Implications for EM, September 3, 2024.

For Asia in particular, any changes in tariff and trade policy should be the key focus given the export-oriented nature of many economies in this region. China is clearly the most exposed considering the potential 50% tariff floated by Trump in his most recent Bloomberg interview.

CNH should depreciate less than in 2018-19: In 2018-19, CNH depreciated by \~80-100% of the increase in tariffs. This time, we don't expect CNH to depreciate by a similar extent given that: 1) The size of the tariff hike that has been floated in this election campaign is at least double that seen in 2018-19; and 2) China's rewiring of exports to new geographies could help to mitigate the impact of tariffs from the US.

Besides CNH, KRW, THB, MYR and TWD also appear to be more vulnerable to any changes in trade or tariff policy on the back of the US election, based on assessing the export orientation of the economies, their trade balances with the US and their sensitivity to risk appetite and moves in USD/CNH. However, THB weakness could be cushioned to some extent by a rise in gold prices if risk appetite weakens.

While India also runs a large trade surplus with the US, its more domestically driven economy, coupled with the RBI's reaction function on the currency, may help to cushion the effect on the currency. INR could thus weaken alongside a stronger USD but is unlikely to underperform relative to the rest of Asia.

## 4. What's happening on the ground in China?

The consensus on China among investors is no doubt cautious. Hence, investors' questions have shifted towards what's happening on the ground in China as they don’t feel the data themselves provide sufficient colour. This is something we have been focusing on for the past few months by launching our China snippets series.

As we discussed in Snippets from China: Special Snippets from Beijing, September 6, 2024, local sentiment has worsened in the past few months after stabilising early this year. Equity prices are close to the trough seen in February 2024. Bond yields are not far away from record lows. Global commodity prices are under pressure and global luxury stocks are selling off.

All the evidence points to the same conclusion: China is slowing down. One key difference we felt on the ground when we were in Beijing is that there are more and more people coming out to voice their opinions about the latest economic situation and there is an open discussion about deflation, the debt trap and negative feedback loops and how the government needs to address it.

In China, the path from general public discussion to final policymakers' decision is likely to be long. So, the good news is that the latest set of data could prompt some policy reaction in the end. The not so good news is that the timing and the size of such policy reaction is uncertain.

## 5. How big is the short CNH carry trade?

## There are six types of carry trades:

i) Hedge funds short CNH: As discussed above, we believe that those short-dated short CNH trades were closed during the volatile August. Hedge funds still own long-dated USD/CNH topside trades and these trades are more structural.

ii) Real money UW CNY: For example, CNY is the biggest UW among GBI-EM investors, i.e., 3.2% versus the benchmark. The past month's rally in CNY could trigger some short covering but we doubt that it will continue. Such an UW is structural and sticky, in our view. Investors are unlikely to cover their UW unless they see CNY rallying a lot from here, which we see as unlikely.

Exhibit 55: GBI-EM investors' FX positions as of July 2024
![](images/47b64f3c8cf6a1611fa784edc1d3ebc4dcd7d329e1b9dc4d665b4088cf93b2ee.jpg)
Source: Company websites, Morgan Stanley Research

iii) Chinese exporters holding USD: It is not a pure carry trade: a carry trade is defined as short high yielder versus long high yielder in order to earn carry. For Chinese exporters, they hold USD because of USD proceeds they receive from exports and they are not really short CNY as they have plenty of CNY to pay domestic capex and wages. As discussed above, we don’t believe that they will convert yet as the bar is quite high.

iv) Chinese banks short CNY versus long USD: Chinese banks have some flexibility to use their balance sheet to do carry trades. Exhibit 56 shows the total USD assets versus USD liabilities across the top 17 banks in China. It shows that USD assets and liabilities largely match each other and total assets were only US\$50 billion more than liabilities at the end of last year.

v) Chinese companies short CNY versus long USD: This involves Chinese companies borrowing CNH or CNY, converting into USD and earning USD return. One proxy would be the Dim Sum bond market size (CNH bond issuance in Hong Kong), which has spiked to almost CNY 1 trillion or US\$136 billion in 2024. If we use 2021 as a baseline, the additional borrowing is US\$85 billion in the past three years. Normally, those companies need CNY or CNH onshore and some of them might convert into USD in order to pay their foreign currency liability. We doubt that they will be active in engaging in long USD/short CNY carry trades because any currency mismatch could expose them to losses should CNY appreciate. Rather, they could hedge the currency when they do that.

vi) Foreign companies short CNH versus long USD: This could be tracked by Panda bond issuance, i.e., CNY bond issuance in mainland China mainly by foreign companies. The market size has increased to CNY 315 billion or US\$44 billion and additional issuance is US \$9 billion. The proceeds from onshore issuance have more regulatory oversight and we believe that foreign companies are more likely to use their proceeds in mainland China rather than moving them abroad or keeping them in USD

Hence, we don’t believe that the unwind of carry trades could drive a stronger CNY.

## 6. Will Taiwan life insurers hedge more into year-end?

FX hedge ratio near the low in recent years: Taiwan lifers had reduced their FX hedge ratios meaningfully in 1Q24 as US yields and USD rebounded. In 2Q24, amid an elevated USD and US yields, their FX hedge ratios were kept stable at 37%, near the lowest in recent years ( Exhibit 59 ). With US yields and USD now weakening simultaneously, it wouldn't be a surprise to see lifers increasing their FX hedge ratios.

US yields and USD are key factors: Whether lifers will continue lifting their FX hedge ratios going into year-end will depend on the durability of the recent move lower in US yields and USD. The outcome of the US election will be a key watch point in this regard.

TWD could outperform if USD weakens: But importantly, given the current low level of their FX hedge ratios, the sharp USD weakness seen recently and lower hedging costs going forward with the Fed embarking on its rate-cutting cycle, there is potential for a

meaningful increase in their hedge ratios should USD continue weakening. Therefore, we see TWD as one of the currencies to outperform in Asia if the trend of lower USD and US yields extends.

The recent change in the FX reserves rule for the lifers does not change the picture much, in our view. Our Taiwan financials analysts believe that insurers will apply for the new rule as TWD has appreciated significantly versus USD. Even though this will increase lifers' pool of FX reserves that can be used to offset their FX losses, their low existing level of FX hedge ratios suggests that they may also increase FX hedging if USD/TWD weakens.

## 7. What's our view on KRW after the rally?

Neutral on KRW: Many investors have been curious about our view on KRW after USD/ KRW broke below 1,340 in mid-August amid USD weakness. We are neutral on USD/KRW at current levels. First, as highlighted in FX & EM Fixed Income Strategy: Bumpy Ride Ahead , the risk backdrop is fragile. With the Fed still signalling a 25bp rate cut, the market debate on whether the US will see a soft or hard landing will continue.

US soft or hard landing offers very different outcomes: The two scenarios have very different implications for KRW. In a soft-landing scenario where Fed cuts are likely to bring inflation expectations up (US real yields likely fall and inflation expectations pick up), KRW could be one of the outperformers in Asia based on historical performance ( Exhibit 61 ). In a hard-landing scenario where US real yields and inflation expectations may fall simultaneously, KRW may end up being one of the underperformers in the region ( Exhibit 62 ).

Risks from trade tensions: Furthermore, the US election remains a close race. Should markets start to reprice the probability of trade tensions, KRW could be one of the more vulnerable currencies in the region due to its trade exposure and high beta. The positive export recovery story for KRW could be put into question.

Preference for TWD over KRW: Domestically, there are also structural outflows from the NPS, which stands in contrast to the potential increase in FX hedging from Taiwan lifers. This is why we are relatively more constructive on TWD versus KRW.
