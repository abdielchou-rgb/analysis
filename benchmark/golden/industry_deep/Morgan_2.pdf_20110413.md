## Developed Markets

## United States:

Overnight, the BoJ maintained its policy rate at 0.25%, as widely expected. However, the press conference was more dovish than market participants had expected as BoJ Governor Ueda struck a more cautious tone. He argued that upside risks to inflation are easing and that there is “room to spend more time” at the current rate level. Further, he added that while the BoJ maintains the view of a US soft-landing as its base scenario, uncertainty for that view has become heightened since August. JPY weakened and JGB futures rallied following the press conference.

During the NY session, front-end USTs initially cheapened as the curve marginally flattened in a partial reversal of the post-FOMC move. This came alongside risk-off price action with declines in equities and risk sensitive currencies.

In his first appearance since the September FOMC meeting, Fed Governor Waller said that he supported the 50bp cut in rates as inflation data was softening much faster than he previously thought. He went on to state that the future pace of cuts will be dependent on data, but he is open to another 50bp cut should the labor market worsen or inflation come in softer-than-expected. In the September Summary of Economic Projections, nearly all members saw risks to the inflation forecast as broadly balanced, with two seeing the risk weighted to the upside. That said, as the balance between the Fed’s dual mandate continues to shift, the potential of downside risks to inflation could become increasingly important.

A statement from Fed Governor Bowman, the only FOMC member who dissented at the September meeting, was released midday. She offered additional insight to her dissent arguing that she was concerned that the larger rate cut could be interpreted as “a premature declaration of victory on [the Fed’s] price stability mandate.”

In response to Fed Governor Waller maintaining the optionality of additional 50bp cuts to come alongside the mentioning of downside risks to inflation, treasuries reversed course and began to rally alongside a sharp steepening of the curve. Risk assets moved in tandem and began to rally following the dovish remarks. Ultimately, the UST curve steepened, though ended the day little changed. The curve bearsteepened over the week with the 2s10s curve \~8bp steeper. The USD also retraced its brief weakening on the day to close up 0.1%, but still ended the week down - 0.4%.

While US equities gained some traction following Fed Governor Waller’s remarks, the momentum was short-lived and the S&P 500 ended -0.2% lower. Despite the decline on Friday, the S&P 500 ended the week 1.4% higher as the Fed’s 50bp cut was seen as supportive for risk assets. Brent crude oil futures were little changed on the day, but gained 4.0% on the week.

## Japan:

In the Tokyo morning session, the JGB curve twist flattened. Long-end JGBs marginally rallied back from Thursday’s aggressive sell-off, while short-end JGBs underperformed on the back of a strong Japan CPI service release. The BoJ released a statement for the September monetary policy meeting and as expected, the BoJ maintained the current policy rate of 0.25%. The statement included an upward revision for the BoJ’s assessment of household consumption, which resulted in a sell-off in short-end JGBs that accelerated into the Tokyo afternoon session.

Super-long sectors also sold off in the afternoon ahead of the next key risk event which is LDP leader election on September 27th. One of the prospective candidates, Sanae Takaich, has called for dovish BoJ monetary policy and expansionary fiscal measures. As August JSDA data suggested, many market participants in Japan have added flattener positions over the past few months. It seemed that on Friday some market participants partly unwound the positions to prepare for a steeper-curve scenario.

Ultimately, the JGB curve bear-steepened; 2y JGB sold off 1.0bp to 0.39%, 10y JGB sold off 1.0bp to 0.86%, and 30y JGB sold off 2.5bp to 2.08% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve also bear-steepened and broadly outperformed JGBs. XCCY basis widened in the long end sectors. At BoJ Governor Ueda’s press conference, his tone was relatively dovish compared to his prior remarks as he offered a cautious stance on the US economy ahead and added that there is "room to spend more time" at the current policy rate level. JGB futures sharply rallied by \~30c after the press conference.

USD/JPY temporarily rose in the early morning to \~142.9 due to increased expectations for a US soft-landing on the back of solid US labor-related data and corporate sentiment. However, the USD/JPY ground lower ahead of BoJ governor Ueda’s press conference. The move lower was also in response to strong Japan CPI service data as well as the BoJ’s statement which suggested that data developments had been in-line with the BoJ's outlook. Otherwise CCY/JPY had traded in tandem with USD/JPY as market participants seemingly broadly anticipated relatively

hawkish BoJ communication. However, in the London morning, USD/JPY surged to \~143.6 before the press conference began and risk currencies such as GBP/JPY outperformed due to the BoJ press conference being more dovish than market participants had expected.

## Europe:

It was a relatively quiet day in European FX. Some of Thursday’s gains in risksensitive currencies were pared in the London morning, but were later boosted by dovish remarks form Fed Governor Waller. NOK was down in the London morning, but reversed course to end the session flat. SEK was unable to fully recover the losses and was down -0.2%. Sterling was little impacted by various UK data releases and speeches, and maintained its upward trajectory to gain another +0.2% against the USD. CHF continued to weaken, although only marginally so, with USD/ CHF up 0.1% and EUR/CHF up 0.2%. The EUR gained 0.1% against the USD.

## Dollar Bloc:

Risk sentiment slowed heading into the weekend which saw some of Thursday’s price action marginally reverse before comments from Fed Governor Waller gave it another boost. AUD and NZD pared some of their early losses to end the London session flat. CAD saw a small gain from the upside surprise in local retail sales data which pushed back on the small risk-off price action and kept the currency unchanged on the day. Ultimately, the DXY USD Index gained 0.1% during the London session.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies saw mixed moves as a dovish BoJ press conference helped moderate FX gains. CNH was one of the outperformers in the region, and gained 0.3% against the USD amid reports that China is considering easing restrictions for non-local homebuyers in major cities, ending distinctions between first- and second-home purchases, and adding measures to shore up the local equity market. IDR continued to outperform, and gained 0.6% to 15,150, likely aided by bond inflows and comments from the BI which said it is not intervening in FX markets on Friday as the IDR volatility is manageable. THB gained 0.3% amid the 0.8% rally in gold prices. KRW continued to underperform, and was down 0.5% to 1,335.

AxJ rates mostly sold off. In China, yields edged lower by \~1bp on the 2y and 10y points of the CGB curve while NDIRS yields were largely steady after the loan prime rates were kept unchanged. Indonesia rates outperformed in the region; 2y and 10y IndoGB rates rallied 12bp as they continued to benefit from the BI’s surprise rate cut on Wednesday. In Thailand, 1y NDOIS yields rose 1bp and the 1s5s curve bearsteepened \~1bp amid comments from the BoT Governor that lowering rates may have a limited impact in lowering borrowers’ debt burden and could trigger inflation. Hong Kong rates underperformed, and 1y HKD IRS yield rose 3bp while the 1s5s curve bear-flattened 1bp as the local equity market continued to outperform. In India, 10y G-sec yields fell 3bp to 6.75% while the 1s5s INR NDOIS curve bearsteepened 2bp.

## CEEMEA:

With the FOMC in hindsight CEEMEA rates returned to a sell-off and were coming from very low levels after having rallied on the back of core rates. In Poland, the curve sold off 9bp. Czech Republic and Hungary followed the yields in the region and were up 6bp each. In South Africa, rates continued their trend from Thursday when the MPC revised up their forecasts for the repo rate for 2024 year-end. The curve bear-flattened with 5y yields up 4bp and 10y yields up 2bp.

In FX, EUR/HUF was lower by 0.3%, EUR/CZK was down 0.2%, and EUR/PLN was lower by 0.1%. ZAR having appreciated 2% in the last 10 days, was flat for the day.

## Emerging Markets

• BI Executive Director for Monetary Management Susianto said the central bank is letting IDR appreciate and is not intervening in the FX market on Friday as the volatility is manageable. • BoT Governor Sethaput said that Thailand households have remained fragile as debts rise due to overspending and insufficient income. He commented that Thai policymakers should focus more on long-term benefits rather than short-term. He said that lowering rates could help boost growth but also trigger inflation, and is not as effective as debt restructuring in reducing the debt burden. Sethaput noted that the BoT does not need to follow the Fed’s rate moves, but needs to consider its impact on many important factors. In his view, Thailand’s financial markets have been orderly following the Fed’s rate cut, and the BoT is closely monitoring THB as it does not want to see high FX volatility. He attributed THB’s recent gains to USD weakness and a rally in gold prices

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
