# India Technology: Internet Strategy

# Staying bullish

Despite a sharp re-rating we are still constructive on India internet stocks. We screen names that look attractive based on our stock selection framework.

## Key Takeaways

Macro and micro factors are aligned for India internet stocks, but the rising dispersion of stock returns implies even greater focus on stock selection.

Framework weighs operating environment, track record, outlook, and valuation. Zomato and PB Fintech ahead of others.

Macro and micro aligned for India internet stocks: Since Jan-23, India internet stocks have delivered 88%+ returns (vs Nifty 50 +32%). We see a similar trend in the US (internet index up 119% vs NASDAQ +69%). To a certain extent, we see macro factors aligning (multiple re-rating for growth stocks in India and other regions, tight PE/VC money keeping the competitive environment benign) with micro factors (strong execution reflected in market share gains, margin improvement driving profit upgrade cycle) and supporting share prices. As a theme, we expect this strong alignment to continue for India internet stocks in 2024, however the rising dispersion of internet stock returns since early 2023 suggests stock-specific factors are driving outperformance in addition to macro factors.

Framework for ranking India internet stocks: We capture a) operating environment – competition and regulations, b) track record on execution and defensibility (including B/S strength), c) growth and margin outlook, and d) valuations adjusted for growth. Our framework shows Zomato and PB Fintech (EW; covered by Subramanian Iyer) screening ahead of others, supported by their strong track record and better outlook. PB Fintech's EW rating factors in its robust business model and the strong phase it is in, but outperformance YTD as well as rich valuations (relative to the coverage of the analyst) makes it vulnerable to volatility especially in case of any negative newsflow. Our ranking framework is aligned with the analyst on fundamentals; the disconnect on valuation is because our reference universe is India internet stocks.

Where we are in the cycle and key catalysts: We see a close correlation between the PE/VC cycle and the performance of India internet stocks (the boom in 2020/21 drove higher market caps, followed by a slowdown creating temporary pain after which competitive intensity improved and the profit upgrade cycle drove higher market caps). We now see companies reprioritizing growth even if it means slower margin improvement in the near term. In the most recent quarter, 3 of 6 companies under our coverage beat revenue estimates. We believe strong revenue growth momentum with a good medium-term outlook for profitability will be a key catalyst. We see room for Zomato and PB Fintech to surprise on revenue growth.

# Investment Thesis: Setting up a framework for stock selection

## Key takeaways

Macro + micro alignment to support internet stocks in India...: The drying up of PE/VC money has kept the competitive environment benign, helping companies deliver steady margin improvements. The India internet market cap index mimics the US index to certain extent, especially since early 2023 onwards, implying the uplift to multiples is not just an Indian phenomenon. We see this setup continuing through 2024. Further strong execution by companies (on market share, profitability) would complement the positive macro setup.

...however stock selection is becoming more important: Despite macro support, we note there is a significant divergence in the performance of internet stocks in India. We have computed dispersion of monthly returns of all key internet stocks listed in India and we note that since early 2023 the standard deviation of returns has been steadily increasing implying stock-specific factors are playing a more important role in driving returns.

Introducing our framework for evaluating internet stocks in India: Our framework is based on four key variables: 1) operating environment, where we assess competitive factors and regulation, b) track record of execution and defensibility of business, c) outlook for financial performance, and d) valuation metrics. We compare these metrics across companies to give a rank to each of them. The final ranking is based on the average of all parameters. Our framework shows Zomato followed by PB Fintech (rated EW) screening well vs other names. Our ranking framework is aligned with the analyst on fundamentals for PB Fintech (strong business model/good track record) however our reference point for a valuation comparison is the universe of India internet stocks vs the coverage universe of the analyst, which creates a disconnect.

We note that One97 appears to be the cheapest stock on valuation, with the largest risk reward skew (upside to downside ratio), however regulatory uncertainty and management changes keep us on sidelines as we wait to see how well it will execute over the coming quarters.

Key catalysts: 1. Revenue growth momentum: At different points in time investors have used different metrics for valuing stocks. During 2022, GMV/ revenue growth was more important while during 2023 improving profitability (profit upgrade cycle) was more important. At this point, we believe revenue growth momentum will again become more important as long as there is no material shift in long-term profitability assumptions.

Three out of six companies under our coverage saw growth accelerate in recent quarters, and many talked about prioritizing growth even if this means sacrificing near-term profits. We see sustaining strong revenue growth as a critical factor for stocks' outperformance. We expect F25 revenue growth of 28% for our coverage, excluding One97, which is in a transition period.

2. Pickup in capital market activity: Any pickup in IPOs could be important to validating valuations.

Relative valuations and positioning. Despite a sharp re-rating in the last six months, we still have a constructive view on stocks as a) we see room for consensus to raise earnings, b) valuation multiples adjusted for growth look fine. On a relative basis we look at growth-adjusted EV/adj EBITDA multiples (EV/adj EBITDA to adj EBITDA CAGR) and FCF multiples – One97, PB Fintech and Zomato screen at the top, however given that One97 is in a transition period we rank Zomato and PB Fintech ahead of others. Lastly, on a relative basis we see scope for a further increase in domestic institutional investors' positioning in One97 and Zomato vs others while we see the least supply overhang concerns for Zomato (based on exits of pre IPO foreign investors).

Where could we be wrong in our stock selection framework? Our framework takes into account a simple average of multiple factors that we believe are important for driving stock returns. However, we note that each of the above factors could have different degree of influence in driving outperformance. For example, the removal of its regulatory overhang could be a bigger driver for One97 than some other factors, while changing the competitive landscape in quick commerce could be more important for Zomato than other factors.

## Key themes

## 1. Macro and micro factors aligned for India internet but rising dispersion of returns shows stock selection more important than the past

The India internet index is strongly outperforming the NIFTY index. The outperformance has become even sharper in the last 6-8 months, barring the recent pullback. We go back in history to see how this index has correlated with global internet indices in the past and attribute some of this correlation to macro related factors.

We note that India internet stocks saw multi-fold gains in 2020-21. We see a similar movement across other geographies for internet stocks however both India and Southeast Asia had a striking similarity during this period. This period was also marked by a PE/VC boom in India and a material markup in valuations in subsequent funding rounds. The index for the stocks peaked in late 2021 and started to see a reversal as macro conditions changed (higher geopolitical risks/higher interest rate outlook in developed markets impacting growth outlook and hence lower valuations).

The India internet index started to bottom out in late 2022/early 2023 and since then directionally the index has seen strong correlation to that of the US internet market cap index. We believe some of the macro factors that were aligned included a) theme around profitability improvement given less competitive environment (drying up of PE/VC money) and b) outlook for interest rate cuts in developed markets (such as the US) potentially supporting higher multiples.

Going forward, we see this setup continuing through 2024 supporting healthy outlook for India internet stocks on growth and valuations. We believe the combination of a favorable macro setup and strong execution by individual companies in terms of market share gains and improving profitability (micro factors) should bode well for stocks in our India internet coverage universe. We see significant divergence playing out in stock returns, especially since early 2023, implying a rising dispersion of stock returns. We believe stock-specific factors have started to matter more now than they did in 2HC22 and 1HC23. In this context, we have come out with our own stock selection framework for the India internet coverage universe.

## 1. Operating environment consists of a) competitive dynamics and b) regulatory environment

Competitive dynamics: In this variable, we look at two things. First, the current market structure and any change in the structure, and second, if any change in competitive behavior is seen in the market. A consolidated market vs fragmented is always better. Further, a significant market share gap between the leader and other players bodes well for the leader. Improving competitive intensity in the market should be reflected in multiple ways such as better monetization, lower customer acquisition costs, and improving profitability.

Well placed: Amongst the companies under our coverage, we see both online insurance and food delivery to be the best placed in terms of both market structure as well as improving competitive behavior (falling marketing costs). We do see higher competitive intensity in quick commerce given potential new entrants in this market however the current market structure is reasonably consolidated within three players.

Moderately positioned: We see the OTA market as generally competitive however there has been a sharp drop in marketing costs pre COVID-19 to post COVID-19 and competition has been benign (all players focused on profitability). We see online beauty/fashion to be in middle of the coverage given a new entrant in the market (Tira in beauty) while multiple large players are already operating in the market (such as Flipkart, Amazon, Ajio, etc).

Higher competitive pressures: We see both ecommerce logistics and payments to be relatively less well placed vs other categories discussed above. The ecommerce logistics market structure is fragmented (multiple competitors) while the customer market is concentrated (low bargaining power with customers). Similarly in payments we see the market consolidated within three large players but there have been new entrants as well (messaging app launching payments, ecommerce companies growing their own payment businesses, etc).

The valuation/internet index (of market cap) has been closely linked to the PE/VC cycle in India.

Post COVID-19 in early 2020, there was a big boom of PE/VC money providing higher risk capital and strong focus on driving GMV/revenue growth. The market cap was linked to variables such as revenues or GMV during that time. This was followed by a listing boom in 2021 when many private companies got listed in public equity markets.

Second half of 2021 onwards: Immediately post many listings in 2021, the PE/VC money started to slow down, impacting growth appetite as well the availability of risk capital. This drove a slowdown in revenue growth and a pivot to profitability for listed stocks and new fundraising at lower valuations than previous rounds for private companies. Due to this pivot, there was a steep valuation correction and the market cap index fell. We note that this was temporary as it ended up benefiting the listed companies that had large capital already in their B/S. This provided an opportunity for listed companies to focus on profitability without necessarily losing market share.

Profit upgrade cycle in 2023: We saw a profit upgrade cycle in 2023, which has continued to a certain extent in 2024, driving share price returns.

Prior to 2023, Zomato was trading on EV/S and during the time period of July 2021 to Oct 2022 multiples were going up while adj EBITDA margin performance was weak. Post 3QF22 margins started to improve and revenue growth fell and valuation took a hit at that time.
