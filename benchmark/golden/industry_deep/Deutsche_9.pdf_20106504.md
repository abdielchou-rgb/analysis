Since we published our last update in October (link), the US-China high-tech tensions have intensified. Most notably, China is advancing its chipmaking capabilities despite tightening US restrictions.

Yesterday, Nvidia briefly surpassed Amazon to be the 4th largest fully listed company in the world. Last week, it was reported that SMIC, China’s top foundry, is aiming to leverage its existing stock of US and Dutch-made equipment to produce 5nm chips this year. This follows its successful production of 7nm chips in August. Nvidia also announced a new \$30bn business unit to design custom AI and cloud computing chips, including for OpenAI.

In 2023, the semiconductor space split dramatically. AI leaders like Nvidia saw unprecedented gains of nearly +240% in 2023. Conversely, broad-based, lagging edge, and cyclical names struggled to keep pace.

In 2024, three pivotal trends will likely define the landscape:

1. Sustaining Artificial Intelligence demand - Will rapid AI growth persist or plateau?

2. Economic cycles shape demand - Which end markets like consumer electronics are most critical?

3. Geopolitics upend supply chains - How are ongoing tech and trade battles reshaping global alliances?

In this chartbook, we analyse these top 3 themes that will likely shape the semiconductor industry in 2024; and then provide an indepth analysis of the equity outlook for the sector. Source: Deutsche Bank, Bloomberg Finance LP, CNBC, Financial Times.

In 2023, the semiconductor sector experienced a significant divide. AI-exposed companies, such as Nvidia and AMD, emerged as winners, with Nvidia's market capitalisation soaring by nearly 240%. Conversely, broad-based and cyclical names struggled to keep pace.

AI leaders surge ahead: AI fuelled a semiconductor boom in 2023, with Nvidia and AMD outperforming the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (65%) and the S&P 500 (24%).

Some signs of cooling: Enthusiasm for AI-exposed companies tempered in H2 2023. Nvidia’s share price rose 14% in Q4 2023 despite 2025 earnings estimates increasing by 21%. Nvidia share price has surged in 2024, up +46% YTD.

But AI's impact remains: Powerful trends like data growth, machine learning, and smart devices ensure AI adoption spreads across industries. AI tech giants like Nvidia, AMD, Marvell Technology and Broadcom still hold significant competitive advantages. They remain positioned for expanding AI opportunity through development in autonomous systems, healthcare applications, and more.

Nvidia dominates and controls approximately 86% of the PC discrete GPU market, a critical component powering AI. AMD is the largest competitor but a distant second with 11% market share.

2023 a banner year but overreliance on Nvidia a potential risk. Data centre processing fuelled by surging AI investments delivered 42% revenue growth in 2023. However, excluding Nvidia, revenues for competitors declined 4%.

Emergence of alternatives could support sustainable expansion. In Jan 2024, AMD released new MI300 chip to rival Nvidia's H100 GPU for memory capacity and bandwidth. To size market’s potential, AMD highlighted a \$400bn AI TAM by 2027. Compelling, supported by Nvidia’s growth, but magnitude of this TAM is equivalent to revenues of the entire chips market in 2023.

Major AI firms developing in-house solutions. Nvidia shortages and high costs prompted firms to design their own chips. In November 2023, Microsoft debuted the Cobalt CPU and Maia AI accelerator tailored for its needs. Google had built tensor processing units since 2015 to free itself from external provider constraints.

Outlook remains optimistic for 2024 and beyond. Revenues projected to jump 51% in 2024 as alternative GPUs and CPUs gain traction. Competition is expected to support more sustainable long-term sector growth. Rather than rely on one company, diversification of solutions provides healthier growth path.

The AI semiconductor market is expected to surge to \$119.4bn in 2027, comprising nearly 20% of the global semiconductor market.

## TREND #2: CYCLES should come back into play after 2023 was defined by AI as a new structural driver

In the final quarter of 2023, as investors questioned the sustainability of the demand for AI, optimism for looser monetary policy and hopes that the market had now passed a cyclical bottom provided an additional boost to close the year. This sentiment saw the SOX Index rally in Q4 (+22%).

\- The significant rally and current high valuations have set a challenging bar for a cyclical rebound in 2024.

\- Broad markets may stabilise: After outperformance in 2023, neutral cyclical conditions are expected.

Rolling recovery: Rather than a swift V-shaped rebound, a rolling recovery period is anticipated due to softer demand, macroeconomic factors, and inventory headwinds.

\- Accelerating recovery: H2 2024 should see momentum build as we anticipate rate cuts in G7 economies.

## TREND #3: The GEOPOLITICS of reshoring - Balancing national security & supply chain realities

## The US CHIPS Act (\$280bn)

Incentivises domestic chip production to narrow 40% cost gap vs offshore. \$39bn for new facilities plus 25% tax credit for equipment spending.

SIA estimates it would cost each region \$1trn in incremental upfront investment to build a self-sufficient supply chain at current demand.

\- Major fabs plan US expansions: 6% yoy capacity growth in 2024 - six new fabs.

TSMC two in Arizona (first delayed to 2025 due to skills shortage), SK Hynix, Samsung, Intel and Micron in Indiana, Texas, Ohio and New York resp.

US TSMC plants expected monthly capacity of 60k 12-inch wafers, est. 4.6% of TSMC wafers monthly. In 2023, TSMC produced 16mn 12-inch wafers. In 2022, North America 68% of net profits.

Defence supply chain: \$35mn to BAE Systems for military aircraft chips.

## The EU Chips Act (€43bn public/private funds)

To double EU's 10% global market share and achieve 2nm production. Currently, most advanced foundry is Intel’s Ireland Leixlip, producing 14nm.

Commitments from TSMC, STMicroelectronics, GlobalFoundries, Intel. 68 projects, but no major progress made yet.

€20bn German budget, but Nov 2023 budget ruling stalling building. TSMC invested 3x this in 2023 for 3nm and 5nm production in southern Taiwan alone.

## China (\$40bn State Investment Fund)

Leading global industry expansion in 2024 with 18 new projects, 12% YoY capacity growth in 2023 and 13% YoY capacity growth in 2024.

Closing gap through regional incentives to secure domestic capacity but face hurdles. Domestic leader SMIC now at 5nm but behind on tech, mixed yields.

## China is facing challenges in scaling advanced chips and…

Next-gen chips remain distant: SMIC, China's leading semiconductor manufacturer, has achieved a 7nm process node in 2023 and aims to develop 5nm chips this year.

Legacy (>28nm) leadership: China and Taiwan hold 80% of the fab capacity for process nodes ranging from 20 to 45nm.

Scalability for advanced chips remains a challenge due to outdated equipment.

Significant strides in providing capabilities for legacy chips production. SMIC and Hua Hong are expected to aggressively expand at legacy nodes, acquiring older equipment from ASML that supports nodes >28nm.

## … the US is grappling with a gap in lagging edge (>7nm) and legacy chip production

Reliance on overseas sources: The US heavily relies on Taiwan, South Korea, and China for legacy chips, specifically those at the 28nm process node or larger.

CHIPs Act: \$2bn for legacy chips, \$10bn for lagging edge chips, \~4% of total CHIPs Act funding.

US capex at the lagging edge projected to decline -9% in 2024 But, this is three times larger than pre-pandemic 10-year average capex.

Outlook: China's aggressive ramp-up at the lagging edge presents increased competition for Western firms selling into China, raising concerns about overcapacity in the medium term.

2022 yielded significant decline in share prices: In-line with the declines in most tech stocks seen in 2022, semiconductor shares (measured via the SOX index) fell –36% in light of higher interest rates and lower investment.

Fears of a cyclical correction post chip-shortages and the resulting overcapacity exacerbated the declines.

2023 saw bifurcation between end markets: AI-exposed names saw significant share price appreciation in 2023 following the introduction of ChatGPT and the inciting of the AI compute "land grab“.

Rolling correction in broad-based end markets resulted in broad-based semis stocks underperforming (albeit still up y/y).
