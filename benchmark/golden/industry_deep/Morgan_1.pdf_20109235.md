## Japan Equity Strategy

## Apr-Jun earnings outlook: Expect strong progress, primarily at overseas demand-driven companies; watch companies with weak progress, undershoots

Apr-Jun results announcements are about to begin. Between July 29 and August 4, 80% of TSE Prime companies are scheduled to announce results (Figure 1). We expect results to be strong relative to the FY2024 guidance that TOPIX companies provided in April-May, which called for sales growth but profit declines. In particular, sharp yen depreciation in Apr-Jun (by 8% against the dollar since end-March) has supported earnings of overseas demand-driven companies but weighed on earnings at companies driven by domestic demand. The June BOJ Tankan survey released July 1 showed improvements in business conditions in the chemical, materials, pulp & paper, textile, and information & communications sectors and deteriorations in conditions in the steel, machinery, auto, retail, and food sectors. Meanwhile, the EPS revision index rose for sectors that benefit from the weak yen, such as banks, insurance, precision instruments, and wholesale, and fell for the auto, electrical equipment, and information & communications sectors. We think the chemical, material, and information & communications sectors have upside that has not been priced in. In FY2023, stock prices moved substantially after Apr-Jun earnings results that were strong/weak relative to fullyear guidance or the consensus estimates. We expect many stocks to show strong progress or overshoots this year as well, so would watch stocks with weak progress or undershoots.

C Likelihood of strong earnings progress: We expect earnings to be strong relative to initial weak FY2024 guidance calling for profit declines. The EPS revision index is also trending upward (Figure 3). The weaker full-year guidance is, the stronger 1Q earnings progress tends to be (Figures 4 and 5). In addition, 1Q revisions to full-year guidance tend to be few given seasonality, and the quality of earnings becomes apparent with progress. 1Q earnings progress, which historically averages 25%, could be higher, at around 27% (in FY2023, 1Q recurring profit was 26.4% of initial guidance, which reflected a 6.2% YoY decline, based on the Tankan survey).

• Weak yen boosts earnings of overseas demand-driven companies, weighs on earnings of domestic demand-driven companies: Yen depreciation YoY and QoQ in Apr-Jun (the yen/dollar rate averaged ¥155.9/\$ versus ¥137.5/\$ in Apr-Jun 2023 and ¥148.4/\$ in Jan-Mar 2024) boosted the earnings of overseas demand-oriented companies but weighed on the earnings of domestic demandoriented companies. Companies' average rate assumption for FY2024 indicated in the BOJ's June Tankan survey is ¥144.8/\$, reflecting 8% yen appreciation versus the Apr-Jun average (Figures 6 and 7). The June Tankan survey also showed an improvement in sentiment among large manufacturers but a slight deterioration in sentiment among non-manufacturers (Figure 8).

C Stock price reactions: Stocks tend to move up or down after Apr-Jun earnings announcements depending on whether earnings are strong or weak relative to full-year guidance (Figure 13). In FY2023, stocks moved substantially in both directions until mid-September. However, stocks of companies that fall short of consensus estimates usually decline by a greater extent than the rise shown by stocks of companies that beat consensus estimates. In 2023, the stocks of companies that fell short of consensus estimates fell significantly, and the difference with the pattern in typical years did not shrink throughout the year (Figure 14).

Update on share buybacks: Compared with the authorized amount of share buybacks in FY2023, the total FY2024 authorized amount announced in Apr-Jun alone was over ¥7 trillion (Figure 15). In the year since the launch of the TSE reforms, broadening moves by companies to improve capital efficiency have been also evident in the increase in share buybacks. The Jul-Sep quarter tends not to have many new buyback announcements, but the amount could increase YoY.
