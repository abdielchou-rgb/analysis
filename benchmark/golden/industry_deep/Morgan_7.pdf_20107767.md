# Venture Vision: Defence - Public vs Private

After a week on the road, we found that investors are clearly hungry for a new theme. Defence is satiating the demand. The top 5 private defence contractors' valuations now make up c3% of the market from a standing start in 2017; much of the private funding in autonomous weapons and drone tech.

## 2024 Institutional Investor ALL-EUROPE RESEARCH POLL

Although investors remain receptive to the AI trade and its broadening out (notably in power and data centres), they are nonetheless looking for the next (non-AI) theme. We're keeping our ear to the ground. Across two weeks of marketing we make three observations:

• Defence is mentioned with increasing regularity and urgency as countries increase their defence spending pledges. In our meetings with American and European investors, opportunities within Aerospace and Defence keep re-emerging as a potential avenue that investors are looking to explore further; in many cases, even if this involves an extension of investment mandates. • US Elections are a key catalyst this year. Our EU Strategy colleagues wrote about this recently. A number of European stocks in the Aerospace and Defence sector are positively exposed to a potential change in status quo US election scenario.

• Rising tensions in multiple geographic locations have brought this theme back to top of mind for Thematic investors looking outside AI. For a theme to dominate in public markets requires earnings upgrades but more importantly still it requires a strong narrative. Continued headlines around national security have made this theme top of mind.

The Multipolar world has been a key focus area for Morgan Stanley Research since 2019. Within the Multipolar world, the key driver is the de-risking (vs decoupling) of Western economies and newly industrialised nations. Verticals exposed to national security, either within Defence spending or critical infrastructure, have been key investment areas for nations trying to re-architecture supply chains. As a reminder to readers, China still controls >30% supply chain production in over half of cutting edge technology, energy transition and basic necessities.

The current status in private markets: While the US remains in a league of its own as far as its private A&D capital markets go, an increasingly interesting development between Asia and Europe has emerged over the last five years. Despite the last two years seeing relatively lower investment in A&D start-ups than might have been expected, Asia is rising up the ranks. While investment is low and therefore difficult to extrapolate reliably, for two years in a row, capital deployed in Asian defence start-ups has surpassed equivalent investment in European defence disruptors.

If we look at the five largest private companies (excluding SpaceX) on a postmoney valuation basis at last primary funding round, the private defence space appears to be thriving. These figures are even larger on a secondary market basis, with these companies trading up to >50% relative to their last primary raise. Continuing the thread above, the private companies raising most capital and generating the largest implied post-money valuations are now those innovating in the autonomous weapon space, particularly in the field of drone technologies.

Today's most valuable private defence companies have grown from near 0 just seven years ago. While the top 10 biggest public defence companies (ex. Boeing and Airbus) have increased their valuation close to 100% since Jan 2017, private counterparts have outperformed this by orders of magnitude - albeit from a substantially lower base. Since 2017, today's top five private companies by postmoney valuation have gone from representing near 0% of the valuation of their public peers to closer to >2% today. Although the scale of the private Defence industry is small in comparison, rapid innovation suggests a strong pipeline among the top private Defence Co's.

## The Fintech Radar (21 Apr 2024)

• #1 Flatpay raises €45m in Series B funding: This week (18th April), Danish POS startup Flatpay announced that it has raised €45m in Series B funding, led by Dawn Capital with participation from Seed Capital Denmark and others. This follows its €15m Series A round in June last year. Founded in 2022, Flatpay provides payment terminals and point of sale (POS) systems aimed at small and medium-sized physical retailers such as restaurants and hairdressers. The company operates a single flat rate for all cards with no monthly fees, keeping costs predictable and transparent for customers. The company plans to use the funding to expand into new European markets and develop new product offerings. The company currently has \~7k customers across Denmark, Finland, and Germany.

• #2 Ramp secures \$150m in Series D-2 round: This week (17th April), US-based spend management company Ramp announced that it has completed a \$150m Series D-2 funding round, raising the company's valuation to \$7.65bn. The latest round was co-led by Khosla Ventures and Founders Fund, and brought new investors Sequoia Capital, Greylock, and 8VC on board, in addition to existing investors. Ramp started out 5 years ago as a corporate card, designed to help companies manage their spending. According to the company, this new funding will be used to deliver new AI capabilities to automate processes, gain deeper insights into spending, and help businesses make better decisions.

• #3 Toku raises \$9.3m to fund LatAm expansion: This week (17th April), LatAm payment solutions provider Toku raised \$9.3m in its latest funding round, led by Gradient Ventures, which included participation from existing investors F-Prime Capital, Clocktower, Y Combinator, Wollef and Funders Club. The round brings Toku's total funding to \~\$20 million. Toku's platform fully automates the payment processing and collection lifecycle for industries with recurring payments. Since starting in 2020, the company has grown revenue \~70% quarterly. According to the company, this allows customers to "reliably collect 5% more revenue while providing a seamless, digital experience for their customers at a 30% lower cost." The company intends to use the funding to drive expansion in Mexico, Brazil, and Chile.

## Real-time Valuation Monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
