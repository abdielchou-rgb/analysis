# Global Exposure Guide 2024 – US

When geopolitical tensions rise or regions lock down, investors often want to know how much revenue or cost exposure a particular company or sector has to a region. Our Global Exposure Guide serves as a reference for investors in times of regional volatility. The guide breaks down revenue exposure at the sector, industry group, and single-stock levels. The guide includes data on 1,067 US & Canadian companies covered by Morgan Stanley analysts.

We estimate that companies in our North America database derive 27% of their revenue from foreign sources ( Exhibit 1 ). The biggest source is Europe at 11%, followed by Asia ex-Japan & ex-China at 5%, Latin America at 4%, and China at 3%. The sectors with the highest foreign revenue exposure are Tech (56%), Materials (44%), and Industrials (29%). The industry groups with the highest exposure are Household & Personal Products (56%), Tech Hardware & Equipment (55%), and Food Beverage & Tobacco (45%).

## Introduction

This edition of our Global Exposure Guide provides an update on foreign revenue and cost exposure by region. The guide serves as a reference for investors, particularly in times of regional instability that can arise on short notice. It is based on a proprietary database built using estimates generated by Morgan Stanley's fundamental analysts and is not available anywhere else.

Our Global Exposure Guide database includes foreign revenue exposure on both an aggregate and stock-level basis. The aggregate view allows us to look at revenue exposure from a macro perspective and see which areas of the economy are most exposed when global events impact multinational companies. However, it is very important to look at stock-level data because the aggregates are strongly influenced by large cap companies (the exposure estimates are weighted by consensus 2024 sales estimates). For these companies, exposure to any one region experiencing instability can have a very large impact on sales.

Overall, companies in our North America database (1,067 US & Canadian companies covered by Morgan Stanley Research) derive 27% of revenue from foreign sources, with the largest source being Europe at 11%, followed by Asia ex-Japan & ex-China at 5%, Latin America at 4%, and China at 3% ( Exhibit 2 ).

## A Note on Methodology

The data in this report is based on estimates produced Morgan Stanley analysts. There were 1,067 companies included in the revenue exposure data and 968 companies included in the cost data. The revenue exposure numbers presented in this guide are weighted by consensus estimates for 2024 sales obtained from FactSet.

Our Energy analysts estimated exposure by using production by region for each company. Oil is a global commodity and identifying end markets is virtually impossible. Because of this, we excluded Energy names from the stock screens and discussions in this report. Semiconductors were excluded from the bar chars in the regional sections and were given their own screens in the China & Asia Pacific sections (see the comment immediately below).

It is important to note that end demand will not necessarily match revenue exposure; for example, Semiconductor companies generate a lot of their revenue in Asia, but their products are used as inputs in final goods sold in the US and Europe. Demand for the final goods is a more important driver for companies in these situations. Because of this dynamic we excluded Semiconductors from the top 5 industry group bar charts and screens throughout this report.

The US & Canada were treated as one "North America"/"Domestic" region throughout this report.

## Sector and Industry Group Overview

## Aggregate Exposures

Foreign revenue exposure varies substantially at the sector and industry group levels — sector exposures range from 56% to 2%, with Tech at the high end and Utilities at the low end. Cyclical sectors dominate the list of sectors with the highest foreign exposure. At the industry group level, exposure ranges from 2% (Telecom & Utilities) to over 50% in Semis, Household & Personal Products, and Tech Hardware.

Within sectors and industry groups, there is additional variation at both the stock level and the specific regions of exposure. To highlight some of this stock-specific variation, we show screens of the top 20 stocks with the highest exposure to each region. Where there are multiple stocks tied for the 20th spot we show all stocks with that level of exposure. Stocks were eligible to be shown in the screens only if their headquarters are located in the United States or Canada or their Top Geographic Revenue location is the United States (if they are Headquartered elsewhere).

Tech generally has the highest foreign revenue exposure with 56% of revenues coming from abroad ( Exhibit 7 ). Semiconductors is partially responsible for the sector's high exposure, with 31% of its revenue coming from China and 27% from Asia ex-Japan & ex-China. We caveat that these numbers may be artificially high relative to end demand as semiconductors bought in these regions are used to make electronics sold in other regions, with many of the finished products ending up in the US and Europe. Despite the effect from Semiconductors, revenue generation in the Tech sector is truly global; Tech Hardware and Software & Services have foreign revenue exposure of 55% and 45%, respectively.

## Revenue Exposure to Europe

Of foreign sources of revenue, companies in our database have the highest exposure to Europe at 12% ( Exhibit 12 ). At the sector level, Tech has the highest exposure to Europe at 19% ( Exhibit 13 ) and is closely followed by Materials at 17%. One thing to note is that this exposure represents an important source of end demand for Tech companies. These companies are not manufacturing intermediate goods in Europe for use as inputs in products sold elsewhere; they are generating revenue from final goods sold in the region. Software & Services is driving this exposure, and Software companies are deriving 23% of their revenue from Europe.

Consumer Discretionary, Communication Services, and Industrials all have revenue exposure to Europe in the double digits. Although Health Care doesn't have much exposure to Europe at the sector level, Pharma, Biotech, and Life Sciences companies generate 21% of their revenue in the region. ## Geographic Cost Exposure

There is minimal disclosure on the regions in which companies incur their costs. To capture data on cost exposure, we asked analysts to estimate the range of costs incurred by each company under their coverage in several regions. The cost ranges we used are 0%, 1-25%, 26-50%, 51-75%, and 76-100%. The regions we used were Europe ex-UK, the UK, the US, Asia Pacific, and the rest of the world. We collected responses for 968 companies. We excluded Materials from the sector tables shown below as there were only 18 companies in our sample. The Covid-19 pandemic laid bare the importance of supply chain management as regional shutdowns toppled established supply chains. While the data we present are estimates of ranges, it is helpful to understand the broad regional exposures companies face when producing goods.

Approximately 77% of the companies in our database incur 50% or more of their costs in North America ( Exhibit 29 ). After North America, Asia Pacific is the largest cost center for US companies, with 7% of companies incurring more than half of their costs in the region and another 5% incurring 26-50% of their costs in the area.

The tables that follow show the percent of companies that fall into each cost range for each region by sector. Asia Pacific is consistently the region sectors with the highest foreign cost exposure. Consumer Discretionary and Tech have the highest cost exposure to the region.
