## Summary of Our Views: Japan and India Still Top Picks in a New Era for Asia Equities

Japan’s ROE Journey. ROE is up 2.5x since 2012 to around 10% and our base case is 12% by 2025 with 1.8x P/B. We forecast robust EPS growth of +9% in 2024e and +9% in 2025e, assuming our FX strategists’ USD/JPY forecast of 140 for 4Q24. We look for the market to re-rate to 15.0x, supported by strong foreign interest and stock buyback (and potentially NISA flows) for a base case Topix Target end 2024 of 2,800. In the most recent quarter, earnings grew 20% yoy with an all time high quantum of buybacks at Yen 9.4trn.

India’s Decade. Multipolar world trends are supporting FDI and portfolio flows, while positive demographics and success in macro-stability underpins a strong capex and profit outlook. We see a secular trend toward sustained superior USD EPS growth versus EM over the cycle, with a young demographic profile supporting equity inflows. Valuations are at a premium (circa 20.0x forward P/E), but in our view this is warranted by superior EPS CAGR (most recent quarter +23% yoy).

China’s Deflationary Challenge. ROE has fallen steadily in recent years and earnings have missed for nine straight quarters. More forceful countercyclical measures are needed to boost demand given incipient balance sheet recession due to headwinds from property and local government austerity. Rebalancing towards household consumption is needed given that an export-oriented approach faces risks from trade tensions.

Sector & thematic views. We still like Korea and Taiwan Semis into an expected 2024 recovery as well as AI-related demand, and Financials in India, Indonesia and Singapore, Industrials in India & Mexico and Consumer Discretionary in India.

Quant & style preference: We are neutral on Value vs. Growth in APxJ/EM as we expect bumpy inflation remains. We recommend investors to reduce momentum exposure for risk management purposes given the strong outperformance YTD.

## Multipolar World in Action

## De-risking or decoupling?

The multipolar world envisioned in our Blue Paper nearly three years ago is now a reality. Witness the different strategies we saw for dealing with Covid, a heightened US-China rivalry for hegemony, and geopolitical decoupling in response to the Russia/Ukraine conflict.

The accelerating adjustments by policymakers and corporates inform our base case: a meaningful but orderly de-risking of critical supply chains and the associated innovation pipeline by 2030. We call this state of US-China relations “Competitive Confrontation,” and we expect it to persist.

However, rising geopolitical and domestic political tensions, combined with accelerating technological diffusion, increase the risk of Decoupling – an abrupt break in trade and investment across multiple industry segments.
