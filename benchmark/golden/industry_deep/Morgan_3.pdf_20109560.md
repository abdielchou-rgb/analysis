# FOMC Reaction: Cuts Coming

Powell states the Fed feels close to cutting. Inflation data is adding to his confidence, while risks to the labor market are becoming more real. Our inflation forecasts point to a September cut, three in total this year. Our rates team keeps UST 2s20s steepeners, enters Sep/Nov FOMC OIS flatteners.

## Key Takeaways

• Rates decision: The FOMC held the policy rate unchanged in a range of 5.25-5.50%, where it has remained since July 2023. Substantial progress in inflation was highlighted, with greater focus on risks to both sides of their mandate in the statement and presser. Powell stated the FOMC feels close to cutting and there was a real debate about cutting at this meeting.

• Outlook: We continue to see three cuts this year, starting in September, informed by our core PCE forecasts that reach a threemonth annualized pace of 2.1% ahead of the meeting. Inflation continues to move convincingly lower, and the Fed cuts every meeting through mid-2025.

• Rates: Our rates strategists see Powell's suggestion that "there was a real discussion back and forth of what the case would be for moving at this meeting" as supporting a continued steepening of the yield curve in an increasingly bullish way. They enter pay fixed September FOMC OIS vs. receive fixed November FOMC OIS.

• FX: Our FX strategists expect USD to soften against JPY as additional Fed cuts are priced. They close their long AUD/NZD trade recommendation as NZD appears more likely to benefit from lower front-end US yields than AUD.

• MBS: Our MBS strategists increase conviction in long basis near YTD tights and shift G2/FN longs into 5.0s and 5.5s.

• Munis: Confidence in cuts should encourage a curve rotation into longer maturity SMA strategies, and to a lesser extent, mutual funds. Tax-free money market yields could stay sideways to higher.

## Cuts Coming

Chair Powell has growing confidence that inflation is on a sustainable path to 2% and a cut is likely in September. Inflation will be the key to cutting in September – inflation data that moves down more quickly or is in line with recent readings meets the bar. The FOMC is more attentive to risks to the labor market, but it does not expect important dislocations before the next meeting. We expect core PCE inflation to average 0.2% over the coming months, kicking off cuts at the next meeting.

## Statement

The FOMC statement showed an important change in the characterization of inflation and the labor market, emphasizing risks to the dual mandate. In line with our expectations, the policymakers have shifted their language from "highly attentive to inflation risks" to "attentive to the risks to both sides of the dual mandate." The statement acknowledged further progress on inflation, adding inflation is "somewhat elevated." The FOMC kept the sentence "In recent months, there has been..." in regards to inflation progress, indicating focus on sequential prints. They also highlight job gains have "moderated" and the "unemployment rate has moved up." Emphasis on the cooling labor market was an important shift to a more balanced tone that we think sets the Fed up to cut in September. They noted that the Committee is now attentive to "both sides of its dual mandate."

## Presser

Powell acknowledges they feel close to cutting: "The broad sense of the committee is that the economy is moving closer to the point at which it will be appropriate to reduce our policy rate." He mentioned that a rate cut "could be on the table at the September meeting" as long as inflation continues to move "down quickly or more or less in line with expectations." A September cut seems to be the base case even if growth remains "reasonably strong." In fact, a dovish comment from Powell was that "there was a real discussion back and forth of what the case would be for moving it this meeting."

Insights into their discussion to cut today, along with his comment that they "can afford to dial back the restriction of the policy rate," provide more certainty that the FOMC will be ready to reduce rates at the next meeting. Inflation is what will meet the bar to cut in September; they are not looking for growth or the labor market to slow further as a requirement.

Powell is comfortable with dissents – "no single person has a veto."

Progress on inflation was highlighted. Right off the bat, Powell noted that inflation has "eased substantially" from a peak of 7%Y to 2.5%Y – only "somewhat" above their 2% goal. Second quarter inflation readings are evidence that recent progress on inflation is giving the Fed more confidence to cut: "The second quarter’s inflation readings have added to our confidence, and more good data would further strengthen that confidence."

Better balance between supply and demand in the labor market. Labor market was characterized as "strong" rather than "relatively tight," "but not overheated." Powell sees the labor market conditions as "normal" after being overheated, but not concerning based a broad range of indicators. Nonetheless, he sees risks to the labor market as real now, and unexpected deterioration in the labor market remains key to larger cuts.

As expected, no forward guidance on pace of rate cuts. The cadence of rate cuts will "depend on the economy," which is highly uncertain.

## Our outlook

We continue to expect three 25bp cuts this year starting in September. Following the reacceleration in 1Q24 inflation, 2Q inflation data has showed a significant step lower, supporting our view that inflation data will give the Fed confidence to start cutting in September. We expect the three-month pace of core PCE inflation will be around 2.1% before the September meeting, slow enough for the Fed to start reducing the level of monetary restraint.

On a 4Q/4Q basis, we forecast core PCE inflation to slow from 3.2% last year to 2.7% this year and 2.1% in 2025. The 12-month inflation rates in 2H24 will remain quite high as we roll into very easy comps to last year's surprising pace of deceleration. This makes the sequential month-over-month path of core PCE inflation more important to follow than year-over-year comparisons when gauging the Fed's reaction function to incoming data. Chair Powell has also underscored the need to focus on month-overmonth sequential prints.

Last week's PCE inflation data added to the convincing evidence the Fed needs to start cutting rates. June core PCE inflation rose 0.18%M, bringing annualized inflation in the last three months to 2.31%, a drop from May-24, which was 2.93%. A key driver of the lower June inflation print was rent inflation, which will continue to be a key driver of lower sequential inflation prints in 2H24. The latest New Tenants Rent index from the BLS and other market rents indicators continued to show weakness in 2Q, consistent with softer rent inflation prints the rest of the year.

## Stay in UST steepeners, enter Sep/Nov FOMC OIS flatteners

For the market pricing of Fed policy, the most important quote from Chair Powell at the press conference reads as follows:

There was a real discussion back and forth of what the case would be for moving it this meeting. You know, a strong majority supported moving, not moving at this meeting. That was the strong sense of the committee. It is a conversation that we had today.

If investors had known – ahead of time – that FOMC participants would engage in a "real discussion" about cutting the policy rate at this meeting, we think the market pricing for July would have reflected a much higher probability of such a move than it did going in.

The idea that FOMC participants discussed moving in July – even though they concluded it wasn't appropriate – raises the specter that they could also discuss the idea of cutting rates by 50bp in September – even if they conclude that it wouldn't be appropriate.

To be clear, our economists look for a 25bp rate cut in September. In addition, Powell seemed to downplay the chance of a 50bp cut in September. But the idea that FOMC participants engaged in a discussion about cutting rates at the July meeting will make it easier for markets to price the risk of a 50bp cut in September, in our view.

• Indeed, if economic activity disappoints and/or inflation surprises to the downside, market prices may reflect a greater probability of a 50bp cut in September than most investors (or economists) may assign in their modal outcome.

In addition, if FOMC participants discuss a 50bp cut at the September meeting, even if they decide against it, investors might assign a higher probability of a 50bp cut at the November FOMC meeting, we think.

In addition, investors may find it easier to assign a higher probability of a 50bp cut at the November meeting for two reasons:

• The November FOMC meeting concludes on November 7 – two days after the US general election on November 5.

• The November FOMC meeting also occurs after the October employment report on November 1; ISM manufacturing and services PMIs on November 1 and 5, respectively.

We think investors should position for a higher market-implied probability of a 50bp rate cut at the November 7th FOMC meeting via paying fixed on the September FOMC meeting OIS rate and receiving fixed on the November FOMC meeting OIS rate, PV01 neutral, at -19.5bp.

In addition to Powell's comments referenced above, we see several more reasons to enter this trade:

• The market is not yet pricing a full 25bp rate cut at the November meeting itself, even though our economists expect one.

• If the Fed does not deliver a rate cut in September, markets could still price in a reasonable probability of a 25bp rate cut in November, especially if Powell suggests the September decision rested on a knife's edge.

• If the Fed delivers the 25bp rate cut in September that our economists expect, markets could still price in non-zero probabilities of a 50bp rate cut in November.

• If the Fed delivers a 50bp rate cut at the September meeting, markets could still price in another 25bp rate cut and/or price in non-zero probabilities of a 50bp rate cut, at the November meeting.

Regarding the broader direction of the yield curve, we remain convicted in a steeper overall yield curve going forward. We think movements in the Treasury curve today support our view. In the morning, the curve bull-flattened after August refunding, where the US Treasury:

• Delivered no change to nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes, in line with our expectations.

• Retained guidance that nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes are not expected to be increased for "at least the next several quarters."

• Released materials from TBAC that noted the lower bound of the optimal bill share is more important than the upper bound.

Taken together, we think these ideas reduced the probability most investors will assign to coupon increases at upcoming meetings, hence the outperformance of the long end of the curve (see US Treasury Refunding Takeaways: August 2024).

Yet, the curve flattening reversed after the release of the July FOMC statement, with the majority of the intraday steepening occurring during Chair Powell's conference where he revealed a rate cut was even a "real discussion" at the July meeting. We think this a good example of how the market-implied pricing of Fed policy, not Treasury supply, drives US duration and curve shape primarily.

## The post-Fed USD outlook

Our rates strategy colleagues expect that markets can price a faster pace of Fed cuts in the coming meetings. Several elements of the meeting stand out:

\- The July FOMC statement noted that "the Committee is attentive to the risks to both sides of its dual mandate" (instead of "to inflation risks" alone previously).

\- The July FOMC statement added the word "somewhat" to its characterization of inflation as "elevated."

\- Chair Powell said in the press conference that "we can afford to begin to dial back the restriction in the policy rate" – instead of using the word "calibrate," for example, as other FOMC participants have done.

\- Chair Powell signaled that the Committee could cut rates in September even if "growth remains reasonably strong and the labor market remains consistent with its current condition."

A shift in market pricing toward a faster pace of Fed cuts can weaken the USD, given the close relationship between the pace of Fed cuts priced and the broad USD. However, we expect any potential USD weakness to be most pronounced against the JPY. And any broader USD declines are likely to be limited.

The last three weeks has witnessed a dramatic shift in the FX carry trade. The returns of a carry strategy developed by our QIS team have roughly halved since June CPI data was released and the Japanese Ministry of Finance was widely perceived to have intervened to strengthen JPY ( Exhibit 4 ).

The implications of a faster pace of Fed cuts also carries lessons for G10 crosses.

• AUD and NZD: Prior to the unwind of the carry trade, the AUD was reliably negatively correlated to near-term Fed pricing – that correlation has collapsed in recent weeks ( Exhibit 7 ). As a result, AUD may not benefit from additional Fed cuts. And our economist no longer expects the RBA to hike rates next week, removing an important near-term AUD positive catalyst. By contrast, NZD has maintained its negative relationship with Fed pricing, meaning it can benefit from more Fed cuts priced. We therefore close our long AUD/NZD trade recommendation.

• CAD and GBP: USD/CAD was not very negatively correlated to near-term Fed expectations prior to the carry trade unwind environment, and remains relatively uncorrelated to 1y OIS rates in the US. Therefore, USD/CAD is not the most attractive way to position for a lower policy rate in the US, in our view. By contrast, GBP/USD has shown an elevated negative correlation to Fed policy rates. As a result, long GBP/CAD positions are likely to benefit from a faster pace of nearterm Fed cuts priced.

While the table above shows the performance across different rate environments, of course there are other factors that drive MBS performance. If we were to draw up the perfect scenario for mortgages from a rates perspective, it would be a steep curve and low vol. We're clearly not there right now – the curve is still inverted while vega and gamma are about 15 and 20 normals above post-GFC averages, respectively. However, it sure seems like we're headed in the right direction. The 2s10s swap curve is roughly -50bp right now, while it's about +10bp 1yr forward, with the 2-year rate expected to rally about 70bp while the 10-year rate rallies 10bp

That means that the market is expecting the swaps curve to steepen about 5bp per month over the next year. Acknowledging that the above table is by no means sacrosanct, the baseline expectations of market participants should be that most weeks should look like a bull steepening, and bull steepenings generally are good for MBS. This would be even better if the Fed were able to execute a programmatic cutting cycle where the market can clearly forecast what will happen, allowing vol to fall.

Where could we be wrong? Plenty of ways. While clearly a macro risk-off event would be bad for mortgages, and recent geopolitical concerns could be a harbinger of things to come, if this results in the Fed cutting, we will likely end up with a steeper curve and lower rates and more demand eventually, but the path to that demand would see widening in the interim. Our larger concern would be what would happen if the market priced in fewer and more delayed cuts.

Into a rally, prepay risk will pick up, and likely will hit Ginnies before conventionals given the increased prepay risk around Ginnies. We think that Ginnies will still see the bulk of the bank demand, but given the rally, we prefer to express the long in 5.0s and 5.5s instead of 5.5s and 6.0s. The risk to G2/FN is increased prepays into a rally based on responsiveness and program changes, along with perhaps less demand into lower yields.

# Munis: A steepener two-step

Mark Schmidt, CFA Mark.Schmidt1@morganstanley.com

We expect a steepener two-step. Supply steepens the curve today. Fed cuts should steepen the curve tomorrow. Though it sets up for a better entry point, a fully disinverted muni curve may take until 2025.

Supply usually rises after Fed weeks – to the tune of \$2-4bn on average over the past year. With >\$7bn of OS mailings this week, we expect this post-Fed pattern to continue. Next week's calendar looks like \$12-14bn. \$5bn+ of new issue ratings activity hints that the week of August 12 could be busy, too (\$10bn+).

SIFMA unlikely to fall – We estimate dealer inventories rose into last week. Retail money fund outflows are less a concern. Instead, we think PMs may issue floaters or sell liquid reserves to buy the calendar.

SMA activity may rise modestly – Net odd-lot demand may rise modestly higher from the present lull of \$150mn/day. After the August 1 reinvest, a \$200mn/day run rate strikes us as possible. We think financial advisors will slowly move cash out of bank deposits and into ladders.

Look for signs of a rotation out the curve, instead of a rotation from cash into bonds – Long ratios may rise from here. We think that would encourage investors to add duration at the margin. Long funds should keep getting inflows at the expense of short funds.

ETF demand should stem ratio widening out long – The long ratio repricing towards 85% makes sense; it and the AA basis could widen a bit more (2-3 ratios or 10-15bp) from here. ETF demand should make for an orderly cheapening.

## Disclosure Section

Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)

Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any premium paid
