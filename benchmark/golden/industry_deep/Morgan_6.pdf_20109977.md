# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## August 22

USTs bear-flatten ahead of Chair Powell's speech; Bunds sell off amid mixed data in the euro area; UK is an outlier with strength in manufacturing PMI; Fedspeak pushes back against a 50bp September cut; BoK on hold; NOK weakens after miss in 2Q GDP; DXY at 101.52 (0.5%); US 10y at 3.852% (+5.1bp).

• USTs bear-flatten following hawkish commentary from FOMC members, in-line jobless claims, and an upside surprise in US services PMI despite underlying PMI details showing less strength.

• Risk-on sentiment in Europe sends Bunds \~5bp higher across the curve amid mixed Europe data, with broadly higher-than-expected Services PMIs coupled with weaker Manufacturing PMIs and a slowdown in 2Q24 euro area wages.

• Gilts rise (10y: +7bp) and GBP outperforms its European peers by ending the day flat as the UK becomes an outlier compared to the US and euro area, with its Manufacturing PMI showing strength and rising once again.

• Fedspeak pushes back against a 50bp September rate cut, with Kansas City Fed President Schmid needing more data before cutting, Boston Fed President Collins supporting easing policy “gradually,” and Philadelphia Fed President Harker saying he is not yet in the “25bp or 50bp camp.”

• The BoK holds its policy rate steady but strikes a dovish tone as it lowers its inflation and growth forecasts and BoK Governor Rhee notes that four board members are open to cutting rates in the next three months; Korea rates rally, outperforming broader AxJ market.

• NOK (-1.0%) underperforms G10 peers following a downside growth surprise in Norway 2Q24 GDP, showing an economy that is a touch weaker than the Norges Bank expected.

Economic Symposium. Markets are focusing on whether Ueda will maintain his hawkish stance, as he had previously suggested an additional rate hike, and whether there will be guidance from Powell on the pace or magnitude of cuts likely to come.

USTs steadily ground cheaper with a handful of catalysts. In the euro area, PMIs were somewhat mixed but broadly showed strength in services while manufacturing remained on the weaker side. The upside surprises in France and the euro area pushed European bond yields higher and supported the cheapening in USTs early in the NY morning. Data in the US also somewhat supported the move higher in yields, as headline values showed some strength while underlying details were a bit more downbeat. Jobless claims came in line with consensus expectations; new home sales rose for the time in five months; and the Kansas City Fed Manufacturing Index, which unlike most regional surveys had correctly signaled the weakness in ISM last month, rebounded (through remained weak) in August.

US PMIs were also a bit of a mixed bag. The S&P Global Composite PMI surprised to the upside, driven by a beat in Services PMI, which expanded further. The data showed business activity in the US remained robust, which helped to alleviate imminent recession fears. Inflation data within the survey also showed it continues to slow. However, Manufacturing PMI fell further into contraction territory as output declined at the fastest rate for 14 months. The release noted that the “manufacturing sector’s forward-looking orders-to-inventory ratio has fallen to one of the lowest levels since the global financial crisis,” which makes the "soft-landing" scenario look “less convincing.” Additionally, employment metrics in the survey fell for the first time in three months with the cooling job market in the manufacturing sector, driven by “growing concerns about the business outlook.”

Despite the bleaker underlying details, USTs seemingly reacted to the beat in the headline figures and continued to sell off. Commentary from FOMC members also weighed on the market as it pushed back against the probability of a 50bp rate cut in September. Kansas City Fed President Schmid was seemingly the most hawkish of the speakers on Thursday as he cautioned that the economy could still see a demand pickup if the Fed is not careful, and added that he needs to see more data before being in favor of cutting rates. Boston Fed President Collins said she believed it will be appropriate to ease policy soon, but supported a “gradual” pace. Philadelphia Fed President Harker struck a similar tone to Collins and said that while he supports a September cut, he is not yet in the camp of 25bp or 50bp cuts as he needs more data.

Following the Fedspeak, STIR pricing slightly lowered the probability of a 50bp rate cut in September to \~25% (P: 38%). Additionally, \~8bp of cuts in 2024 were priced out, with \~95bp of cumulative cuts priced in through the end of the year.

Risk-off sentiment ahead of Fed Chair Powell’s speech at the Jackson Hole Economic Symposium on Friday weighed on US equities. The S&P 500 fell -0.9%, with the technology sector leading the losses.

## Japan:

Early in the Tokyo morning, short- to medium-term JGBs began to sell off. With higher domestic stock prices and steady USD/JPY, markets seemed to be pricing in to some extent an additional BoJ rate hike ahead of BoJ Governor Ueda’s testimony on Friday. After a slightly weak TAP auction for the 5-15.5y sector, short- to mediumterm JGBs continued to sell off, and the curve twist-flattened into Tokyo close. Meanwhile, the long end outperformed on the curve and accelerated its rally into Tokyo close, supported by strong demand. Ultimately, the JGB curve twist-flattened; 2y JGB sold off 1.0bp to 0.355%, 20y JGB rallied 1.5bp to 1.685 %, and 30y JGB rallied 2.0bp to 2.06% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve also twist-flattened and underperformed JGBs especially in the longer end. XCCY basis tightened on broad sectors especially in the longer end.

Ahead of Ueda’s speech on Friday, USD/JPY traded choppily in a range of 144.85 to 145.75, and failed to break meaningfully below the 145.00 mark. In the London morning, GBP/JPY moved higher toward 191.10 in response to the steady UK preliminary PMI data release.

## Europe:

GBP, which saw demand on the back of strong UK PMIs offset its otherwise high sensitivity to equities, outperformed its European peers and was up marginally on the day. Similarly, for safe-haven CHF, the risk-off sentiment and higher US yields roughly balanced out, keeping the currency flat on the day. NOK was one of the G10’s underperformers, falling -0.8% on Thursday, dragged down by a downside surprise in Norway's 2Q24 GDP. SEK fared only marginally better at -0.6%. The EUR started the London session slightly on the back foot with mixed readings from euro area PMI constituents but a material step down in 2Q24 negotiated wage growth. However, ultimately, EUR closed -0.4%.

Despite the mixed preliminary PMIs, which showed higher services and weaker manufacturing, a risk-on tone dominated the price action during the London morning. European equities were higher and the 10y Bund erased all its gains from Wednesday’s trading. The stronger PMIs in the US, combined with in-line jobless claims, pushed 10y Bund yields another 2bp higher likely due to unwinds given the long positioning at present. The 10y Bund ended the day at 2.42% (+5bp d/d) and underperformed on a cross market basis.

At the very front end of the market, the curve moved higher almost in a parallel fashion across Euribor packs, and overall pricing remained stable during the day. The curve exhibited a mild bear-steepening as opposed to the bear-flattening witnessed on the US curve.

Moving to the cash curve, long-end France curve steepened on the 10s30s box versus Germany during the London morning, likely driven by some idiosyncratic flows as other EGB curves moved in the other direction at the back-end of the curve. Conversely, credit spreads stayed resilient during Thursday’s sell-off, and both BTP/Bund and OAT/Bund closed flat at the end of the trading session.

## Dollar Bloc:

Higher US yields combined with a weak risk sentiment as evidenced by a fall in US equities gave the USD a strong bid across G10 FX. In the antipodeans, AUD was down -0.6% and NZD was down -0.4%. CAD only yielded -0.1%, as market

participants awaited Friday’s local retail sales data. Ultimately, the DXY USD Index rose +0.5%.

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened on Thursday alongside mixed moves in equities. CNY and CNH held steady at 7.13. The spread between USD/CNY and the fixing widened to \~150bp. IDR was the main regional underperformer amid heightened political uncertainty and a larger-than-expected widening in the current account deficit. USD/ IDR was up 0.7% while local equities fell (JCI Index: -1.14%). USD/KRW rallied 0.2% as the BoK kept interest rates on hold and offered dovish guidance. Local equity markets rose, with the KOSPI index up 0.2%. PHP was an exception and appreciated 0.3%. THB and SGD weakened \~0.2%. USD/TWD rose 0.1% with a higher-thanexpected unemployment release. MYR remained steady at 4.38 even as inflation moderated in July. INR/USD traded in a narrow range around \~84.0 as markets awaited the release of the RBI monetary policy minutes

AxJ rates were mixed. China rates outperformed following reports of the government assuring markets that the authorities are not attempting to control bond yields. 2y and 10y CGB yields rallied \~2-3bp while the NDIRS curve remained steady. Indonesia yields sold off amid local protests; 5y and 10y INDOGB yields were up 3bp. India G-secs were mixed; 5y and 10y bonds rallied 1bp while 3y yields sold off 1bp. South Korea rates outperformed following the BoK monetary policy meeting. The short end of the curve rallied 2-3bp after a relatively dovish BoK stance, while 10y KTBs were down 2bp. In Malaysia, 10y yields sold off 3bp.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Minutes from the July ECB meeting revealed that the September meeting was “widely seen” by policymakers as a good time to re-evaluate the level of monetary policy restriction, and that it should be approached “with an open mind, which also implied that data dependence was not equivalent to being overly focused on specific, single data points.

• Boston Fed President Collins said that the most recent economic data give her more confidence on the path to 2% inflation, and that it may be appropriate to begin easing policy soon

• Philadelphia Fed President Harker said he is not “in the camp of 25bp or 50bp cuts” and that he needs more data. Nonetheless, he stated that the Fed should start the process of rate cuts and “keep it moving”

• Kansas City Fed President Schmid cautioned that the economy could still see a demand pickup if the Fed is not careful. On rate cuts, he said he needs to see more data before supporting rate cuts

## Emerging Markets

• The Bank of Korea (BoK) maintained its policy rate at 3.50% at its August monetary policy meeting, in line with our economists' and consensus expectations. The central bank said that although inflation has continued its downward trend and the recovery in domestic demand has been modest, concerns remain in the housing market that are affecting real estate prices. The BoK lowered its projections for both growth and inflation for 2024, downgrading the growth forecast to 2.4% (P: 2.5%) and consumer inflation to 2.5% (P: 2.6%). The board said they will examine the proper timing of rate cuts while they maintain a restrictive monetary policy stance (for more, read here)

• BoK Governor Rhee said that four board members were open to cutting rates in the next three months, while the remaining two believed the current restrictive policy should be maintained. He added that the focus of the BoK remains on financial stability, despite inflation showing signs of continuing its downward trend toward 2%

## Economic Release Notes

## Developed Markets

• In Japan, Weekly Ministry of Finance International Transactions in Securities data for the week of August 11-17 suggested Japanese market participants continued to be decent net buyers of foreign long-term debt securities (+JPY 1.85tr). We believe banks were the main contributor to such strong net purchases. While they had seemingly taken profit on their tactical foreign bond exposure in June and July as UST yields approached their recent lows, they might have begun to expand their foreign bond exposure in August as the timing of possible Fed rate cuts approaches. Otherwise, overseas market participants were large net buyers of Japan long-term debt securities (+JPY 1.53tr) and modest net sellers of Japan equity/funds (-JPY 47.9bn)

• In Norway, Mainland 2Q24 GDP grew 0.1% q/q (C: 0.2%; P: 0.2%). The main driver was household consumption, up 1.6% q/q, mostly due to car purchases. Even without car purchases, household growth was strong at 0.7% q/q. On the downside, fishing and construction dragged down production in 2Q24. In sum, our economists note the release shows a touch weaker economy than Norges Bank expected

• In France, preliminary Composite PMI rose to 52.7 in August (C: 49.2; P: 49.1), driven by services which rose to 55.0 (P: 50.1) while manufacturing fell further to 42.1 (P: 44.0). Services were likely boosted by the Paris Olympics

• In Germany, preliminary Composite PMI fell to 48.5 in August (C: 49.2; P: 49.1) with declines in both services and manufacturing

• In the Eurozone, preliminary Composite PMI rose to 51.2 in August (C: 50.1; P: 50.2), driven by Services PMI which rose to 53.3 (C: 51.7; P: 51.9) while Manufacturing PMI edged down to 45.6 (C: 45.8; P: 45.8). Manufacturing in euro area ex-France and Germany appeared to do better, supporting the continued contribution of non-core countries to total EA growth. Export demand remained weak and the labor market, while weakening slightly, was generally robust and pointed to firms’ reluctance to lay off workers. Price data indicated that firms increased margins for the first time in a while, although our economists note it is difficult to extrapolate any meaningful conclusion from this

• In the UK, Composite PMI rose to 53.4 (C: 53.0; P: 52.8) as the Manufacturing PMI increased to 52.5 (C: 52.2; P: 52.1) and Services PMI rose to 53.3 (C: 52.8; P: 52.5) in August. The momentum in services remained solid and consistent with growth at 0.3% q/q, which is where our economists 3Q24 forecasts are. New business in the sector is rising at a robust pace, with the release noting that domestic demand is solid but export orders are softening. The input costs index was down and indicated an increase in line with 4Q19, while the prices charged index softened a touch less, but was still among the slowest seen since the start of 2021. # G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
