# Venture Vision: 60% Bankruptcy Growth

Venture-backed bankruptcies are rising 60% YoY; Chapter 11 filings are at their highest in 12 years. Prior cycles suggest a peak 24-months after the Fed stops hiking...July 2025 on this basis. Economic leading indicator or simply 2021's great unwind?

The strength of the US economy is front and centre for most investors. Our Strategists are in the soft-landing camp and our Economists retain their longstanding call for a 25bps cut by the Fed in September. While helpful, bankruptcies have always peaked long after rates begin to fall.

We offer a different, left-field perspective that investors may have missed. Small companies have - unsurprisingly - led the rise in bankruptcies each cycle back to the 1990s. What fewer people will be aware of is that start-up, venture-backed business bankruptcies are currently growing at 60% YoY and accelerating.

![](images/0f2fec763a7c1d44871a0813f1105526798f9d96846b09293d6b7f1aa8369e27.jpg)
Source: Bloomberg, Morgan Stanley Research

The data, which macro onlookers may miss, comes from Carta. The company provides cap table management (shareholder structure services) to start-up companies. It is typically hard to track the number of small company bankruptcies, as in most countries these companies need not file any paperwork to dissolve the company. However, these data from Carta show the specific numbers of companies folding because they do inform Carta as to the reason they stop using their services. Carta has a leading market share in this field and so provides a strong proxy for bankruptcy trends in the rest of the venture-backed industry.

Top down indicators such as Chapter 11 business filings of bankruptcy proceedings in the US have been rising steadily from artificially suppressed 2021 lows - albeit these relate to larger companies typically. Carta's latest quarterly print for venture-backed companies shutting down has been rising at an accelerating pace. From the 2019 inception of this data set, the level of venture-backed bankruptcies has risen by >7x.

This matters, given (1) public markets' alertness to recent weakness in employment data and (2) the clearly interrelated dynamic with US bankruptcy data and said employment data. One could point to the past two years of bankruptcy filings diverging from the unemployment rate as evidence that we should perhaps not read too far into rising bankruptcies as a lead indicator for US unemployment. However, given VC-backed firms employ c4m employees across the US, in addition to the PEbacked employees of c12m, we argue that a rapid rise in VC-backed business shutdowns should not be dismissed out of hand.

One can in theory use Carta's market share to scale up the likely economy-wide level of shut-downs. Rather than this method, we simply rebase to 100 the figures from the date at which Carta started reporting these figures in 2019. This, though, is the major caveat to this data series' usefulness. Without through-cycle comparables, we cannot say with confidence how reliable it is as a leading indicator for either (1) subsequent bankruptcy trends in larger companies in the economy or (2) whether it is sending a false-positive signal, or whether it has done in the past.

Exhibit 4: All venture shut-downs and priced-equity company shut-downs
![](images/21b4a0a6b668ee8a0c1bb1f087c7f7fe5b85179ff27b06b7e6e429bc9fce138c.jpg)
Source: Carta, Morgan Stanley Research

What we are able to tell, however, is that bankruptcy pressure is a trickle-up effect, rather than a trickle-down effect. This is common sense, but not easy to prove from available data beyond Carta's. Priced seed rounds (i.e. companies that have raised external capital with a priced equity issuance) tend to be trying to find productmarket-fit, are highly unlikely to be profitable and will most likely need to raise capital again. Their cash runway is shortest (i.e. highest cash burn rates). Their bankruptcy trend is up >3x since 2022. What concerns us more is that Series A but particularly Series B companies, which should have found product market fit and be pushing towards break-even profitability, are starting to see heightened shut-down rates 3-4x higher than 12-18 months ago.

Our economists are generally more sanguine on recent employment and macro data points. We therefore do not recommend putting great weight on this dataset in isolation. However, we do suggest it is worth incorporating into investors' broader arsenal of data points for assessing macroeconomic deterioration from the bottom up.

One alternative explanation for the above data is not necessarily that there is a consumer-led deterioration forcing companies to shut down. Rather we think it is equally or even more likely that an abnormally high number of companies raised an abnormally large amount of money during 2021-2022, which is still yet to flush out in any material way. It could be argued this is now happening, albeit later than we had implied (here and here).

It does not change the main conclusion though. If several million US jobs are only viable on the premise of cheap venture-capital, which has become more expensive and thus scarcer, then any combination of the following would need to happen in order to mitigate venture spillover risks to the rest of the economy: (1) unprecedented consolidation of weaker venture-backed businesses through acquihire (where the target is predominantly the people rather than the technology itself), (2) a rapid and unlikely pivot to profitability for most early-stage companies or (3) further lay-offs to extend cash runway but which most businesses have already stretched to their limits through 2020-2022 (see here).

## THE FINTECH RADAR (11 AUG 2024)

• #1 Flywire Corporation acquires Invoiced: This week (7th August), US-based Flywire Corporation (covered by James Faucette) announced the acquisition of Invoiced, a SaaS platform that enables the automation of the critical order-to-cash process. Invoiced allows B2B finance teams to automate accounts receivable (A/R) processes through the management of invoices, communication with payers, and reconciliation of the payments to their ERP systems, which is made possible through Invoiced's deep integrations with accounting systems and ERPs. Through the acquisition, Flywire's global payment network and Invoiced A/R automation software will provide a full-suite software and payments solution that streamlines workflows for finance departments. The acquisition of Invoiced reiterates Flywire's long-term plans to expand its business lines organically and through M&A. Total consideration was \$55mm, with Invoiced expected to contribute c. \$2m of revenue in 2024 with "software-like" gross margins. Flywire also expects to reinvest the majority of the margin to drive growth to the combined business.

• #2 NCR Voyix enters agreement to sell digital banking platform: This week (6th August), NCR Voyix (covered by Erik Woodring) signed a definitive agreement to sell its cloud-based digital banking business to an affiliate of Veritas Capital for c. \$2.45bn (20x FCF) in cash, with an additional contingent consideration of up to \$100m. Voyix's digital banking platform enhances consumer and business digital banking through optimised customer experience. The platform serves over 1,300 financial institutions and over 20m active users. The sale will enable NCR Voyix to focus on its core restaurant and retail customers and is part of the company's larger strategic initiative to simplify its operations, allowing it to focus on its core software and service offerings. The proceeds from the sale will be put towards paying down debt, which should reduce net leverage ratio by 2x turns, to \~2.0x net debt/CY24 PF Adj. EBITDA (of \$430m). The transaction received approval from the NCR Voyix’s Board of Directors and, subject to customary closing conditions, is expected to close by 4Q24. (For more details please see: NCR Voyix Corp.: Taking Action To Unlock Value)

• #3 Lucky One raises \$3m in convertible note: This week (8th August), the Egyptian fintech start-up, Lucky One, raised \$3m in funding, in the form of a convertible note. The funding round involved existing investors, namely, KEM, Lorax Capital Partners and DisrupTech Ventures. Lucky One provides consumer credit solutions to unbanked Egyptians, offering credit, discount and cash back services to over 13m registered users. The injected capital is aimed towards expanding and developing their credit platform and supports the expansion into new markets, with the aim to become profitable by 1Q25. Lucky One's strategy aligns with the Egyptian government's initiative to expand credit accessibility to an underbanked population.

• #4 Stori secures \$212m in equity and debt financing: This week (7th August), Mexican-based fintech unicorn, Stori, secured \$212m in equity and debt financing, marking the largest funding round in Mexico in the past year. Within the \$212m raise, \$105m was in equity financing, led by BAI and Notable Capital, with participation from ACE Redpoint Ventures, General Catalyst, Tresalia, Goodwater, GIC, and Lightspeed Ventures. The \$107m in debt financing was provided by Davidson Kemper Capital Management LP and Goldman Sachs. Stori offers credit cards and deposit accounts to Mexico's underserved population (c. 3m customers). The company achieved unicorn status in 2022 following a \$50m equity funding round that resulted in a £1.2bn valuation. The capital secured will support Stori in rolling out new products and further expanding their current offerings, allowing them to maintain and strengthen market position against peers including Nubank and Uala.

• #5 Nuvei to acquire Pay2All: This week (5th August), Canadian fintech, Nuevei Corporation, announced that it has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Pay2All, a Brazilian licensed Payment Institution. Nuvei is a global payment technology provider, offering a payment solution suite across 200 markets. The transaction will strengthen Nuvei's presence in the region and reinforce its mission to provide localised and modern payment solutions to businesses in Brazil. The payment solutions license, a regulatory requirement needed to provide transactional services for authorized operators in the online gaming / sports betting market, will further allow Nuvei to access the Brazilian iGaming market worth c. \$2bn. The transaction is subject to customary closing conditions.

Unless otherwise indicated, the news reports referenced above have not been confirmed.

## Real-time valuation monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
