Looking ahead to next week, focus turns to the May CPI report as well as the FOMC statement and press conference on Wednesday. We forecast headline CPI rose 0.17% (consensus: 0.1%), with the NSA index rising to 314.520, while core CPI rose 0.33% (consensus: 0.3%), not far from rounding to 0.4%. Meanwhile, the market fixing is currently trading at 314.330, which we estimate is implying roughly a 0.26% m/m increase in core, suggesting upside risks to breakevens next week, if our forecast comes to fruition (Figure 5Markets picd fo0.26% n CPI y,lgh bwu ). However, we think there is limited room for breakevens to outperform versus the print itself, given the hawkish implications for the Fed. Within the details of the report, we see OER and rent CPI rising 0.40% and 0.36%, slowing only modestly versus recent averages, while we see used cars rebounding 1.5% in May, following decent declines over the prior two months (see Economics). Overall, we expect the report is likely to show core inflation still running too high for the Fed<sup>’</sup>s comfort even while slowing modestly. Moreover, from a technical perspective, if our forecast is correct, carry on long TIPS and breakeven positions is set to moderate further in July.

Turning to the Fed, we expect focus will be on the SEP and dot plot, as we look for little change in the statement and the message from the press conference is still likely to emphasize both patience and optimism. We think the median dot for 2024 is likely to show two eases this year, down from three at the March meeting (see FOMC preview, Michael Feroli, 6/7/24). Such an outcome would be unlikely, in our view, to dislodge breakevens from their recent trading range.

Beyond next week, we expect benchmark breakevens will continue to trade in a fairly tight range over coming months. On the back of this morning<sup>’</sup>s employment report, we now look for one cut this year (in November) versus three previously, given that job growth has proved more resilient and wage growth more persistent than we previously expected (see Strong jobs overshadow rising unemployment, Michael Feroli, 6/7/24). However, we still expect a quarterly pace of rate cuts thereafter, against a backdrop of gradually cooling labor markets as well as a moderation in growth and inflation. Recall that our fair value framework regresses seasonally-adjusted benchmark breakevens on our JPMCCI broad commodity curve index as well as the shape of the 3m3m/15m3m OIS curve—a macro risk proxy, with breakevens covarying positively with both factors. With the market currently pricing 35bp of easing this year and 83bp of easing next year, we think this curve should remain in a narrow range over coming quarters.

Meanwhile, we expect commodity prices to rebound higher in the near term, before softening gradually into early 2025. Notably, our commodity strategists view last Sunday<sup>’</sup>s OPEC announcement as market neutral for global crude oil balances and prices in 2024—they believe oil prices are currently cheap and continue to make a bullish case for oil in 3Q24, especially as we enter the peak summer driving season (see Oil Markets Weekly, Natasha Kaneva, 6/3/24). However, pressure on prices could build after that, as non-OPEC supply rises and demand slows.

Overall, taking into account the changes in our Fed and nominal yield forecasts, we now see less room for narrowing in breakevens in the third quarter, and mark up our forecasts accordingly. However, our forecasts had already incorporated a recovery in breakevens in 4Q, and our targets beyond the upcoming quarter remain largely unchanged. We see 5- and 10-year breakevens ending the year at 230bp, with breakevens narrowing modestly over the first half of 2025 (Figure 6We rvisupbakn tgfo hcm q, wl  y d). Alongside the revisions to our nominal yield targets, we also revise up our real yield forecasts over coming quarters: we now look for real yields to be relatively rangebound over 2H24, with 10-year real yields at 2.10% at year-end, before declining in 1H25.

## An update on inflation swap trading activity

Utilizing data through the end of May from the DTCC swap data repository (SDR), we can observe that inflation swap activity has continued to grow thus far in 2024. Indeed Figure 7 illustrates that inflation swap average daily trading volumes have continued to rise, increasing 12% from an average \$3.63bn last year to \$4.08bn YTD. In addition to absolute levels, inflation swap trading volumes are also rising on a relative basis vis-à-vis cash transactions and now comprise 22.6% of TIPS average daily trading volumes. More granularly, average daily inflation swap trading volumes rose to \$4.34bn in February, before declining slightly in recent months to \$3.85bn in May. Digging into the composition of activity, we note the maturity profile continues to shift shorter, with 53% of all inflation swap transactions concentrated in the 0-5 year sector, the highest share since 2018 (Figure 8).
