## US Rates

 Following softer June employment and inflation data, together with dovish remarks from Chair Powell, we now expect the Fed to first cut in September, with a quarterly cadence of eases thereafter. We adjust lower our interest rate forecasts, seeing 10-year yields reaching 4.15% by YE24, from 4.40% previously. While this is supportive of adding duration, we remain neutral given negative carry and with OIS pricing in a more dovish forecast than our own. We maintain 5s/30s steepeners, but recommend hedging over the near-term by selling a beta-weighted amount of 2-year Treasuries

 We forecast a slightly wider \$1.9tn FY24 deficit. Treasury will likely leave coupon auction sizes unchanged until late 2025 and prefer to keep T-bill share of total debt elevated above 20%. Demand continues to rotate from price insensitive to price sensitive sources. A split Congress is likely to temper any Presidential outcome. A Trump victory likely results in marginally higher yields while a Biden victory likely means modestly lower yield

## International Rates

 The journey of DM central banks towards a gradual removal of monetary policy restriction made a further step this week. The ECB kept policy rates unchanged but with increasing confidence that incoming information broadly supports the medium term inflation outlook.

## Commodities

 The events of the past few weeks, President Biden’s poor debate performance, followed by Saturday’s assassination attempt on former President Donald Trump, have led to a clear shift in the tone of the elections and increased probability for a sizable Red Wave. With 16 weeks to go until th elections, a lot can change between now and November 5.

 While there is a lack of a clear pre-election trade given the range of scenarios that could play out, gold stands out as a winner. Structurally bullish drivers for gold, like growing concern on complacency over the rise in fiscal debt, tariffs, and trade retaliations as well as broad “America First’ rhetoric, are likely to remain intact.

## Currencies

 US elections featured prominently this week and betting market odds have shifted materially, but USD is flat to softer on net. We attribute that to a mix of positioning, valuations, and broadly-lower US yields. Our US economics & rates strategists’ findings complement the bullish skew of risks for USD around the elections via inflation and bear steepening risks.

 We continue to think that the pullback in the dollar will not be sustained over the medium term and should be shallow, given the uncertain cyclical backdrop outside the US and trade conflict risks from the US elections. Strong USD seasonality during August can offer a tactical pillar of support.

## Emerging Markets

 US elections are starting to dominate the newsflow, with polling moving towards scenarios that will challenge EM assets. Despite that, we find little evidence that EM markets have priced a lot of risk premia directly related to US elections. While EM FX and rates have underperformed since late May, this is mostly due to Latam weakness at the start of June related to Mexican elections, Brazilian fiscal concerns and commodity price falls.

## Oil Markets: Intentions to substantially increase domestic production to bring down inflation

• Trump intends to reduce inflation by increasing domestic oil production, that harks back to Trump’s speech at the National Republican Senatorial Committee, where he stated that “energy prices remain high because we keep supply in the ground”. The 3 mbd growth target is short on details. It is not far off from our modelled 2.4 mbd growth in oil liquids supply (crude, condensate and natural gas liquids) in 2025-2028, but is more than three times higher than our projected 0.8 mbd increase in crude and condensate supply during the same period. For scale, US total oil liquids production will likely rise by 3.8 mbd in 2021-2024, during Biden’s term.

• We estimate the equilibrium price of WTI oil at around \$70 per barrel and reject suggestions that increasing US oil production to bring the price of oil down to \$40/bbl, with the ultimate goal of bringing down inflation, have merit. Even at \$60/bbl, prices are too low to incentivize production, potentially leading to a spike to \$100/bbl in the following year.
