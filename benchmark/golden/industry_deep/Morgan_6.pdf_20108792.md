# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 20

BoE on hold, but minutes lean dovish; NOK strengthens while CHF weakens amid respective central bank meetings; USD/IDR reaches new recent high; USTs sell off; JGBs twist-flatten after MoF considers new issuance plan; MXN strengthens intraday; DXY at 105.66 (+0.4%); US 10y at 4.259% (+3.7bp).

• BoE leaves rates unchanged in a 7–2 vote, but minutes indicate that for some members the decision to maintain the policy rate was “finely balanced”; market-implied probability for an August rate cut increases to 62% (P: 33%).

• Norges Bank maintains rates and provides upward revisions to policy rate path forecasts, which support NOK (EUR/NOK: –0.5%; USD/ NOK: +0.1%), whereas SNB delivers its second 25bp cut, weakening CHF (EUR/CHF: +0.4%; USD/CHF: +0.8%) .

• USD/IDR (+0.4%) reaches a new recent high of 16,430 as BI keeps rates on hold; BI Governor Warjiyo says the USD/IDR's fundamental value is below 16,000, and he expects IDR to strengthen going forward.

• USTs sell off absent clear fundamental catalysts despite economic data continuing to allude to increasing economic weakness and auctions continuing to be met with strong demand.

• JGB curve twist-flattens following reports that the MoF is considering a plan to shift more of its bond issuance to shorter maturities.

• MXN strengthens 1.0% to 18.26 per USD intraday alongside President-elect Sheinbaum’s announcement of cabinet officials.

In the UK, the BoE maintained its rate in a 7–2 vote. The minutes leaned dovish as they highlighted that for some members who voted to keep rates steady, the May inflation data did not “alter significantly the disinflationary trajectory that the economy was on” and hence the policy decision was “finely balanced,” which to some market participants signaled that a rate cut may be more imminent than previously expected. Gilts rallied and cuts were pulled forward, with market-implied pricing for an August BoE rate cut up to 62% (P: 33%).

The USD strengthened after the SNB cut rates, and gradually continued to move higher overnight alongside a rise in UST yields. The sell-off continued into the NY morning absent clear fundamental catalysts, though it might have been aided by lingering concerns over the increase in the CBO’s forecast for the US budget deficit, which was released on Tuesday.

In the NY morning, economic data was mixed but overall continued to show easing economic conditions. Housing starts and business permits contracted in May, jobless claims were higher than expected, and the Philadelphia Fed Business Outlook fell to 1.3 in June (P: 4.5), still in expansion but much less so than in April. However, the Philadelphia Fed Prices Paid Index rose 4 points to 22.5, and 26% (P: 19%) of firms surveyed reported increases in input prices. The strength in prices might have also contributed to the sell-off in UST yields in the NY morning.

Despite the sell-off, the demand for Treasuries remained intact as the 5yr TIPS reopening auction delivered strong results. The offering stopped out at a yield of 2.05%, \~2.4bp through. The 2.52x bid-to-cover ratio was largely in line with the average from the last four auctions, but the strength in the demand of the auction was showcased by having the lowest dealer takedown on record. Allotment to direct and indirect participants increased from the December auction; indirect allotment grew to 79.1% (P: 78.3%) and direct allotment grew to 18.5% (P: 17.8%). This left primary dealers with an allotment of just 2.3% (P: 3.9%), the lowest level recorded.

In the NY afternoon, Treasury prices rose from session lows, and yields slowly ground lower. By the end of the NY session, USTs bear-steepened, with 2y yields up 3bp and 30y yields up 4bp. The USD maintained its overnight strength, and the DXY USD Index rose 0.4% on Thursday.

In equities, the S&P 500 breached 5,500 intraday before it fell from session highs and closed -0.3% lower. The decline was led by technology stocks as there had been profit-taking in chipmaker stocks. The dampened risk sentiment that resulted from higher UST yields also led the move lower.

Geopolitical tensions and momentum continued to support Brent crude oil futures (+0.7%), which rose on the back of a weekly decline in US crude and gasoline inventories. Gold (+1.4%) rose following weakening economic data that may support Fed cuts later this year.

## Japan:

The JGB curve twist-flattened in the Tokyo morning as the after-hours rise in US rates, and media reports that the MoF is considering increasing issuance of shorter maturities and reducing super-long JGBs, continued to weigh on market sentiment. The belly of the curve underperformed ahead of the 5y auction, which was digested smoothly as it was in line with consensus expectations. However, in the Tokyo afternoon, the underperformance of the belly accelerated, and the 20y JGB yield also started to rise ahead of the sector’s auction next Tuesday. 5y JGB sold off 0.5bp to 0.51%, 10y JGB sold off 2.0bp to 0.95%, and 30y JGB rallied 0.5bp to 2.12% on a simple yield basis.

TONA-OIS curve bear-steepened and outperformed JGBs for tenors below 30y, supported by demand to square positions, while 30y and 40y ASW richened. XCCY basis widened particularly in the belly of the curve.

USD/JPY initially traded lower in the early Tokyo morning with few clear catalysts amid a closed US market, but climbed higher from market open at \~9am JST. Higher US rates in the after-hours trading contributed to a rise in USD/JPY. For other CCY/ JPY pairs, EUR/JPY was generally in a range between 169.7 and 169.9 during the Tokyo session. In the London morning, USD/JPY temporarily surged to above 158.4 after the SNB cut its policy rate for the second consecutive meeting.

## Europe:

Low-yielding currencies weakened the most against USD, while oil-sensitive currencies outperformed in G10. The BoE kept the bank rate unchanged, but included a softer tightening bias than markets had expected after services inflation had surprised to the upside on Wednesday. Gilts outperformed in DM bond markets, but the FX reaction was relatively muted. GBP (-0.4%) weakened in line with the broader USD movement, while EUR/GBP closed up only 0.1%. Norges Bank kept rates unchanged, but revised its policy rate path to be higher than what analysts had expected. This forward guidance from the Norges Bank, combined with higher oil prices, supported NOK (EUR/NOK: –0.5%; USD/NOK: +0.1%). NOK/SEK gained 0.7% on Thursday. Contrary to some expectations, the SNB delivered a second rate 25bp cut, which weighed on CHF. USD/CHF (+0.8%) rose above back above 0.89 (EUR/ CHF +0.4%).

European duration traded heavily on Thursday, and the 10y Bund sold off 3bp to 2.43%. On a cross-market basis, the euro area slightly outperformed the US during the London session. The sell-off was broad-based across tenors as the curve moved in a parallel fashion. On country spreads, the tightening in both OAT/Bund and BTP/ Bund continued, and they respectively tightened by 2bp on Thursday.

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly against the USD as the PBoC set the USD/CNY fixing at the highest level since last November (7.1192). USD/CNY traded up to 7.2606, not far from the upper bound implied by the fixing (7.2615). USD/CNH gained 0.1% post-fixing and ended the Tokyo session at \~7.2870, alongside an

underperformance of local equities (CSI 300: -0.7%). IDR was the main underperformer in the region as BI kept rates on hold; USD/IDR gained 0.4% to a new recent high of 16,430 and reached the highest level since April 2020. KRW, THB, and SGD weakened 0.2%, with spillover effects from RMB. USD/INR was also up 0.2% to 83.65, and reached a historical high even as local equities gained (NIFTY: +0.2%) and foreign portfolio inflows continued. TWD was an exception; USD/TWD weakened marginally amid a continued outperformance in the local equity market (TAIEX: +0.9%), \~US\$703mn of foreign equity inflows, and stronger-than-expected May export orders data. MYR and PHP remained relatively stable.

Most AxJ rates sold off following the move in USTs. China and Thailand were exceptions. China rates held steady in both the CGB and NDIRS curves amid weak local risk sentiment, and as the loan prime rates were kept unchanged as anticipated. Thailand rates rallied \~1bp across the NDOIS curve as PM Srettha reiterated the need for the digital wallet program and said the BoT’s suggestion to limit the program will have to be discussed. Korea rates were the main underperformers after outperforming in the past three sessions; 10y KRW NDIRS yield rose 5bp, and the 2s10s curve bear-steepened 2bp. Hong Kong rates also underperformed, particularly in the front end of curve; the 1y IRS yield rose 4bp alongside a 5bp rise in 1m HIBOR. In India, 10y G-sec yield rose by \~1bp to 6.98%, while 5y NDOIS yield rose more meaningfully, by 3bp, and the 1s5s curve bearsteepened. In Indonesia, 10y INDOGB yield remained stable at 7.12% post the BI decision.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The Norges Bank kept the policy rate unchanged at the June meeting, with the forward guidance still indicating that the policy rate will be kept at current levels "for some time ahead," in line with expectations. Our economists note that the policy rate path was revised upwards by more than expected, which indicated that should the economy evolve as expected in the June projections, the policy rate will be kept at terminal until the end of the year. Inflation was revised down in 2024 by 10bp on both core and headline, on the back of softer data, but up in 2025 which warranted the change in the policy rate path. GDP was revised up to 0.8% y/y in 2024, in line with our economists’ forecast, and up to 1.3% y/y in 2025

• The SNB cut its policy rate 25bp to 1.25% at its June meeting. The central bank kept its two-way bias about FX market activity in its statement. Domestic services was once again flagged as the main driver of inflation. The updated conditional inflation forecast kept 4Q24 unchanged at 1.4% y/y, reduced 4Q25 to 1.1% y/y (P: 1.2%), and reduced 4Q26 to 1.0% y/y (P: 1.1%). During the press conference, SNB Chair Jordan mentioned that: (1) the current policy rate is not expansionary; (2) rent makes up roughly half of domestic services inflation, and the impact from rental reference rate should start to fade; (3) on FX, there is no target and no indication of intervention bias in either direction; (4) wage growth is compatible with price stability; and (5) the real neutral rate is close to or slightly above 0%

• The BoE kept the bank rate unchanged at its June meeting. Our economists note that much like the May meeting, the minutes were dovish and set up a rate cut despite market participants' overreaction to recent inflation data. Not only did Ramsden and Dhingra vote for a rate cut again, but more members nearly joined them. Our economists note that while the message is dovish, the follow-through is lacking. The path ahead looks thorny, and our economists' base case remains an August cut

• ECB Governing Council Member Knot remarked that disinflation is underway despite being bumpy. He reiterated that the high uncertainty on the economy warrants a data-dependent approach

• Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari said he is confident that the Fed will get inflation back to the 2% target, but estimated it will likely take a year or two to get it to the target

## Emerging Markets

• Bank Indonesia (BI) decided to keep its key rate unchanged at 6.25% in its June monetary policy meeting (MSe: 6.25%; C: 6.25%). The growth, inflation, and current account deficit forecasts were also maintained. The BI also said it will issue a new rule that allows it to set the maximum limit for banks’ foreign funding in the range of 25-35% of their capital, compared to the current cap of a fixed 30%

• BI Governor Warjiyo said the rate decision is a proactive, forward-looking strategy to guarantee that inflation stays within the target range of 1.5-3.5% in 2024 and 2025. To keep the rupiah stable, he stated the central bank will continue to intervene in the spot, DNDF, and secondary government bond markets. He also said BI believes USD/IDR's fundamental value to be below

16,000 and sees a trend toward the IDR strengthening in the future. He attributed the currency's weakness to the Fed's still-uncertain policy path, geopolitical uncertainties, and strong corporate demand for USD. Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
