# Asia EM Equity Strategy | Asia Pacific

# Stay OW Japan, India & Singapore; D/grade Mexico and Indonesia; U/grade South Africa

After four pivotal EM elections, we reiterate our preference for India and Japan as preferred picks in EM and DM Asia-Pacific but turn more cautious on Mexico (to EW) and Indonesia (to UW). We upgrade South Africa on a cyclical trough and potential upside risks. We adjust Focus Lists accordingly.

Overall stance on Japan vs EM: In our Global Strategy and Asia EM Equity Mid-Year Outlook, we emphasized a preference for Japan vs EM equities. Our neutral stance on EM looks justified in light of post-election volatility, a short-lived China/HK rally and challenging market conditions in LatAm and Middle East. Instead, we keep a preference for reflation and reform in Japan and Semis/Hardware for secular AI growth and non-AI tech recovery into 2H25.

Stay OW India for a redoubled growth focus: India remains a secular opportunity, with the BJP-led coalition likely to redouble their focus on growth rather than shift toward redistribution, in our collective view (see Modi 3.0: This Remains India's Decade). We expect volatility to settle back down as evidence that the pillars of macro-stability remain upright going into cabinet formation and the July Budget.

Downgrade Indonesia to UW and Mexico to EW: Indonesia and Mexico had high investor positioning and expectations into their elections, and investors are closely monitoring for a continued commitment to prudent and productive, capex/FDI-led growth models after decisive victories. Both markets have seen earnings revisions turn negative, while our Latin American equity strategy team have downgraded Mexico to EW on risks that stability backstops may be tested in coming months (see Mexico Economics & Strategy: Revisiting Backstops). Inauguration dates are October 1 in Mexico and October 20 in Indonesia.

Upgrade South Africa to OW: South Africa saw the least clear election outcome, with coalition talks (or confidence/supply agreement) potentially ongoing for a while. Our macro/strategy team lay out the implications of electoral/reform scenarios here. Low valuations and an expected underlying earnings recovery suggest a positive risk/reward skew and have moved South Africa up to #3 ranked and OW in our framework, but elevated uncertainty is likely to remain a feature.

Focus List changes: We switch into Firstrand Bank (GEM), Hon Hai Precision (APxJ, GEM) and Tencent (APxJ, GEM) - all Top Picks from our analysts, while removing Nari Technology, as well as Mytilineos and BCA. In APxJ, we also switch into CSL from TLS, turning more balanced on growth/value. In our Japan Focus List, we add Kao and Tokyo Seimitsu, removing OBIC Business Consultants and Suntory.

## APxJ/EM Market Allocation Changes

• India remains preferred pick in APxJ/EM - We expect India to continue to deliver superior earnings growth supported by macro stability and a productive capex-focused growth agenda.

• Upgrade South Africa to OW - Low valuations and a cyclical trough see risk-reward tilted to the upside post elections, although uncertainty will be high in the short-run and potentially into the medium-term. While the political situation remains highly uncertain, there is potential for improved reform momentum under a broader coalition, which would unlock earnings improvement and equity re-rating.

• Maintain OW stance on Singapore, Poland and Greece - We still like the defensiveness and structural opportunities in Singapore, alongside secular recoveries in Poland and Greece. Valuations in all three markets remain attractive.

• Downgrade Mexico to EW - 2024 general election results may see Mexico's institutional backstops being tested. We downgrade to equalweight, alongside our Latin America equity strategy team, and prefer secular growth opportunities in the region (MercadoLibre, Nubank in our GEM Focus List).

• Downgrade Indonesia to UW - We see near-term uncertainty over the direction of future fiscal policy stance as well as some weakness in the fx market amid still high US rates and a firm US dollar outlook.

• Upgrade Egypt to EW - Egypt looks committed to delivering structural reforms and is moving into a better funding dynamic following large FX/ rates adjustments, with a meaningful equity valuation recovery underway.

## India Still Preferred Pick within APxJ/EM given Structural Earnings Runway

As 2024 general elections conclude, BJP-led NDA is set to form a government for the third term. There was a single-day correction in India indices as BJP was confirmed to be losing their outright majority, with investors concerned about policy visibility and continuity. However, our India strategy team attributes the seat losses to non-economic issues and expect the government to still emphasize on macro stability as its anchor to economic policy.

Coming back to fundamentals, we remain bullish on Indian equities given superior earnings growth driven by structural reforms. Multipolar world trends are still supporting India's manufacturing boom, and energy transition and improvement in digital infrastructure are ongoing. Macro indicators are largely encouraging, with March-quarter GDP growth exceeding expectations at 7.8% YoY, buoyant credit growth at 15.8% YoY, and PMIs in the expansionary territory. We see a strong manufacturing and capex cycle underpinning India's growth and competitiveness in the global export market. As the economy grows strongly we should see significantly improving Indian per-capital income, which could further unlock domestic discretionary consumption. Our macro team are expecting it to more than double from the current US\$2.5k to US\$5.8k by F2034.

## Downgrade Mexico to EW as Election Concluded with Policy Uncertainty

The strength of Mexico's 2024 general election result came as a surprise with the new president-elect Claudia Sheinbaum leading by 30pp, compared to the 10pp gap expected. This gives the Morena coalition a stronger-than-anticipated position in Congress and puts institutional backstops at risk, in our economists' view (see Mexico Economics & Strategy: Revisiting Backstops).

Fiscal prudence is a necessary condition for Mexico's macro stability, although in February the Executive sent a series of controversial reforms to Congress that require a qualified majority as they involve constitutional changes and many involve fiscal costs, and no proposal has been made to increase revenue in order to sustainably fund the new initiatives.

Our LatAm equity strategist Nikolaj Lippmann downgraded Mexico within LatAm and is cautious on LatAm Equities across the board, and recommends defensive exposure within the region. For more details see Mexico Economics & Strategy: Revisiting Backstops (3 Jun 2024) and Latin America Model Portfolio: Mexico to Equal-weight (4 Jun 2024).

We downgrade Mexico in our regional APxJ/EM market allocation process from the previous OW to EW as we wait for more policy clarity given its importance for key infrastructure and longer-term policy alignment with the nearshoring trend. MSCI Mexico forward P/E is below the historical average though the market is seeing a negative earnings revision trend, with a cautious macro view, and currently ranks #10 in our market allocation framework.

## Downgrade Indonesia to UW and Move up Egypt to EW

Post elections in February 2024, investors are focusing on the process of cabinet formation and looking for policy certainty on the future conduct of fiscal policy. Campaign pledges, such as the proposal for government provision of lunches and milk for students and pregnant women may impose a substantial fiscal burden, although the President-elect has committed to maintaining the legislated 3% deficit and 60% debt ceilings.

Externally, the US dollar environment staying stronger for longer should continue to pose headwinds, and indeed Bank Indonesia surprised with an additional rate hike in April. Both these factors may keep market-based interest rates high in the near term, potentially weighing on the capex recovery. Our economists expect the BI to commence rate cuts in 1Q25 and growth to recover next year, but we may see a period of uncertainty in the lead up to the inauguration of the new administration on 20 October. For more details on the macro outlook, see Indonesia: Navigating Through Uncertainties.
