# January 30

USTs twist flatten, early month-end activity supports long-end; European curves bear-flatten; Brent settles up after Saudi directive; USD/MYR falls despite Malaysia political uncertainty; ECB's Vujcic prefers 25bp cuts; CLP weakens before BCCh; DXY at 103.42 (-0.2%); US 10y at 4.032% (-4.2bp).

• USTs twist flatten after unexpected rise in Job Openings inches the vacancies per unemployed worker ratio to 1.44 (P: 1.43). Long-end strength accelerates into NY close, month-end dynamics in focus.

• European curves bear-flatten after both 4Q23 Euro Area GDP and preliminary January Spanish CPI are above consensus expectations; 10y BTP-Bund spread widens \~3bp.

• Brent crude oil futures settle 0.6% higher after the Saudi government gives a state-owned oil company a directive to no longer raise production capacity.

• USD/MYR finishes down slightly, lower UST yields overnight outweigh Malaysia political uncertainty.

• ECB Governing Council Member Vujcic says, “twenty-five basis-point moves are preferable to larger moves that usually happen when you feel you’re a bit behind the curve.”

• CLP (-0.1%) depreciation continues, ahead of the BCCh meeting where our economists expect the central bank to cut the policy rate by 100bp, to 7.25% (C: 7.25%).

## Japan:

JGBs bull-flattened on Tuesday after the rally in global rates overnight on Monday. Front-end JGBs traded weaker ahead of the 2y JGB auction (new issue of JN457), which was weaker than expected with a tail of 8.2bp (C: 7.7bp). However, a higher bid-to-cover ratio (3.74x) suggested relatively strong demand to buy the dip. After the 2y auction, the next leg of the JGB rally was driven by the 20y sector, which has traded cheap on the curve amid concern for the BoJ normalization; 20y JGBs rallied by 3.0bp d/d, to 1.515%. We will closely monitor if month-end rebalancing activity will materialize into the 3:00pm Tokyo close at January month-end.

The JGB purchase operation schedule for February was released, but the purchase amounts were unchanged across the maturity buckets. We believe it will be difficult for the BoJ to reduce purchase sizes further under the overshoot commitment. TONA OIS bull-flattened alongside JGBs but broadly underperformed against matched-maturity JGBs. XCCY basis tightened across the curve.

During Tokyo hours on Tuesday, CCY/JPY traded almost range-bound after a decent decline alongside lower global bond yields overnight on Monday. USD/JPY traded below 145.50 as UST yields ground lower. EUR/JPY sold off toward 159.20 alongside dovish comments from ECB officials overnight on Monday, but the pair recovered from its initial sell-off amid strong European GDP data.

## Europe:

It was another choppy session for G10 FX in London hours. The London morning kicked off with EUR weakness, as market participants expected the data calendar would confirm a technical recession in the euro area. However, an upside surprise to 4Q23 euro area growth and stronger-than-expected preliminary January Spanish CPI reversed EUR’s prior losses. Ultimately, the move lost steam, and EUR closed the London trading session flat against the US dollar.

GBP was weaker after soft UK money and credit data for December and significantly lower mortgage approvals. GBP closed the London session 0.2% weaker against the US dollar. SEK outperformed and gained 0.3% against USD, as market participants looked ahead to Thursday’s Riksbank meeting and positioned for increased risk of pushback to current front-end pricing.

Most European GDP data came in stronger than expected, especially in Spain and Italy. This led to an above-consensus preliminary 4Q23 euro area figure of 0.0% q/q (C: -0.1%; P: -0.1%). Preliminary January Spanish CPI was also stronger than expected at 3.4% y/y (C: 3.0%; P: 3.1%). This led to a front-end sell-off, led by late whites (\~5bp) and 10y Bund yields closed 3bp higher at 2.27%.

In the London afternoon, higher-than-expected US JOLTS job openings fueled a bearflattening. March and April ECB meetings repriced 1.5bp and 1.5bp higher, respectively, to imply less than 25bp of cuts by April. Curves bear-flattened; in swaps 2s10s was little changed, while 10s30s flattened by 1bp. Inflation fixings rose 2-3bp, amid the Spanish inflation data and rally in crude oil (+1.0%). Core ASW richened in the London morning but eventually closed little changed to (Schatz: - 0.5bp). G4 spreads against Bunds widened and BTPs underperformed peers (10y BTP-Bund spread: +3bp).

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies gained against the US dollar as US yields fell following lowerthan-expected US quarterly refunding estimates. THB outperformed, USD/THB fell 0.2%, to 35.270, amid a recovery in gold prices. KRW also outperformed, USD/KRW fell 0.5%, to 1328.70. USD/TWD fell 0.3%, to 31.137, in early hours of trading before a close \~31.15. USD/IDR fell 0.2%, to 15,779, after BI said it had purchased bonds in the secondary market to support IDR. BI Governor Warjiyo reiterated the central bank will continue to intervene in the market to ease short-term pressures on IDR. USD/MYR fell 0.2%, to 4.7265, as lower US yields outweighed politics, given continuing opposition pressure on Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim’s government. CNY remained steady. PHP was the outlier as USD/PHP rose 0.30% to 56.456.

AxJ rates rallied alongside US Treasuries. Korea outperformed as the 10y yield fell 8bp, to 3.35%. China NDIRS moved lower by 4bp as the 10y CGB yield fell to a 22- year low of 2.46%, given anticipation of further monetary easing. Singapore 10y yield fell 3bp, to 2.91%, while the Thailand 10y yield fell 3bp, to 2.63%. Indonesia 10y yield fell 2bp, to 6.60%.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies finished mixed on Tuesday. CLP (-0.1%) continued to depreciate ahead of the BCCh meeting on Wednesday; BRL ended the session flat. MXN (+0.4%) and COP (+0.3%) appreciated, despite few local developments. In rates, TIIEs continued to sell off, with losses concentrated in the belly (5y: +4bp). DIs also sold off; January 2026s ended 5bp higher, while the front end of the IBR curve rallied (1y: -3bp).

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
