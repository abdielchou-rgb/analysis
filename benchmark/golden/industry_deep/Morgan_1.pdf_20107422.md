Our portfolio has delivered mixed performance since we last published as FX markets grapple with overlapping signals from yields, growth, and commodities. We retain a constructive bias on the USD and have added a new USD/ CHF call spread between publications, though US CPI next week presents a key tactical risk. Short-CHF is one of our higher-conviction views following the surprise SNB rate cut, and we are now short vs USD, JPY & SEK. We continue to favor SEK within the G10 high-beta bloc and add a new EUR/SEK short this week given attractive entry levels. When coupled with our longer-dated EUR/USD digi put, this new trade also increases our short-EUR exposure ahead of the ECB next week. Our remaining positions are largely oriented around relative-value opportunities stemming from a combination of policy divergences, valuation, and/or carry (long SEK/CZK, short CHF/JPY). We are also short CAD (vs 50:50 basket of USD & AUD) heading into next week<sup>’</sup>s BoC decision with expectations of a dovish pivot ahead of a first cut in June.

We’ve scaled up our long-USD exposure in recent weeks and retain a bullish medium-term bias. Incoming US activity data continues to point to a strong US economy (including this week<sup>’</sup>s NFP release), even if there has been some choppiness in the FX price action (e.g. USD rallied on solid ISM mfg, but fell on soft ISM services the next day). Nevertheless, the US yield curve has bear steepened over the past two weeks and supported the USD commensurately. This has coincided with post-FOMC Fedspeak turning incrementally more cautious around the timing of rate cuts, and the Fed OIS strip now prices fewer <sup>‘</sup>24 cuts than the SEP median dot — an atypical development for this cycle (Figure 1). While this might suggest risks to the USD from potential dovish Fed repricing, we think the move higher in the long-end of the yield curve is indicative of a more durable dollar support. All in all, we<sup>’</sup>ve seen neither sufficient weakness in US activity data nor a sustained rates rally such as to warrant a rethink on our broader USD-bullish bias. We are tactically mindful of USD-negative seasonality during April, but note that the USD has historically rebounded to even higher levels during May (Figure 2).

## ● Short CAD vs 50:50 basket of AUD & USD in cash

CAD has underperformed both USD and AUD over the past two weeks, thus generating two-sided positive returns for our beta-neutral basket trade. The recent CAD weak ness likely reflects the signals from local activity data, which point to a continued moderation in inflationary pressures and growing slack in the labor market. This week<sup>’</sup>s Business Outlook Survey delivered positive readings on three key factors the BoC has cited as important for gauging the disinflationary trajectory: (1) inflation expectations (only 27% of firms expect inflation to persist above 2% beyond three years), (2) wage growth (firms expect slower wage growth over the next 12m vs the past 12m), and (3) corporate pricing behavior (fewer firms plan to make unusually large or frequent price increases over the next 12m). Then later in the week, CA payrolls unexpectedly contracted (-2.2k vs 25.0k consensus) with the unemployment rate jumping three-ticks to 6.1% (consensus: 5.9%), its highest level since Jan<sup>’</sup>22.

Taken together, the recent CA data releases suggest heightened odds of more overt dovish guidance at next week<sup>’</sup>s BoC MPR meeting. Our economists see a stronger case now for the first BoC cut in June (in line with their longheld call), which suggests a more dovish shift in the BoC<sup>’</sup>s rhetoric (which has otherwise been cautious thus far) at the upcoming decision. We initiated this trade two weeks ago as a tactical event-driven position that can benefit exactly such a dovish outcome. The basket structure aims to insulate the trade from broader beta factors, and places the onus on CAD to drive net returns. This has largely played out in recent weeks, as USD/CAD has rallied on USD-higher sessions whereas AUD/CAD has rallied on USD-lower sessions (and in certain instances such as the weak CA payrolls data, CAD has underperformed both currencies in the same session). We hold the position with increased conviction heading into next week<sup>’</sup>s BoC decision.
