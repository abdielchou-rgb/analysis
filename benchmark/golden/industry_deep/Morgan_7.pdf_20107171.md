# March 22

Bunds bull-flatten, risk-off reigns on escalation in tensions; Treasuries end at session highs; PBoC fixing spurs CNH weakness; USD/CAD above 1.360; COP gains, BanRep delivers expected cut; ECB's Nagel talks odds of first cut before summer break; DXY at 104.43 (+0.4%); US 10y at 4.198% (-6.9bp).

• Bunds bull-flatten and outperform on a cross-market basis as a result of risk-off sentiment following comments from a Russian government spokesman about the conflict in Ukraine.

• Treasuries follow Bunds higher and end at session highs with a bellyled rally.

• PBoC fixes USD/CNY above 7.10 for the first time since March 7 and the CNH weakness spills over across the region; USD/CNH (+0.8%) rallies to 7.28.

• USD/CAD (+0.5%) closes above the 1.360 level, despite strong Canadian retail sales data.

• COP (+0.2%) outperforms regional peers as BanRep delivers an expected 50bp rate cut to bring the policy rate to 12.25% (C: 12.25%).

• ECB Governing Council Member Nagel says the probability the ECB delivers its first cut before “the summer break” in August is increasing.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

Outperformance of safe-haven currencies was the main theme to end the week. US dollar strength began overnight after the PBoC set the USD/CNY fixing above 7.10 and continued amidst a later potential escalation in tensions between Russia and Western countries. Other low-yielding currencies, JPY and CHF, gained alongside the US, while risk-sensitive currencies traded notably weaker. A rally in global sovereign bonds was led by the bull-flattening of the Bund curve, while Treasuries and GoC gains were led by the belly. Cyclical stocks (Russell 2000: -1.27%) partially retraced post-FOMC gains, while the Nasdaq 100 (+0.10%) displayed more “defensive” qualities. Atlanta Fed President Bostic delivered the first post-March FOMC Fedspeak and cemented his stance at the more hawkish end of the spectrum. He projects only one rate cut this year, from two previously, and expects this cut to come later in the year than previously anticipated. He also supports slowing the pace of balance sheet run-off “relatively soon.”

Central bank monetary policy decisions were the main focus this week. The BoJ ended NIRP, YCC, the overshoot commitment, and ETF and J-REIT purchases, while the CBC delivered a surprise hike. The RBA remained on hold but removed its tightening bias as it omitted reference in the statement to "a further increase in interest rates." The BoE remained on hold, but for the first time since September 2021, there were no votes on the MPC to hike interest rates. Banxico delivered its first cut of the cycle and signaled an openness to the possibility of continuous cuts. SNB became the first G10 central bank to lower rates, with a surprise 25bp cut. The FOMC remained on hold and despite a hawkish set of Summary of Economic Projections, Fed Chair Powell stressed it would be appropriate to cut rates “at some point this year.”

## Japan:

Although Japan nationwide CPI (ex-food and energy) was weaker than expected, market participants continued to price in a sharper BoJ rate hike cycle and the frontend of the JGB curve underperformed. An economist survey now suggests 60% of economists believe the next BoJ hike will come by the October meeting. The BoJ conducted JGB purchase operations for the 1-3y, 3-5y, 5-10y, and 10-25y sectors with no changes in purchase sizes. The results were stronger than expected and JGBs retraced the initial sell-off to an extent. 10y and 20y JGBs traded relatively resiliently, as market participants seemingly restored some duration exposure. 30y+ JGBs lagged ahead of the 40y JGB auction on Wednesday. TONA OIS sold off alongside JGBs while XCCY basis twist flattened.

Although risk markets traded resiliently, funding currencies outperformed. USD/JPY traded rangebound \~151.50, given both USD and JPY are funding currencies. Early in the Tokyo morning, relatively weak Japan CPI on a US-style core (ex-food and energy) basis did not have a material impact on the FX market. AUD/JPY traded below the 99.00 level, alongside a weak CNY after the high fixing by the PBoC, above 7.10.

## Europe:

USD/CHF remained relatively stable, but EUR/CHF (-0.4%) partially reversed gains from Thursday, after the SNB unexpectedly cut its policy rate. The Scandinavian currencies were the underperformers in Europe.

European duration traded sideways in the London morning and rallied in the afternoon, in a risk-off manner alongside developments on Europe-Russia relations. The deterioration in risk sentiment sent equities lower (SX5E: -0.42%), credit indices wider (X-over: +12bp), and the 10y BTP-Bund spread 4bp wider to 131bp. In a continuation of this week’s price action and likely supported by the risk-off sentiment, core ASW widened, led by Bund ASW (+1.5bp). The EUR 2s10s curve flattened over 2bp while 10s30s closed flat; the 5s10s30s butterfly richened 0.5bp, despite a deterioration in risk sentiment (2s5s10s: -1bp).

## Dollar Bloc:

Risk-off sentiment drove G10 currencies on Friday after Chinese authorities set a weaker-than-expected daily fixing of its currency, which supported broad-based USD strength. The DYX USD Index rose 0.5% in the NY afternoon, while risk-sensitive G10 currencies underperformed. The Antipodean currencies weakened by over 0.8% against USD. USD/CAD shrugged off stronger-than-expected retail sales data in Canada and USD/CAD (+0.5%) continued to grind higher throughout the NY trading session.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies fell broadly as weakness in CNH spilled over to the region. After the PBoC fixed USD/CNY above 7.10 for the first time since March 7 and USD/CNY broke above 7.20, USD/CNH rallied 0.7% to \~7.27. This drove USD/INR 1m NDF 0.6% higher, above the key 83.5 level to as high as 83.79. KRW underperformed the most in the region due to its high beta and weak equity markets, 1.2% weaker to 1,338, almost a full reversal of gains from Thursday. IDR underperformed, 0.7% weaker to 15,783 amid the sell-off in INDOGB (10y: +2bp) and weak risk appetite. Most other AxJ currencies weakened by \~0.4%. THB and TWD were relative outperformers, down 0.2% and 0.3% respectively. THB was unaffected by the BoT comment that it has no policy issue with the current THB level. Relative TWD resilience was supported by TAIEX (+0.2%) outperformance, amid modest foreign equity inflows of US\$68.8mn.

Most AxJ rates sold off following the rise in front-end UST yields on Thursday. Korea was the exception; 10y NDIRS yield fell 3bp and the 2s10s KRW NDIRS curve bull-flattened 3bp. In China, yields rose across the NDIRS curve by \~2bp despite weakness in domestic equity markets. In the bond market, the sell-off remained concentrated in the long-end of the curve; 10y and 30y CGB yields rose 2bp. Thailand rates showed limited reaction to a BoT comment that it will assess if there is a need to recalibrate its neutral monetary policy stance in April. In Hong Kong, short-term liquidity gradually tightened as 1m HIBOR continued rising, by 6bp, to 4.74%, a high since January. 2y HKD swap yield rose 3bp; the 2s10s curve twist flattened by 4bp. In India, the NDIRS curve shifted 3bp higher while 10y G-sec yields rose 2bp to 7.07%.

## Latin America:

Most LatAm currencies ended weaker driven by general risk-off sentiment. CLP (- 1.0%) led losses and BRL (-0.4%) followed with few local developments. COP (+0.2%) outperformed peers as the central bank cut rates in-line with consensus expectations and the IBR curve bull-steepened ahead of the meeting (2s10s: -5bp). TIIEs were mostly unchanged; the long-end underperformed (10y: -3bp) after higherthan-expected CPI, while DIs also had losses in the long-end (January 2033s: +6bp), despite lack of a local catalyst.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• ECB President Lagarde said Euro Area inflation was expected to decelerate while growth should pick up over the course of 2024, driven by an increase in purchasing power

• ECB Governing Council Member Nagel said the probability the ECB delivers its first cut before “the summer break” in August is increasing. This echoes recent comments from Governing Council members about the potential for a cut in June. He said cuts will not be automatic thereafter “I do not see that there is a kind of an automatism […] It is data dependent and it is not a done deal that everything is going smoothly for the rest of the year or maybe for next year. So we have to be vigilant, we have to be cautious”

• Atlanta Fed President Bostic said he now projects only one rate cut this year and has this cut later in the year than previously anticipated. He said there are “some troubling things” underneath the headline inflation figures, such as the breadth of items in the basket rising at an elevated rate. He reiterated a resilient economy gives the Fed “space for patience.” He supports slowing the pace of balance sheet run-off “relatively soon”

## Emerging Markets

• BoT Assistant Governor Piti said the economic picture is evolving as the central bank had expected back in February. He noted at the April meeting, the BoT will consider whether there is a need to recalibrate its neutral stance and if so, when to do so. He considers it a recalibration, instead of an easing cycle to support the economy, as Thailand is likely to experience a near-term cyclical growth pickup. He has no big policy issue with the current level of THB, and expects the currency to receive support going forward from the Thailand growth recovery and narrowing interest rate differentials as the Fed has more room to cut rates compared to Asian central banks where policy rates are closer to neutral

• According to a statement from the BoK and the Korean Finance Ministry, financial firms that participated in the pilot test operations for extension of onshore won trading hours gave positive reviews, and said they were able to supplement any flaws found in the trading procedures, such as payment, payment confirmation and accounting, and the bid and ask prices were relatively competitive compared with the KRW NDF market

• BSP Board Member Recto said he expects the central bank may cut rates by less than previously thought this year, with possibly only two cuts, instead of the four previously expected. He highlighted the inflation path will be bumpy but is expected to return to the target band this year. He said the

government is considering a downward revision to its 2024 GDP growth target, with his personal base-case GDP estimate at 6%

• BanRep cut their policy rate by 50bp to 12.25% (C: 12.25%) via a split decision (5: 50; 1:75; 1:100). The board focused on declines in inflation and inflation expectations and revised down their own internal inflation projections. While it was a marginally dovish statement, a move in pricing a 75bp cut for the April meeting (currently -56bp) would be dependent on downside CPI surprises

## Economic Release Notes

## Developed Markets

• In Japan, Nationwide CPI (ex-fresh food) rose 2.8% y/y in February (C: 2.8%; P: 2.0%) and was driven by base effects in electricity and city gas charges. Large declines in utility prices, due to subsidies, began in February 2023, this raised y/y growth in energy items. CPI (ex-fresh food and energy) was 3.2% y/y in February (C: 3.3%; P: 3.5%) while CPI (ex-all food and energy) edged down to 2.5% y/y in February (P: 2.6%). Goods inflation has stabilized, especially food inflation, as the lagged impact from previous high import prices has waned. Private services have not declined, but there are not signs of an acceleration either

• In Japan, Weekly Ministry of Finance International Transactions in Securities data for the week of March 10-16 suggested Japanese market participants were decent net sellers of foreign bonds (-JPY 803.9bn) as UST yields rose ahead of the March FOMC meeting. Japanese market participants remained large net sellers of foreign equities (-JPY 522.2bn). Some entities may have reduced positions on their balance sheet ahead of the Japanese fiscal year-end, with realized losses from foreign bonds offset by realized gains from foreign equities. Overseas market participants were large net buyers of Japan long-term debt securities (+JPY 2.16tn), despite media reports which suggested a March BoJ hike was highly likely. Foreign ccy denominated JGB ASW demand may pick up due to the recent cheapening of JGB ASW across the curve. Overseas market participants sold Japan equities in large size (-JPY 1.46tn) on concerns of a possible March BoJ hike

• In the UK, GfK Consumer Confidence remained at -21 in March (C: -19). Consumers who earn under 15k per year reported the biggest boost in sentiment, perhaps a reflection of the near 10% rise in the minimum wage. Consumers still reported subdued purchasing intentions for large-ticket items, but the assessment of future personal finances improved. Inflation expectations for the one-year series fell

• In the UK, Retail Sales (ex-Auto Fuel) rose by 0.2% m/m in February (C: - 0.1%; P: 3.4%). The largest boost came from clothing sales, where retailers promotions supported sales. Overall, retail sales volumes are still down and still below pre-Covid levels

• In Germany, the IFO Business Climate Index rose to 87.8 for March (C: 86.0; P: 85.7)

• In Canada, Headline Retail Sales fell by 0.3% m/m in January (C: -0.4%; P: 0.9%) as consumers pared spending on motor vehicles. Core Retail Sales (ex-gasoline stations, fuel vendors, and motor vehicle parts dealers) rose by 0.4% m/m in February (P: 0.5%). In volume terms, retail sales rose by 0.2% m/m. The advance estimate of retail sales for February suggests a rise of 0.1% m/m (for more, read here)

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
