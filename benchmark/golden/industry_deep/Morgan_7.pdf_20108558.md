## Policy adjustment to manage downside risk

After a strong start in 1Q, economic activity softened in China. Industrial production rose 6.7%oya in April on a 0.4%m/m sa decline, retail sales growth came in below expectations at 2.3%oya on a 0.5%m/m sa decline, and fixed investment growth moderated to 3.6%oya in April (vs. 4.5%oya in 1Q). The bright spot was exports, which rose 1.5%oya on a 2.8%m/m sa gain (in USD term), or rose 9.8%oya in volume terms in April. Entering May, the PMI readings pointed at a mixed picture: while it is encouraging that Caixin manufacturing PMI rose to a two-year high, it contrasted with a decline in NBS manufacturing PMI to below 50. Both surveys show output above 50 alongside falling new orders and export orders.

The slowdown in economic activity is in part driven by the faded LNY effect, and in part related to a temporary withdrawal of policy support. April TSF recorded the first monthly decline since 2005, along with a contraction of nearly 100bn yuan of government bond issuance. The near-term weakness mainly came from the housing market: new home sales and new homes started both fell 25.7%oya ytd by April, significantly below our full-year forecast of a 10% decline. Weak income expectations (especially among upper-middle income families) seem to have dragged consumption and housing demand. In addition, deflation pressure lingered on due to insufficient demand, and the expansion of exports via competitive prices started to receive pushback from some trading partners.

The government started to adjust policies to manage downside risk. The politburo meeting in late April emphasized the importance of policy continuity, explicitly called for accelerated pace of government bond issuance and use of rate cuts and RRR cuts by the PBOC. Following that, new government bond issuance rose to around 1.2 trillion yuan in May. In addition, a new round of housing policy relaxation was announced on May 17 that not only included additional demandside easing (relaxation in mortgage downpayment and mortgage rates) but also included a new initiative to reduce housing inventory (300bn yuan re-lending by the PBOC to support government purchase of unsold homes).

We maintain our 2Q growth forecast at 3.5%q/q saar (vs. 7.4% in 1Q). On the policy front, the PBOC<sup>’</sup>s policy actions are worth watching. We forecast two 10bp policy rate cuts (in June and 4Q, respectively), a 25bp RRR cut and PBOC purchase of Treasury Bond in the open market in 3Q. The risk to rate cut forecast is biased to one 10bp rate cut only (in 3Q), if the Fed cuts rates later and smaller than JPM baseline forecast (three rate cuts in 2024 starting from July), putting pressure on CNY and capital outflows, or if banks continue to face NIM compression pressure. In addition, the 3<sup>rd</sup> Plenum will be held in July, focusing on deepening structural reforms. We are hopeful on key reform areas such as fiscal reform, rural land reform, and fostering new productive forces that include equal support for manufacturing and service upgrade as well as between SOE and non-SOEs, followed by forceful implementation.

## Manufacturing PMI (May)

## May 31

China<sup>’</sup>s May NBS PMI shows a continued softening in manufacturing activity and the absence of service activity recovery, with the NBS mfg PMI falling 0.9-pt to 49.5 in May, while the NBS non-mfg PMI edging down 0.1-pt to 51.1. Meanwhile, the Caixin mfg PMI moved up 0.3-pt to 51.7, hitting the highest in the past two years. The improvement in May Caixin mfg PMI reading stands in contrast to the decline in NBS mfg PMI, which is not unusual. This can be attributable to the differences in sampling methods and statistical approaches, but may also point to uncertainties in the economic outlook at enterprise levels.

## Fixed investment (May)

## June 17

Fixed asset investment came in below expectations, growing 4.2%oya for Jan-Apr, or growing 3.6%oya in April. Further breakdown shows continuous weakness in real estate investment (which fell 10.5%oya in April), with ongoing policy support for manufacturing investment (+9.3%oya) and moderate easing in infrastructure investment growth (+5.1%oya). Besides, private investment inched down 0.1%oya in April (vs +0.6%oya in March).

Besides seasonal factors, the weak April data was partly driven by a dial-back of policy support, as reflected in the disappointing April credit report. On the positive side, the Politburo meeting in late April seems to have noticed the problem, emphasizing importance of policy continuity. There remains notable fiscal space to provide growth support in 2H24. Divergence in fixed investment, and more broadly economic activities, reflected differences in industry policies. On the positive side, supportive policy measures continue to focus on the manufacturing sector (industry upgrade, core technology, NEV, green economy, AI, etc.), helping to boost manufacturing FAI and production activity. On the other hand, policy tightening has dragged some sectors, most notably the housing market but also in a few services sectors.

The PBOC announced three easing measures in May, namely relaxation in down payment requirements and mortgage rates. A new policy initiative for the government to buy unsold homes has been launched, which is a desirable policy move to reduce housing inventory, increase public housing investment and indirectly encourage consumption from beneficiary households. Overall, we expect fixed asset investment to grow 4.0%oya in May.

## Merchandise trade (May) June 7

China<sup>’</sup>s export activity continued with solid sequential growth in April. Seasonally-adjusted, exports rose 2.8% m/m sa in April (and up 24.5% 3m/3m saar). In volume terms, exports rose 9.8%oya in April. By product breakdown, the April trade report shows broad-based gain across major export product groups, including low-end consumer goods exports (up 0.6% m/m sa), exports of mechanical and electrical products (up 4.3% m/m sa) as well as high-end products (up 2.4% m/m sa). Auto exports fell modestly by 1.4% m/m sa in April. By major destination, exports to the DM markets , including the US (up 5.1% m/m sa) and Europe (up 4.0% m/m sa) showed notable growth in April. Exports to EM Asia (the ASEAN region in particular), as well as exports to other key EM markets, including LatAm and Africa, also showed a generally solid growth trend. Meanwhile, nominal imports came in above expectations in April, rising 2.5% m/m sa (or up 3.4% 3m/3m saar). We expect exports to fall 1.3%m/m sa (or +7.1%oya) in May, as the export order component in both Caixin and NBS mfg PMI declined in May.

## Fiscal position (May) June

Headline fiscal revenue growth stayed in contractionary territory as of April, down by 2.7%oya. Headline fiscal expenditure picked up lately, rising 6.1%oya in April or 3.5% in Jan-Apr. Land sales remained sluggish, down by 21.2%oya in April or 10.4% in Jan-Apr despite a low base. The Ministry of Natural Resources issued a notice which requires cities with an inventory month of >36 to suspend land sales, while for cities with an inventory month of 18-36, land supply should be based on how much inventory has been digested. This likely suggests downside pressure for land sales going forward. Weekly land sales have indeed stayed soft throughout May.

There were concerns of a repeat of the fiscal drag last year that contributed to a rapid loss of growth momentum from 1Q to 2Q. Facing the risk of an activity slowdown, policies have been adjusted since the late April Politburo meeting, which emphasized policy continuity. While there was no major change in the magnitude of fiscal policy support (vs. what was laid out in the NPC meeting), government bond issuance has significantly picked up momentum. Ultra-long central government bond issuance has also kick-started. If the government fulfills the full-year bond issuance target at around October, we estimate average monthly government bond net supply may reach around 1.2 trillion yuan in the coming few months.

## Currency exchange rate

## Daily release

In May, USD index moved lower from 106.2 to 104.7. Meanwhile, CNY/USD has been hovering around 7.23. As we have been flagging, the PBOC has shifted its focus of the managed floating RMB exchange rate regime from “floating<sup>”</sup> to “managed<sup>”</sup>. Amid lingering uncertainty around the timing and pace of the Fed<sup>’</sup>s pivot, CNY assets still appear less attractive as a low yielder. Despite a marginally higher CNY fixing, a touch above 7.10 since mid-April, the gap between CNY fixing and the spot price from the previous trading day remains elevated. Technically, the CNY basket, CFETS TWI index has largely followed the dollar move with appreciation against most other major currencies in recent months. For example, CNY appreciation against JPY and KRW explains 54% of the CFETS gain in April. In this regard, if the PBOC maintains a strong CNY fixing bias, CFETS may stay relatively elevated around current levels, before trending down into the year-end following the anticipated fading of dollar strength.

June 7
