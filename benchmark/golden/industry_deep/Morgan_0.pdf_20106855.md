# Japan Equity Strategy: Monthly Wrap

## Growing focus on wage hikes as Nikkei 225 hits new record; capex growth strong

The Japanese equity market performed strongly in Feb, particularly the Nikkei 225. Semiconductors and other blue chips drove the market, and the Nikkei 225 climbed 7.9% during the month to hit a 34-year record-high on Feb 22. The faster-thanexpected market rally was driven by expectations of a delayed FRB rate cut, yen weakness, and anticipation of AI demand growth due to Nvidia<sup>’</sup>s results. However, real wages, 4Q GDP, and personal consumption were weaker than expected, and there are questions on the sustainability of rising share prices. Capex announced Mar 4 was strong, up 16.4% YoY, demonstrating the resilience of fundamentals, and we think the stock market should also find support from the growing likelihood of stronger wage hike guidance compiled on Mar 15 versus last year, and from expectations that the BoJ will normalize policy at a gradual pace following the spring end to negative interest rates factored in by the market. Signs of an upturn to growth in real wages as results of the Shunto spring wage negotiations are announced would likely lead to the flow of funds ramping up further.

Stock performance: The Japanese equity market performed strongly in Feb, particularly the Nikkei 225. TOPIX rose 4.9% (+2.7% in dollar terms), with strength similar to the S&P 500 (+5.2%), but the Nikkei 225 comprised of core large-caps climbed 7.9%, outperforming TOPIX and US equities by a wide margin (Figures 1, 2). The Nikkei 225 topped the end-1989 bubble-era record for the first time in 34 years Feb 22 and also reached a new high the following week. Semiconductor names such as Tokyo Electron, Advantest, SoftBank, and Shin-Etsu Chemical rose across the board. TOPIX is still 7% short of its all-time high. We feel a broader increase in share prices will be needed to sustain rising equities, including a record for TOPIX.

Sector trends: Autos, banks, insurance, machinery, trading companies, precision machinery, and electrical equipment outperformed TOPIX in Feb, while textiles & apparel, marine transportation, mining, foods, transportation, construction, and electric power & gas were weak. The end-February NT ratio (Nikkei 225/TOPIX ratio) rose sharply to 14.6x (from 14.2x at end-Jan) on strength in semiconductors/electronic equipment. According to the QUICK monthly survey, as of early-Feb investors were still overweight in financials, electrical machinery/precision machinery, materials, and consumer-spending sectors, and switched to overweight in steel/machinery (neutral in Jan survey) and communications (underweight in Jan). Meanwhile, investors remained underweight in pharmaceuticals/food, utilities, and automobiles, and also switched to underweight in construction/real estate (overweight in Jan).
