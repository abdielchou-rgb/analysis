# Who is buying and who is selling - Dec'23

## Household flows down more than 50% vs 18M ago...

Every buyer has a seller in the market. We take a holistic approach to rounding out market flow by estimating net buying/selling by all participants so that net flow at the market level is zero (including primary share issuance). Our analysis of Dec'23 shows households through mutual funds continue to be the biggest buyer (US\$9.1bn) followed by FIIs (US\$2.3bn) (Figure 7). While retail has been selling in the stock market (US\$1.7bn in the quarter), and non-SIP flows into mutual funds have weakened considerably, SIP investments are continuing strong (Figure 1 Househld fwint q(rc a) vb Sp'23 D  J. O 0). Aggregate flows from households towards markets in the last 18 months at US\$34bn compares with US\$78bn in the preceding period. We estimate net primary issuance of US\$5.6bn in the quarter, including US\$0.5bn of fresh issues in IPO. We find retail investors have turned more positive after end Dec'23 - based on derivatives market positioning (Figure 29 ) - while DIIs and FIIs have turned more negative (Figure 30FIs haveturnd pimc  log  q D'23, Figure 31DIs arecontiug hdxp mk by  f v ).

## …but SIP still holds steady

Over the last 6 quarters, discretionary retail flows (direct buying + SIP flows into MFs) have mostly evaporated while non-discretionary flows (SIP flows into equity) have held strong (Figure 18 and Figure 23). Retail direct investment into equities registered a US \$1.0bn outflow in the 6 quarters ending Dec'23 vs. US\$36.5bn inflow in the preceding 6 quarters ending Jun'22 (Figure 18). Similarly, Retail non-SIP flows into mutual funds have seen outflow in 6 out of the last 12 quarters (Figure 23). SIP flows continue, however (US\$32bn in last 6 quarters vs US\$25bn in the preceding 6 quarter period) Retail (direct) sold/bought large caps/SMIDs respectively, in Dec'23. They have bought Industrials and Consumer discretionary in Dec'23 (Figure 9). Also Figure 6 shows top 5 large cap and SMID stocks bought and sold by retail investors in Dec'23.

## Foreign investors turn positive in CY23; DII buying picked up

FIIs net bought +US\$20bn in CY23, a sharp swing vs -US\$17bn outflows in 2022 (Figure 24) - although we note there has been heavy selling in 2024 YTD (US\$3.4bn). Our EM active fund tracker shows that EM funds' OW position in India is at the lowest levels in the last 5 years (Figure 37). Over the last 12 months, FIIs have reduced active weight in financials and industrials while trimming their UW position in IT and materials (Figure 4 ). DIIs: Mutual funds were the buyers in H2CY23 (\$16.8bn) - this is to be seen as reflective of household flows into mutual funds. They invested into Financials, Industrials and Health care in Dec'23. Details of top stocks bought and sold by FIIs and DIIs are in Figure 35 and Figure 36. Promoters: Promoters net sold US\$1.4bn in Dec'23 and US\$2.7bn in Sep'23. While Adani Group promoters who net sold US\$3.4bn in H1CY23 bought back US\$2.6bn in H2CY23. Special note on HDFC Bank: As of Dec-23, 59.4% of HDFC Bank's foreign ownership limits have been utilised (up 12bps QoQ). This needs to fall by \~400bps before an MSCI upweight is triggered.

## Stay cautious: watch for household flows to markets closely

We believe the key variable to track for market direction is the buoyancy in household flows - the primary reason behind Indian market valuations reaching extraordinary levels while company fundamentals remain ordinary (see link1 and link2). We would be skeptical of these retail flows sustaining, since: 1) these are very sensitive to bank deposit rates, and in the context of low savings behaviour and tight deposit growth situation for banks, the risks for deposit rates could be to the upside rather than downside, 2) market exposure is no longer small for households at 20% of financial assets.
