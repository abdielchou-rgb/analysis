# Venture Vision: S-1-thing's Happening

Two things of note are occurring in the crossover world: (1) the divergence between S-1 filings and actual IPOs executed is wider than any point in history; (2) mentions of "AI" in these documents is on the cusp of overtaking "China".

What is an S-1?: An S-1 Filing is a mandatory registration form that companies must submit to the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) prior to being listed on a public exchange. The below data are, therefore, indicative of trends for companies looking to list shares in the US market. There is no fixed amount of time within which a company must go public after filing an S-1, the registration remains effective for as long as the company continues to make timely updates. The SEC typically requires financial statements to be no older than 135 days old at the time of listing. These details, as well as updates to operations, risks and market conditions, can be amended relatively swiftly with an S-1/A, allowing the IPO market to recover relatively rapidly by international standards when opportunistic windows open.

Are S-1s leading or lagging?: This is a more complicated question than it may seem. Clearly, an S-1 needs to be registered before a company can go public and so should therefore generally be a leading indicator for IPO activity. Indeed, there is some evidence of S-1s bottoming ahead of IPO activity, albeit not reliably through the cycle. However, they tend to lag in downcycles and proved to be counter-cyclical during the GFC.

Notable this cycle is the divergence between S-1 filings and the recovery of IPO activity from the lows. While US IPOs have inflected from the lows and headed towards the pre-Covid range of 20-40 monthly IPOs, they are still lagging S-1 filings by a widening margin. The current level of S-1 filings, according to Bloomberg, were recently as high as 118 per month – a level not seen since November 2021.

While we see no single explanation for this activity – market conditions, the 2021 pull-forward and geopolitics are often cited – we think another driver is worth flagging. Analysing the keywords within these S-1 documents gives a glimpse into the mindset and strategies of pre-IPO management teams. AI/ML has been on the rise for a number of years as companies have tried to help the market understand and value their data assets and strategy. Mentions of China, by comparison, have fallen since 2020 and on a relative basis are at a comparable level to S-1 filings mentioning AI/ML.

With funding of AI start-ups now amounting to over one quarter of all venture/ growth dollars (FY24 YTD, according to Pitchbook), the proportion of AI exits (either through acquisition or IPO) – and thus its focus in future S-1 filings – is likely to continue to rise over 2025 and beyond.

## FINTECH RADAR - ADAM WOOD & MARK HYATT

## THE FINTECH RADAR (4 AUG 2024)

• #1 Monto secures \$9m in seed funding: This week (31th July), US-based AI-drive B2B payments solutions start-up, Monto raised \$9m in Seed funding, led by Scale Venture Partners with participation from Verissimo Ventures, Firsthand Alliance, F2 Venture Capital, and Room40 Ventures. The round included participants including, Ariel Maislos and industry professionals with backgrounds from Intuit, Salesforce, and Plaid. The capital raised will be put towards the start-up's expansion into the US and their technology development. Monto's technology offering aims to simplify B2B payments (mainly Automated Cleaning House and Real-Time Payments) to a straightforward process by integrating financial system (particularly focusing on linking ERPs with accounts payable portals).

• #2 Cartona raises \$8.1m in Series A extension: This week (31th July), Egypt-based e-commerce B2B platform, Cartona raised \$8.1m in a Series A extending funding round. The platforms connects FMCG wholesalers and suppliers with retailers in Egypt. The funding round was led by Algebra Ventures with additional participation from existing investors, the SANAD Fund for MSME and Silicon Badia. The extension following their Series A \$12m funding round in July and includes both equity (\$5.6m) and debt (\$2.5m, secured in local currency) capital. Cartona plans to use the funds to further accelerate their expansion and further development across different verticals:

• \$5.6m equity capital will help the company increase market share in Egypt through strengthening its operations in FMCG and HORECA industries and expansion to regional markets (i.e KSA)

• \$2.5m debt capital (raised from GlobalCorp and Camel Ventures) support the company in achieving working capital requirements for local retails who have difficulties accessing capital.

• #3 Cowbell raises \$60m in Series C: This week (29th July) Cowbell, a cyberinsurance provider to SMEs, announced that it has raised \$60m in Series C funding from Zurich Insurance Group. Cowbell provides SMBs with adaptive cyber insurance that is powered but its AI-assisted underwriting platform. The injected capital will be utilised to scale operations as they plan to expand into key international markets and launch new cyber insurance solutions. The company aims to leverage generative AI to expand its tech infrastructure to drive further efficiencies and support decision making. According to Cowbell, cybercrime is estimated to reach \$24tn by 2027, with ransomware alone costing small businesses c. \$1.7m per incident on average.

• #4 Aveni secures £11 in Series A: This week (29 July), Aveni announced it secured

£11m in a Series A investment, which was lead by Puma Private Equity with participation from Lloyds Banking Group, Par Equity, and Nationwide. The Scottish start-up develops large language models and AI products that cater to the financial services industry. Over the past 2 years, the company has experienced growth in its 'Aveni Detect' and 'Aveni Assist' compliance and productivity solution. The £11m investment raise is part of Aveni's partnership (with Lloyds and Nationwide) to support the further development of its technology (FinLLM), strengthening its presence within the financial services industry. The goal of FinLLM is to provide a responsible and transparent adoption of Generative AI across the UK financial services. Unlike other LLMs, FinLLMs are trained on financial services data and reviewed by experts in the industry to provide particular use cases in the financial sector.

Unless otherwise indicated, the news reports referenced above have not been confirmed.

## Real-time valuation monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
