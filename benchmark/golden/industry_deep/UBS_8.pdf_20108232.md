# Global Rates Strategy June/Sep Treasury Futures Rolls

## Significant switch potential in US and WN, uncertainty in TY

This means the DV01 of many contracts may be rather uncertain, further compressing a roll that was already going to be tight due to the Memorial Day Holiday. For many contracts we suggest rolling early, and then doing cleanup trades after the weekend to eliminate the tails. With positioning and open interest high in most contracts, there could be congestion after the holiday weekend.

## Asset manager longs/HF shorts have surged in TU and FV, are up in TY and US

The cause is unclear, although the strength of demand for credit suggests this may be asset managers buying futures and credit rather than off the run Treasuries, as we discussed in Treasury basis trade and credit risks. Those large positions plus a compressed calendar to roll due to the holiday make us weight positioning very heavily.

## June/Sep Calendar rolls

The two most interesting themes in this roll are the switch potentials and the uncertainty on why asset manager longs/HF shorts have surged in TU and FV, with meaningful increases in TY and and WN.

## Switch risk/tails

US: On a parallel shift of slightly over 20bps, the June US contract CTD switches from the Aug 39s to the Feb 41s. That would extend the duration of the June US contract by about 5.5%. A 25bp selloff would shift the September CTD from the Feb 40s to the Feb 41s (a 3.7% jump in DV01). Where it gets very interesting is on a 33bp selloff the CTD switches to the Nov 42s, causing a 14.4% increase in DV01. That duration shift means the Feb 40 basis would start to rise quickly if the market moves more than 35bp.

WN: A parallel shift of 17bp would cause the CTD for the June contract to switch from the Nov 53s to the Aug 49s. That would push the DV01 of the contract up 7.4%. The September contract has the same switch on a 16bp parallel selloff, leading to a 7.1% increase in DV01.

TY: Unless there is a very sharp rally that means we get a much lower coupon on the 7yr that will be auctioned on May 23rd, the upcoming 7yr will be CTD for the September contract. Until we see the coupon on that issue, we won't know the duration of the future.

As a result, we look for the TY roll to be extremely compressed. We think many will wait until they know the size of the tail before they roll, and with an early close on Friday and Memorial Day on Monday, that means much of the rolling is likely to happen on the 28th and 29th. We would suggest doing part of the roll earlier to avoid any congestion, and then cleaning up the tail after the auction.

## Massive jump in AM longs/HF shorts

There was a pronounced increase in asset manager longs in TU and FV, with sizable increases in TY and WN. The cause for the increase is not obvious. There has been strong demand for credit, which suggests part of the increase was driven by core bond funds building a credit overweight (buying futures and corporates rather than off the run Tsys), as we discussed in Treasury basis trade and credit risks.

But we didn't see the net basis get to extremes as we normally would with a surge in asset manager demand. Part of that might be that HFs who don’t see many other RV opportunities have been quick to take the other side of the trade. SOFR volumes were high, suggesting the basis trade is likely part of this. But we still have some uncertainty about the cause.

Nonetheless, for most contracts that positioning will be a large part of the recommendation to roll early.

## WN: Sell June/Buy Sep

Both valuation and the asset manager long suggest rolling early.

Basis: September looks slightly cheaper to its CTD than June.

CTD RV; Both contracts have the 11/53s as CTD, although there is some switch risk in both contracts. A 22bp parallel sell off would push the June CTD to the lower coupon 11/49s, and a 21bp parallel selloff would make the 11/49s CTD for the Sep contract.

Positioning: As always, duration overlay strategies by asset managers leaves them long WN with HFs on the other side. Open interest remains high, but trading volumes are down, suggesting the asset manager long will dominate the roll.

Duration difference: Assuming no switches, the contracts have very similar risk with June's DV01 being 10bp higher than September.

If there is a switch to the second CTD, the June DV01 climbs 7.4% while September climbs 7.1%.

## US: Buy June, Sell Sep

While there will be a large asset manager roll, we think this will be more than offset by shorts rolling to get exposure to the switch option in the Sep contract.

Basis: September looks cheaper to its CTD than June.

CTD RV: The 8/39s are currently CTD for June, but a 20bp parallel selloff would cause a switch to the higher coupon 2/41s. For September, the current CTD is the 2/40s, but a 23bp parallel selloff would trigger a switch to the 2/41s. A 33bp selloff would make the Nov 42s CTD. We prefer the 8/39s to any of the other issues.

Positioning: The asset manager has faded slightly, but it remains the dominant position. However, we assume that some shorts will be motivated to roll as there is more optionality in being short the September contract. We may also see new shorts enter.

Open Interest: Open interest is extremely high. We assume part of that is people taking advantage of the switch options, suggesting there should be motivated shorts rolling forward.

Duration difference: If there are no switches, September will have a DV01 1.6% higher than June. But some of the switches can create much larger gaps.

## UXY: Neutral

We are neutral on this roll

Basis: June looks slightly cheaper to its CTD than September.

CTD RV: Double old 10s are CTD for June and old 10s are CTD for September. We don’t see any major RV differential.

Positioning: Asset manager longs are 61% off their late 2023 highs, with the leveraged short 65% smaller. At the same time open interest is higher, suggesting that positioning won't be a dramatic factor.

Duration difference: The DV01 of Sep is about 3.8% higher than the DV01 of June.

## TY: Sell June/Buy Sep

The asset manager long rolling forward should drive the roll. We can't say much about relative value or the tail until we see the coupon on the 7yr being auctioned this month.

Basis: Both contracts look fair to their CTD.

CTD RV: The 4.25s of 2/28/31 is currently the CTD, although the 4s of 1/31/31 are just a small fraction of a tick away. That said their durations are close enough that it would take an 85bp rally to cause a switch. Instead any delivery of the Jan 31s would be a cause of an idiosyncratic cheapening. Unless we get a dramatic rally before May 23rd that would sharply lower the coupon, the May 7yr will be CTD for September. Until we see where the Sep CTD trades, we can't have a view on RV.

Positioning: Asset manager longs are at new highs despite the fact that open interest is down. That suggests that longs rolling forward are likely to dominate this roll.

Duration difference: We won't know the DV01 of the September contract until we find out the coupon of new 7yr being auctioned on May 23rd.

## FV: Sell June/Buy September

The sharp rise in positioning should be the primary factor.

Basis: The September contract looks marginally richer to its CTD than June.

CTD RV: The June CTD is the 4.375s of 8/28, while the September CTD will be the 4.375s of 11/30/28. There is no switch value in either contract. We slightly like the June CTD versus the September CTD.

Positioning: The asset manager long and HF short jumped dramatically in recent weeks. We assume that is related to the demand for credit, so real money is buying futures and corporates rather than off the run Tsys. Those longs rolling forward should dominate the roll.

Duration difference: The Sep DV01 is 5.3% higher than the June DV01.
