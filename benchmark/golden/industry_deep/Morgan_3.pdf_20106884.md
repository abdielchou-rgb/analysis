# Chase the Growth Rabbit

USD's frustrating stability is explained by the balanced forces of simultaneous US equity and US growth outperformance. We're neutral on USD for now but think that the USD-positive growth and rate outperformance forces will eventually win out. We stay short EUR/USD via options.

USD has been caught between… Confucius said "if you chase two rabbits you will lose them both." USD is currently caught between two roughly balanced forces of US equity outperformance on the one hand and US growth outperformance on the other. We find that, historically, these two forces combined create choppy but low FX returns and dampen volatility.

...USD-positive US growth outperformance on the one hand... Ultimately we think that USD will chase one of these rabbits more than the other, and the more likely one is growth outperformance. The strong correlation of US to RoW interest rates has made it more difficult for FX markets to price in clear policy divergence themes, but persistent growth differentials should make this apparent over time.

….and USD-negative US equity outperformance on the other: Until this transpires, we remain neutral on USD overall, but express our USD-positive bias via EUR/USD puts.

Stay long EUR/USD 1.07 puts: We continue to see Europe as the 'weak link' here, where already below-trend growth, amplified by weakness in China, should generate disinflationary forces via a negative output gap. A faster and deeper pace of cuts from the ECB versus the Fed should manifest itself in a lower EUR/USD.

## Chase the Growth Rabbit

A common question we get from investors is: why are you still neutral on USD? Why aren't you more bullish?

After all, US growth continues to look impressive when compared to the rest of the world. Investors are increasingly focused on supply-side factors like immigration and productivity that suggest a higher potential growth rate for the US. Contrast this with RoW growth, where fiscal support in China continues to underwhelm and Europe growth, while beating expectations, still suggests a negative output gap.

In fact, if we take consensus estimates for 2024 growth between the US and the DXYweighted RoW, we're left with a nearly 1.6pp gap ( Exhibit 1 ), which we've only seen three times in the past 20 years excluding the Covid era ( Exhibit 2 ).

Despite this clear growth differential, there are two reasons why we're not sold on the idea that USD is a clear buy at this point:

• US and RoW rates have been too correlated, diminishing the US-RoW growth divergence pass-through to FX;

• US equities have outperformed the RoW which, paradoxically, is more USDnegative.

The lack of rate divergence has surprised us given the noticeable growth divergence we've seen. The correlation between US and EU rates, for example, is well above historical averages ( Exhibit 3 ). As noted here, there may be some good rationales for why the timing of the first cut might be correlated, but over time this should correct as investors focus on the pace and depth of cuts.

But another clearer factor has been restraining USD's strength: the outperformance of US equities. We explored this topic in 2020 when the US equity market was outperforming the RoW meaningfully and FX correlations to equities were near historical highs ( Exhibit 4 ).

Investors asked us: wouldn't the US equity outperformance attract capital into the US, turning the correlation positive? We found, in fact, the opposite: the more the S&P 500 outperforms global peers, the weaker USD gets, not stronger ( Exhibit 5 ).

But how does this interplay with rates? After all, if expectations for US data continue to expand versus global peers, then the correlation between US and RoW rates could break down, but the US and RoW equity divergence could continue.

How does USD perform when both US equities and US yields are outperforming the RoW? USD gains marginally: To test this thesis, we calculate the average returns since 2007 in scenarios conditioned on US equities outperforming/underperforming RoW peers and the US 10-year yield rising/falling relative to global peers.

The results present some interesting takeaways ( Exhibit 6 ). First, rate outperformance and equity outperformance pull in opposite directions. USD strengthens most when RoW equities and US yields outperform, and weakens the most when US equities outperform and US yields underperform. Both rates and equities rising more than peers (or falling less than peers) or vice versa see a mixed bag.

Second, when US equities are outperforming and US yields are compressing towards others, the market tends to trade in a uniform fashion of USD weakness with a low level of dispersion. This is consistent with this scenario likely corresponding with regime 1 of our four-regime USD framework, which sees widespread and uniform USD weakness.

Third, the scenario of both US equity and US rate outperformance sees surprising weakness in oil-sensitive currencies like CAD and NOK. We think that this is more a function of oil, though, given that 31% of the OSEBX (Norway) and 18% of the TSX (Canada) are energy shares, compared to 4% in the US S&P 500.

Finally, and perhaps most relevant for our current period, the scenario of simultaneous US rate and equity outperformance sees the lowest absolute average returns and dispersion that seem mostly driven by local stories. As noted above, CAD and NOK underperform likely because of higher energy exposures. We also see noticeable divergence in other risk currencies; AUD weakens while NZD strengthens, for example.

In sum, though, we think that this broader scenario of simultaneous US equity and rate outperformance suggests a neutral approach to USD. Varied and idiosyncratic but generally low average returns possibly help to explain the low-volatility FX environment we find ourselves in. While it does perhaps suggest a modest USD-upside bias, it appears difficult to trade.

## Trade idea: Maintain long 1-year EUR/USD 1.07 put at 1.35% (expiry February 18, 2025)

We maintain our short EUR/USD exposure via long-dated puts to position for a potential change in market regime and a potential breakdown in the high US-EU rate correlation. But beyond this, we think that relative value trades and trades benefiting from carry are likely to remain in vogue for investors.
