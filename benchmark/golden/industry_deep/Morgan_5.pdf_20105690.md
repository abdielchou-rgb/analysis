# Venture Vision: 2024 Predictions

We highlight what we got right and wrong in 2023 and what we expect for 2024.

• If you would like access to our 45,000 deal database, please ask

Through 2023, we highlighted the need for a Private/Unicorn valuation reset before a material recovery in exits could occur. As the year progressed, we also described why we remain correct on the thesis but were wrong on the timing of this – owing largely to the fact that the US consumer/economy remained more robust than most had appreciated. Moreover, the resilience of the funding markets post SVB allowed founders and investors to move swiftly past this otherwise potentially existential event. Below, we explain (1) our main expectations and the trends of 2023, (2) how we think these will evolve in 2024 and (3) more specific predictions for 2024.

4. An un-uniform unicorn split: >50 new AI unicorns minted, >3 more companies valued >\$100bn, but which offsets an overall fall in the number of US unicorns vs 2023.

5. Smaller number & size of funds: Average number and size of VC/PE funds will decline in 2024 as downrounds are digested and smaller AI-enabled businesses scale up, with less need for venture/growth capital.

6. Lean distributions & fundraising: Markets will rebound from extreme trough levels, but disappointing fund distributions will keep new buyout capital and venture fund raises below trend.

7. Exit excitement: A flurry of EBITDA profitable IPOs in 1H24 will signal the return of exits and kickstart a virtuous cycle, but it may take into 2025 to see the backlog of unicorns transact, re-price or exit. Continuation funds will take greater share and help accelerate exit volume in VC and PE.

8. Non-traditional investors: 2024 will see the return of non-traditional investment participation from troughing c90% lower from peak in 2023 in deals and value terms.

9. Growth to trump profitability: Growth will retake profitability as the most important metric for companies and investors at all stages of Venture/ Growth.

10. Policy to split funding: Regulation such as crypto ETFs and IRA debates ahead of the US election will bifurcate funding markets. The crypto/ tokenisation momentum will build but with emphasis on centralisation tools rather than decentralisation.

## The Fintech Radar: Summing Up the Year (17 Dec 2023)

• #1 SumUp raises €285m/\$307m at >\$8.5bn valuation in funding round led by Sixth Street Growth and Bain Capital Tech Opportunities. This week (11th Dec), London-based payments fintech SumUp shared its plans to deploy the capital to support global expansion and to grow its SME business offering. SumUp operates mainly in the B2B segment – currently servicing 4m businesses across 36 markets worldwide. It offers POS solutions, digital payments capabilities (including QR codes), and financial management tools, as well as additional tools, such as gift card issuance. It also launched into the B2C space in Sep-22 with its digital wallet loyalty app "SumUp Pay". According to a spokesperson from SumUp, the funding was raised at a higher valuation than the \$8.5bn valuation implied by the last raise in 2022. Also, at the start of the year Groupon sold off a stake in SumUp at an implied valuation of €3.9bn. Given the wider compression of fintech valuations over the past two years, we would view this as a significant achievement for SumUp and a positive signal for a potential 2024 rebound in activity for the fintech sector.

• #2 ABN AMRO announces acquisition of Dutch neobroker BUX for undisclosed sum. BUX has 500k clients across 8 markets, and ABN plans to leverage the acquisition to grow its wealth offering and support the bank in its Pan-European growth strategy. BUX reported a loss of -€16m in FY22, on revenues of €2.5m, according to its annual report filing with the Dutch Chamber of Commerce. The press release highlights the acquisition excludes BUX's cryptocurrency offering. ABN had already been involved with BUX via its venture arm (previously ABN Amro Ventures), which was one of the first investors in BUX, and the bank has been partnered with BUX since 2019 through ABN Amro Clearing. The deal is subject to regulatory approval and is expected to close in 2024. It is expected only to have a minimal impact on ABN's CET1 ratio. We think this is a good example of an incumbent picking up a fintech rather than developing technology in house, a trend we would have expected to see more often this year, given lowered fintech valuations.

• #3 Kapital raises \$165m in series B financing for Mexican/LatAm expansion. This week (14th Dec) Kapital co-founder and CEO Rene Saul shared that the funding round, led by Tribe Capital, was split \$40m/\$125 in equity/debt. It also included participation from Cervin Ventures, Tru Arrow, MS&AD Ventures and Alumni Ventures. The Mexico-based fintech specialises in catering to SMEs which struggle to access traditional financial services, offering products including loans and credit cards. According to Saul, Kapital is also planning to embed AI into its platform to generate user insights and automate payments. He also mentioned that Kapital

may consider a potential IPO in a "short period of time". The valuation at which the funding was raised has not been disclosed.

Unless otherwise indicated, the news reports referenced above have not been confirmed.

## Real-time Valuation Monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
