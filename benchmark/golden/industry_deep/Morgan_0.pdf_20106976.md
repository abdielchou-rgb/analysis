## Farewell, NIRP and YCC

Takeshi Yamaguchi & Masayuki Inui

## Shift from unconventional easing to conventional easing

We expect the Bank of Japan (BoJ) to revise existing unconventional monetary policy at the March monetary policy meeting (March 18/19), as the BoJ will likely judge that sustainable and stable achievement of the 2% price stability target is finally coming in sight. While there is a possibility of a few policy board members opposing the revision, even if this happens, we think the policy revision is likely to pass with a majority vote of 7- 2 or 8-1. Since early January, our base case has been removal of the negative interest rate policy (NIRP) and yield curve control (YCC) at the March policy meeting. We believe the prospect has risen further of policy revision at the March meeting, rather than at the April meeting, which is our risk scenario.

BoJ Governor Ueda has actually gradually distanced himself from unconventional monetary policy introduced under former Governor Kuroda, although he repeatedly made dovish comments. The Bank has taken various steps since last year to lay the groundwork for a NIRP exit, and we believe it has already completed preparations.

We expect a strong average wage hike in Rengo’s first tabulation result for Spring wage negotiations (TBA: Mar 15) ( Exhibit 1 ) (Japan Economics: Revising Up Our Shunto Forecast: 1st Tabulation Wage Increase to Exceed 5% (7 Mar 2024)). We also believe the Bank has bolstered its confidence in this year’s strong wage hike momentum via its own interview surveys conducted by its regional branch offices. Furthermore, Japanstyle core CPI has been +2% YoY or more in 22 consecutive months. Underlying measures of inflation remain strong against the backdrop of upturn in service prices as seen in recent re-acceleration of the weighted median CPI. The latest MoF Corporate Stats show robust recovery in private capex, resulting in an upward revision of Oct-Dec 2023 GDP to positive QoQ growth.

## Details of what we expect

We anticipate the following specific policy revisions.

Removal of NIRP and YCC: We expect the BoJ to abolish the existing YCC framework that controls long-term and short-term interest rates. We anticipate return to the uncollateralized overnight call rate as the main operating target for market operations and expect the Bank to encourage it to remain at "around 0 to 0.1%.”

Removal of the tiered structure for current account deposits and raising the policy rate to +0.1%: We think the Bank is likely to remove the existing three-tiered structure of BoJ current account deposits and put +0.1% interest to a whole excess reserves. This change should shift the trading range of uncollateralized overnight call rate in the shortterm money market from -0.1% to 0% now to 0% to 0.1% and thereby constitute a 10bp rate hike effectively.

Elimination of the “around 0%” target for the long-term interest rate: We expect elimination of the “around 0%” target for the long-term interest rate (10yr JGB yield) but continuation of the Bank’s long-term JGB purchases with the aim of avoiding a disruptive rise in the long-term yields. We think the Bank is likely to utilize a flexible format for increasing JGB purchases in a scenario of sharp rise in the long-term interest rate.

Announcing a guideline for the pace of gross JGB purchasing: There was a media report on March 8 (Jiji Press) that the Bank might consider the possibility of pre-announcing the monthly JGB purchase amount. The report about the BoJ considering the possibility of maintaining the current gross purchasing pace of JGBs at slightly below ¥6trn per month sounds reasonable to us, given the recent comments by Gov. Ueda and Dep. Gov. Uchida to avoid discontinuous changes. Even if it takes this approach, we expect the BoJ to introduce some flexibility by adding “about” or “outlook” to the purchase amount. The BoJ, which prefers having leeway for discretion in its policy, is unlikely to embrace a method of announcing the start of QT and implementing run-off at a fixed pace, in contrast to the Fed. We are also interested to see if the BoJ signals moves to shorten the average maturity of JGBs to be purchased, even if it maintains the overall purchasing amount.

Abolishment of ETF and J-REIT purchases: We anticipate removal of equity ETF and J-REIT purchases, though there is a possibility of stating the policy of not selling ETFs or J-REITs in the market for an extended period in the statement. Such a statement would be intended to minimize impact on the market. Regarding CP and corporate bonds, we expect the Bank to maintain the current buying policy at the point when it eliminates NIRP. That said, we think it is likely to reduce the pace of purchases.

Removal of the inflation-overshooting commitment: We expect the Bank to remove the inflation-overshooting commitment on the monetary base (“It will continue expanding the monetary base until the year-on-year rate of increase in the observed consumer price index (CPI, all items less fresh food) exceeds 2 percent and stays above the target in a stable manner”), accompanying the NIRP removal.

Elimination of the easing bias: We anticipate elimination of the phrase the Bank “will not hesitate to take additional easing measures if necessary.”

## Communication at the press conference

We expect comments by BoJ Gov. Ueda at the press conference to be mixed, including both dovish and hawkish elements.

We think Gov. Ueda is likely to emphasize the stance presented by Deputy Governor Uchida in a February speech that “it is hard to imagine a path in which it would then keep raising the interest rate rapidly. The Bank would, I think, maintain

accommodative financial conditions.” Additionally, the real interest rate is currently at a substantial negative level, and Gov. Ueda might state that he does not expect a major change in that situation. The possibility of suggesting a future rate-hike schedule is extremely low, in our opinion.

Regarding the actual short-term policy rate path, meanwhile, we expect the Bank to adopt a stance that it depends on future economic and inflation developments. The Bank is unlikely to rule out rate hikes at a moderate pace in the future. It might adhere to the fundamental approach of “set the policy interest rate at an appropriate level so that CPI inflation will be at around the 2 percent target.”

## Will the Bank eliminate the tiered structure?

Fixed income investors are keenly interested in the Bank’s stance toward surplus reserves when it removes NIRP. Will it adopt a two-tiered structure of 0% and 0.1%, restore the previous policy of paying 0.1% interest on whole excess reserves, or introduce some other format? Depending on the system design, we expect a substantial difference in the interest income received by financial institutions and the uncollateralized overnight call rate (TONA) level. We also anticipate major disparity in the impact on the BoJ’s finances. For details of a technical discussion of the tiered structure, refer to our other report scheduled for release this week.

When the Bank introduced NIRP in January 2016, it divided BoJ current account deposits into three tiers with the aim “to realize negative interest rates while taking the impact on financial institutions' earnings into consideration.” Specifically, the three tiers are the “basic balance” with a positive interest rate (+0.1%), the “macro add-on balance” with a 0% rate, and the “policy-rate balance” with a negative interest rate (-0.1%). While the TONA in the short-term money market trades in a range of -0.1% to 0%, it has already been near 0% recently because many financial institutions believe the Bank is on the verge of removing the NIRP.

If the Bank adopts a two-tiered structure, we think it might raise the interest rate applied to the policy-rate balance to 0% and then split current account deposits into the basic balance (+0.1%) and the remaining portion (0%). In this case, we think the Bank might have concerns about the risk of TONA being traded too low near 0%. Furthermore, it might be difficult to design a framework like the Swiss National Bank (SNB) that divides the reserves by setting an individual threshold (for example, by the amount of individual minimum reserve requirements multiplied by the applicable threshold factor) in Japan due to the presence of financial institutions that are not subject to the reserve requirement system.

If the Bank pays a +0.1% interest rate on the whole excess reserves, while the textbook view is that this should set the market’s lower bound of the interbank rate at +0.1%, in Japan’s case, it would become the upper bound due to arbitrage transactions between financial institutions that are subject to the reserve requirement and those that are not. In this scenario, we expect TONA to trade close to 0.1%, and the Bank might not need to worry about risk of its undershooting. We think the BoJ paying an interest rate on excess reserves is a simple and traditional approach that it might prefer, in contrast to the three-tiered structure’s arbitrary setting of the basic balance. Since excess reserves are massive, meanwhile, this approach increases the negative impact on BoJ finances. Conversely, it considerably increases interest rate income for financial institutions covered by the reserve deposit requirement system.

From an economist's perspective, elimination of the tiered structure has positive implications in terms of sending a signal that the BoJ will not return to a negative interest rate in a future economic downturn. If the Bank adopts a policy framework with an outlook for its JGB purchase amount, as reported by some media sources, we think it is likely to not return to NIRP and instead utilize quantitative easing in a future recession.

## Our BoJ outlook after removal of NIRP and YCC

We then expect a 15bp rate hike from +0.1% to +0.25% in July 2024. While it is unlikely that the BoJ will enter an aggressive rate-hike cycle like the Fed in the US, the BoJ would not endorse keeping its policy rate unchanged after a NIRP removal. We currently do not expect further hikes beyond +0.25%, but there is the risk that the Bank raises its policy rate to +0.50% in this year if inflation remains above our forecasts. That said, financial conditions are likely to be kept accommodative, with our estimated nominal neutral interest rate appearing to be close to 1%.

## Japan Macro Strategy

## Koichi Sugisaki

## JPY Rates: Closely monitor the guidance for future normalization path and future JGB purchase schedule

With various speculative headline coming into the market ahead of March BoJ MPM, market also raised the probability of March NIRP removal. That said, what' matter more from JPY rates development would be the future policy trajectory post NIRP removal.

Our economists are forecasting that the BoJ will kick off its “normalization” process in March but see that any subsequent rate hikes will come only gradually. This is in part because exogenous conditions and domestic data do not currently look conducive to inflation accelerating once again prior to reaching +2%.

US interest rates have dropped back towards their recent floors as this month’s economic data have left much to be desired (see Exhibit 3 ).

And domestically, the MoF’s Financial Statements Statistics of Corporations by Industry have shown a strong surge in corporate capex. Meanwhile, the February Tokyo CPI print pointed to somewhat of a quickening of price rises for the leisure-related sector (including hotel charges and certain goods), but the overall story remains one of continuing disinflation (see Exhibit 4 ).

Another question for market participants is what might be done about the current “inflation-overshooting commitment” whereby the BoJ has effectively promised to keep expanding the monetary base until CPI ex fresh food exceed 2% in a stable manner.

Our economists expect this commitment to be ended (along with the NIRP) at the March meeting. As discussed in "Japan | Lower BoJ Expectations Meet Positive Carry Trades", the BoJ would find it difficult to reduce JGB purchase size further under this commitment, as the current average monthly gross purchase size is almost equivalent to the expected redemption from the BoJ's holdings.

Removing the commitment would make it possible to commence (de facto) quantitative tightening. The ASW curve as a whole has cheapened as such a scenario has been partially priced in (see Exhibit 7 ), but whether or not this cheapening continues is ultimately likely to depend on what actually happens to the BoJ’s JGB purchases following a NIRP exit.

Governor Kazuo Ueda addressed this point in his recent Diet testimony by saying that the BoJ intends to continue buying JGBs with a view to avoiding “discontinuity”. Our economists see little possibility for the BoJ to reduce its balance sheet size at a rapid pace to avoid any unintended volatility in the JGB market.

Indeed, one media headline suggested that the BoJ may consider a new quantitative scheme, and would likely keep buying nearly ¥6trn JGBs per month under the new scheme. As such, we see little danger of JGB market supply/demand deteriorating dramatically owing to a rapid curtailment of BoJ absorption.

As we discussed in "Japan | Cheaper ASW", recent ASW cheapening would also appear to owe at least something to a lack of demand from domestic investors. That said, the aforementioned QUICK survey showed domestic investors to be more dovish than the market as a whole, with the consensus forecast pointing to a BoJ policy rate of 0.1% as of end-2024 and 0.25% for end-2025 (see Exhibit 8 ). Those believing that the BoJ will not move any further in 2024 after ending the NIRP will obviously have good reason to be

focusing on carry + rolldown.

We thus see ample potential for (at least) domestic investors to start showing renewed interest in JGBs after the NIRP is actually ended, albeit with the possible exception of the city banks that have recently seen the value of their strategic cross shareholdings surge so dramatically. If BoJ suggest very gradual normalization post NIRP removal, we see the room for JGB yield sell-off to be limited.

## JPY FX: NIRP exit impact should be limited, but technical flows may warrant attention

If the BoJ communicates its intention to proceed only very gradually with rate hikes following an exit from NIRP, then we would see little prospect of markets pricing in a significantly more hawkish trajectory. We would also envisage only limited impact on USD/JPY given that expectations for the Fed-BoJ monetary policy gap should not require much more than a minor tweak.

But as we discussed in "JPY | Contemplating JPY Flows: A Better Explanation Than “New NISA”?", the potential impact of technical flows will also need to be taken into consideration. For example, depending on what happens to domestic stock prices from this point forward, it is possible that some overseas players investing on an FX-hedged basis will look to maintain their hedge ratios via spot USD/JPY trades (lower Japan equity lead to lower USD/JPY and vice versa).

Over the medium term, the trajectory of USD/JPY will probably continue to depend in significant part on expectations for the Fed’s policy rate trajectory. A “soft landing” scenario of only gradual rate cuts would imply a significant Fed-BoJ gap even some years from now, and thus be conducive to a maintenance of yen carry trades and correspondingly supportive for USD/JPY.

Although not our economists' base case, we are also mindful of the potential for USD/JPY to decline more sharply if a “hard landing” starts to be viewed as more likely and markets accordingly price in a steeper Fed rate cut trajectory and a sub-neutral terminal policy

rate.

## Japan Equity Strategy

## Sho Nakazawa

## Japanese Stocks: Validating Hypothesis that Rates Will Remain Near Zero for Some Time after End of NIRP

The TOPIX Banks index is currently trading at a P/B of around 0.8x, the same level as before the BoJ adopted its negative interest rate policy (NIRP). We believe that the prevailing view in the equity markets is for interest rates to remain near zero even after the NIRP ends. Is this market bias justified? We should focus on any guidance on future policy at the March BoJ Monetary Policy Meeting (MPM).

Even if the BoJ begins normalizing monetary policy at the March MPM (as our economists think it will), we do not think any other information on further policy changes will be implied at the meeting. As we wrote previously in an earlier Japan Macro Strategy , if JPY swing is limited, volatility in the equity market will be curbed.

To find how changes in BoJ policy impact share prices for each sector, in Exhibit 9 we track the sensitivity of the 33 TOPIX sectors to exchange rates and to interest rates since 2013, when the BoJ adopted its unprecedented monetary policy (Exhibit 6). For exchange rates, we use the USD/JPY pair; for interest rates, we reference Yen 10year swap rates, which have proven an effective indicator in the past.

Should interest rates increase and the USD/JPY fall because of changes to BoJ monetary policy, share prices would very likely decline for those sectors in the lower right quadrant (Exhibit 6) and rise for sectors in the upper left quadrant.

## Value Factors Could Keep Edge Over Longer Term on Continued Expectations for Changes to BoJ Policy

As we wrote in our March 5, 2024 report, Japan Equity Strategy: Expect Continued Relative Advantage for Value in Japanese Stock Market (5 Mar 2024) , we think the value factor could maintain its relative advantage over the longer term on continued expectations for changes to BoJ policy. TOPIX-relative returns for TOPIX-17 sectors since 2016 (when NIRP was first adopted) show that since Dec 2022 (when the BoJ tweaked its yield curve control (YCC) policy), relatively undervalued sectors began generating positive excess returns ( Exhibit 10 ). The return for the only sector without a positive return (Construction & Materials) has been only slightly negative since Dec 2022.

We think that value-sector stocks may have advanced ahead of those in other sectors on the back of inflation expectations. On this point, increased prospects for continued rises in wages and prices could enable stocks in other sectors to advance even if they are not particularly undervalued, mainly because their performance may have suffered amid a deflationary environment. Although we are not saying that growth stocks will comfortably gain the upper hand in terms of returns, we think that investors may watch for a rebound for such names after an ultra-long period of accommodative monetary policy.
