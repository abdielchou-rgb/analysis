# Shifting Risk-Reward – Take Profits on IT, Upgrade Consumer Staples to OW

We book profits on our long-standing IT OW, moving to EW, on elevated relative performance, valuations and crowded ownership. We note that change in Asia market leadership historically occurs ahead of first Fed cut; we upgrade Consumer Staples ex-China to OW for defensive domestic-oriented growth.

Taking profits on Asia/EM Technology: We upgraded Asia/EM IT to OW in October 2022, but we think it is now time to move to the sidelines and adopt an EW recommendation. Overall Asia equity market valuations have historically peaked, and IT/Staples rotation taken place, in the run-up to the first Fed rate cut, which MS Economics expects in September.

Stretched valuations, positioning and technicals: IT is a high-beta growth cyclical with strong current earnings revisions and secular support from AI diffusion. However, Price-to-Sales for the key Asia/EM IT names are now at or above the prior 2021 peaks, and the overall sector is trading at a valuation level that has been historically associated with poor forward returns on a 1- to 3-year view. Recent price action in key names has been exponential, and long-only investors now hold significantly OW positions vs benchmark. We remove TSMC, SK Hynix, MediaTek, Tokyo Electron and Tokyo Seimitsu from our Focus Lists, while retaining Samsung Electronics and Hon Hai Precision which are lower beta and more exposed to the impending AI smartphone cycle.

We raise Consumer Staples to OW from UW, adding CP All, Woolworths, Amore Pacific, JBS, Cosmos Pharma and Isetan Mitsukoshi. Consumer Staples is trading at a 20-year valuation relative low to IT, with superior through-the-cycle ROE. It has, in our view, less demanding consensus earnings growth expectations through 2026 and classic defensive characteristics in a global growth slowdown.

Asia Technology Research view: Tech conditions are still improving, and our inflection signposts suggest it is too early for outright caution from here. That said, the risk-reward is no longer as attractive as when we upgraded our Global Tech sector view to Attractive from Cautious on October 4, 2022. Returns are often strongest at the end of the cycle, but we have seen a year's returns delivered in a few months and see sense in reducing some exposure. Charlie Chan and Shawn Kim are moving their Industry View for Greater China Semis and S. Korea Technology to In-Line from Attractive respectively (see Greater China Semiconductors: Move to In-Line and Asia Technology: Downgrading to In-Line), given signs of AI cannibalisation and a W-shaped foundry demand recovery, alongside pressure on mature node utilisation from Chinese capacity expansions.

Tokyo Electron has been in our Japan Focus List since February 2024, with Tokyo Seimitsu was added in June 2024.

With this report, we are removing TSMC, SK hynix, MediaTek, Tokyo Electron and Tokyo Seimitsu from our APxJ, GEM and Japan Focus Lists to implement our shift to an EW recommendation. This means we retain two Semis & Hardware names in APxJ – Samsung Electronics and Hon Hai Precision, as well as Renesas in Japan.

Within large cap Consumer Staples we prefer exposure via Japan (DM), Australia (DM), Korea (EM) and Thailand (EM) rather than China (EM) and India (EM). For China, the issue investors in the consumer sector face currently is the impact on spending patterns of a deflationary environment. Whilst this has mainly impacted big ticker discretionary, there has also more recently begun to be pressure on smaller-ticket items as the impact of the wealth shock from property and slower wage growth is felt. Meanwhile, in India there is a very different challenge of high valuations in names with strong business models (vs Consumer Discretionary, Financials and Industrials where we concentrate our Focus List exposure).

In contrast, Japan and Australian consumer names offer on average reasonable valuations and good visibility on earnings and profitability, given our broadly constructive view on

growth in both geographies, whilst MS analyst Kelly Kim views Amorepacific as an interesting recovery play on non-China revenue growth increase through 2026.

## Valuations – Elevated for IT

Valuations alone are never sufficient to drive an investment recommendation, in our view. That also requires analysis of earnings and the macro and micro factors that drive the cycles. However, expensive valuations are necessary for a meaningful performance drawdown to take place, requiring portfolio adjustment. We think this is the case for IT and in particular the Semiconductor industry group in Asia and EM equities currently.

There are a number of potential valuation approaches that we monitor in historical and market-relative terms, with each having trade-offs on accessibility vs complexity. Given the deeply cyclical nature of Asia semis, price/earnings valuation metrics are not generally particularly effective, and instead we prefer to look at revenue-based measures and ROE vs P/B frameworks. Indeed, we find P/Sales is an underappreciated metric more broadly, and we and our colleagues have shown in prior work that this metric does the best job of predicting 1- to 3-year forward returns (see Best of Times, Worst of Times).

The valuation context also needs to take into account the secular AI diffusion theme alongside the standing cyclical patterns for non-AI tech, as evident in the 2016 upcycle and 2018 downcycle. On the secular front, we see the 2020/21 peak in internet valuations as relevant in terms of investor dynamics and crowding, but there may be a major difference in terms of the competitive dynamics in the AI supply chain currently, versus e-Commerce and Internet (where intense competitive dynamics were fostered by capital availability and consumer incentives). That said, supply dynamics will always be a primary consideration across industries, and our colleagues have launched a framework to help track this in the AI space (see Thematics: Tech Diffusion: Navigating AI Oversupply).

## Why do we prefer to look at Price-to-Sales?

Price-to-Sales does a better job than other valuation metrics in predicting 1- to 3- year forward returns for US equity markets, in our view (see The Price/Sales Ratio by Martin Leibowitz and Anthony Bova). This is probably because the metric is "clean" in the sense that it is not subject to accounting choices that can cloud both earnings and book value calculations.

We have also shown that Price-to-Sales does better than metrics such as Forward P/E, Trailing P/B or Equity Risk Premium models in both EM and Japan (see Best of Times, Worst of Times). We used elevated Price-to-Sales as a key reason to downgrade China internet stocks in early 2021.

# Earnings Revisions and Estimates – 3-year Cycles Previously

Major market and sector inflexion points tend to come ahead of bottom-up consensus analysts' estimate-revision cycles. This relationship is shown clearly for major Asia/EM IT stocks (as shown in Exhibit 32 to Exhibit 36 ). The job of the strategist, therefore, is to attempt to predict beforehand when such inflexions are coming, or where risk/reward has shifted sufficiently to warrant portfolio hedging.

Exhibit 26 and Exhibit 27 decompose EPS growth expectations by industry group. Consensus expects three strong years of EPS growth for 2024, 2025 and 2026 for both Semiconductors/Semi Equipment and Technology Hardware & Equipment – this, after both sectors had meaningful year-on-year declines.

For Semiconductors, the sequences of IBES Consensus EPS growth is now expected at +69%, +33% and +16%. For Technology Hardware, it is +83%, +36% and +12%. If delivered, this would be the strongest EPS growth sequence ever for these industry groups. Again, to be clear, we think consensus is likely broadly to be accurate about 2024 (if not too cautious), and probably 2025. However, a three year sequence of substantial earnings growth (through end 2026) has been uncommon in history and looks to carry more risk of disappointment, particularly after revisions seen over the last 12 months.

It may seem too soon to be worried about 2026 EPS growth, but the market is more than capable of thinking 12-18 months out, in our view. Indeed, the major decline in EPS growth in 2023 was of course why these industries performed poorly from mid-2021 to the end of 2022.

## Technicals – Crowded ownership with peaking RSI

Technical analysis is also helpful to identify potential inflexion points. Investment history is littered with price bubbles in stocks (as far back as the South Sea Bubble), commodities and even on occasion bonds. Most recently, extreme price dynamics have taken place in crypto currency and meme stocks. Indeed, bubbly conditions appear to us to be becoming more frequent in various pockets of the market, as investors find it easier to share information via social media and app-based investing becomes the norm.

## Standard deviations to 200-day moving average as well as 14-day relative strength index (RSI) show overbought conditions

On top of fitting price curves, other tools that are useful include deviations in moving average and RSI. These technical indicators help identify the current market state and flag if market is overbought or oversold. The MSCI AC APAC Semiconductors and Semiconductor Equipment - IG Index has been well above its 200-day moving average since it's crossover around Nov 2023. The index sits 30% above it's 200-day moving average. The 14-day RSI for the said index, was above 70 in early July and dipped below it only in the second week of July 2024.

In contrast, the MSCI AC APAC Consumer Staples Index has been below its 200-day moving average from around August 2023, only to have marginally crossed over in the second week of July 2024. # Impact of Global Business Cycle Dynamics on Asia Equities and IT

Asia/EM IT has historically been an early cycle cyclical – in our view, it tends to do well around troughs in economic activity into the acceleration phase and worst around the peaking of economic activity. In Exhibit 45 we use an average of the US Manufacturing and Services PMI to proxy global macro momentum to highlight this pattern. In late 2022, the call we needed to make in upgrading the sector to OW was therefore in part that US/ global economic activity would not resolve to a recession – this was a controversial view at the time.

## The Fed beginning to cut is not bullish for Asia/EM equities, in our view (and particularly a cyclical sector such as Asia/EM IT)

In our view, the closer we are to the first Fed rate cut, the more dangerous the business cycle becomes for Asia equities. In Exhibit 47 we show that APAC equity valuations were not boosted by the start of Fed rate cuts. # Asia Technology Research – Peak Cycle Playbook

## Shawn Kim

It is always difficult to predict cyclical turns, and investors' natural temptation is to follow the playbook and lock in gains, rather than wait for the upgrade cycle to fully unfurl. However, tech conditions are still improving and not losing steam, and our inflection signposts suggest too early for caution from here. That said, the risk-reward is no longer as attractive when we upgraded our global tech sector view from Cautious to Attractive on October 4, 2022 (Asia Technology: Ice Age – End In Sight) because it fitted our framework of a cyclical re-rating coincident with a semiconductor cycle bottom. We may be entering late stages of the tech cycle in duration, but we are also mindful that returns tend to be highest during the final transition from optimism to euphoria. We offer some insight on what might make us change our sector view. Nevertheless, if taking a little off the table today on tech – just reflecting on the fact that we have achieved a year’s worth of returns in just a few months of the year – we think that makes sense.

## Peak Cycle Playbook

Past cycle peaks and downturns have occurred for many reasons, but typically are supplydriven and the result of imbalances that build up in capacity coming on line and the economy, and that ultimately need to be corrected. Demand-led downturns are rare events and caused by severe economic downturns or unexpected shocks triggering large demand cuts, which is not our current base case.

Each cycle peak different, but there are clear similarities. For example:

• The 2016 downturn was caused by excess inventory in the supply chain, which is a function of supply creeping higher. The 2021 downturn is similar due to significant pull-forward of consumer demand from the pandemic.

• The current AI-led tech investment build is similar to the 2018 contraction was caused by significant excess capacity builds in the technology sector, anticipating a new secular wave of demand from cloud.

Manufacturers must project output at least 9-12 months in advance because supply tends to lag demand, causing major fluctuations in investment and returns. Given the large capital expenditures and long lead times to add new facilities, the bulk of supply comes in steps, eventually causing an imbalance. Over time, the high level of production eventually becomes unsustainable as inventory starts to build, leading to an oversupply. Compounding this is the constant threat of inventory obsolescence and order cancellation.

## Where Are We In The Cycle?

The current YoY inflection based on global semiconductor consensus revenue forecasts point to a 1Q25 peak; however, numbers change often, and stronger near-term earnings can pull forward that perceived peak of the cycle. Korean exports of semiconductors – often used as another leading indicator of activity in the tech sector – have gone through a sharp recovery and are moving closer to previous cycle peaks.

## Signposts To Watch – What Indicators Can Signal A Cyclical Peak?

Many factors can contribute to a downturn, and the main causes often change. Therefore, it is helpful to look at several different aspects of the supply and demand to better assess where excesses and imbalances may be building. Keep in mind that any indicator should be viewed more as a milestone than a destination sign. Despite the impossibility of pinpointing the inflection or exact start of a downturn, there are some generally reliable signals worth watching closely in a late-cycle phase of the semiconductor market.

• The rate of change (+). Tech stocks tend to sell off into a YoY peak and rally into a YoY trough (which was 4Q22). The performance flattens out 6-12 months after but still yields negative returns. No one knows exactly when this technology cycle upturn will end, and the market will begin to look through peak cycle, but we do not see signals that suggest we should turn to caution yet.

• Wafer foundry revenues (+). The trend of monthly sales of Taiwan-listed foundries to global semiconductor sales forms a good indicator of future demand. The foundry composite predicts a further increase in chip sales in the short term. Taiwan-listed companies report their monthly revenues about 10 days after the close of a month, so they can be a very timely indicator of industry performance. The latest June 2024 revenue data are still showing strong momentum in terms of YoY growth but likely masked by overbooking owing to the perceived shortage of supply.

• Book-to-bill ratio (+). Order backlog is a reported operating metric in the semiconductor industry and defined as product sold that cannot be invoiced because it is unavailable in inventory. Generally, an increase in order backlog is associated with future growth and a leading indicator of future sales growth and can be useful in identifying trends.

• Inventory cycles (+). A key factor influencing swings in the cycle is inventory. Inventory plays an important role in causing swings in customer purchase behavior and often decelerates the rate of increase as the value of holding inventory increases sharply with prices rising, and vice versa on the way down.

• Capex (=). It is a predictable sequence – when profitable, semiconductor makers tend to invest in new manufacturing capacity as returns on investments are

justified. China has been stockpiling on wafer fab equipment (WFE) for geopolitical reasons, but the bulk of the semiconductor industry globally is just beginning to invest, and we forecast that wafer fabrication equipment market investment will increase 6% YoY in 2025.

## What could be different this time?

Covid, supply chain issues and the bifurcated market created by AI have distorted the current cycle and caused cycles within tech to be out of sync. Unlike past cycles, the current cycle has mainly benefited the AI (NVIDIA, GPU) supply chain, which has accounted for the bulk of growth and profits, while consumer demand, such as smartphone and PC, has remained stagnant, and auto and industrial demand has remained weak throughout, due to excess inventory. This means significant divergence is likely on the way down.

We are still seeing a massive amount of capex from the hyperscaler side to fund AI computing, which is creating a wall of worry on the application from the enterprise side, which is still in the experiment stage. This is true – enterprises tend to move slower – but we like the breadth of enterprises that are experimenting and building AI applications. The risk is for AI computing and supply chain, which have to keep clearing a higher bar. But in evaluating AI as a long-term investment, it is important to note that tech companies operate in a cyclical industry. This means while AI provides a boost today, at some point, the companies involved will eventually face an industry downturn.

# Greater China Semiconductors – Moving to In-Line, but still favor AI vs non-AI

## Charlie Chan

Just a year ago, on Jul 6, 2023, we Upgraded Greater China Semiconductors to Attractive, citing Cycle Bottomed. We predicted that tech deflation (price elasticity) coupled with secular AI semi demand would together trigger the next logic semi upcycle. Semi revenue turned positive Y/Y in 4Q23, with inventory days starting to decline. Historically, the decline in semi inventory days is a positive signal for semi stock price appreciation, and vice versa.

However, we have decided to move our Industry View on Greater China Semis to In-Line. The non-AI semi demand recovery remains sluggish in 1H24 (see our report here). Semi inventory days went up again to 121 days exiting 1Q24, suggesting slow channel inventory digestion (see Exhibit 65 ). Here are our key observations on the current logic semi cycle:

• AI cannibalization: We see a slower pace of broader semis (e.g., general computing, consumer, automotive) as enterprises need to invest in AI. The value of AI semis comes from software (e.g., NVIDIA's CUDA) beyond just semiconductor hardware.

• “W-shaped” recovery: The previous 1H23 downcycle (wafer foundry utilization cuts) indeed translated into inventory depletion in 2H23, followed by short-lived rush orders in 4Q23. But the cycle recovery has been slow in 2024, and we saw another small dip of foundry utilization in 1Q24.

• China fab supply continues to ramp: China capex becomes stronger and more sustainable, which causes mature node foundry utilization recovery to be even slower. While we expect TSMC can hike wafer price for leading-edge process, mature nodes prices should stay the same, given the likely ongoing oversupply in 2025.

Our sector valuation is at +1 standard deviation, also less compelling than a year ago. Taiwan semis group 2024 YTD performance of 55% has been driven more by P/E multiple expansion (30%) than by EPS growth plus dividend yield (25%).
