China<sup>’</sup>s industrial profits recovery accelerated in November. The National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) announced that total industrial profits (including all industrial companies with annual sales from principal business exceeding 20 million yuan) contracted 4.4%oya in Jan-Nov, narrowing from the 7.8%oya decline in Jan-Oct. Concurrently, accumulated sales revenue grew 1.0%oya, following +0.3%oya in Jan-Oct. Cost of sales increased by 1.1%oya. Profit margin increased to 5.82% in Jan-Nov, compared to 6.09% in full-year 2022. The NBS estimates that industrial profits increased by 29.5%oya in November, following +2.7%oya in October, +11.9%oya in September, and +17.2%oya in August. Meanwhile, sales revenue increased 6.1%oya in November, following +2.5%oya in October. Accumulated industrial profits contraction across ownership types narrowed, while year-to-date profits of private enterprises turned positive.

Equipment manufacturing sector’s profit growth accelerated, while raw material sector<sup>’</sup>s profit contraction narrowed. We estimate the share of mainstream raw material and machinery & equipment manufacturing sectors in total industrial profits increased to 47.0% in Jan-Nov (vs. 45.8% in Jan-Oct), the share of upstream mining sectors fell to 17.6%, while the share of downstream manufacturing sectors fell to 26.0%. As PPI deflation continued, the upstream mining sector<sup>’</sup>s profits declined by 18.3%oya in Jan-Nov, following the 19.7%oya decline in Jan-Oct. The manufacturing sector<sup>’</sup>s year-to-date profit contraction further narrowed, falling 4.7%oya in Jan-Nov (vs. -8.5%oya in Jan-Oct). According to the NBS, the raw material manufacturing sector<sup>’</sup>s profit contraction narrowed significantly, led by the profit growth in non-ferrous metal, ferrous metal and chemical sectors, making the largest contribution to overall industrial profit growth. Equipment manufacturing sector<sup>’</sup>s profits grew 16.2%oya in November, led by the profit growth in automobile and electronics equipment manufacturing sectors.

## Looking ahead, industrial enterprise profits should improve further

On the domestic front, the impact of latest policy support measures on domestic industrial and consumer demand will take time to feed through. We maintain the view that the government likely will keep its annual growth target unchanged in 2024 at “around 5%<sup>”</sup>. Fiscal and monetary policy will continue the growth-friendly policy stance, but the probability of bolstered policy stimulus is low. On the PPI front, our global commodity team expects the Brent oil price to average at \$83 in 2024 (link). We think the PPI print likely will stay in moderate annual deflation zone through most of 1H24. On the external front, it is worth noting that, amid lingering concerns of geopolitical pressure and supply-chain relocation, China<sup>’</sup>s share of world exports has held up better than expected in the near term, coming in at 14.9%, sa by 2Q23, likely reflecting Chinese exporters<sup>’</sup> efforts to diversify export markets and product groups. We hold a relatively steady outlook on next year<sup>’</sup>s export trend. Total exports (in USD terms) may be flat or show low single digit growth in 2024. As global demand growth softens China<sup>’</sup>s export growth in volume terms might be softer than in 2023, but negative price effects will likely fade. Overall, we expect the contraction in year-to-date industrial profits to narrow further, and the profit growth recovery to continue into 2024.
