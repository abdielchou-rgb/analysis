# US Oil Supply and Demand

US crude production growth remained at +0.9 mb/d YoY in October, with an output of 13.2 mb/d flat on September levels of production. Demand rose by 0.6 mb/d MoM, led by gasoline and middle distillates. Large cuts to crude imports coincided with refinery turnarounds.

US crude production maintained YoY growth of 0.9 mb/d in October and was flat MoM. The slowing in growth rate from summer levels is due to a softening of output growth from the 'shale states'.

Crude production in October maintained September's record 13.2 mb/d of production. Small MoM increases in PADDs 1, 3, 4 and 5 were offset by a production decrease in PADD 2 caused by declines in the Bakken basin of North Dakota. The weekly data implies production levels remaining at this level through November and gaining a slight boost near end-December.

Production from the 'shale states' was broadly flat in October. North Dakota, which has been the main driver of recent shale growth, experienced a MoM decline in output (- 31 kb/d) for the first time since March. Despite the increases in production of 0.68 mb/d YoY, the rate of annual growth slowed for the second consecutive month in October.

## Supply Continued…

Baker Hughes data saw oil rig counts start to tick up in October, marking the first consecutive week-on-week rise since December 2022. The gains were concentrated in the Eagle Ford basin, which added four rigs. Rig counts in the Permian and Bakken were flat over October. Looking forward, a drop in the rig count occurs in the first week of November, followed by a slow but relatively steady rise in the rig count through the latter half of November and December.

The rig count beginning to tick up in October is likely a knock on effect of the upwards trend in oil prices in 3Q 23, that began with the 'Saudi lollipop' announced in July. A slowly rising rig count and improvements in well efficiency, which allows more wells to be completed with the same number of rigs, have helped the US reach new production highs in 4Q 23.

Crude imports fell significantly for the second consecutive month, falling by 505 kb/d MoM. This was led by declines in imports from Saudi Arabia and Brazil. Overall the decline in crude imports is likely a consequence of the high global oil prices seen in October, causing refiners to delay purchases and cut runs. Additionally, seasonal maintenance at refineries meant appetite for crude remained depressed in October. Crude imports from Canada increased in October, driven by the weakening of western Canadian crudes vs WTI as Canada ramps up output further. Crude exports had a minor decline in October. Greater volumes to NE Asia and NWE Europe were countered by a decrease in volumes to SE Asia and Southern Europe.

## Demand

Oil demand rose in October to reach 20.7 mb/d, an increase of 0.59 mb/d MoM. This corresponds to 3.4% YoY growth. The rise in demand in October was concentrated in gasoline, diesel and fuel oil. Meanwhile, jet-kero demand seems to have stagnated in the US, as flight numbers have been trending downwards since August. Overall the 0.5-0.8 mb/d fluctuations in total product demand monthly since June means demand has been broadly flat at around 20.5 mb/d over the last 5 months.

## Imports/Exports

Total net exports of crude and products were up MoM by 0.68 mb/d. This was the result of decreased total imports (-7.9% MoM), driven by a sharp decline in crude imports. Total exports stayed roughly flat in October, with a decline in crude exports countered by an increase in finished product exports. Another month of positive net exports solidifies the US's position as a net exporter of oil, and this looks set to continue if demand remains broadly flat.

## Inventories

US total crude and product inventories at the end of October were \~20 million barrels lower than at the end of September. The main drivers of this inventory decline were the finished product categories of middle distillate fuel oil and kerosene, alongside asphalt. Crude stocks built slightly, despite the drop in crude imports, as refiners slashed runs in October. There will be limited builds in crude stocks in November as refinery run rates pick up post end of seasonal maintenance. Overall, US commercial inventories remain below the 5-year seasonal average.

## Crude Adjustment Factor

There was a negative crude adjustment for the third consecutive month in October, equalling -370 kb/d. This is relatively rare, as the adjustment factor is usually positive. This corresponds to a 11.6 mln bbls downward revision in crude supply over the course of October and takes the sum of adjustments for the year close to zero.
