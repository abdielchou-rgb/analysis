# China Economic Perspectives China by the Numbers (January 2024)

## Our guide to Chinese monthly data

What the numbers are, what they mean, and the outlook going forward.

## Q4 GDP growth edged up to 5.2%y/y on low base

Q4 GDP growth slowed sequentially from Q3 but a low base pushed up the y/y growth to 5.2% (with a negative GDP deflator of -1.4%y/y). 2023 full-year GDP growth recorded 5.2% in real terms and 4.6% in nominal terms, in line with our expectation. The low base drove up the y/y growth of retail sales, exports, infrastructure FAI and manufacturing FAI, as did industrial production growth. December data suggest that property activities are yet to bottom, with sales and starts declining further from the November level. Retail sales decelerated as low base effect faded, while both manufacturing and infrastructure investment growth picked up. Growth of export volume picked up further in December, leading to a notable rebound in Q4 from Q3 (see Q4 & Dec data comment).

## Revise up 2024 GDP growth forecast to 4.6%

The slightly stronger Q4 GDP growth outturn (5.2% vs 5.0% of our original forecast) and the revision of q/q growth in the previous quarters push the 2024 annual growth to 4.6% (vs. 4.4% previously envisaged), without any revision of our q/q sequential growth forecasts. In the coming months, we expect property activities and property market to stabilize, helping to underpin household confidence. That should support continued post-Covid consumption recovery along with the economy and labor market, though at a slower pace. Infrastructure investment growth is not expected to accelerate despite more explicit fiscal support. We forecast exports to improve modestly on the upswing of the global tech cycle despite the expected slowdown in US growth.

## More modest policy support ahead...

We expect more policy support ahead, though likely modest in scale. We see a headline fiscal deficit of 3.5-3.8% (potentially including issuance of new special CGB), a larger special LG bond quota and additional debt-swap LG bonds. We see limited monetary policy actions, potentially a 10-20bps MLF rate cut and 25-50bp RRR cut, and liquidity facilities to help accommodate fiscal expansion. The Augmented Fiscal Deficit may expand modestly by +0.8ppt of GDP in 2024 vs +0.4ppt in 2023. We expect additional PSL quota of RMB 300-500bn following its recent increase of RMB 500bn (o/w RMB 350bn injected in Dec 2023). See more in 2024 key themes and CEWC takeaways.

## ...and new PSL in focus to support property activities

We expect more property policy easing including more credit support to developers and using the low-cost funding underpinned by PSL to support the purchase of existing housing inventory. It is not clear how much of the low-cost funding underpinned by PSL can be deployed by local governments and related entities to purchase existing commodity homes or subsidize households, which would help to lower housing inventory and provide additional cash flow to developers. But such a use faces criticism of high cost and potential moral hazard. Meanwhile some cities are building new social housing projects, which can boost real estate investment but may generate more housing supply, potentially worsening the demand-supply balance. We are looking for clarity on the size and use of PSL funding in the months ahead, but expect much smaller impact on the housing market than that of shanty town renovation in 2015-18.

## UBS Activity Indicators

 UBS Physical Activity Index (3mma) improved further to 8.5%y/y in December, with industrial activities, transportation activities, construction activities and power generation all improving from November, partly helped by low base, while agriculture activities held stable.

 UBS Expenditure index improved in December, as net exports, consumption and investment all show signs of improvement on 3mma basis.

Property sales slid in January, high base to weigh on y/y growth. Property sales in major cities declined further to -31.4%y/y in the first 21 days of January, from -11.5%y/y last December. On the other hand, FTL traffic index growth rebounded to 44.6%y/y in the first 21 days from 7.2%y/y before. Auto retail sales growth accelerated to 18%y/y in the first 14 days on a low base (vs 9%y/y in Dec). Crude steel production improved to 10.5%y/y in the first 10 days (vs -1.7%y/y in Dec). Port cargo and container throughput improved further to 16.3%/15.1%y/y in the first 21 days from 5.5%/7.1%y/y before. Subway passenger turnover level stayed elevated, while its growth softened to 66.5%y/y in the first 20 days from 125%y/y in December on a fading low base effect. Overall, our UBS China Activity Index (y/y growth) likely softened in the first half of January, mainly dragged by property sales and base effect (see UBS China Daily Activity Tracker).

## Business Indicators

December NBS manufacturing PMI dropped by 0.4ppt to 49, staying <50 for 3 months in a row. New order dropped by 0.7ppt to 48.7. Production softened by 0.5ppt to 50.2, but remained >50 for 11 months consecutively. New export orders weakened by 0.5ppt to 45.8. Raw material inventory and finished goods slid by 0.3/0.4ppt, while quantity of purchase declined by 0.6ppt. Input price rose by 0.8ppt to 51.5, while output price dropped by 0.5ppt to 47.7. Employment edged down by 0.2ppt to 47.9, reflecting lingering pressures of the labor market. On the other hand, Caixin manufacturing PMI edged up by 0.1ppt to 50.8, with both new orders and output index picking up. As we discussed before, such divergence between NBS and Caixin PMIs is not uncommon, largely due to the difference in survey sample selection, while we may need other high frequency data to cross-check the underlying strength of the manufacturing sector.

NBS non-manufacturing PMI edged up by 0.2ppt to 50.4, remaining >50 throughout 2023. Services PMI stayed stable at 49.3, while construction PMI improved by 1.9ppt to 56.9, reflecting improved construction activities at yearend. By sector, PMIs of outgoing and consumption related sectors, i.e., water transport, air transport, accommodation, household services, all stayed below 46, partly due to cold weather. On the other hand, PMIs of postal service, telecom, monetary & financial services, and insurance services stayed above 55.

## Property

Property activities weakened further in December, while the y/y contraction narrowed in Q4. Dec property sales declined by another 12.7% (vs -10.3%y/y in Nov), while new starts fell by -10.4%y/y. Property investment contracted more by -12.5%y/y (vs -10.6% previously) partly due to base effect. After seasonal adjustment, property sales and new starts volume both dipped further from Nov, suggesting property activities are yet to bottom. Developers source of funds remained in deep contraction of -14%y/y in Dec. Due to a lower base last year, Q4 new starts and property sales both narrowed y/y decline, but real estate investment stayed in deep contraction.

Expect property sales volume to stabilize at low levels in the coming months; new PSL in focus. The current sharp property downturn was driven by a combination of weakening fundamentals, previous policy tightening, and sliding home purchase intention. The government started to ease policies in Q4 2021, and more notably after July 2023 Politburo meeting, especially since late August. The seasonally adjusted property new starts volume was largely flat in Q4 from Q3, while property sales dropped further in Q4. We expect property activities to stop falling on a seasonally adjusted sequential basis within the next 6 months, with the help of additional property policy easing, more progress in urban village renovation and social housing construction, and improvement of developer financing. The new PSL may help to reduce inventory and stabilize housing market, though it would depend on the size, pace, and use of such funding. Overall, we expect housing starts and sales to improve modestly in H2 2024, leading to a smaller fall in property sales/starts/investment, by 5%/10%/5%, respectively, vs -8.5%/-20.4%/-9.6% in 2023.

## Fixed Asset Investment

Overall FAI growth improved in December and Q4. Infrastructure growth picked up more than expected to 10.7%y/y in December from 5.4% in November, partly due to deployment of special CGB issuance. Manufacturing investment growth edged up to 8.2%y/y from 7.1%y/y previously. Together, they offset the weakness from property investment, pushing overall FAI growth up to 4%y/y in December (vs 2.9% previously). The 4-year annual average growth from 2019 picked up pace for overall FAI in December, as well as for manufacturing and infrastructure FAI. Strength in December lifted the Q4 average growth of both manufacturing & infrastructure FAI, and the overall FAI (to 2.7%y/y in Q4 from 1.9% previously).

Expect both infrastructure and manufacturing growth to slow in 2024. We expect the central government to provide more explicit support to infrastructure FAI going forward, increasing central government and special LG bonds issuance in 2024 (the latter likely RMB 4 trillion). This support should partly offset the decline in property related local government revenue and limited LGFV new financing. We expect infrastructure FAI to grow by 5-6% in 2024 (vs 8.2% in 2023). We see more growth potential in new energy investment, hydraulic & water projects, city-cluster transport networks and public utility and facilities. In the manufacturing sector, FAI in the sectors along the property supply chain will likely be held back by weak demand and profits, although FAI related to the green transition should stay robust while FAI along the tech supply chain could improve. Overall, we see manufacturing investment growing by 5-6% in 2024E (6.5% in 2023). See more in our 2024-25 outlook.

## Industrial Production

Industrial production growth edged up in December and Q4 on a low base. December IP growth grew by 6.8%y/y, slightly faster than 6.6% in November. The official seasonally adjusted IP level also increased by 0.5%m/m in December, following 0.9%m/m increase in November. Value-added growth of auto sector held strong at 20%y/y and auto production at 25%y/y, both helped by low base. VA of communication appliance stayed solid at 9.6%y/y, as production of cell phone and electronic IC strengthened further. Coupled with improving trade activities of electronic ICs, these are reflections of tech cycle improvements. On the other hand, VA of ferrous metal slowed while production of crude steel and cement both dipped into y/y contraction, despite low base last year, with subdued property activities to blame. Overall speaking, low base, an upward tech cycle, improving infrastructure investment and retail sales together boosted Q4 average IP growth to 6%y/y from 4.2% in Q3, while the official sequential growth of IP improved to 1.8%q/q in Q4 from 1.1% q/q in Q3.

China's inventory growth edged down to 1.7%y/y in nominal terms (vs 2%y/y in October) in November, while held up at 4.3%y/y in real terms (vs 4.2% previously) . Industrial inventory to sales ratio (seasonally adjusted, 6mma) has risen notably since 2022, now still well above the historical average, which means it takes a few more months to destock.

## Consumption

Base effect dragged down December retail sales but boosted the Q4 average growth. Overall retail sales growth softened to 7.4%y/y in Dec partly due to a fading low base, from 10.1%y/y in Nov. We estimate that the seasonally adjusted retail sales level (in real terms) edged up by 0.2%m/m in Dec, following +0.1%m/m in Nov. Partly due to fading low base, goods retail sales growth decelerated, led by auto sales. That of property related products remained subdued. On the other hand, catering sales growth picked up further due to a low base, while retail sales of services accelerated in December. Going-out related products also saw acceleration of sales growth, such as cosmetics and clothing. Despite the slowdown in December, average retail sales growth in Q4 improved to 8.4%y/y from 4.2%y/y in Q3, mainly due to low base. Our seasonally adjusted retail sales level (in real terms) improved in Q4 by 2% q/q (vs -1%q/q in Q3).

Consumption recovery should continue at a slightly slower pace in 2024. Strong consumption rebound in Q1 2023 has been slowing down in Q2, due to low consumer confidence, cautious hiring and slow labor market improvement, and continued property downturn. CEWC called for boosting consumption and income, while highlighting that support should focus on consumption areas with high potential. We believe the government is still reluctant to roll out large-scale direct income and/or consumption subsidies. In 2024, we believe the expected stabilization in property activities should help underpin household confidence. That will help to support continued post-Covid consumption recovery along with the economy and labor market, though at a slower pace. Chinese household accumulated more savings in Q4 2024. Given lingering labor market weakness, soft income growth and subdued consumer confidence, we expect the release of excess savings in 2024 to be small and gradual.

## Inflation

CPI narrowed contraction to -0.3%y/y in December from -0.5%y/y previously. Food price declined less, by -3.7%y/y (vs -4.2%y/y before), while nonfood prices edged up by 0.1ppt to 0.5%y/y. In particular, transportation disruption due to bad weather and seasonal holiday demand have pushed up prices of fresh vegetable and fruit. Travel demand also lifted price of travelling, entertainment and household services ahead of New Year holidays. Core CPI stabilized at 0.6%y/ y. On a sequential basis, food price turned positive to +0.9%m/m, while non-food price declined less. Full year CPI inflation recorded 0.2% in 2023.

PPI declined less by -2.7%y/y in December vs -3%y/y before, and continued to drop by -0.3%m/m on a sequential basis. The weakness was primarily from the decline in global oil prices and subdued industrial demand. Investment goods improved from -3.4%y/y to -3.3%, o/w prices of mining and quarrying and raw material improved both improved y/y but declined sequentially, while manufacturing prices weakened both y/y and m/m. Consumer goods price stabilized around -1.2%y/y (-0.1%m/m). PPI declined by -3% in 2023.

CPI inflation to record 0.8%y/y in 2024 while PPI to stay in a small full-year deflation. We expect CPI to climb out of recent deflation and average 0.8% in 2024 as pork price switch to an upcycle, energy prices stop falling, and domestic consumption continues to recover modestly. On the other hand, prolonged weakness in property construction and only modest growth of infrastructure investment should lead to generally soft demand for upstream products. The relatively high inventory and low capacity-utilization in the industrial sector may weigh on producer prices for a bit longer. We expect PPI to narrow y/y decline gradually and turn positive after Q2 but may still come to a slightly negative reading of -0.5% for the whole of 2024 (see 2024 Key themes). Therefore, China’s GDP deflator is likely to recover from a decline in 2023 to a small positive reading in 2024.

## Money & Credit

Credit growth edged up further. Headline new RMB loans recorded RMB 1.17trn, slightly weaker than market expectation. New M&L corporate loans (at RMB 861bn) was a key drag again, which extended its y/y weakness to -350bn y/y (vs -291bn y/y in November). Meanwhile, new M&L household loans printed RMB 146bn, slightly softer than a year ago, echoing weak property sales in recent months. Government bond issuance recorded a strong reading of RMB 928bn as expected. As a result, new TSF printed RMB 1.94trn in December, slightly lower than expected but still RMB 634bn higher than a year ago. Official and our adjusted TSF credit growth edged up by 0.1-0.2ppt to 9.5-9.6%y/y. Credit impulse turned to a positive reading of 1.6% of GDP (vs 0% previously) while new credit flow softened to 28% of GDP from 30% on 3mma basis. See our comment.

Resilient credit growth in 2024. As 15-20% of new credit may be used to pay interest on existing debt in 2023, and to accommodate the quasi-fiscal expansion and ensure sufficient credit to the real economy, we believe there needs to be more credit support in the coming months. Looking into 2024, we expect property sales and mortgage loans to stabilize gradually in the coming months, and corporate credit demand to improve later in the year as growth momentum stabilizes. Government bond issuance may stay strong in 2024, as we expect general budget deficit to expand to 3.5-3.8% of GDP (including possibly new issuance of special CGB) and another RMB 2-3trn of special refinancing swap LG bonds. As such, we expect credit growth to stay resilient at 9.5%y/y at end-2024.

## Base Money & Liquidity Operations

 Base money growth edged down by 0.1ppt to 7.9%y/y in December (3mma), after adjusting for RRR cuts (e.g. Jan 2020, July/Dec 2021, Apr/Dec 2022, Mar/Sep 2023 and various targeted RRR cuts) and changes from other liquidity facilities.

The PBC increased liquidity support with a net MLF injection of RMB 216bn in January, RMB 800bn (record-high level) in December and RMB 600bn in November, while it kept the MLF rate unchanged at 2.5%. The net MLF injection in Nov and Dec is larger than a 50bps RRR cut. The previous round of RRR cut in September has brought down the average RRR to 7.4% from 7.6% and released liquidity of around RMB 500bn (see RRR cut comment).

 Expect continued accommodative monetary and credit policy but no aggressive easing. We expect to see 25-50bps RRR cut in 2024, together with other liquidity facilities, to accommodate the expected large bond issuance. That said, if the PBC continues to use more MLF net injections, there may be fewer or no RRR cuts.

## Interest Rates & Bond Market

 PBC last cut policy rates in August. Interbank rates typically move up near month-ends on seasonal liquidity demand and edge down at the start of the following month. The PBC cut policy rates (REPO, SLF, MLF) by 10bp in June 23, and cut REPO rate by 10bps and MLF rate by 15bps in August. DR007 averaged 1.94% in 2023 and hovered around 1.8% lately.

Yields edged down as property downturn deepened. 10-year CGB yield rose in early 2023 on China's rapid reopening, trended down since Q2 due to subdued growth momentum, edged up in mid-October on policy relaxation and signs of growth bottoming, but trended down to \~2.5% lately as growth momentum remained subdued. Corporate credit spreads edged down slightly. Issuance of corporate bonds and government bonds was both stronger than a year ago.

Expect additional modest policy rate cut. In 2022, the PBC cut MLF rate by 20bps , 1year LPR by 20bp and 5-year LPR by 35bp. The PBC has lowered the MLF rate further by 25bps in 2023, followed with 20bp cut in 1-year LPR and 10bps cut in 5-year LPR. In addition, commercial banks were asked to lower rates of outstanding mortgages. Even though PBC didn't cut MLF rate as expected in January, we still see another 10-20bps MLF rate cut in 2024. We don’t see more aggressive rate cuts as policy rates are not believed to be the main hurdle by most policy makers. In addition, the depreciation pressure on the RMB and the need to protect bank NIM may also constrain PBC’s policy rate cuts.

## Trade

Exports recorded growth for two months in a row, by 2.3%y/y in December. Imports also returned to 0.2%y/y growth, taking monthly trade balance to \$75.3bn. Taking 2019 as the base, exports and imports grew by 7.2% and 4.9% in December, respectively. Full year exports contracted by 4.6%y/y and imports contracted by 5.5%y/y in 2023, after growing 5.6% and 0.7% in 2022. In real basis, exports and imports expanded by 4.6% and 2.9% in 2023.

Shipment growth to smaller trading partners improved, smart phone boost faded in Dec. Exports to G3 (the US, EU, Japan) registered a slightly wider y/y contraction at 4.9% y/y (vs. prior contraction of 4.7%). Shipment contraction to ASEAN economies narrowed to 6.1% y/y (vs. prior contraction of 7.1%).Export growth to the BRI economies (Brazil, Russia and India) moderated slightly to 15.3% y/y from 17.9% y/y. Exports to other trading partners accelerate significantly to 18.2% from 7.2%. Mobile phone exports swung down sharply to 0.4%y/y contraction in December, from 54.6%y/y growth in November, but the continuing growth in ICs exports and narrowing contraction of PC exports are reflections of tech cycle improvements. Exports of auto and auto parts reaccelerated, that of consumer goods recorded a narrower y/y drag.

A slight trade recovery in 2024. We think the current data combination reflects some improvements in the tech cycle, supporting exporting economies along the supply chain. On the other hand, a strong rebound in the overall external demand momentum is still missing for China. In the near term, we expect export growth to hover around current level while chance of a sharp rebound is still limited. The projected US recession and a high base may place downward pressures on exports in Q2. Nonetheless, for full year 2024, we do expect exports to recover to a small growth of 1.2%, and imports to grow by 1% (see latest forecast update).

## FX Reserves and Capital Flows

 Headline FX reserves rose by \$66.2bn to \$3.24trn over December. China’s FX reserves increased by \$110bn in 2023, declined by \$123bn in 2022, increased by \$34bn in 2021, following a gain of \$109bn in 2020, a gain of \$35bn in 2019 and a loss of \$67bn in 2018.

December's reserve improvement was partly driven by reserve currency valuation effect. We estimate the positive valuation effect from major reserve currencies' movement has likely been around \$23bn due to incremental USD softness at end-Dec vs end-Nov, while the global financial market rally also helped. Wider trade balance has been a contributing factor too. Overall capital outflow pressure likely eased for the month. The balance of total foreign holdings of China's onshore bonds turned positive in Sep-Dec (RMB 492bn in total) after falling for most of 2023, printing full year with a RMB 282bn gain, after a RMB 616bn loss in 2022 (including a sharp loss of RMB 740bn in the 10 consecutive months over Feb-Nov 2022).

We expect the new normal to continue, with larger 2-way capital flows. The rise in capital outflows will mainly reflect non-government entities’ accumulation of overseas assets, especially as controls on capital outflows gradually ease over longer term. Recent sharp drop in FDI into China is partly due to decline in retained profits and high USD costs, but also reflects pressures of decoupling. We expect FDI to recover in 2024-25, but not back to its pre-pandemic levels. Meanwhile, we believe China's continued financial market opening and decent economic growth potential should continue to attract more inflows (see our deep dive).

## Exchange Rate

RMB appreciated to the range of 7.1 by end of December, vs. 7.13 at the end of November; amidst further weakness in the USD DXY index. Meanwhile, the CNY depreciated against the CFETs basket by around 0.8%. Full year RMB weakened by 2% vs USD while strengthened by 1% against the basket in 2023. This follows 9% depreciation vs the USD and 2.4% weakness vs the basket in 2022, and 2.5% appreciation vs the USD and >8% vs the basket in 2021.

RMB exchange rate has been driven by various factors. Resilient trade balance is a supporting factor, while the negative China-US rate differentials and concerns on China's growth momentum slowdown are main headwinds. The PBC lowered RRR for FX deposits by 200bp/100bp in Sep/Apr 2022. The SAFE raised the macro-prudential adjustment parameter for cross-border financing from 1 to 1.25 in Oct 2022, and further to 1.5 in July 2023.

RMB may strengthen to 7.0 by end 2024E. In recent weeks in 2024, the CNY weakened against the USD again amidst rising US yield and fading rate cut expectation.The dynamics of the interest rate differentials, USD index, as well China's growth data, will still bring episodes of volatility ahead. Further monetary easing in China could see USDCNY softening again in Q1. PBC will likely use various tools (including counter-cyclical factor in daily fixing) to prevent rapid episodes of depreciation. Given our expectation of a stabilization of China’s growth momentum, the projection that the US economy will decelerate more notably and fall into recession, as well as the expected Fed interest rate cuts, we see RMB to strengthening a bit to 7.0 by end-2024E.

## Financial Markets

Total industrial profit growth picked up to 29.2%y/y in November from 2.6%y/y before, driven by margin improvement and better revenue growth (up from 2.9%y/y to 7.6%y/y), especially the former. By sector, mining profit narrowed y/y contraction from -17.5%y/y before to -3.3%y/y. ## Fiscal

November fiscal revenue improved, while expenditure softened. Headline fiscal revenue improved to 4.3%y/y in November from 2.6%y/y previously (-1%y/y in Q3). Fiscal expenditure softened to a still robust pace of 8.6%y/y from 11.9%y/y previously (4.1%y/y in Q3). Corporate income tax revenue improved to 9.9%y/y from 0.6%y/y previously, while personal income tax edged up to 0.4%y/y from - 1.4%y/y before. Thus, November recorded a larger-than-usual monthly fiscal deficit of RMB 1 trillion. LG land sales revenue narrowed its decline to -1.5%y/y from -25.4%y/y previously, leading to a YTD decline of -18%y/y.

Efforts are under way to address LG debt problem. July Politburo meeting called for comprehensive resolution plan for implicit LGFV debt risk. Local government issued RMB 1.39 trillion of special refinancing LG bonds to swap for qualified LGFV debt and/or pay arrears to corporate suppliers in H2 2023. In 2024, we expect debt restructuring with banks to remain the main channel to reduce debt risk and financial constraints for local governments. We estimate an additional RMB 2-3 trillion of refinancing LG bonds may be issued. The PBC will likely keep the emergency liquidity facility open for emergency debt payment as well. In the long term, a revamp of central-local fiscal relations and the government's role in the economy will need to be carried out for fiscal sustainability, as highlighted in Central Financial Work Conference.

Modest fiscal support in a more explicit way in 2024. We estimate the Augmented Fiscal Deficit saw a y/y tightening of 1% of GDP in H1 2023, partly due to sharp government revenue shortfall. Issuance of RMB 1 trillion additional CGB (lifting headline fiscal deficit to 3.8% for 2023) may indicate that China is now moving toward supporting the economy with more explicit fiscal support and budgetary expansion, instead of relying more on implicit LGFV debt. We expect the government to set a budget deficit of 3.5-3.8% in 2024 (including potential issuance of new special CGB), raise the special LG bond quota to around RMB 4 trillion, and restrict additional implicit debt cumulation by LGFVs. In total, we expect the AFD to expand modestly by 0.8ppt in 2024E, vs 0.4ppt in 2023E (see key themes & surprises and CEWC takeaways).
