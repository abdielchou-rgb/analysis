# Biweekly Perspectives: Opportunities From Momentum Laggards in Asia/EM

In February, we have observed a broad style rotation away from "Momentum" to "High Beta stocks" in APxJ/EM, while "Large Cap Momentum" continues to lead in Japan and Global equities. This report provides ideas of where we see rising opportunities from the price laggard stock universe.

QUANTWISE

## Key Takeaways

The very different fund flows dynamics in APxJ/EM vs. Japan led to a decoupling of Momentum factor performance in these two markets.

Our model estimates that the Momentum trade in Japan is highly correlated with "US Momentum," "High Japan Beta," and "Large Cap Value" exposures.

In APxJ/EM, however, the Momentum trade is correlated with "Low Market Beta," and neutral on "Value" and "Growth."

To enhance returns via hedging out Momentum exposure, we suggest using "Quality Growth" in APxJ/EM and "Small-Mid Cap Quality Value" in Japan.

Latest market dynamics: Price actions in APxJ/EM have reflected significant reversal basis over the past two weeks, with "Price Momentum" factor losing 4.4%. Realized Momentum factor volatility was highest among major style categories, sinking its risk-adjusted return from the best-performing style in January to the weakest in February MTD. In Japan, Momentum stocks continued to lead the gains, with Large Cap Low Quality Value stocks standing out. Still, its factor volatility also rose quickly and is ranked among the top volatile styles.

Our thoughts: Per our last Asia Quant Biweekly, we suggested trimming Momentum exposure in Asia/EM to manage risk of a potential factor reversal. The reversal is now evolving in APxJ/EM, but not yet in Japan. We continued to observe a high and rising stock correlation among outperformers with declining market breadth. Momentum factor risk is increasing. History suggests a Momentum reversal usually happens first in Asia/EM, followed by the US ( Exhibit 1 ). Thus, we reiterate our stance on controlling risk around Momentum exposure in Asia/EM.

Enhancing return via hedging momentum risk: Our model estimates that Quality style could provide the best diversification against Momentum exposure in both APxJ/EM and Japan. Additionally, if we take the macro backdrop into account, our model suggests overlaying Quality style with "Growth" in APxJ/EM and "Small-Mid Cap Value" in Japan to improve Sharpe ratios. ## Our Recommendations

For investors with horizons of 6 months and beyond, we suggest remaining neutral on Value and Growth in Asia/EM. We recommend looking for outperformers via High Dividend stocks and Quality Growth stocks in Asia/EM, while we expect broader style rotation between Value and Growth to be muted during the early stages of Fed Pivot.

For investors with horizons less than 3 months, we prefer Value and suggest selling into any strength in Momentum stocks. We think the global macro environment in the near term appears likely to keep pricing out Fed rate hike expectations in 2024. The backdrop of a strengthening US Dollar and rising US treasury bond yields might delay the timing of style rotation from Value to Growth in Asia/EM.

For risk management, we believe Momentum factor deserves closer monitoring than Value vs. Growth rotation in the near term. We believe prudent portfolio risk management at this stage is to neutralize Momentum exposure. We also think taking net short exposure on Momentum factor might be a bit early and not warranted on a riskadjusted basis - i.e., its rising factor volatility might drag the Sharpe ratio of a portfolio.

## Macro and Market Dynamics That Matter

The below charts and paragraphs help elaborate our latest views on market dynamics and style recommendations. Our model continues to highlight a high reversal risk of Momentum Factor in the next one to two months. We do not view it as an immediate risk, but believe it would be prudent to start neutralizing portfolio exposure on 12M Price Momentum.

i) The very different fund flow dynamics in APxJ/EM vs. Japan: From a market microstructure perspective, the key driver behind Momentum factor is all about "fund flows." Continuous inflows to or outflows from a market could lead Price Momentum factor to outperform, and the direction of fund flows could determine whether Price Momentum would be correlated with High Beta or Low Beta stocks, respectively.

Looking at the Asia/EM region, Momentum factor was the best performing style in both APxJ/EM and Japan markets. However, Momentum stocks in Japan were correlated with High Beta and Large-cap Value stocks. The correlation with High Beta stocks in Japan was driven mainly by the strong net inflows from foreigners, while the outperformance of Large-cap Value stocks was due mainly to a JPY depreciation environment.

In APxJ/EM, fund flows have been with occasional net outflows. Momentum stocks were correlated with "Low Beta" exposure the most. As overall fund flows in APxJ/EM have been relatively marginal, our model estimates a relatively higher reversal risk of Momentum factor there than that in Japan.

v) For Growth investors, stay with Quality Growth stocks: There are two major clusters within the Growth stock universe - 1) Quality Growth - stocks with high growth and profitable business models, and compounding returns on equity to deliver long-term capital gains to investors, and 2) Low Quality - stocks with loss-making businesses and negative cash flows in the near term, but demonstrating strong top-line growth.

In our quant model classification, the former group could be taken as a compounder strategy for investors looking beyond the policy rate cycle, while the latter is simply long duration exposure and highly sensitive to the policy cycle or even Fed guidance. ( Exhibit 11 )

Since Fed tightening in 2022, underperformance of Low Quality Growth stocks has persisted, while Quality Growth stocks have outperformed the market. We expect this alpha opportunity to continue, as we do not see any signals to trigger an immediate shape pivot of the Fed rate cycle. ( Exhibit 10 )

## Methodology

Factor return calculation: To evaluate historical factor performance, we rank stocks as at month-end within defined universes, and form quintile portfolios based on the underlying factor values. We go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile (i.e., long the top 20th percentile of stocks within the defined universe and short those in the bottom 20th percentile), rebalanced monthly. We then calculate the performance of the time series of this L/S market-neutral portfolio over various time horizons.

Composite formation: To calculate the composite factors, we first z-score the individual factor values at each monthly observation within the defined universe. Individual factor zscores are then averaged into composite scores for each stock on an equal-weighted basis.
