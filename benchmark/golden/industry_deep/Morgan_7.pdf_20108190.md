# China 1H24 CIO Survey: Persistent Negative Impact From Deflation

Despite the YTD macro stabilization in real terms, CIOs are cutting 2024 IT budgets across the board by a sizable margin, resulting in an L-shaped growth outlook. Hampered by the sustained deflation and late cycle nature, enterprises' long-term IT investment confidence continued to sink.

## alphawise α

Our AlphaWise China CIO Survey suggests a 5.4ppt cut for 2024 IT budget with a 3.7ppt downward revision for 2024 growth and a 1.7ppt lower base in 2023 budget final review. The current 2024 expected budget growth of 10% is slightly better than 8% in 2023. The weak enterprise IT investment sentiment spread to software, hardware and IT services, both near term and long term. Software continues to have the highest growth potential, but no rebound for growth rate, while IT services saw the biggest cut in spending. These contrast sharply with the YTD macro economic stabilization in real terms. We continue to believe the nominal macro metrics matter more for enterprise decision making, which is still under pressure. And IT investment is at the late stage of the cycle.

AI overtook digital transformation becoming the top priority for CIOs. This change happened just six months behind US/EU CIOs. Attention and budgets are rapidly shifting towards this area, despite the lack of mature enterprise level product. We see a clear trend that hardware players and public cloud vendors are now demonstrating better momentum than pure LLM players.

Public cloud development is facing big pressure, not only on the lower intention of cloud migration after price cuts, but also the on-going cloud optimization.

ERP continues to demonstrate its resilience given its mission critical nature. This was a key finding in our last survey and was supported by vendors' solid earnings in recent quarters. This trend will continue as CIOs cite ERP as the area least likely to get a budget cut. We also see a meaningful acceleration in ERP localization.

Cybersecurity has a declining focus from CIOs and the budget outlook suggested a sustained market growth slowdown.

Reiterate cautious industry view and continue to recommend two themes: going overseas and market share winners. The survey suggested strong share potential for Kingdee, WPS (Kingsoft's office software), Beisen as well as Huawei and its value chain, and our preferred names are all H shares (all OW): Kingdee (0268.HK), Kingsoft Corp (3888.HK), Beisen (9669.HK) and CSI (0354.HK).

## Key Findings

Top down: China enterprise IT spending growth sustained the L-shaped curve after the 2022 decline, with no rebound.

• Compared with the last survey, there is a 3.7ppt downward revision for 2024 overall IT budget growth rate (from 13% to 10%), from a 1.7ppt lower 2023 base (from 10% to 8%), which translates to a 5.4ppt cut for 2024 spending.

• Downward revisions across the board for software, hardware and IT services. Software as the fastest growing segment, but the 2024 expected growth demonstrating no recovery from the 2023 level. IT services witnessed the biggest cut in percentage terms. IT source trend fades slightly due to lack of incremental projects.

• Both the actual spending growth in 2023 and the expectation for 2024 are still lower than the 2022 covid lock down level, an L-shaped curve.

• Both short-term (2024 potential revision) and long-term IT investment (next 3- year IT investment as % revenue) confidence sink.

• Growth ranking: SOE > POE > MNC, no change vs. previous survey. All three categories are revising down their 2024 IT budgets.

• Resilience of top two: ERP, digital transformation; Bottom two: consulting, IT outsource.

## Revising a few key trends: Localization is solidifying, AI is rising, public cloud is fading.

• AI is the bright spot: AI booked the biggest increase in spending with a massive intention increase, overtaking the long-term No.1 of digital transformation, lagging US/EU CIOs by six months. AI budget takes 5.7% of the IT spending in 2024, almost double vs 2.8% in 2023, though most of them are early stage pilot trials. Analytics and security software continue to see CIOs' highest willingness to pay a price premium for AI features.

• Localization trend intact: Strong momentum for ERP and Office software. The concern on functionality for ERP is dropping while cybersecurity concern is increasing.

• Public cloud is fading away: There is no sign of a pick-up in public cloud migration, though leading cloud vendors cut prices in the past year. Cloud optimization is also happening in China, with a key objective to reduce cost. Almost half of the CIOs have such a plan with only 11% completed. Unlike in the US, the short-term pain is not translating to long-term gain as China CIOs do not plan to allocate a lot more workload to cloud after the optimization.

## Segment dynamics:

• All the key submarkets see a meaningful growth slowdown in 2024 compared with the previous survey. For ERP and OA, CIOs expect some growth rebound in 2024. The outlook for cybersecurity suggests a continuous slowdown going forward.

• Notable share winners in their markets: Kingdee (ERP), Kingsoft (Office), Beisen (HR Software), Huawei (AI, public cloud and data base).

# Detailed implications for Software and IT Services industry

## No Signs of Recovery in Near and Long Term Expectations

Downward revisions continue: According to our 1H24 CIO Survey, the final read for the 2023 IT spending budget was 8.1% growth, representing another 1.7ppt downward revision from our last survey results. Our 2024 IT budget saw a 3.7ppt downward revision to 9.7% with software growth of 10.3% (-2.4ppt) and service growth of 6.2% (-3.6ppt). Although software remains the fastest growing segment, its 2024 growth rate falls to a similar level to 2023. While for the IT services budget, it registered the lowest growth rate of 6.2% and largest downward revision in this survey. Overall, we have seen no signs of improvement from a top-down perspective.

Intention for revision and long-term outlook also declined: Only 46% of the CIOs expect a potential upward revision to the budget this year vs 20% of them expecting a downward revision, resulting in an up-to-down ratio of 2.3x, down further from 3.0x in the previous survey. We believe CIOs have a positive bias on this as the ratio in our survey always stays above 1x, but we have only seen two upward revisions (2H21 and 1H23, and on both occasions the ratio was above 4x). Only 31% of the CIOs expect an increase in IT spending as a % of total revenue in the next 3 years, an all-time low, while 17% expect a decline. The majority of the CIOs expect it to remain stable. The long-term IT investment confidence remains low.

Cancelling, delaying and downsizing IT projects last year will have a lasting negative impact on future spending. 35% of the CIOs placed smaller orders last year in relation to IT procurement and project upgrade, while 33% were unchanged. On average, 17% of the projects last year were impacted by cancellation, postponement or changed order size. When asked about 2024, 91% of the CIOs still expect to see projects getting somewhat negatively impacted with 30% of them expecting to see a greater amount of projects, extremely bearish sentiment in our view.

IT staffing and outsourcing: In terms of IT staff wages, 38% of the CIOs expect wages to stay flat YoY and 43% expect to see an increase. The average growth in wage is 0.3%, a large slide from 2.1% in 2H22, the last time we asked this question. In terms of IT outsourcing, 39% of the CIOs expect to see equal allocation in IT outsourcing and standardized product, while the willingness to increase internal headcounts dropped from 17% last survey to 11%. The total intention to give a discount slightly edged up to 84% from 82% last survey.

## Priority and resilience: AI is the bright spot; ERP remains resilient

AI became the top priority for enterprise spending: AI spending replaced digital transformation as the top priority for IT spending, with a score of 21%, a leap from 9% in the 2H23 survey. AI lagged the US/EU survey by \~6 months to become the top priority, but no doubt AI is the number 1 area of focus for global CIOs.

ERP resiliency continued: ERP continued to rank as the most resilient spending segment within enterprises, with 12% of the CIOs seeing it as the least likely item of spending to be cut. Digital transformation and cloud computing ranked 2nd and 3rd, respectively.

## Focus on AI

AI significance on the rise: According to our 1H24 survey, 46% of the CIOs saw no impact of LLM on their investment priorities in 2023, but this number reduced sharply to 21% when asked about 2024. 51% of the CIOs are expected to at least start pilot AI projects in 2024 vs only 24% in 2023, out of which 27% are expecting to see significant projects coming in. In terms of budget allocations, AI expects to be 5.7% of T spending in 2024, almost double vs 2.8% in 2023. The majority of the AI budget will be allocated to applications and models (41%) followed by hardware (33%) and services (26%).

Who is the best AI/ML vendor? In terms of LLM vendors, Huawei overtook Baidu to become the company with the best product suite in the CIOs' eyes. When asked about the AI/ML share gains, Huawei is expected to see the largest share gain in both 2024 and the next 3 years, followed by Alibaba and then Baidu. Most CIOs still expect hyperscalers to be the primary vendor assisting with their AI adoption, but we are seeing increases in preference for AI model development vendors or in-house development.

Where will AI/ML workloads reside? In the next twelve months, public cloud (36%) is still the most preferred environment for AI workloads, followed by on-premise data center (23%). However in the next 3 years, we see a significant increase in preference for a combination of on-premise and cloud from 4% in 12 months to 26%.

Willingness to pay a price premium: In terms of software product, BI/analytics and security software continue to see the highest willingness to pay a price premium, with the expected premium reaching 9.4%/7.1%, respectively, for the above-mentioned software. The willingness to pay for office software is only 44%, further decreased from the last survey, while the expected premium was 3.5%, edged down from 3.9% in the last survey.

## Software localization

Localization incentives remain stable: The incentives for localization budget remain solid with 67% of the CIOs expecting a higher share of budget allocation to domestic vendors. 29% of the CIOs expect localization at all business units vs 25% in the previous survey. In terms of technology areas, we observe rising interest in substitution in almost all technology areas. BI analytics still ranked as the top area to be substituted (59% of CIOs) although the score declined from the previous survey.

Localization across all enterprise types: By enterprise type, 69% of the SOEs are expecting higher allocation to domestic vendors followed by POEs at 67% and MNCs at 57%. This indicates that localization is happening at a broad level not just for SOEs and government institutions.

## ERP

ERP spending is still finding the bottom: The survey results indicated that ERP spending growth further moderated to 4.9%YoY vs 6.8%YoY in the previous survey, a 1.9ppt decline amid a weak macro environment. Even the mid-term outlook declined from 9.9% in the previous survey to 6.1%. However, we are also aware that the industry mix in this survey has changed slightly with less cyclical industries such as energy and materials, but more tech firms. Cloud migration incentives also slowed with 65% of the CIOs currently without a plan to migrate vs 60% in the last survey.

Localization continued to drive SOE adoption for domestic vendors: Yonyou is the largest ERP vendor among the respondents with a 33% share followed by SAP at 27% and Kingdee at 14%. Out of the ERP vendors, Kingdee and Inspur saw the largest expected share gain of 3% followed by SAP and Workday at 2%. Yonyou and Oracle are expected to lose 3% and 2% share, respectively. By enterprise type, we see a clear trend of SOEs moving away from foreign vendors (SAP and Oracle) and Inspur captured the most share (+3 SOEs). Meanwhile, POEs still prefer SAP the most (5 expected to shift) followed by Kingdee (+1 POE). For MNCs, Oracle and SAP are expected to see the most share gain while Yonyou will see 1 enterprise shifting out.

Performance and functionalities are still the top concerns: Performance and stability is the top concern for CIOs, cited by 27% of them, followed by functionality at 21 and cost to replace at 19% when considering switching to domestic vendors. Interestingly, cybersecurity concern saw the largest increase from the previous survey, cited by 7% more CIOs.

We believe this set of results reads positively for Kingdee with share gain momentum in both domestic POEs and SOEs, while Yonyou seems to see share losses over the next three year horizon.

## Cloud optimization initiatives – Public Cloud

47% of CIOs have plans to optimize public cloud spending. Around 11% have completed public cloud optimization initiatives, while 30% of CIOs have started/are planning to start optimization plans in 2024 for public cloud spending, and 6% plan to start in 2025. More notably, 83% of CIOs have increased efforts to rein in cloud spending today compared to 12 months ago, with 12% highlighting significantly more efforts.

Cost reduction and enhancing cloud utilization are the main reasons for the launch of such initiatives, at 38% and 37% respectively. For public cloud optimization, 83% of CIOs expect to reduce cloud spending through the initiatives, with 41% expecting to cut spending by more than 20%; only 4-5% expect to accelerate public cloud spending. Most CIOs (56%) plan to reduce public cloud spending by optimizing compute and storage costs on the delivery of existing applications and services; Other popular strategies adopted by CIOs are to 1) consolidate cloud spending with fewer vendors, likely hoping to get better vendor discounts with increased volume; and 2) diversify cloud vendors to introduce more price competition in the tender process.

Short-term pain not necessarily for long-term gain for public cloud. While a majority (80%) of CIOs expect to complete cloud migration within 12 months, private cloud remains the most prevalent choice for China's CIOs today and in N3Y, with 40% of workload now residing on-premise (non-cloud) and 31% in private cloud. Only 24% of workloads are on public cloud (including SaaS, PaaS and IaaS). CIOs expected on-premise workloads to decrease to 32% and 24% in 2025 and 2027, and the workloads will be shared by private cloud and public cloud, with private cloud gaining more share (from 31% currently to 37% and 42% in 2025 and 2027). However, one silver lining is that CIOs see a 2 percentage point increase in workloads residing in public cloud after the cloud optimization plans, to 41%, but they also expect it will take on average 4.6 years to achieve the target.

AliCloud and Huawei secured the top 2 vendor positions: 59% of CIOs expect to consolidate vendors for public cloud spending in the next 12 months, while 21% expect to diversify among multiple vendors. In public cloud spending, AliCloud is the most preferred choice for CIOs at 41% in 2024, and remains the top vendor in N3Y at 31%; Huawei Cloud is selected by 25% of CIOs to gain the largest incremental spending in 2024, and increased to 30% in the next 3 years. Overall the survey results look somewhat different from the hyper growth for Chinese SOE telcos, and we believe this is due to different definitions of cloud and sample selection bias (we have very few government entities in our CIO sample).

Read-through to data center demand: We think the recent cloud optimization initiatives for public cloud spending and overall increasing workloads residing in the private cloud in 2027 will introduce near-term and long-term pain for public cloud vendors and IDC operators with substantial exposure to AliCloud and Tencent Cloud.

## Cybersecurity: CIO survey suggests meaningful dip in enterprise spending

Cybersecurity growth outlook in next 3 years ("N3Y") showed meaningful dip. On average, CIOs expect their cybersecurity spending to grow 8.8% for 2024 (down from 9.3% in the 2H23 survey), and 8.5% p.a. for the next three years compared to 11.9% in the last survey. We note a key downside risk to our survey results is that we do not include government demand (accounts for \~30% of the market), and overall the sample is skewed to big enterprises and 50% are SOEs, where demand is usually more resilient than the government segment and SMEs.

No change to the long-term penetration direction, but some weakness compared with previous target. Compared with the narrow 7.7-7.9% range of the N3Y cybersecurity as a % of IT spending target in the past three surveys, the target falls to 7.5% this time, still much higher than the 6.4% expected level in 2024. Of respondents, 70% have an independent cybersecurity budget today, while 81% expect an independent budget in the next three years.

Increase in data security, and traditional products remain in high demand: Data security ranked as the top area to focus on in the next 12 months, named by 56% of respondents, followed by traditional products firewalls, IDS/IDP and Network security, at 44%, 43% and 43% respectively. We believe there is still substantial room for penetration and upgrade requirements for enterprise customers for mature products.

Sangfor has been ranked as the top share gainer since 2H20: Sangfor is the most preferred vendor in the current year, selected by 37% of respondents; NSFocus saw an increase in preference to 13% compared to single-digits in the past 4 surveys. Qi An Xin continues to rank among the top 3 preferred vendors at 12%. In terms of budget allocation, Sangfor is expected to get an average of 27% of budget, followed by Huawe Cloud (7%) and QAX (6%).
