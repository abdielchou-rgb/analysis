## EUR rates under pressure in the near term

The European rates will likely remain under pressure in the next weeks amid stronger global data and uncertainty surrounding the policy rate path. Recent signals from central bank communication and r\* uncertainty, US inflation, US and UK wage data and activity indicators all have fuelled the "higher for longer" narrative. Furthermore, positioning appears to be closer to neutral levels, possibly indicating a lack of clear market direction (Figure 2Positng aper bul d, c  fmRX. ).

The market will likely await more clues from the February inflation report (out on 1 March) and, more importantly, the ECB meeting on March 7, which will provide a new outlook for inflation and economic growth. Nevertheless, EUR rates appear more attractive after recent data and EUR OIS front end repricing, with 10y Bunds currently trading at 2.35%. We anticipate buying interest to increase, if levels moves closer to 2.5% levels, following record January issuance (Figure 9) and with the supply burden gradually receding into the background.

The ECB is focused on developments in wages and profit margins, but it will only get data on compensation per employee and corporate profits for Q4 right after the March ECB meeting. With the likelihood of a Fed cut in March off the table and the ECB following a data-dependent approach, the bar for an April cut from the ECB is now higher. The market is currently pricing in c.11bps of cuts through April, and a cumulative c.112bps of cuts in 2024. The UBS baseline scenario still assumes the first ECB cut in April and 100bps of cuts in 2024. But there is a risk of the cutting cycle starting later, in June. This would naturally keep rates under pressure in the near term. The ECB President Lagarde also warmed this week that the ECB should avoid a hasty rate cut.

But inflation in the Euro area is tracking below the ECB's December macroeconomic projections. According to the Bank of Spain Governor De Gos, "the forecast errors have been very small and even negative in recent quarters," indicating that inflation figures have fallen below projections. Moreover, UBS (and the market) expects the disinflation process to continue in the Euro area, with inflation projected to fall below the 2% target in the summer before experiencing a moderate rebound in the second half of 2024 (Figure 3). Equally, this could support EUR rates over time as inflation continues to fall, setting a ceiling on the level at which rates sell off.

The market's focus next week will be on the February PMIs and the final January HICP release, both scheduled for February 22. The Eurozone Composite PMI rose by 0.3 points to reach a six-month high of 47.9. The ECB GC member Isabel Schnabel mentioned the recent increase in demand, as indicated in the latest PMI survey, as a challenge for the disinflation process.

## BTP Valore supportive for BTP spreads

We anticipate that the demand for the third BTP Valore issue next week will remain high (expected €15-17bn), resulting in tighter BTP spreads approaching the 140bps level. Despite the upward repricing in the ECB policy rate path and the record EGB issuance, BTP spreads continued to remain supported. The 10y BTP-Bund spread, currently at c.150bps, is trading closer to the lower end of the recent trading range.

Nevertheless, BTP spreads still appear cheap when compared to closely correlated risk factors such as the iTraxx Europe Index (Figure 4). We do not anticipate any immediate political event in Italy that could increase pressure on BTPs. Furthermore, UBS credit strategists have recently updated their projections for credit spreads in Europe. They remain optimistic about credit spreads in the first half of 2024 as the disinflation process in the Eurozone continues.

The market's focus next week will be on the third issuance of BTP Valore, scheduled to take place from Monday, February 26, to Friday, March 1. The BTP Valore issuance was a significant factor in supporting BTP spreads last year (Figure 6). The first two issues (issued in June and October of last year) attracted a total of \~€35bn, alleviating the funding pressure on the Italian Treasury to issue more conventional bonds to finance the revised budget deficits.

The third series of BTP Valore will have a 6-year maturity (compared to 4 years in the first issue and 5 years in the second issue), an increasing progression of coupon rates (stepup), and a final bonus equal to 0.7% for investors who hold it until the final maturity. We estimate that the stock of excess household savings in Italy is nearly €100bn higher compared to pre-pandemic levels (Figure 5). This, together with favourable tax treatment compared to other financial assets, would continue to attract buying interest for this retail bond product and support BTP spreads.

Despite the challenging market conditions in January, we saw strong pickup in demand across EGB-10 syndications (Figure 8Demand loctus rEGB-10 yi J24 ). Euro area peripheral countries such as Italy, Spain, Portugal and Greece saw record orders for their syndication books, highlighting the strong market sentiment towards peripheral bonds. EGB syndication’s January order books increased by \~€500bn vs January 2023, while the bid-to-cover ratios have also increased over the same period. Therefore, EGB issuers have front-loaded 2024 bond issuance cashing in favourable investor demand (Figure 7). Italy's syndications accounted for roughly 30% of the overall January syndication from EGBs with record bid of around €234bn (Figure 8). We anticipate that the current market demand will result in retail bids for the BTP Valore issuance this month.

We expect nominal BTP net supply to be nearly € -14bn over the next four weeks. The conventional BTP supply support, combined with expectations of high BTP Valore demand, could be supportive for BTPs in the next few weeks.

## UK: The UK OIS front end could "catch up" with other markets

Last week, we recommended fading the steepening of the SONIA March 24 vs December 24 curve vs EURIBOR (entry: 28bps, current: 24.5bps, target: 0bps, stop: 38bps), driven by valuation factors and the UK's inflation and growth outlook. Just like a roller coaster, the UK rates shifted higher on the back of stronger-than-expected UK wage and US CPI data on Tuesday before rallying on Wednesday amid a softer CPI report. The trade would have been better protected if it had been expressed relative to SOFR, considering the higher beta of UK and US rates.

Nevertheless, we continue to think that the UK front-end could "catch up" further as disinflation accelerates. Following sharp movements this week, the SONIA curve is steeper compared to other curves (Figure 10). Inflation and wage growth outlook in the UK, as well as the volatility of US data, will likely continue to affect UK front-end pricing. But the market has already priced out more than 70bps of cuts since the beginning of the year. After two months of stalling disinflation process, inflation is expected to fall further in the coming months. UBS expects headline and core inflation to fall by 0.4pp each to 3.6% y/y and 4.7% y/y in February.

Further, inflation momentum shows similarities to the trajectories of the Euro area and the US inflation momentum (Figure 11). Additionally, implied policy rates continue to appear restrictive. This was also confirmed more recently by the Chief Economist, Huw Pill. The growth outlook does not look very promising either. The UK Q4 GDP contracted by -0.3% q/q, much weaker than expected (consensus: -0.1% q/q; UBSe: +0.1% q/q; BoE: 0% q/q), confirming a technical recession in the second half of the year. The BoE Governor Bailey downplayed the significance of a potential recession in the fourth quarter of 2024 earlier this week, citing signs of an “upturn” in the economy. UBS economics , however, argue that the downside surprise in Q4 GDP could pose significant risks to the 2024 GDP growth forecasts of 0.6%, which could increasingly shift market's sentiment.

Despite short-term data volatility, we still find the current levels of UK rates attractive. This is due to the strong repricing of BoE policy rate expectations and the softer UK CPI and Q4 24 GDP data. We continue to see value in the 30y Gilt vs France spread (enter: 126bps, current: 128bps; target: 90, stop:140). Next week's focus will be on February PMIs ( out on 22 February), which could provide further insights into the UK's Q1 24 growth outlook.

## CHF: Front-end SARON flatteners remain appealing

SARON March vs December flatteners have been our favourite expression for some time now due to attractive valuations, dovish comments from SNB officials and, more importantly, the Swiss inflation outlook. Following the weaker-than-expected Swiss inflation data this week, the front end of the SARON strip has shifted lower, resulting in a flattening of the March vs December part of the strip. Although market pricing is now closer to our expectations of a 75bps cut from the SNB by year-end, we think that there is still potential for further flattening in this part of the curve.

After the Swiss CPI release this week, the probability of rate cuts in March has significantly increased. This is reflected in the pricing of the March SNB meeting. The market is pricing c.15bps of cuts for the March meeting, up from c.7bps of cuts before the CPI release. Our UBS economists' baseline forecast still assumes that the SNB will start cutting rates in June, followed by two more 25bps cuts in September and December, to a terminal rate of 1%.

This is based on the view that the SNB will want to wait to ensure that domestic price pressures stemming from higher rents no longer pose any upside risk to inflation. With the March Fed and ECB cuts likely off the table, the threshold for a March rate cut from the SNB is higher, in our view. If this scenario materializes, the March cuts could be pushed towards the end of the year, leading to additional flattening in the March vs December curve.

Furthermore, we do not consider the c.73bps of cuts priced by December to be excessive, despite the sharp repricing in policy rate expectations. We cannot rule out a March cut by the SNB. Inflation is expected to decrease further in February to 1.2% y/y and stay comfortably within the SNB's target range of “below 2%” in 2024 and 2025. UBS expects an average inflation rate of 1.4% in both 2024 and 2025. An early March cut from the SNB, along with considerations regarding the Swiss franc appreciation, could pave the way for additional cuts at the upcoming policy meetings, driving December SNB meeting pricing down.
