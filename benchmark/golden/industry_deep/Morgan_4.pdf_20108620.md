Notably, the May employment report showed a 272K rise in nonfarm payrolls (with prior months revised down 15K on net). Average hourly earnings increased 0.4%, taking the yearago increase to 4.1%. Meanwhile, the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.0%, the highest since January 2022, and most other metrics from the household survey were less favorable or outright weak. Over the past 12 months the unemployment rate is up 0.4%-point, and during the same period nonfarm employment growth averaged 230K jobs per month. The confluence of these two facts points to (i) the importance of immigration in boosting trend, or breakeven, job growth, and (ii) the fact that the monthly establishment survey is likely overstating the pace of job growth (see Strong jobs overshadowing rising unemployment, Michael Feroli, 6/7/24).

After the May FOMC meeting, when the u-rate was 3.8%, Chair Powell said “a couple of tenths in the unemployment rate<sup>”</sup> would not be sufficient to ease, as he would need to see “broader<sup>”</sup> weakening. Last month<sup>’</sup>s perky job growth and persistent strength in wage growth are still very far from meeting that broader test, and a July ease now looks highly unlikely. It<sup>’</sup>s possible that the weakness in the household survey broadens out in the three employment reports between now and the September FOMC, but given the recent momentum in job growth, we think it may take the five reports available between now and the November FOMC meeting to meet the test. We now see the first ease in November, followed by a quarterly cadence of rate cuts next year.

At next week<sup>’</sup>s meeting, focus is likely to be on the SEP and dot plot. We think the 2024 median dot will show two eases this year, down from three at the March meeting. This is a close call and could easily go to one if Chair Powell and the leadership are so minded. We think the median continues to look for three eases next year and think the longer-run median dot could move up to 2.75%. At the press conference, we expect Chair Powell will express confidence that the economy is still on the right path and that the FOMC can be patient in gaining confidence that inflation is heading toward two percent (see FOMC Preview, Michael Feroli, 6/7/24).

Following the stronger-than-expected payrolls reports, 10-year Treasury yields are only 9bp lower on the week as the curve bullishly flattened. While the aggressive rise in yields on Friday create a tempting opportunity to reconsider longs, we remain patient on adding back duration exposure after taking profits on our equi-notional 2s/5s flatteners on Thursday.

Pre-FOMC cyclicals suggest yields could rise in coming days and next week<sup>’</sup>s 3- and 10- year note auctions could require greater concessions ahead of CPI and the FOMC meeting on Wednesday. Accordingly, we stay neutral on duration over the near term, but would consider adding duration exposure pre-FOMC if yields retrace toward their late-May highs in the coming days. Separately, we make revisions to our interest rate forecast to reflect a longer Fed on hold (Figure 2). We raise our YE24 10-year yield forecast by 40bp to 4.40% and our year-end 2-year forecast by 45bp to 4.60%. Overall, we project more modest steepening at the long end than our prior forecast, and we project the front end will remain inverted, as policy is expected to remain restrictive even a year from now (see Treasuries).

We similarly exited our Fronts/Greens curve flatteners ahead of payrolls this week and no longer view outright curve positions as attractive. Policy uncertainty is once more on the rise and our analysis shows that a high-for-long expectation is becoming more ingrained in the near term, while uncertainty is migrating further out (Figure 3). Amidst such a backdrop, we recommend well-hedged carry trades with front-end exposure, such as 3M forward 10s/30s back end steepeners with M5/Z5 3M SOFR futures flatteners. We also remain neutral on outright volatility. However we still recommend pairing front end curve flatteners with long volatility in order to create carry-efficient positive convexity positions. We like buying 2Yx5Y swaption straddles on a delta hedged basis versus 6Mx1Y/18Mx1Y flatteners. We maintain our swap spread widening bias in the front end together with a steepening bias on the 7s/20s and 20s/30s swap spread curves. We recommend initiating exposure to the 2s/7s swap spread curve flatteners to position for mispricing along the curve (see Interest Rate Derivatives).

In inflation markets, TIPS breakevens largely reversed last week<sup>’</sup>s widening, alongside lower commodity prices and nominal yields, but remain near the middle of their recent trading ranges and valuations appear fair. In addition to the FOMC meeting, the May CPI is also set to be released Wednesday and we forecast the headline index rose 0.17% while core rose 0.33%, not far from rounding to 0.4%. Within the details of the report, we see OER and rent CPI rising 0.40% and 0.36%, slowing modestly versus recent averages, while we think used cars prices rebounded 1.5% last month. Meanwhile, the market fixing is currently trading at 314.330, which we estimate is implying roughly a 0.26% m/m increase in core, suggesting upside risks to breakevens next week, if our forecast comes to fruition (Figure 4Markets picd fo0.26% n CPI y,lgh bwu ). However, we think there is limited room for breakevens to outperform versus the carry implications of the print, given the hawkish implications for the Fed. Indeed we expect the report to show that core inflation is still running too high for Fed comfort while slowing modestly. Taking into account the changes in our Fed and nominal yield forecasts, we now see less room for narrowing in breakevens in the third quarter, and mark up our forecasts accordingly. However, our forecasts had already incorporated a recovery in breakevens in 4Q, and our targets beyond the upcoming quarter remain largely unchanged (see TIPS).

Separately, this week, the Fed released the results of its March Senior Financial Officer Survey, which surveyed 98 domestic and foreign banks holding over 75% of the total reserve balances in the banking system. Of the respondents, 70% expected the size of their bank<sup>’</sup>s balance sheet to remain roughly unchanged over the subsequent six months. Importantly, a plurality of respondents reported that their bank expects to take actions to maintain its level of reserve balances and HQLA. Furthermore, over 75% of the respondents noted that they prefer to keep “additional reserves<sup>”</sup> above their LCLOR estimates. Importantly, among that group, over half reported that they prefer to keep “additional reserves<sup>”</sup> 11-50% of LCLOR and another 40% noted greater than 50% of LCLOR. Elsewhere in money markets, we have seen a resurgence in non-financial CP issuance, increasing 22% to \$325bn YTD, the highest level of outstandings since before the pandemic, comprised mainly of Tier 1 issuers and mostly from Health Care, Energy, and Consumer companies. Notably issuers have kept a higher CP maturity profile this year, likely against the backdrop of a flatter CP yield curve and as money market funds extend maturity demand (see Short-Term Fixed Income).

Further out the credit curve, high grade corporate credit spreads trended wider over the week alongside the rally in yields, before narrowing on Friday following the robust payrolls print to 102bp. This is a reflection of the strong yield-driven investor base and indeed the nearterm direction of travel will likely be dictated by CPI and the Fed next week. If yields move lower again, we do not think this will accelerate supply; issuance YTD at \$796bn is already 24% above last year at this time, so issuers have not been waiting for lower yields. The effect on demand remains more ambiguous as lower yields have made the hedging math for Eurozone investors less attractive, but positive returns should be a bellweather for increased fund inflows. Taking a step back yields still remain above the 90<sup>th</sup> percentile over the past 10 years, and we think strong demand alongside the seasonal trend for lighter supply this summer should be supportive for spread compression. Turning to liquidity trends, we note that while HG bond trading volumes have declined in recent weeks — which is typical as we approach summer — it is notable that portfolio trading share has risen to all-time highs this past week. We think this reflects both the market<sup>’</sup>s growing comfort with this way of executing and a tight spread environment where investors are particularly focused on managing transaction costs (Figure 5, see Corporates).

Meanwhile high yield spreads widened 6bp to a six-week high as the recent rise in rate volatility has continued, and this was most concentrated up in quality with BB spreads widening 5bp, whereas CCC spreads only widened by 2bp. May was the first month recording no defaults since December 2022, but this comes alongside increased distressed exchange activity. We reduced and raised our 2024 forecast for high yield and leveraged loan default rates by -75bp and +50bp to 2.00% and 3.75%, respectively. Following our rates forecast changes, we now reduce our YE24 HY bond spread forecast by 45bp to 380bp and our total return forecast is unchanged at 6%. We also lower our YE24 LL spread forecast by 50bp to 500bp whereas our return forecast is unchanged at 9%. Meanwhile 2024<sup>’</sup>s institutional loan issuance is proving robust with a total of \$594bn (only 10% non-refi/repricing) issued YTD. Given this backdrop, we revise higher our 2024 gross loan issuance forecast by \$300bn to \$1tn while maintaining our net forecast of \$145bn, which would be the most active year on record for gross (see High Yield).

Similarly US CLO new issue supply of \$25bn in May was the second-highest month ever, though clients recognize that outperforming CLO new origination can only go on so long as leveraged loan raw material creation remains tepid with the majority of issuance related to refi/repricing. Indeed CLO new issue T1 AAAs recently reached our mid-year target of 140bp and we think spreads may reach our YE target of 130bp soon amid strong demand for spread products. However, the very bullish tone in the CLO market is making us concerned that rapid compression to our target is essentially a “Goldilocks<sup>”</sup> outcome and leaves no downside protection on the table given the tighter spreads and higher-for-longer weighing on cost of capital for highly-leveraged borrowers. We noted an increasing interest among investors in US transactions issued to local market investors to the extent they offer value and diversification and comply with EU risk retention requirements (see CLOs)

In contrast to corporates, mortgage spreads continue to display a modest positive sensitivity to rates and vol, tightening alongside the rally in rates and vol before giving up these gains on Friday amid the sharp rates sell-off. We expect that technical factors will keep mortgage spreads positively correlated with rates, and so the upcoming FOMC meeting and CPI report are key factors in how the basis should perform over the next week. Turning to sources of demand, we note money managers remain overweight MBS, despite continued middling performance of the MBS index vs. Treasuries, and are positioned mostly UIC which have performed well in a rangebound rate environment. Conversations with money managers indicate the case for UIC has grown as shorter duration corporate spreads have inched closer to all-time tights (Figure 6 & Figure 7). Fund flows will thus remain a key determinant of demand for MBS, which have seen a recent uptick alongside the recent rally, but whose pace has fallen since the start of the year. We note the wings of the mortgage stack offer a spread pickup vs. 4.5s-5.5s (see Agency MBS).

Agency investor securitization issuance has surged this year, surpassing last year<sup>’</sup>s total with \$1.6bn YTD. As such, we have fielded more questions on relative value in higher coupon investor vs. Jumbo. We note our models show investor 6 cpn PT to have lower ZV than jumbo but higher TOAS due to lower option cost. Within credit we have fielded questions on new Fannie CAS vs. STACR A1s, which are both 3-year bullet-like floaters. We think A1 notes offer value compared to short A/BBB corps but are tighter compared to CLOs and multifamily SASB floaters (Figure 8CRT A1notes frvalu cmpd h/B b ig LO y S). Meanwhile, home prices continue to outperform with the CoreLogic index increasing 1.2% in April, or 3.3% YTD. Housing inventory for sale increased to 1.2mn homes, the highest post-pandemic level but well below anything seen before the pandemic, while sales have also jumped. This level of supply/demand points to mid-single digit home price growth for the year (see RMBS).

ABS spreads remained mostly unchanged as investors work through the flow of new issues, with healthy demand trends seen in subordinate auto paper in primary and secondary. Indeed the bottom BBB rated class D of this week<sup>’</sup>s subprime auto ABS deal was the most heavily oversubscribed tranche and priced 15-20bp inside initial guidance. Thus far in 2024 our indicative BBB subprime auto ABS spread has tightened 85bp to Treasury + 140bp while our BBB-AAA curve has flattened to just 70bp differential from 125bp. While there is limited room for BBB outperformance versus AAAs, we still see value in BBB subprime auto ABS as limited supply and elevated demand should leave deals oversubscribed and this should translate into better bids in secondary (see ABS).

This past week, CMBS spreads showed mixed performance, with longer duration bonds holding better than shorter ones, possibly due to deal fatigue from a surge in shorter format issuances spurred by a rate rally. Indeed, CMBS issuance has seen a resurgence this year, led by growing private label and, in particular the return of Blackstone to the SASB market.

At the same time Agency CMBS issuance has declined as a high-for-longer rate environment subdues acquisition activity. Thus we revise up our 2024 private lebel CMBS forecast from \$62bn to \$85bn, while we lower our 2024 Agency CMBS issuance forecast from \$145bn to \$120bn. Combined, our total 2024 US CMBS forecast is roughly unchanged at \$205bn vs \$207bn before.

CRE fundamentals continue to remain challenged; the May remit period saw a decline in serious delinquencies for private label CMBS to 4.4%, which is likely to prove temporary, as the refi success rate remains relatively low. Meanwhile DQs rose for conduit CMBS last month to 4.76%, and notably about \$650mn of conduit office loans were reported as nonperforming matured for the first time in May, further highlighting how difficult it has become for borrowers to refinance office loans. The CMBS spread rally in recent weeks against worsening fundamentals may look strange but seasoning is playing a large role with seasoned low coupon vintages with high office exposure still trading to large dispersions. New issue that has largely been sanitized of problems that plague pre-QT vintages has benefited the most from the rally across the stack (Figure 9). While the “first AAA loss<sup>”</sup> headlines are reverberating across the CMBS investor base, we note that AAA loss risk is not systemic as distressed SASB AAA bonds are levered to the secularly stagnating segment of CRE and diversified exposure should help (see CMBS).

Turning to emerging markets, we note that a better growth outlook despite the recent stall in momentum and the outperformance of risk versus rate-sensitive assets is providing a distinctive mid-cycle feel. Indeed the strong global macro trend for EM does not look like a great risk/reward strategy to us at the current juncture amid diverging US-global growth and election uncertainty. That said, we think EM provides good idiosyncratic carry in places where default and inflation cycles have abated but risk premia is still being priced in. Harvesting this idiosyncratic carry while remaining hedged against market beta is how we are currently recommending to position within EM. In terms of trading themes, we have OWs in Latam and EMEA EM while UW in Asia. Within EM sovereigns, we stay MW EMBIGD even as spreads grind tighter as US recession risks remain out of sight. We focus our OWs on current yield in select lower rated names. Turning to EM corporates, we also remain MW given CEMBI outperformance and compressed relative spread. We prefer EM Europe and Africa HY for further spread compression and carry trades in Latam. We prefer non-financials over financials and like commodity segments (see Emerging Markets).
