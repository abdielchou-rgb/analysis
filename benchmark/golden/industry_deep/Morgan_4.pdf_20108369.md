# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## May 23

Inflationary impulse in US PMIs sends US rates cheaper; JGBs twist-steepen, lack of sellers at BoJ purchase operations; eurozone PMIs send EGB yields higher; BoK on hold; ECB's Villeroy remains confident in disinflation; USD recovers, equities fall; DXY at 105.04 (+0.1%); US 10y at 4.477% (+5.5bp).

• Treasuries sell off after upside surprise in preliminary May S&P Global Composite PMI, underlying data show inflationary pressures; STIR markets imply less than 1.5 rate cuts this year.

• JGB curve twist-steepen and retrace prior weakness after media reports suggest there was a shortage of sellers at the 1-3y BoJ purchase operation.

• European duration bear-flattens amid strong preliminary May PMIs in euro area, with particular strength in manufacturing; 2y Bunds: +7bp.

• Korea rates and currency (KRW: 0.0%) outperform as the BoK holds inflation forecasts unchanged, with optionality for a rate cut, despite stronger-than-expected 1Q24 growth.

• ECB Governing Council Member Villeroy says the ECB remains “confident in the disinflationary process,” adding they should not over-interpret the data.

• Data which support a higher-for-longer Fed policy rate provide for US dollar recovery and weigh on equities, with nearly 90% of the S&P 500 (-0.74%) lower for the session.

Strong preliminary global May PMIs defined price action across rates, currency, and equity markets on Thursday. US PMIs showed strength in business activity and tight labor markets, which market participants interpreted would translate to a higherfor-longer policy rate. This resulted in a global duration sell-off and fall in US equity prices.

USTs traded range-bound in the overnight session despite strong PMIs in the euro area. In the NY morning, strong economic data sent UST yields higher. S&P Global Manufacturing and Services data surprised to the upside and the Composite PMI increased to 54.4 in May (C: 51.2; P: 51.3), the highest level in over two years and the largest monthly increase since February 2023. The data reflected strong US business activity growth, which signals another solid GDP gain in 2Q24; the improvement was led by the service sector.

Notably, input prices continued to rise sharply and companies sought to pass higher costs onto consumers through higher selling prices. The data showed that inflationary pressures now came from manufacturing rather than services, which is notable given recent deflation in goods. The underlying inflationary pressure from the data release spurred a UST sell-off (2y: +7bp). Short-term interest rate markets repriced higher and removed \~5bp of easing from 2024 to imply less than 1.5 rate cuts this year. Into the NY afternoon, yields stabilized but were unable to shed the weakness from the NY morning.

In equities, a strong earnings report in the artificial intelligence ecosystem initially sent equity futures higher, but gains could not be sustained amid the strong US data. The S&P 500 (-0.74%) closed near session lows, with 89% of index constituents in the red. The upside surprise in PMIs, which market participants interpreted as reinforcement that the Fed will maintain rates higher for longer, sent equities lower; cyclical sectors pared recent gains and exhibited notable weakness.

## Japan:

Early in the Tokyo morning, JGBs bear-steepened driven by supply/demand concerns especially in the super long-end sectors. There was less demand to buy from Japanese real money market participants, even though 30y JGB yields breached 2%. The BoJ announced the size of JGB purchase operations for the 1-3y, 3-5y, and 5-10y sectors would be unchanged. While sectors less than 10y stabilized, the long end continued to sell off sharply into the Tokyo afternoon.

After media reports suggested there was a lack of sellers for the 1-3y BoJ purchase operations, the front end rallied modestly. It appears to us that banks had already divested their short-end JGB positions and have been hesitant to further reduce exposure. Accordingly, the long end also retraced the initial sell-off. By the end of the Tokyo session, the JGB curve twist-steepened; 2y JGBs rallied 1.0bp d/d to 0.335% while 30y JGBs sold off 2.0bp d/d to 2.160% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve twist-steepened and outperformed JGBs on the 30y+ sector. XCCY basis tightened, led by the long end.

USD/JPY ground higher toward 156.80 in response to the hawkish FOMC minutes overnight on Wednesday. During the Tokyo morning session, USD/JPY traded choppily \~156.7. After the BoJ announced purchase operation sizes would be unchanged, USD/JPY modestly accelerated its rise and reached \~156.90, before a gradual move lower into the London morning without clear catalysts.

On Thursday, the PBoC set the USD/CNY fixing at the highest level since January, and the US dollar moved higher instantaneously. During the London morning, EUR/ JPY surged sharply toward 170.00 in response to strong eurozone and German PMI.

Other CCY/JPY pairs moved in tandem with EUR/JPY. USD/JPY rose again from 156.57 toward 156.80.

## Europe:

EUR/GBP gained after Eurozone PMIs were mostly stronger-than-expected, combined with a miss in the UK composite and services PMIs. The cross gave up initial gains after the US PMIs were stronger-than-expected and closed almost flat. NOK strengthened broadly, reaching its highest level versus EUR and USD since mid-March. NOK/SEK (0.3%) broke above 1.0050 during NY trading hours.

European duration continued to trade heavily on Thursday. In the London morning, strong preliminary May PMIs in Europe, especially manufacturing PMIs, weighed on Bunds despite weakness in prices charged. PMIs for prices charged in services were down in May, at the lowest level since October 2023; inflation fixings closed \~1bp lower (for more on euro area PMIs, read here). Into the London afternoon, initially duration partially recovered, but economic data releases in the US: below-consensus initial jobless claims and strong PMIs drove another leg of the sell-off. 10y Bunds cheapened 6bp to 2.6%; 2s10s bear-flattened \~1bp and 10s30s flattened \~2bp on the German curve. In money markets, reds underperformed and around half of a 25bp cut was priced out by the end of 2025, in sympathy with OIS-implied Fed policy pricing. ASW continued to trade with duration and cheapened, led by Buxl (- 2bp). EGB spreads were range-bound and closed the London session little changed.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar reversed overnight losses after an upside surprise in S&P Global PMI, which pointed to robust growth momentum and increased price pressure in goods. The DXY USD Index (+0.1%) closed above 105.00. CAD underperformed in G10 (USD/CAD: +0.3%) as front-end yield differentials widened (US: +6bp, CA: +2bp).

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies exhibited mixed moves despite hawkish undertones from the FOMC minutes released overnight. USD/CNH edged higher by 0.05% to 7.2575 after the USD/CNY fixing was increased to 7.1098, the highest since late January. TWD, SGD, and KRW outperformed in the region. TWD led the gains, up 0.1% to 32.2 per USD as strong earnings in the artificial intelligence ecosystem drew US\$551mn of foreign equity inflows and led to outperformance of Taiwan equities (TAIEX: +0.3%). Initially, KRW fell 0.3% to \~1,368, but pared losses to finish the AxJ session 0.04% higher, after the Korean government announced a US\$19bn incentive package to support its chip sector, more than double the amount initially discussed (US\$7.3bn). ASEAN currencies underperformed led by THB; USD/THB rose 0.2% to 36.53 as gold prices declined. MYR and PHP fell 0.1%. India and Indonesia markets were closed for local holidays.

Most AxJ rates rallied slightly despite the overnight sell-off in USTs. Korea rates were the main outperformer as the BoK lacked a more hawkish tone despite stronger-than-expected 1Q24 growth; KRW NDIRS yields fell 1-2bp across the curve. China rates also outperformed alongside the underperformance of Chinese equities;

CNY NDIRS and CGB yields fell \~1bp across the curve. Singapore and Malaysia rates underperformed. In Singapore, 2y swap yields rose 2bp and the 2s10s curve bearflattened by a similar amount amid higher-than-expected final 1Q24 GDP and in-line April inflation. In Thailand, the NDOIS curve was stable while 10y ThaiGB sold off 3bp.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• ECB Vice President de Guindos explained “we are taking a prudent approach [regarding the June meeting decision], which would argue in favor of a reduction of 25 basis points.” He added “there is a huge degree of

uncertainty. We have made no decisions on the number of interest rate cuts or on their size. We will see how economic data evolve”

• ECB Governing Council Member Villeroy commented on wage data, arguing there was a “German exception” due to one-offs, while wages decelerated significantly in other countries. He said “we should not over-interpret [the data], we keep confident in the disinflationary process”

## Emerging Markets

• BoK kept its policy rate unchanged at 3.5% with a unanimous vote as widely expected. The central bank raised its 2024 GDP growth forecast to 2.5% (P: 2.1%) but cut its 2025 GDP growth forecast to 2.1% (P: 2.3%) and held its inflation forecasts unchanged. BoK Governor Rhee revealed one member was open to a 25bp rate cut in the next three months, unchanged from the last meeting. He added the timing for a rate cut has become more uncertain with increased upside risks to inflation, but the likelihood of a rate hike is currently limited and rate cuts could still be considered in 2H24 if inflation trends slow to the lower 2% range. Our economists continue to expect the first cut in October 2024

• CBT maintained its One-Week Repo Rate at 50% and retained a tightening bias in line with expectations. This was followed by broad-based hikes in required reserve ratios and the introduction of a new monthly growth cap on FX loans, which should support the transmission of the policy rate to the interbank rate and to broader financial conditions (for more, read here)

• CBE maintained its Deposit Rate at 27.25% (C: 27.25%) at its May meeting

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
