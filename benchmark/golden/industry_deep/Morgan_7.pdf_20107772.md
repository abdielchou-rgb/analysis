# Global Macro Commentary | Global

## April 23

Broad USD weakness ensues post-US PMIs; Tsys twist-steepen, record sized 2y auction comes 0.6bp through; ECB's Nagel talks data-dependent June cut; EUR, Bund yields rise; strong UK PMIs lift GBP, gilt yields; LatAm currencies gain amid USD softness; DXY at 105.69 (-0.4%); US 10y at 4.600% (-0.8bp).

• Broad US dollar weakness ensues after preliminary April S&P Global US PMIs are surprisingly soft; the DXY USD Index slips 0.4%, below the 106 level, to session lows.

• Treasuries rally after the S&P Global Manufacturing PMI dips just into contraction territory; the curve twist-steepens as front-end strength persists after the \$69bn 2y auction comes 0.6bp through.

• ECB Governing Council Member Nagel wants rate cuts to be conditional on the data being so reliable that the ECB can move in June, as he notes signs of an acceleration in German manufacturing.

• EUR (+0.4%) advances and Bund yields rise following Nagel's remarks and strong PMI data.

• Strong preliminary April S&P Global UK PMIs support cable while UK gilts sell off mildly.

• Most LatAm currencies benefit from USD weakness; MXN (+1.0%), BRL (+0.7%), and COP (+0.2%).

## Developed Markets

## United States:

Divergent preliminary April S&P Global PMIs shaped price action across rates and currency markets and was a tailwind for global equities. EUR (+0.4%) advanced and Bund yields rose after particular strength in the French PMIs but also in Germany and the Euro Area. These moves were aided by comments from ECB Governing Council Member Nagel who advocated for rate cuts to be conditional on the data being so reliable the ECB can move in June, as he has observed signs of an acceleration in German manufacturing. Strong preliminary April S&P Global UK PMIs supported cable while UK gilts sold off mildly. Softer-than-expected US PMIs translated to sharp US dollar weakness as the DXY USD Index (-0.4%) settled at session lows. ## Japan:

JGBs sold off, particularly in the super long-end. The 2y JGB auction was largely in line with market pricing at 99.985 and was digested smoothly, supported by demand to square short positions. Despite a lack of major macro catalysts, JGBs bear-steepened largely due underperformance of the super long-end; 20y JGBs sold off by 1.5bp d/d to 1.66% on a simple yield basis, while 5y JGBs closed the Tokyo session at 0.49% on a simple yield basis. TONA OIS bear-steepened alongside JGBs, and XCCY basis continued to tighten marginally.

Throughout Tokyo trading hours, USD/JPY traded range-bound \~154.80, as market participants remained concerned about potential FX intervention. Japanese Finance Minister Suzuki suggested they have already prepared for a potential intervention, and this drove USD/JPY lower to 154.70. However, the downside was very limited. EUR outperformed from the London session following strong French PMIs, led by services; EUR/JPY broke above the 165.00 level. Strong UK PMIs also buoyed GBP.

## Europe:

Preliminary April PMIs dictated price action in the G10. EUR (+0.5%) rallied alongside strong euro area PMIs, with strength in Services. Front-end pricing diverged as US PMIs were milder than expected. GBP (+0.8%) gained against the US dollar as market participants pushed out pricing of BoE rate cuts; risk-on sentiment also boosted GBP. Both NOK and SEK rallied by 0.7%. Safe-havens were little changed given their negative correlation to risk; JPY and CHF gained 0.1%. The DXY USD Index ended the London session 0.4% weaker.

In the London morning, European PMIs surprised to the upside, led by services, and this weighed on European duration. In the London afternoon, US PMIs came in weak, and this led to a rally in Treasuries. Overall, 10y Bunds sold off 2bp to 2.50% and underperformed Treasuries by \~3bp. In swaps, 10s30s steepened by 1bp, while 2s10s was little changed. Risk assets continued to trade well and the 10y BTP-Bund spread edged \~1bp tighter. Core ASW cheapened, led by Schatz ASW (-0.8bp).

## Dollar Bloc:

The risk-on tone supported the Antipodean currencies, AUD (+0.6%) and NZD (+0.4%), while CAD (+0.3%) tailed its dollar bloc peers.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies were mixed and traded in a narrow range, as regional equities largely printed in the green. USD/CNY hovered \~7.2466, near the upper bound of the 2% limit from the PBoC fixing. CNH underperformed and weakened 0.1% to USD/CNH 7.2590; offshore yuan liquidity eased sharply, 1m CNH HIBOR plunged 52bp to 4.58%. THB also underperformed; USD/THB rose 0.2% to 37.06 alongside weakness in gold prices. TWD outperformed; USD/TWD fell 0.2% to 32.60, aided by outperformance of local equities (TAIEX: +1.0%) and foreign equity inflows of US \$46mn. PHP also benefited from a rally in local equities (PSEi: +1.0%), 0.1% stronger to USD/PHP 57.51. KRW gained 0.1% to USD/KRW 1,378 after Korean PPI rose for the fourth consecutive month in March. IDR gained 0.1% to USD/IDR 16,220 ahead of the BI decision.

Most AxJ rates rallied and bull-flattened on Tuesday. In China, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-flattened by 1bp, Both 5y and 10y CGB yields fell 2bp, after China’s finance ministry called for the PBoC to increase trading of government bonds in OMO. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-flattened by 1bp and the KTB curve shifted 2bp lower. In India, 10y G-sec yields fell 2bp to 7.17% while 2y yields rose 1bp; the INR NDOIS curve shifted 2bp lower. In Thailand, the 2s10s NDOIS curve bullflattened by 2bp, while ThaiGB yields remained stable. Hong Kong and Singapore rates led regional gains, given their high correlation to USTs. The Hong Kong 2s10s swap curve bull-steepened by 1bp while the Singapore swap curve bull-flattened by 3bp. Indonesia was the exception; 1s10s twist-steepened by 6bp, 10y INDOGBs sold off by 2bp and 1y yields fell 4bp.

## Latin America:

Most LatAm currencies were stronger, driven by a weaker USD. MXN (+1.0%), BRL (+0.7%), and COP (+0.2%) all benefited amid few local drivers. CLP (-0.1%) was largely flat and reversed gains of as much as \~0.7% earlier in the session. LatAm rates were mixed; TIIEs and DIs underperformed and sold off by 1-2bp across the curve, while front-end CAM outperformed (1y: -10bp) with no local driver.

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
