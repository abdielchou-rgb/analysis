## August 23

Fed Chair Powell says "the time has come" to adjust rates; BoJ Governor Ueda maintains his hawkish stance; USTs bull-steepen; gilts rally alongside USTs; USD tumbles while equities advance; MXN and ZAR lead EM gains; DXY at 100.68 (-0.8%); US 10y at 3.799% (-5.3bp).

## Developed Markets

## United States:

USTs bull-steepened, the USD tumbled, and equities rallied after Fed Chair Powell’s remarks at the Jackson Hole Economic Symposium appeared unabashedly dovish and further cemented market participants' belief that the Fed will cut rates in September.

Friday’s session was met with two key speeches from policymakers. First, BoJ Governor Ueda spoke at the Diet. Market participants were curious to see whether he would maintain his hawkish stance from the July monetary policy meeting or offer more dovish remarks similar to those of BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida in early August, where he said the BoJ would not hike rates if markets were in a state of turmoil. Ultimately, Ueda’s remarks leaned hawkish as he reiterated that the BoJ would hike rates dependent on future data. JPY weakened following the remarks, but JGB activity was limited as the potential for additional BoJ hikes had already been priced in ahead of Ueda’s remarks.

The second closely watched speech was from Fed Chair Powell at the Jackson Hole Economic Symposium. The remarks were overwhelmingly dovish as Powell said “the time has come for policy to adjust.” His speech made clear that the balance of risks has shifted toward the labor market, as he flagged that it has cooled considerably and that the market is unlikely to be a source of elevated inflationary pressures. He went on to say that the Fed does not “seek or welcome further cooling in labor market conditions.”

While Powell did not comment on the size or pace of the cutting cycle, saying it will be dependent on “incoming data, the evolving outlook, and the balance of risks,” there was language which some market participants viewed as leaving the door open for a 50bp cut in September. One of the more dovish remarks was that the current level of policy rate gives the Fed "ample room to respond to any risks.” Some interpreted the word “ample” as setting a relatively low bar for a 50bp cut at an upcoming meeting.

Following the dovish remarks, USTs immediately bull-steepened and largely maintained the gains for the remainder of the day. The 2s20s curve steepened by \~7bp as 2y yields fell by \~10bp. USD tumbled, with the DXY USD Index down -0.8% as it reached it lowest level in over a year. STIR repriced lower and implied over 100bp of cuts by year-end, with over a 35% chance of a 50bp cut in September.

The Jackson Hole Economic Symposium benefited risk assets as well, as it solidified the view for Fed easing. The S&P 500 gained 1.2%, led by industries that benefit from lower rates. All sectors gained on the day, but there was particular strength in real estate, consumer discretionary, and technology stocks. Brent crude oil futures gained 2.3%.

## Japan:

JGB yields traded higher in the Tokyo morning along with the sell-off in USTs. The main focus on Friday was BoJ Governor Ueda’s remark at the special Diet sessions, the first at the House of Representatives in the Tokyo morning and the second at the House of Councilors in the Tokyo afternoon. Ueda’s remarks were relatively hawkish compared to Deputy Governor Uchida’s dovish speech on August 7. Ueda reiterated the BoJ’s rate stance depends on data developments. That said, the activity in JGBs was relatively muted, as JGB futures had already sold off to some extent overnight. In the Tokyo afternoon, JGBs initially sold off on broad sectors, particularly in the 10y sector, but retraced some of the weakness into Tokyo close. The JGB curve ultimately bear-flattened; 2y JGB yields sold off 1.0bp to 0.365% while 10y JGB yields sold off 2.0bp to 0.89% on a simple yield basis. Super long sectors traded relatively firm on the curve, and 30y JGB yields were unchanged at 2.06%. TONA-OIS curve bear-flattened and underperformed JGBs except in the 10y sector. XCCY basis were mixed; while the 10y and 20y sectors widened, the 5y sector continued to tighten.

USD/JPY declined to \~145.6 on the back of Ueda’s lower house testimony. His tone was relatively hawkish compared to Uchida’s dovish speech on August 7. There seemed to be no new catalysts at the upper house testimony, and USD/JPY stayed at \~145.7 into the London morning. It rose to \~146 in the London morning ahead of Fed Chair Powell’s remarks at the Jackson Hole Economic Symposium.

## Europe:

Price action in European FX was driven by the USD weakness following dovish remarks from Fed Chair Powell. EUR (+0.7%) hit a new YTD high and traded at levels last seen in July 2023, while GBP (+0.9%) reached its highest level since March 2022. CHF’s gains were slightly more modest (USD/CHF: -0.5%) as stocks rallied after Powell's comments.

Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar weakened sharply (DXY USD Index: -0.8%) after Powell said “the time has come for policy to adjust,” while emphasizing that developments are more of a concern than before. The DXY USD Index found support at 100.60. The decline was broad-based. NZD (+1.5%) outperformed against the US dollar.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies gained broadly following the further decline in the US manufacturing PMI and the rally in JPY due to Ueda’s relatively hawkish speech on Friday. CNY bounced back to 7.1370 per USD amid a rally in local equities (CSI 300: +0.5%). IDR advanced relative to regional peers; USD/IDR weakened 0.7% to 15,490, almost a full reversal of its gains from Thursday. Other AxJ peers strengthened in a narrow range, as markets awaited Powell's speech at the Jackson Hole symposium. KRW gained 0.4% to 1,337 per USD despite its local equities ending relatively unchanged and a sell-off in local bond markets. Both THB and SGD appreciated 0.2% to 34.27 and 1.308, respectively. MYR, INR, and TWD gained slightly with limited macro catalysts.

AxJ rates trade mixed. In China, the 2s10s CGB yield curve bull-steepened 5bp; 2y CGB yields fell 5bp to 1.57%, while 10y CGB yields were unchanged at 2.15% given that the new 10y CGB auctioned for CNY 130bn on Friday was met with a relatively low bid-to-cover ratio of 2.5x. Meanwhile, the CNY NDIRS curve remained steady. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve shifted up by 1bp while the 3s10s KTB yield curve bear-flattened 2bp; 3y KTB sold off 5bp to 2.96% and 10y KTB yields rose 3bp to 3.01%. Hong Kong’s 2s10s swap curve bear-flattened 2bp following the overnight moves in the UST curve. Singapore swap rates rose 1bp across the curve. In India, both G-sec yields and INR NDOIS remained largely stable. In Indonesia, short-term 1y IndoGB yields fell 4bp to 6.29% while long-term yields held steady.

## CEEMEA:

Fed Chair Powell’s comment at the Jackson Hole conference caused a rally in CEEMEA rates and the US dollar to weaken against the regional currencies. EUR/ PLN was lower by 0.3% and ZAR appreciated by 1.7% after market participants took Powell's comments to be dovish as he signaled a shift in focus toward the weakening labor market. South Africa rates rallied across the curve with an average move of \~-6bp. Yields in CEE3 were quiet. The primary focus in the week ahead is the NBH meeting on Tuesday. Our economists expect central bank members will exercise caution and hold rates steady at 6.75% as inflation has recently surprised to the upside against the central bank’s forecast. Our economists expect the next rate decision will be a 25bp cut in October.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• BoJ Governor Ueda spoke at a special Diet session. In contrast to some market participants’ expectations, his remarks were relatively more hawkish than Deputy Governor Uchida’s comments on August 7. He reiterated the Bank’s stance that a future rate hike depends on data developments ahead. Ueda also emphasized that the current real neutral rate is deeply negative (for more, read here)

He also said that policymakers "do not seek or welcome further cooling in labor market conditions." Powell did not commit to a particular pace of rate cuts as the Fed remains data dependent

• Also at Jackson Hole, Atlanta Fed President Bostic said "it may make sense" to pull forward his view on rate cuts

• BoE Governor Bailey reiterated that monetary policy needs to stay restrictive. He mentioned that the persistence of inflation is likely to decline, but risks remain

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
