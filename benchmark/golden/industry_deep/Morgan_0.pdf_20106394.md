Inflation is the most important macro variable. We think it continues to moderate, allowing policy rates to fall rapidly towards neutral. While don’t see the first Fed/ECB cut until June, the direction of travel, rather than the precise starting point, is more important to credit, in our view.

## Asia Credit Singapore and Hong Kong Marketing Client Feedback

In January, we met with at least 50 clients in Singapore and Hong Kong to share our thoughts on our 2024 Asia credit outlook and themes. The clients we met were broad-based, including asset manager (AM), bank treasury, insurance and private bank accounts.

In short, we felt banking clients have pressure to put cash to work, while sentiment for the rest of the account types was lukewarm. We believe there are a number of key takeaways worth highlighting:

• Chinese banks have strong investment needs: Adding bond redemptions (their portfolios are short-dated), coupons and a growing USD investment book, Chinese banks need to deploy cash in the near term. Given the negative net supply in USD China credit, banks have no choice but to look at non-China USD credits to fulfill their investment needs.

• Client sentiment and activities felt lukewarm: Clients felt disincentivised to act at the start of the year due to three key factors: 1) Outflows pressure reduced the investment need; 2) A tight Asia credit spread makes it hard to trade the market; and 3) Lack of supply makes it hard for them to make adjustments to their portfolios.

• Insurance accounts had been struggling to add long duration risk: Given the lack of long duration supply in Asia credit, insurance accounts are using US IG and GCC IG to fulfill their long duration need.

• Japan and Australia credit are becoming a key focus for AMs: Compared to our September 2023 marketing, we now have many more AM accounts that are expanding their investment horizon into Japan and Australia credit. More AMs are telling us that they have coverage for Japan and Australia now and are very willing to look at credit from this space.

• Clients expects inflows to return once the Fed starts cutting rates: Although outflows had slowed down, AMs still face lingering outflow pressures, which are offset by the current negative net supply.

## Firmly in Stage 2 of Our Asia Credit Normalisation Framework

We believe the current technical backdrop is consistent with stage 2 of our Asia credit normalisation framework. In stage 2, we expect net supply to remain low but outflows are stabilising. We see Asia credit technicals moving into stage 3 of our framework as we approach 2H24, due to our expectation of the Fed starting its rate cut cycle in June.
