# AI: Adopters Are (Still) Adopting

For every transcript mentioning "AI" since ChatGPT was launched, the market cap of global AI Enabler stocks has increased by over \$400m. Trillions of dollars hinge on continued consumer and enterprise adoption. As market volatility rises, our new adoption tracker provides a reassuring signal.

There are currently three high-level debates on AI:

1. Are valuations reasonable or not? - Here, our top-down strategists' views are more bearish than our analysts' bottom-up views.

2. What is the ROI on this theme? - Here, the hyperscalers see more risk of underinvesting than overinvesting in this early secular theme.

3. Have we hit a demand air-pocket? – Our new Adoption tracker in this report indicates we have not.

As we explained in our original AI Adoption report, no single metric can adequately measure where we are in the adoption trajectory. However, the table below and the supporting detail in this report go some way to show that, while equity markets have sharply de-rated the Enablers in recent days and weeks, the AI Adopters are still adopting. Consumer AI use and enterprise experimentation remain robust for the time being.

Within the Adopters, we continue to favour those with high pricing power. On consensus EBITDA, valuation multiples and returns, these names have outperformed Adopters with low pricing power by over 20% since ChatGPT was released.

## Consumer Adoption

As we explained in our original Adopters are Adopting report, there is no one catch-all metric for tracking enterprise or consumer engagement with AI tools. We favour a combination of the various metrics in this report to track the progress of AI adoption:

## (1) Search interest momentum

There have been 35bn visits to ChatGPT since it launched in November 2022, 14.4 billion of those visits have been in 2024 alone, according to SimilarWeb. Unique visitors to ChatGPT as well as leading alternative web-versions of consumer-facing chatbots are rising steadily through 250m per month. There have been >250 million mobile app downloads of both the free and paid versions of the leading consumer-facing bots. If we look just at Google search interest in the current most popular platforms, ChatGPT continues to dominate mind- and search-share. In aggregate, search interest in ChatGPT is up 11% and 67% YTD and YoY respectively, albeit with broadening competition and share gain by other platforms. If we include all major consumer-facing LLMs, this rises to 28% and 89% respectively.

In summary – whilst clearly (and increasingly) seasonal around summer and christmas breaks – the product is proving to be sticky with consumers.

## Caveats to the above data include the following:

• These data include universities, students and individuals experimenting with AI models. This can give an overinflated view of enterprise adoption.

• The ebbs and flows between open- and closed-model preference by enterprise adopters (due to cost or quality of foundational models) may give a false impression of speed of adoption ( Exhibit 23 ).

• There will be a long tail of models that will see very few downloads and are therefore noise rather than signal when it comes to the true picture of Adoption.

• By only using the data from one vendor – Hugging Face – growth in models listed will also reflect their own market share gain or loss as a variable not easily extractable from these data.

• For inference/hyperscaler stocks (rather than GPU players) it could be a false or misleading correlation. The vast majority of what makes up cloud generated AI revenues is inference against the existing models; nothing to do with downloads from Hugging Face.

Exhibit 23: Open source model quality converging with closed models (5-shot MMLU Performance)

On this topic of efficiency gains claimed by enterprise, again catch-all statistics are challenging or nuanced and so should be viewed with caution. However, one metric may be worth continuing to watch as a proxy for companies' early aggregate success in deploying generative AI.

The Customer Service function is routinely cited by companies – particularly financials and consumer companies – as the lowest-hanging fruit for AI adoption and thus theoretically offering positive AI ROI soonest. It is hard to disaggregate what is AI's impact versus a long-standing push across many industries to reduce the amount of costly volume

through their call centres. The number of people globally searching for "Customer Service" or "Customer Phone Number" has rolled over in the last couple of years after a decade or more of structural growth. This has had a material effect on the largest outsourced customer service stocks, such as Teleperformance (covered by Remi Grenu).

The YoY growth figure of individuals (across geographies and sectors) searching for customer service phone numbers is now falling 10% YoY. As stated above, this is hard to pin down to AI specifically versus structural digitalisation. It is, however, a notable leg down since ChatGPT and other foundational models (on which customer services tools can be personalised) were launched.

## (6) Census Bureau

The Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS) provides insight into the state of the US economy by providing timely data for key economic measures and business expectations about future conditions. By providing detailed geographic and subsector data, BTOS provides local, state, and federal officials near real-time data for policy and decisionmaking.

The BTOS sample consists of approximately 1.2 million businesses split into 6 panels (approximately 200,000 cases per panel). Businesses in each panel will be asked to report once every 12 weeks for a year. Data collection will occur every two weeks. The BTOS is comprised of a set of core questions and supplemental content, which is included as needed.

Since late 2023, businesses across the US – a far broader sample than most of our analysis typically focussing on larger/listed equity market adoption – have been asked for their current investment in AI and future expected usage. The aggregate figures have been grinding higher very gradually since late-2023 from 4% to c5%, but which across the whole dataset implies an incremental 12,000 businesses adopting AI. Ambitions to adopt over the coming 6 months are higher still at >7% of all businesses, but which have not yet been matched by actual adoption over the history of the survey.
