# US Economic Perspectives June CPI Preview: Another moderate increase

## Headline CPI: UBSe US Econ 0.03%, UBS Nowcast 0.11%, consensus 0.08%

 Next Thursday's (July 11) CPI release is likely to show a second month with a soft headline CPI change following strong increases in the first four months of this year. We project a 3bps increase for the June CPI (seasonally-adjusted), which is a little below the current Bloomberg consensus average forecast (8bp) and the projection from our Nowcasting models (11bp).

 A 4% decline in gasoline prices is the largest single factor holding down the June CPI, though increases in food (+21bp) and core prices (+17bp) are also projected to be relatively moderate.

 Headline CPI inflation is expected to fall from 3.3% in the 12-months through May to 3.1% in the 12-months through June.

 The projected not-seasonally-adjusted core CPI level, at 314.525, is essentially in line with current CPI swaps fixings.

## Core CPI: UBSe US Econ 0.16%, UBS Nowcast 0.20%, consensus 0.23%

 The 16bp seasonally-adjusted core CPI increase that we project for June would be similar to last month's 16bp increase and not far from both the June increase last year (19bp) and the average monthly increase from January 2017 to December 2019 prior to the pandemic (17bp). Our projection is also a little below the current Bloomberg consensus average of 23bp.

Like May, the June change should be held down by around 4bp more than likely appropriate by seasonal factors that have swung around during the pandemic. On a not-seasonally-adjusted basis we project a June core CPI increase that is slightly above the range seen in the June prints from 2016 to 2019. (Figure 9)

 With the core CPI increase in June expected to be similar to June of last year, 12- month core CPI inflation edges down only 2bp from 3.42% through May to 3.40% through June in our projection.

## Key components: OER, used cars slowing; core non-rent services stronger

 Compared to the May core CPI, the largest downward push comes from used vehicle prices, which return to a notable decline after increasing last month. The weaker used vehicle prices contribute 5bp less to the core CPI increase in June than in May.

 We also expect owners' equivalent rent (OER) to slow to a mid-30bp increase in line with the projections from our models based on a pass-through from new tenants' rents.

On the upside, we expect notably more strength in core non-rent services prices in June (+25bp) than in May (-4bp). The May CPI showed surprising weakness in prices for transportation services (particularly vehicle insurance) but also for recreational services and various personal services. We expect the weakness in these components lessen, but there is little high-frequency and leading indicator data for many of these non-rent services prices to help guide our near-term projection.

 A full category roundup of our projection is below in Figure 22 and additional details are available in the US inflation forecast spreadsheet on our website.

## Risks and signals: OER & transportation biggest drivers of uncertainty

 We feel the range of uncertainty remains elevated, though there is no clear skew in the distribution of that uncertainty. We would not be surprised to see a core CPI increase anywhere from 7bp to 27bp.

OER: We project OER to slow from a 43bp increase in May to a 34bp increase in June. However, we have expected OER to slow before and have been surprised by its resilience. If we are wrong, and OER again increases about 43bp, it would add 3bp to the June core CPI increase.

Transportation services: We forecast the transportation services CPI to increase 47bp in June, about ⅔rds of the way back from the 50bp decline in May to the 98bp average increase from November to April. However, there is considerable uncertainty surround this category and either another 50bp decrease (which would subtract 8bp from our current core CPI projection) or a bounce back to a 1% increase in June (which would add 4bps) is possible.

Over the past six months the not-seasonally-adjusted core CPI increase has averaged 9bp below the increases 12 months prior. With the significant slowing in core CPI increases starting in June of last year we expect both the increase in June, and the average increase over the six months through November 2024, to be only 1bp below their levels of a year earlier. However, if the June core CPI increase again runs well below the pace of 12 months prior it might be a sign that base effects will not be a significant impediment to slowing inflation in the second half of this year.

## Looking ahead: A slow step-up toward October

We currently project the headline CPI to increase around 21bps in July with early month gasoline prices suggesting a small increase in CPI motor fuels in July. Meanwhile the core CPI increase is projected at 21bps in July as seasonal factors cease pushing down the monthly change. 12-month headline CPI inflation is projected to edge down a few basis point to the edge of 3.0% and 3.1% in July while core CPI inflation also edges down slightly to the border of 3.3% and 3.4%.

 Looking further out, we project core CPI inflation will continue to run in the 3.3% to 3.4% range through the end of the year and the monthly pace of core CPI increases will gradually rise through October as overly-sensitive seasonal factors swing from pushing down the monthly core CPI increases to pushing them up.
