May inflation: so close, yet so far

Our UK Inflation Chartbook recaps the latest monthly inflation data, presenting our key takeaways, including measures of underlying inflation. We also include an update of our inflation projections for CPI, core CPI, and RPI.

May CPI at target, but services CPI still some way away. UK CPI slipped marginally below our expectations, landing almost exactly at 2% y-o-y. Core CPI, was as expected too, slowing to 3.5% y-o-y, dragged lower by a more subdued core goods CPI print (which slipped into deflation territory for the first time since Oct-16). But the fly in ointment remains: services CPI came in around 10bps above our expectations, and nearly 0.2pp above consensus, slipping only to 5.7% y-o-y. What drove the upside surprise to services? While weak goods demand pulled core goods prices down, strong spillover effects from April lifted services CPI higher in May. Moreover, a stronger airfares print coupled with a spike in mobile phone application fees pushed services CPI a little higher than we thought.

What next? The UK has won the race in getting headline CPI back to target – even if temporarily. And despite the slight bump higher in services CPl, our near.
term projections are broadly unchanged. The bad news? UK CPI won’t spend too much time at, or around, the Bank of England’s 2% target.

We now see Q2-24 CPI running closer to 2.1% y-o-y (previously: 2.2% y-o-y), narrowing the wedge between our projections and the Bank’s forecasts. What about June? Our preliminary forecast for June has CPI staying put at 2% y-o-y, with core CPI also staying put at 3.5% y-o-y. Services CPI, we think, will drop 10bps to 5.6% y-o-y. Thereafter, we see CPI moving north of 2.5% y-o-y through H2-24.

Where to now? Since our inflation preview out last week, we have made further – albeit marginal -- tweaks to our inflation forecasts. First, we have marked to market our pump price projections, accounting for the latest market changes in Brent futures – lowering our oil price forecast modestly through the rest of the year. We now see pump prices falling by a little more than 2% m-o-m in June. Second, we have tweaked up our dual fuel bills projection from 5% to 6% in October, and now assume a 1% fall in Jan-25.

Where’s the silver living? The skew of inflation risks have steadied from our last chartbook. After accounting for some of the upside risks in our projections – particularly across energy and core goods inflation – survey data have become marginally more encouraging. Micro price data also point to normalising in price rises, while we remain optimistic around increasing discounting and sales promotions. We expect headline CPI to track around 2.7% y-o-y (previously: 2.7%) across 2024. Thereafter, we see headline CPI slipping from 2.7% this year to 2.2% y-o-y next year (previously: 2.2%). Core CPI, we think, will likely end the year just around 3.4% y-o-y (previously 3.4% y-o-y) before running at around 2.5% through 2025. And services CPI will likely edge lower to around 5% (previously 5% y-o-y) by year-end before meaningfully slowing to 4% in 2025. Our projections for RPI have been revised lower marginally, given the slight miss to our May forecasts. We now see RPI running closer to 3.7% y-o-y (previously: 3.9% y-o-y) in 2024 and 3.8% y-o-y in 2025 (previously: 3.9% y-o-y).

Bank of England call now in the balance. While calls for an imminent rate cut will grow, given headline CPI’s descent to 2%, there’s likely to be growing concerns around the stickiness surrounding services inflation. Indeed, today’s data showed an increase in the MPC’s forecast error, with services CPI – the key inflation gauge monitored by the MPC – drifting 40bps higher compared to the Bank’s May MPR projections.

As we noted last week, an August rate cut is not a done deal. And today’s CPI report will - we think - raise the bar for a summer rate cut. Much will now depend on how much stock the MPC puts in the spot (and arguably) backward looking services and private sector wage data. While there’s plenty of uncertainty, one thing is for sure: the MPC will need to take a broader view on price and wage dynamics to keep an August rate cut on the table.
