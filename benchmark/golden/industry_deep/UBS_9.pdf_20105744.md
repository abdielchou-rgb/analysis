# China Economic Comment China Economic Calendar 2024; December & Q4 Data Preview

## Data preview: mixed Dec activities, better Q4 GDP growth on low base

December NBS manufacturing PMI dropped by 0.4ppt to 49, while Caixin PMI edged up by 0.1ppt to 50.8. NBS non-manufacturing PMI rebounded slightly by 0.2ppt to 50.4. New year tourism activities recovered further. High frequency data showed slightly less bad 30-city property sales growth, stronger growth of steel production, subway passenger turnover and FTL traffic index on low base, but weaker auto sales growth in December. For the upcoming December and Q4 data release, we expect continued large decline in property sales and property investment, better export growth (2%y/y) and IP growth (6.9%y/y), but slower retail sales growth (7%y/y) as low base effect fades. TSF credit growth likely rebounded to 9.7-9.8%y/y, while CPI and PPI likely saw smaller y/y decline. Overall, Q4 GDP q/q growth may have slowed slightly, while a low base likely pushed its y/y growth up to 5.4%y/y. Please see our UBS China Activity Tracker and the Appendix High Frequency Data Monitor for more details.

## Modest policy support ahead in 2024

CEWC set a supportive policy tone and called for better policy coordination. We see additional property easing measures in 2024, including pushing for urban village renovation and social housing construction and increasing credit support for developers. We expect a decent headline fiscal deficit at 3.5-3.8% of GDP (including possible issuance of new special treasury bonds), larger quota of special LG bonds at RMB 4 trillion, and another RMB 2-3 trillion of special refinancing LG bonds to swap qualified LGFV debt and/or repay corporate arrears. We see 10-20 bps rate cut and 25-50 bps RRR cut in 2024, and a robust credit growth at 9.5%. See more in our CEWC takeaways and 2024 key themes & surprises.

## PMIs and high frequency data update

December NBS manufacturing PMI dropped by 0.4ppt to 49, staying <50 for 3 months in a row. New order dropped by 0.7ppt to 48.7. Production softened by 0.5ppt to 50.2, but remained >50 for 11 months consecutively. New export orders weakened by 0.5ppt to 45.8. Raw material inventory and finished goods slid by 0.3/0.4ppt, while quantity of purchase declined by 0.6ppt. Input price rose by 0.8ppt to 51.5, while output price dropped by 0.5ppt to 47.7. Employment edged down by 0.2ppt to 47.9, reflecting lingering pressures of the labor market. On the other hand, Caixin manufacturing PMI edged up by 0.1ppt to 50.8, with both new orders and output index picking up. As we discussed before, such divergence between NBS and Caixin PMIs is not uncommon, largely due to the difference in survey sample selection, while we may need other high frequency data to cross-check the underlying strength of the manufacturing sector.

NBS non-manufacturing PMI edged up by 0.2ppt to 50.4, remaining >50 throughout 2023. Services PMI stayed stable at 49.3, while construction PMI improved by 1.9ppt to 56.9, reflecting improved construction activities at year-end. By sector, PMIs of outgoing and consumption related sectors, i.e., water transport, air transport, accommodation, household services, all stayed below 46, partly due to cold weather. On the other hand, PMIs of postal service, telecom, monetary & financial services, and insurance services stayed above 55.

High frequency data showed UBS China Activity Index (y/y growth) picked up on low base. Property sales in major cities narrowed contraction modestly to -11.5%y/y in December from -15.2%y/y in November. That said, top 100 developers' contract sales volume declined further to -45%y/y (vs -29%y/y in Nov). Steel production improved to 1.4%y/y in the first 20 days of December (vs -4.8%y/y in Nov) on a low base. FTL traffic index growth edged up to 7.9%y/y in December from 7.2% before on a low base. Subway passenger turnover level stayed elevated, and its growth rebounded further to 125%y/y in December on a low base, vs 82%y/y last month. Port cargo and container throughput improved to 5.5%/7.1%y/y in December from 4.1%/4.3% before. On the other hand, auto retail sales and wholesales softened to 7%/17%y/y in the first 24 days from 25%/27%y/y in November. For the upcoming December and Q4 data release, we expect:

December industrial production growth likely edged up further to 6.9%. NBS PMI edged down by 0.4ppt to 49, while production index down by 0.5ppt to 50.2. On the other hand, Caixin PMI edged up by 0.1ppt to 50.8, with a stronger production index (up by 0.5ppt to 52.3). Such mixed signals suggest sequential growth momentum of manufacturing activities likely stayed soft. The operating rate of electric arc furnaces averaged 64% in December, +8ppt y/y (vs +3ppt previously). Steel production rebounded to 1.4%y/y in the first 20 days from -4.8%y/y previously. Operating rates of cement mills lowered to 33%, +0.6ppt above last year, while that of asphalt facilities weakened to 33%, but 1-2ppt higher than last year. Logistic activities growth likely improved on a low base, as the Full-Truck-Load traffic index continued to grow by 7.9%y/y in December (vs 7.2%y/y previously). Overall, we expect November IP growth to have edged up to 6.9%y/y, thanks to a low base last year.

Property sales likely stayed subdued. High frequency data showed property sales in 30 major cities narrowed the decline to -11.5%y/y in December from -15%y/y in November. That said, top 100 developers' contract sales volume declined further to - 45%y/y (vs -29%y/y before), and sales value also weakened to -37%y/y (vs -32%y/y previously). 100-city local land transaction volume improved to 5%y/y from -44% y/y previously. Overall, despite recent acceleration of policy easing since end-August, we expect national property sales likely stayed subdued at a decline of around -10%y/y (vs -10.3%y/y in November). New starts growth may have reached 0%y/y, while property investment likely cooled to -15%y/y (vs -10.6%y/y previously) due to a high base.

Retail sales growth likely slowed to 7%y/y as low base effect fades. 18-city metro passenger turnover stayed elevated at 69mn per day and its growth picked up to 124%y/y (vs 81% previously) due to a low base. Auto retail sales growth slowed to 7%y/ y in the first 24 days vs 25%y/y previously, while auto wholesales also cooled to +17%y/y vs +27%y/y previously. If the overall auto retail sales followed the improvement of high frequency data, it may weigh on headline retail sales growth by 1-1.5ppt. As the boost from low base effect fades, we expect retail sales growth to have decelerated to 7%y/y last month from 10.1%y/y in November.

Credit growth likely rebounded further. Banks likely continued to offer credit support for the real economy, but recent launch of LGFV debt swap may have reduced some of existing bank loans, corporate bonds and shadow credit. Headline new RMB loans may have printed RMB 1.35 trillion, slightly lower than last year (-50bn y/y). Meanwhile, net issuance of government bonds likely recorded RMB 950-1000bn (+700bn y/y). New corporate bonds may have seen a decline of RMB 300bn (+190bn y/ y). Shadow credit may have recorded a small increase of 50-100bn (+190bn y/y). As such, overall new TSF likely printed RMB 2.35trn (+1trn y/y), pushing adjusted TSF and headline TSF up to 9.7-9.8%y/y. Credit impulse likely turned positive to 1.9% of GDP.

FX reserves likely increased by around \$50bn. Capital outflow pressure in December likely continued to ease from Q3, as RMB strengthened by 0.5% vs the USD last month thanks to weak dollar index and some seasonal FX settlement demand. We estimate the valuation effect from major reserve currencies' movement has likely been a notable gain of \$20-25bn, while financial market rally may have contributed to additional valuation gains. Taking other factors into account (larger trade surplus and stable net FDI inflows), China's FX reserves may have increased by \$50bn to \$3.222 trillion.

Q4 GDP growth likely edged up to 5.4%y/y on low base. Q4 industrial production likely averaged 6.1% (+2ppt from Q3), retail sales improved to 8.2%y/y (+4ppt from Q3) in nominal terms and to 9.6% in real terms (+4ppt from Q3), and nominal FAI slowed to 1.5%y/y (-0.4ppt from Q3). Nominal exports likely narrowed decline to -1.6%y/y (+8ppt from Q3) with smaller trade surplus at \$202bn (-14bn y/y). Property sales likely declined by -10%y/y (+2ppt from Q3) and new starts by -6%y/y (+16ppt from Q3). Therefore, we see Q4 GDP q/q growth likely softening slightly from Q3, while its y/y growth edging up by 0.5ppt to 5.4% on low base, leading to 2023 full year growth at 5.2-5.3%.

CEWC set a supportive policy tone and called for better policy coordination. We see additional property easing measures in 2024, including pushing for urban village renovation and social housing construction and increasing credit support for developers. We expect a decent headline fiscal deficit at 3.5-3.8% of GDP (including possible issuance of new special treasury bonds), larger quota of special LG bonds at RMB 4 trillion, and another RMB 2-3 trillion of special refinancing LG bonds to swap qualified LGFV debt and/or repay corporate arrears. We see 10-20 bps rate cut and 25-50 bps RRR cut in 2024, and a robust credit growth at 9.5%. See more in our CEWC takeaways.
