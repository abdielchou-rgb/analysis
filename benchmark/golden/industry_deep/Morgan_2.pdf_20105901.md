## Developed Markets

## United States:

Market attention was centered around monetary policy implementation tools this week. This first part of the week was dominated by the reaction function related to Fed balance sheet normalization while the second was dominated by a repricing lower of the Fed policy rate path, after unencumbered inflation data.

Soft healthcare services and airfares in PPI lowered our economists' forecast for December core PCE inflation to 0.17% m/m, which translates to a 1-month annualized rate of 2.06%. This accelerated the front-end led rally, which began after December CPI data showed no worsening in the sticky components of inflation.

Not only did STIR markets imply an increased probability (80%) for a Fed rate cut in March, but the magnitude of rate cuts also priced in for this year rose. As markets nearly priced in seven 25bp rate cuts this year, SFRZ3Z4 hit record lows. With this notable two-session drop in 2y UST yields, it took yields on this tenor (4.14%) to the lowest since May 2023.

Before the week began, Dallas Fed President Logan advocated to slow the pace of balance sheet runoff as “ON RRP balances approach a low level.” This confirmed that balances at the ON RRP facility would be a key guidepost for the balance sheet normalization reaction function.

In her view, normalizing the balance sheet more slowly reduces the likelihood for a premature end to QT. Lowering the runoffs caps allows for a less brisk redistribution of liquidity at a time when the distribution of reserves in the banking system have become more concentrated. This reduces the odds of acute pressures in funding markets or a breakdown in market functioning, which provides for a longer QT runway.

While Logan’s comments may have skewed risk toward an early start to QT taper, little in her speech indicated the end of QT was around the corner. An argument can even be made that risk of an early start to QT taper is debatable considering Atlanta Fed President Bostic’s comments in which he said he plans to work with the team "over the next six months" to get a better view of how balance sheet policy should evolve.

Perhaps now well understood that the “unusual and exigent circumstances,” which necessitated creation of the Fed’s Bank Term Funding Program (BTFP) facility had passed, there was little reaction in markets this week to Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Barr’s signal that the facility would not be extended. Barr stated the facility had “worked as intended” and it was established “as an emergency program for that limited purpose.” Banks can continue to borrow under the program until March 11 and refinance loans until 2025.

## Japan:

The recovery in USTs after strong CPI data on Thursday led to another rally in JGBs on Friday, led by JGB futures. Media reports which suggested the BoJ may revise down its growth and inflation outlook at its upcoming January meeting also fueled the bond market rally.

Long-end JGBs lagged ahead of the 30y JGB auction (new issue of JX81) at noon in Tokyo hours. The auction result was very weak with a tail price of 99.0 (C: 99.5), without demand from domestic real money market participants. Despite the weak auction, long-end JGBs traded firmly post-auction and the 30y tenor gradually retraced initial weakness, supported by dip-buying demand; 30y JGBs rallied by 1.0bp d/d to 1.57% on a simple yield basis. TONA OIS rallied led by the 10y tenor, as market participants continued to square paid positions. XCCY basis curve continued to flatten, with the long-end better received.

During Tokyo hours on Friday, it was quiet in FX markets. USD/JPY broadly retraced the initial rally after strong US CPI overnight on Thursday and hovered \~145.00 throughout the Tokyo session. Other CCY/JPY pairs traded sideways. However, weak US PPI led to a broad rally in USTs and USD/JPY fell toward 144.50.

## Europe:

An increase in the price of Brent crude oil supported NOK, which lifted NOK/SEK (+0.4%) close to parity where the pair encountered resistance. SEK underperformed against both USD and EUR ahead of the Swedish December CPI data on Monday, where our economists expect a large drop in both core and headline measures due to base effects. An upside surprise in UK GDP failed to support the currency, as global factors dominated (GBP: -0.1%).

European duration opened higher on Friday, after a rally in USTs overnight on Thursday. RXH4 then traded sideways but rallied sharply alongside release of soft US PPI data with downward revisions to the prior month. 10y Bund yields finished 5bp lower at 2.18%. European curves bull-steepened aggressively, especially 2s10s which closed 5bp steeper in swaps (against 6m); 10s30s steepened 2bp to -16bp.

In money markets, ECB OIS meetings repriced lower, in sympathy with Fed pricing.

The April meeting rallied 5bp to -22bp (to imply \~90% probability of a 25bp cut). Reds rallied \~14bp and brought forward a return of the depo rate to 2% by July 2025. Non-core continued to outperform core and the 10y BTP-Bund spread tightened by \~2bp d/d, \~10bp tighter over the past week. Core ASW widened in the London morning as duration opened richer; Schatz then traded sideways (+0.5bp) while the rest of the complex cheapened. The Schatz/Bund ASW box flattened 1bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

G10 FX movements were relatively muted on Friday. JPY outperformed as front-end USTs rallied sharply, which put downward pressure on USD/JPY (-0.3%). A slight deterioration in risk sentiment throughout the NY afternoon provided some support for the US dollar (DXY USD Index: +0.1%), which initially weakened after a downside surprise in US PPI.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies had mixed performance and traded in a narrow range. KRW, TWD, and MYR underperformed. KRW fell 0.1% to 1,314 amid US\$325.6mn foreign outflows from local equity markets, mainly driven by US inflation data. TWD also slipped 0.1%, to 31.13 ahead of the presidential election. INR, CNY, and PHP outperformed in the region. INR gained 0.1% to 82.92 alongside 1.3% local equity market rally. CNY appreciated 0.1% to 7.16 on the back of rising exports in December. THB, IDR, and SGD little changed alongside flat local equity markets.

AxJ rates rallied broadly. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-steepened by 3bp. In Malaysia, the NDIRS curve shifted down by 3bp while the 10y bond yield was steady at 3.81%. In China, the swap curve shifted 2bp down given weak inflation data while 10y bond yields rose 2bp to 2.52% after one-month rally. In India, the NDOIS curve fell by 1bp, and 10y G-Sec rose 1bp to 7.18%.

## Latin America:

Despite USD strength, LatAm currencies ended the session stronger. MXN (+0.3%) rallied and ended below 17.00. BRL (+0.4%), COP (+0.3%), and CLP (+0.2%) also ended the session stronger amid few local developments. LatAm rates ended the session lower, largely driven by core rates. TIIEs had gains concentrated in the belly (5y: -11bp) while the Andean curves rallied, mainly in the front-end (1y CAM: -14bp).

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
