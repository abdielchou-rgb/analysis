# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 26

Upside surprise in Australian CPI send ACGB yields higher; USD/ JPY breaches 160; USTs bear-steepen; ECB's Rehn sees two cuts this year as reasonable; BOB weakens amid reports of a coup; banks pass FRB stress test; DXY at 106.05 (+0.4%); US 10y at 4.329% (+8.2bp).

• An upside surprise in May Australia CPI increases the risk of an RBA hike, with market-implied odds of a hike rising to \~35% (P: \~1%); AUD outperforms, rising 0.6% following the release, and ACGBs sell off across the curve (2y AGB: +18bp).

• Vice Minister of Finance Kanda expresses concern around the “rapid weakening of the yen,” adding the MoF will take “necessary actions against any excessive movements”; USD/JPY breaches 160, rising beyond levels that preceded intervention in the market in April.

• USTs bear-steepen absent clear local catalysts as market participants continue to digest the unexpected reacceleration in Canada and Australia inflation alongside technical factors driving yields higher leading into quarter-end.

• EUR (-0.3%) weakens as ECB Governing Council Member Rehn says the market-implied pricing of two cuts by the end of the year and the terminal rate around 2.25-2.50% are “reasonable expectations.”

• Boliviano weakens amid multiple reports of attempts of an active coup in Bolivia (USD/BOB: 0.4%).

• Results of the Federal Reserve's annual bank stress test show that the 31 largest banks are well positioned to weather a severe recession and stay above minimum capital requirements.

Despite the recent sell-off in yields, demand persisted as UST auctions continued to be digested smoothly. The \$70bn 5y auction stopped .04bp through the whenissued yield, with a cut-off yield of 4.331%. The bid-to-cover ratio of 2.35x was slightly below the six-auction average of 2.39x. However, the allotment to both direct and indirect participants grew to 17.7% (P: 15.4%) and 68.9% (P: 65.0%), respectively. SOFR increased from 5.31% to 5.33% as of June 25, ahead of quarter-end and UST net settlements. Recently, SOFR had returned to RRP+1bp, which indicated that excess liquidity in the front end still needs to come into balance. Over the coming months, we expect SOFR to remain largely anchored to the RRP rate, with moves higher around identifiable dates such as month-end (lower dealer intermediation capacity) and large UST net settlements. There are \$49bn of coupons settling on June 28 and \$95bn settling on July 1, which help explain the move higher in SOFR (for more on short duration, read here).

In the NY afternoon, after equity markets closed, the Federal Reserve released results of its annual stress test. The results showed that all 31 of the largest banks that were tested passed, and are well positioned to weather a severe recession and stay above minimum capital requirements. However, Vice Chair for Supervision Barr highlighted the need for banks to hold on to more capital. This year's tests also included various crisis scenarios for hypothetical recessions, some of which were inspired by recent news events. The Fed concluded that “the banking system is able to withstand a funding stress under the moderate and severe economic conditions included in the exploratory analysis."

According to the weekly EIA Crude Oil Inventory Report, US crude oil inventories rose by 3.59mn barrels and now sit at the highest levels since April. This prompted a 40c drop in WTI crude oil futures, which pared prior gains and ultimately ended the day flat.

Late-day gains managed to push the S&P 500 (+0.2%) slightly higher, driven by a boost from consumer discretionary stocks which outperformed the broader market.

Japan:

JGB yields traded higher in the Tokyo morning session, driven by the long end. Uncertainty regarding the BoJ’s QT plan, the resurgence of BoJ rate hike expectations following a hawkish Summary of Opinions from the June monetary policy meeting, and the sustained rally in USD/JPY all continue to weigh on real money market participants’ demand for the super long sector. However, there remains some demand for the medium-term sector, which curbed the rise in 2y yields. In the Tokyo afternoon, the sell-off in the long end continued, and the medium-term sectors also sold off ahead of Thursday’s 2y auction. The JGB curve bear-steepened; 5y JGB sold off 0.5bp to 0.55%, 10y JGB sold off 2.5bp to 1.02%, and 30y JGB sold off 4.5bp to 2.235% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS curve bearsteepened and outperformed JGBs in sectors over 10y, while 2y and 5y ASW richened. XCCY basis tightened, led by the belly of the curve. The 2y auction is scheduled for Thursday. We expect the auction to be digested smoothly, led by demand from real money market participants who want to park surplus cash. However, given the increasing expectation for another BoJ rate hike in July, the uncertainty could weigh on the sentiment.

Hawkish remarks from Fed Governor Bowman supported the Fed higher-for-longer narrative and pushed the DXY USD Index higher overnight. Accordingly, USD/JPY climbed higher throughout the Tokyo trading hours and touched 160 in the London morning alongside the sell-off in UST yields. With USD/JPY testing the 160 level, the expectation for BoJ’s rate hike in July increased. Meanwhile, AUD/JPY surged above 106.5 during Tokyo morning hours as Australian CPI data for May was stronger than expected.

## Europe:

EUR (-0.3%) weakened as ECB Governing Council Member Rehn pointed out that he expects two additional rate cuts in 2024, slightly more than the ECB policy path currently priced for 2024. EUR/SEK gained (+0.3%) ahead of the Riksbank's June meeting. Market participants expect the central bank to keep its policy rate unchanged, which is in line with previously communicated guidance from the Riksbank. NOK/SEK held steady at 0.9900 ahead of the rate announcement.

European duration traded very weak on Wednesday in what somewhat felt like a risk-off day, without a clear catalyst driving it, as country spreads and the German ASW complex headed wider. The 10y Bund sold off 4.2bp to 2.45%, despite outperforming on a cross-market basis compared to USTs and gilts. In the front-end market, the sell-off resumed, and the bulk of the underperformance was led by the reds pack, which sold off by 5bp. Nevertheless, the curve exhibited bear-steepening pressures, and the 2s10s slope re-steepened 4bp on the day. On the curve, in the rewidening of country spreads, the 5y tenor underperformed on the 5s10s30s fly, which spiked higher by 2bp on the day and closed at 24bp versus 6m. In spreads, the 10y OAT/Bund spread drifted wider by 2.5bp and closed at 77.5bp, while the 10y BTP/Bund widened by 3bp to 153.8bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar strengthened broadly on Wednesday, and the DXY USD Index (+0.4%) followed US yields higher. USD/JPY (0.7%) broke above 160, reaching its highest level since 1986, which reignited speculation around potential FX intervention by Japan's MoF. AUD outperformed after May CPI data surprised to the upside. Headline inflation reaccelerated to 4.00% y/y (P: 3.6%). AUD/NZD (+0.8%) surged above 1.09 and reached its highest level in over a month.

## Asia:

AxJ FX drifted lower against USD amid the rise in USD/JPY and USD/CNH. USD/CNH rallied after the USD/CNY fixing continued moving higher to 7.1248 (P: 7.1225), and ultimately broke above the 7.30 level as USD/JPY rose above 160. USD/CNH finished the Tokyo session 0.2% higher, and reached the highest level since last November, while USD/CNY gained just 0.05%, to 7.2667. Most other AxJ currencies also weakened \~0.2% (INR, IDR, SGD, MYR, PHP). The IDR weakness was in line with peers despite the BI reiterating that it will be in the market to ensure the balance of FX demand and supply. TWD underperformed the most in the region, down 0.4% to 32.57, amid USD strength, higher global yields, and continued foreign equity outflows (-\$253mn). THB also underperformed and fell 0.3% to 36.85 following the 0.5% fall in gold prices. KRW was a relative outperformer, and USD/KRW gained 0.1% to 1,389 while 1m NDF weakened 0.1%, possibly aided by \~\$280mn of foreign equity inflows.

AxJ rates sold off broadly following higher-than-expected Australia May inflation data and the Tuesday overnight sell-off in USTs. China was an exception; 10y CGB yield fell 1bp to 2.22%, and broke below the low seen in April. Yields also fell \~1bp across the CNY NDIRS curve. Korea was also a relative outperformer despite being one of the most sensitive to US rates in the region; 2y KRW NDIRS yields rose just 2bp while 10y yield edged up just 1bp. Indonesia rates also sold off only modestly, and the 10y IndoGB was up 1bp. A similar pattern was seen in India, where 10y G-sec yield edged up by just 1bp to 6.99% as foreign inflows remained steady ahead of Friday’s index inclusion. Yields moved more on the NDOIS curve, with the 5y INR NDOIS yield up 3bp, possibly aided by the RBI governor’s cautious tone on inflation. Singapore rates underperformed the most in the region given their high correlation with US rates, with the underperformance led by the belly of the curve. 5y and 10y SGS sold off 6bp, while 2y and 30y SGS yields were up by a more modest 3-4bp. Hong Kong rates also underperformed, and yields rose \~4bp across most points of the IRS curve.

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. ## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
