## Developed Markets

## United States:

Firmer-than-anticipated US PPI data, with a strong translation to core PCE, resulted in a sharp bear-flattening of the US Treasury curve. The magnitude of the sell-off eased into the NY close, alongside a capitulatory move lower in US equities, which left market pricing of Fed policy and Treasury yields close to Tuesday’s closing levels. Equity resilience in the wake of the surge in front-end yields waned late in the NY afternoon but the S&P 500 (-0.48%) twice found support at the 5,000 level in the session. An initial boost in the US dollar post-PPI faded and left the DXY USD Index flat for the session. G10 currencies ended with modest moves, with slight outperformance in the Scandinavian and Antipodean currencies.

Perhaps lulled into a false sense of security after the annual US CPI revisions confirmed recent progress on inflation, January CPI and PPI provided a sharp reminder of the volatility that upside inflation surprises can bring. Although residual January seasonality cannot be discounted, the most interesting aspect of the CPI data was the highest monthly OER (+0.56%) reading since April came alongside a further m/m deceleration in rent (+0.36%). This divergence between two components which typically move closely together now poses the question if OER should be stronger than rent more permanently going forward or if there will be a reversion to the historical relationship over the next few months.

Outside of an already well-refined core PCE estimate there is little in the form of economic data likely to reverse the bearish momentum in the rates market until February nonfarm payrolls, released March 8. Thus, Fedspeak will play an important role in determining the direction of the rates market during this three-week period. We flagged in The Real Drivers of the US Treasury Bond Market Rout, the role the

Fed reaction function to growth and inflation data played in the rise in Treasury yields over 3Q23.

Should the mosaic of Fedspeak once again turn to upside risks to the outlook, Treasury yields may find difficulty moving lower. Atlanta Fed President Bostic said reports from his business contacts across the Southeast show businesses “sitting on pause, awaiting the optimal time to deploy assets and resume hiring to expand their operations.” This constitutes a new upside risk to his outlook as it “carries the ring of expectant optimism—perhaps even pent-up exuberance—with the potential to unleash a burst of new demand that could reverse the progress we have observed toward rebalancing supply and demand across the economy.”

## Japan:

Initially, JGBs twist-steepened ahead of the liquidity tap auction for the 15.5-39y sector at noon in Tokyo hours. The front-end traded resiliently after weak US retail sales data drove USD/JPY lower, to an extent. The liquidity tap auction was strong and market participants quickly squared short positions in the long-end and this sector outperformed into the 3:00pm Tokyo close; 30y JGBs rallied by 2.5bp d/d to 1.775% on a simple yield basis. The belly of the curve sold off post-auction, as the gradual rise in USD/JPY above 150 encouraged market participants to position for a longer BoJ normalization cycle; 5y JGBs sold off 1.5bp d/d to 0.345%.

During the Tokyo evening session, a media report suggested the BoJ is on track to exit negative rates in the coming months and suggested April seemed most likely, after the weak 4Q23 GDP data. The reaction in markets was relatively muted as a full hike was already priced in for April. TONA OIS twist flattened alongside JGBs, but broadly outperformed JGBs except for the long-end. XCCY basis tightened, led by the long-end.

During Tokyo hours, it was quiet in FX markets but the gradual sell-off in UST yields pushed USD/JPY above 150 as FOMC officials expressed little urgency to cut rates. Other CCY/JPY pairs ground higher as risk assets continued to trade resilient; AUD/ JPY ground higher above 98.00.

## Europe:

The Scandinavian currencies outperformed amid the recovery in US equities in the NY afternoon. GBP shrugged off a significant upside surprise in UK retail sales, as seasonality adjustments appeared to have distorted both December and January data. GBP (+0.1%) gained slightly with the broader USD weakness.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar eventually reversed gains, which followed an upside surprise in US PPI. The DXY USD Index (-0.2%) ended lower, as risk demand rebounded despite the sell-off in US Treasuries. Ahead of next week’s Canadian CPI data, CAD (-0.1%) underperformed most G10 currencies as front-end yield differentials moved an additional \~3bp into USD’s favor. The Antipodeans led dollar bloc performance as risk appetite recovered.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies remained steady ahead of US PPI data. THB outperformed; USD/THB fell by 0.4% to 36.02 while local equities rose. TWD gained and USD/TWD opened lower. Taiex notched a record high supported by market participant optimism for AI technology. MYR and SGD remained steady, with little volatility. Initially, KRW strengthened and USD/KRW opened 0.4% lower, amid lower-than-expected unemployment data but eventually rebounded to 1335.55. INR remained rangebound while IDR closed the AxJ session unchanged ahead of an increase in the domestic NDF fixing. China markets were closed.

AxJ bonds fell slightly, but remained stable overall. In Singapore, 10y yields rose by 1.7bp to 3.05%. In India, G-Sec 10y yields rose 1.3bp. South Korea and Thailand rates were mostly stable. In Indonesia, the 10y yield was little changed at 6.61%.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended mixed despite initial weakness after higher-than-expected PPI in the US. CLP (-0.4%) continued its sell-off from Thursday, while COP and MXN ended the session flat with few local developments. BRL (+0.2%) outperformed peers in the session. LatAm rates largely followed USTs, with losses in the belly and long-end. TIIEs twist-steepened (1s5s: +5bp) along with the IBR curve (1s5s: +7bp), while the long-end of CAM sold off 8bp. Front-end DIs underperformed other curves; January 2026 contracts sold off 7bp.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Atlanta Fed President Bostic said the Fed has to be patient, as there is still some work to do. He expects two rate cuts this year, but with positive data he could support three cuts

• Richmond Fed President Barkin said January CPI data confirmed why the Fed needs more confidence before cutting rates

• San Francisco Fed President Daly wants the Fed to be patient but prepared to move as the economy evolves. She noted an improvement in the inflation picture but reiterated progress is not victory, specifically citing economic momentum as a risk to the inflation outlook. Although, she reminded it was still a risk that the labor market could falter. Daly described the median forecast of 3 rates cuts this year as "reasonable" and disagreed the last mile of inflation would be the hardest

## Emerging Markets

• RBI Governor Das advised other nations to adopt India's approach of coordination for the government and central bank to address both the supply and demand sides of inflation. Das pointed out financial instability could arise from hiking interest rates alone in an attempt to reduce inflation, given the significant danger of sovereign indebtedness in certain nations

## Economic Release Notes

## Developed Markets

• In Japan, the Weekly Ministry of Finance International Transactions in Securities data for the week of January 4-10 suggested Japanese market participants were large net buyers of foreign long-term debt securities (+JPY 1.50tn), meanwhile the inflows to foreign equity/funds appeared limited (+JPY 22.1bn). Overseas market participants were slight net buyers of Japan long-term debt securities (+JPY 175.9bn). Overseas market participants remained large net buyers of Japan equities (+JPY 621.3bn)

• In France, final January Headline CPI came in at 3.1% y/y (C: 3.1%; December: 3.7%), in-line with the preliminary estimate

• In the UK, Retail Sales (including auto fuel) rose by 3.4% m/m in January (C: 1.5%; P: -3.3%). This was the “largest ever” rise and reversed the “largest ever” drop in retail sales in December, which our economists believe were both a result of seasonality adjustments around Christmas. Yet, the retail

sector had such a poor 2H23 it is hard not to expect some recovery over 1H24. Our economists highlight few BoE implications, with the focus firmly on the PMIs next week

• In the US, January PPI data pointed to an acceleration in all PCE inputs: domestic air transportation; acceleration in health services; financial services and insurance. Our economists finalize their forecast for core PCE inflation at 0.43% m/m in January (December: 0.17%) and headline at 0.36% m/m (December: 0.17%). A data release in-line with our economists’ forecast would put the 3-month annualized rate of core PCE at 2.7% in January (December: 1.5%)

• In the US, Housing Starts fell by 14.8% m/m in January (C: 0.0%; P: 3.3%) to a 1331k annual pace while Building Permits fell by 1.5% m/m in January (C: 1.3%; P: 1.8%) to a 1470k annual pace. Much of the decline was in the routinely volatile multifamily category. There was also a drop in single-family starts but the broader uptrend in single-family permits suggests this was weather-related

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
