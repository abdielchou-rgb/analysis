## The world at a glance

• Macro conditions have not changed in the last few weeks, despite markets gyrating a lot

• The US economy still has momentum, and inflation in the West has dropped considerably

• Financial markets over-reacted in the December risk-on, and are now resetting

• Our call is unchanged – too many 2024 cuts are priced in, and we like stocks over bonds

## Ignore the ISM, focus on the labor market

• The 7 point drop in ISM services employment makes no sense to us <sup>1</sup>

o It is inconsistent with regional surveys, initial claims and service sector employment

o We believe this ISM report gives a false signal, and markets eventually agreed on Friday

• On the other hand, the payroll report was more mixed than appeared at first glance <sup>2</sup>

o The rise in AHE and the drop in LFPR were decidedly hawkish

o However, the work week fell, there were net downward revisions and the household survey was weak

o While it is just one month of data, we think the Fed will focus most on whether labor supply is tightening

• Zooming out a bit, the US jobs market is still in very good shape, even if softer than 12 months ago

o The payroll proxy is rising at over 4% annualized over the last three months, while core PCE inflation has been 0.4%

o In other words, real incomes are now growing strongly, and the Atlanta Fed is tracking 2.5% Q4 GDP growth

• So far, the US is showing every indication of growing at or above trend for the foreseeable future

## The rest of the world – still in fair to middling shape

• Our views on the rest of the world have not changed in the new year either

• The euro area economy is weaker than the US, but still not near a significant recession <sup>1</sup>

o The upside December final PMIs suggest that activity troughed in October

o The inflation profile continues to improve, with core inflation continuing to fall

o Despite the recent increase (energy and base effects), headline inflation could approach 2% in Q1

• China continues to struggle, as seen by very weak NBS PMIs as well as other soft indicators <sup>2</sup>

o President Xi admitted as much on Dec 31<sup>st</sup> and vowed to ‘strengthen… economy…

o While Caixin PMIs were a bright spot, China faces deepening deflation pressures

o We expect imminent cuts in both the policy rate and RRR, as the PboC focuses on ‘…price recovery’

• In contrast, India continues to do well at the start of a new year

o Services PMIs rose to 59 in December, cost pressures are easing, and new export orders indicate expansion <sup>3</sup>

• A weak China, a buoyant India and a stagnant Europe – early 2024 suggests a repeat of late 2023

## Making sense of it all

• In our last GMT of Dec 2023, we turned neutral on risk assets after what seemed an euphoria-driven rally <sup>1</sup>

o But, as mentioned before, the positive macro fundamentals in the US have not changed

o After last week’s re-pricing, we recommend turning overweight US equities again

• The fall in inflation has been considerable, and should persist this week (the PCE-CPI gap notwithstanding) <sup>2</sup>

o The Fed has been unwilling to push back strongly against the cuts being priced in

o The Fed minutes expressed concerns about staying restrictive too long, and related downside risks <sup>3</sup>

o The Richmond Fed’s Barkin refused to push back against March pricing, saying he would not ‘prejudge meetings’ <sup>4</sup>

• Our US rates strategists argue that longer rates are too low, based on several different metrics <sup>5</sup>

o For example, Kim-Wright term premium is below pre-GFC, despite much higher oncoming Treasury issuance

o Similarly, 5y5y real rates currently reflect very little term premium

o And with the US economy doing well, there is no reason to expect the ‘expectations’ component to drop from here

• We expect longer US yields to rise over the next few weeks, even if this week’s CPI report pleases the market
