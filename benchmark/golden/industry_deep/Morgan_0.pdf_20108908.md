# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 28

USTs bear-steepen amid US election attention; US PCE data reaffirms inflation deceleration; RBA's Hauser says its a "bad mistake" to set policy on one number; USD/CNH falls following lower USD/CNY fixing; JGBs bull-steepen; BanRep cuts 50bp; DXY at 105.85 (-0.1%); US 10y at 4.396% (+11.0bp).

• USTs bear-steepen amid growing attention around the US Election following the first presidential debate and a plethora of Supreme Court decisions that could have impact on the election, economy, and financial sector; the bear-steepening accelerates into NY close driven by quarter-end fundamentals.

• US PCE data reaffirms the May deceleration in inflation supporting a temporary UST rally with San Francisco Fed President Daly highlighting that the data is a sign that “monetary policy is working.”

• RBA Deputy Governor Hauser says it would be “a bad mistake to set policy on the basis of one number” referencing the recent inflation data adding that the RBA “does not intend to do that;” Australian bond yields fall across the curve (2y ACGB: -10bp).

• USD/CNH falls 0.2% as the USD/CNY fixing is set lower for the first time in seven sessions and the PBOC reiterates that it will stabilize RMB through comprehensive policies.

• JGB curve bull-steepens following strong result of BoJ purchase operations, particularly in the 10-25y sector, with month-end fundamentals also supporting the rally.

• BanRep lowers its policy rate by 50bp as expected to 11.25% with statement and press conference suggesting that inflation expectations continue to adjust downward, supporting future easing; COP partially retraces earlier gains against the USD following the meeting.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

USTs bear-steepened in a busy quarter-end session and the 2s30s curve ended the day \~9bp steeper. Overnight, the UST curve modestly bear-steepened during the first US presidential debate. However, the move lost momentum and USTs traded in narrow range for the remainder of the overnight session. Initially, it seemed that although the debate would be consequential to polling and the future of the presidential campaigns, it was having a minimal impact on markets on Friday morning. Market participants instead shifted their focus to the May US PCE data in the NY morning.

The May PCE data was in-line with consensus expectations and Core PCE rose 0.1% m/m while Headline PCE prices were flat m/m. Personal income was slightly stronger-than-expected rising to 0.5% (C: 0.4%; P: 0.3%) while spending was softerthan-expected at 0.2% (C: 0.3%) and April spending was revised down to 0.1% (P: 0.2%). Overall, the data confirmed the deceleration of inflation in May which continues to give the Fed the additional confidence it needs in order to begin cutting rates. USTs rallied following the release with yields down \~3-4bp.

The rally was short lived and the curve began to bear-steepen into the NY afternoon. This came alongside growing attention towards the US election following the debate on Thursday night that may have led to market participants adding trades to support various election outcome views. The bear-steepening was also alongside reports of various Supreme Court decisions that could have impact on the election, economy, and financial sector. The decisions included an overturning of the Chevron doctrine, which could have some implications for the regulatory agencies' policymaking authority (for more, read here), and a decision around a January 6 capital riot defendant.

Into the NY close, the bear-steepening accelerated as a result of quarter-end technical factors that drove a sell-off in USTs. 2y yields rose 4bp, 10y yields rose 11bp, and 30y yields rose 13bp. The steepening in 2s30s was the largest d/d steepening of the pair since March.

Quarter-end dynamics could also be found in short duration markets and the Fed’s RRP rose above \$600bn for the first time since January and had the highest amount of users since February. Repo markets remained volatile given quarter-end factors and Treasury settlements.

The S&P 500 retraced intraday gains and fell 0.4% amid the political uncertainty following the US presidential debate. This resulted in the first weekly loss in the index since late May, but the index is still up nearly 16% since the beginning of the year.

## Japan:

JGB yields initially traded higher across the curve, but the super-long sectors began to rally toward the end of the Tokyo morning. BoJ’s announcement of unchanged sizes of monthly JGB buying for the 3-5y, 10-25y, and 25y+ sectors partially supported market sentiment. The strong result of the purchase amount, particularly in the 10-25y sector, accelerated the momentum for the rally in the long end into the Tokyo 3pm close. It seems to us that the rally was partially driven by demand from real money market participants on the back of month-end rebalancing as a result of recent strong equity performance. Medium-term sectors also rallied in the Tokyo afternoon and given the decline in yields, market implied probability of a July hike fell despite the elevated USD/JPY level. The JGB curve bull-flattened; 5y JGB rallied 1.5bp to 0.58%, 10y JGB rallied 3.0bp to 1.04%, and 30y JGB rallied 4.5bp to 2.23% on a simple yield basis. TONA-OIS also bull-flattened but underperformed JGBs. XCCY basis widened driven by the short end.

In the Tokyo morning, USD/JPY initially moved lower but surged to \~161.3 as the US presidential election debate began. It eventually lost the upside momentum and fell to 160.8. Media reports emerged that the MoF’s top currency official Kanda Masato will be replaced by Mimura Atsushi on July 31 as a scheduled personnel change. In the Tokyo afternoon, USD/JPY temporarily rose again but stayed in a range of 160.9 to 161.0 into the London morning. For other CCY/JPY pairs, EUR/JPY stayed at a higher level of 172.0 to 172.2 without any major catalysts. On the other hand, AUD/ JPY corrected and fell to \~106.6 as RBA’s Hauser said that it would be “bad mistake”

if they formulate a policy in response to a single inflation report.

## Europe:

Most G10 currencies strengthened slightly against the USD. EUR/USD and EUR/CHF were little changed (+0.1%) ahead of the first round of the French parliamentary election taking place over the weekend. EUR traded sideways after the release of several national CPI reports, including France, Italy, and Spain, ahead of the Euro Area release next Tuesday. The inflation reports were largely in-line with expectations. NOK underperformed and weakened 0.3% against the USD without a clear macro catalyst. Oil prices were close to flat, while May retail sales surprised to the upside in Norway.

## Dollar Bloc:

The Dollar Bloc was relatively muted after May US PCE matched consensus expectations and FX markets had reacted little to the US presidential debate overnight. AUD outperformed (+0.3%) the other dollar bloc currencies and the rest of G10. An in-line GDP release in Canada did not have a material impact on USD/ CAD (-0.1%). The DXY USD Index (-0.1%) was down slightly on the day.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies advanced against the USD. USD/CNH fell 0.2% to \~7.2925 following a lower USD/CNY fixing, for the first time in seven sessions, and PBoC’s reiteration that it will stabilize RMB expectations and use comprehensive policies to correct deviations. Most other AxJ currencies (IDR, SGD, PHP) also appreciated by \~0.2%. KRW was the outperformer amid the soft USD, strong equities (KOSPI: +0.5%) and failure of USD/KRW to break above 1,400 in prior sessions; USD/KRW weakened 0.7% to 1,376. THB and TWD also saw relatively larger gains of 0.3%, with the former aided by a 0.3% rise in gold prices and the latter aided by \$274mn of equity inflows. MYR continued to underperform and stayed stable against the weak USD. INR was also a relative underperformer given the weak USD backdrop; USD/INR declined just 0.1% to 83.39.

Most AxJ rates rallied following a similar move in USTs on Thursday. China and India were exceptions. China rates stabilized on both the CGB and NDIRS curves after rallying throughout the week. In India, 10y G-Sec sold off 1bp to 7% alongside \$94mn of foreign outflows on the first day of India’s inclusion in the GBIEM index. Hong Kong, Singapore and Korea rates outperformed given their higher correlation with US rates; 10y swap yields fell 5bp, 3bp, and 3bp, respectively, and the curves bull-flattened. Indonesia rates also outperformed and the 10y IndoGB rallied 5bp to 7.05%. In Thailand, 10y ThaiGB and 5y NDOIS yields fell 3bp, and the 1s5s curve bull flattened 2bp, aided by the government extending the cooking gas price cap for another three months and considering a gasoline price subsidy for welfare cardholders, as well as weak May manufacturing production data.

## Central Bank Monitor

• Riksbank’s Deputy Governor Bunge said that the central bank needs to evaluate the impact of its policy normalization. She also mentioned that she had some questions around the recent rise in services prices

• San Francisco Fed President Daly said that the May PCE inflation data was additional news that monetary policy is working, adding that if inflation comes down like it did last year, the Fed can respond

## Emerging Markets

• In its 2Q24 MPC meeting report, PBoC reiterated that the RMB should be kept stable at a reasonable, equilibrium level, and the central bank will prevent one-sided expectations and guard against the risk of "exchange rate overshooting." It pledged to fully recognize new changes in supply and demand in the property market and pushed for the implementation of already announced housing policies. It reaffirmed it will step up countercyclical adjustment and promote a rebound of consumer prices

• BSP expects the Philippines’ inflation to settle within 3.4-4.2% in June, with increases in prices of rice, vegetables, meat, and fish, along with PHP depreciation and higher domestic oil prices, being the main sources of upward inflationary pressures

• BanRep lowered its policy rate to 11.25% at its June monetary policy meeting (C: 11.25%; P: 11.75%) in a split vote similar to the March meeting with two dissidents favoring larger cuts (75 bp). A data-dependent approach to upcoming decisions was maintained. Elements of the text and press conference suggested that inflation expectations continue to adjust downward which our economists note indicates further easing ahead. Our

economists expect another 50bp cut at the next meeting to 10.75% and yearend rates at 9.25%

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
