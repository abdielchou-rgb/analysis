# FX & EM Fixed Income Strategy: Is the Carry Trade Over?

James Lord

The EM carry trade has come under significant pressure in June. But the catalyst was not what most investors were concerned about. In fact, US CPI surprised to the downside, UST yields have come lower and US equities reached new highs, which is all very benign and supportive of EM. The risks were local to Latin America and Europe, pushing investors to de-risk in popular markets like Mexico and Poland. With the US presidential election debate and French legislative elections coming at the end of the month, there are risks ahead which could undermine FX and keep USD strong. However, we expect local rates to stay resilient and in markets where we have seen curves back up recently – i.e., Mexico and Brazil – we believe that there is an opportunity now to add risk back.

## Is the carry trade over?

Halfway through June, and the theme of the month in EM local markets is risk reduction – unless of course you are managing a book of South Africa local assets, in which case the mood music has encouraged risk taking. While USD/ZAR hasn’t moved a great deal, the outperformance versus its high-beta peers so far in June ( Exhibit 1 ) is remarkable and a testament to the importance of the local election results and the coalition that has been negotiated over the past few weeks. We turned bullish and bought into SAGB 2035s on May 31, and continue to target 11.0%.

Outside South Africa, though, there has been a substantial unwind of long EM carry positions in currency markets at least. It is ironic that this has happened just as US CPI/PPI data came in well below expectations to keep hopes of Fed rate cuts alive this year, and should have given a welcome sigh of relief to a broad range of risk assets.

Judging by the performance of the S&P 500, some risk assets certainly took the news positively. Unfortunately for investors in EM, other factors have conspired to undermine performance, at least for currencies.

We continue to believe that USD will remain strong in the near term, and EM FX is our least preferred asset class, with credit and local rates more preferred.

There are a few risk events to consider, which should keep USD on the front foot:

Over the next 2-3 weeks, markets are likely to focus on the first US presidential election debate (on June 27) and the first and second rounds of the French legislative elections (held on June 30 and July 7). The impact that the election results we've seen so far in June have had on markets suggests that investors will focus on these risk events, particularly given their potential implications for policy.

Our core view remains that EUR/USD should move lower due to expected changes in yield differentials, as over the course of the cycle we expect markets to price in a more dovish path for the ECB relative to the Fed.

We explain below how the US election debate and the French presidential elections interact with this core view and we are happy to maintain our short EUR/USD exposure over this period.

US election risks: We think that USD is likely to gain against risk-sensitive currencies if investors see a rising likelihood of a second Trump administration due to perceptions regarding trade tension and the fiscal outlook. These gains could be particularly sizeable if Republicans are seen as likely to control both the House and Senate as such an outcome would likely raise expectations for further deficit spending.

As we discuss in USD | USD sensitivity to policy risks, June 15, 2024, the Trump administration's trade and immigration policy actions were associated with softer risk demand, a lower AUD/USD and a lower USD/JPY. USD/JPY declines may be limited by JPY's shifting correlation to risk and by higher BoJ policy rate expectations than in 2018-19 (implying that local yields have more flexibility to drop if growth is impacted by US tariff policy). However, history suggests that short AUD/JPY positions may again serve as an effective hedge to rising risks of trade policy confrontation in a potential second Trump administration.

French election risks: OAT-Bund spreads widened out and EUR weakened as President Macron announced that legislative elections would be held at the end of June. Our economists expect that a Rassemblement National majority in the French National Assembly would lead to a 1.8-2.5% of GDP increase in the government's fiscal deficit, while a majority for the union of left-wing parties could raise the deficit by 3.5-5.0% of GDP. Both outcomes could put additional pressure on OAT-Bund spreads, but its less obvious what the fiscal response would be.

Greater fiscal spending would be good for growth, inflationary and all else equal lead to a more hawkish ECB. This should be supportive of EUR. However, a substantial widening of the deficit that leads to credit rating downgrades could at least partially offset that policy support for EUR.

However, in our view the biggest risk to EUR from the French elections has little to do with fiscal policy, but what a RN majority would mean for the French presidential elections in 2027 (or before, should it be called earlier) and France's position in the EU.

We can see that the EU risks are the bigger focus for markets by simply observing that the French CAC40 equity market dropped 6-7% in the past week, and the EuroStoxx 50 index was down \~4%. If the focus were on the pro-growth implications of potentially looser fiscal policy associated with the election outcomes, then most likely equities would not have fallen so much.

As we note here, RN has stopped advocating the idea of 'Frexit', which helps to mitigate a key risk for EUR. However, EUR could still face some downside pressure in anticipation of RN adopting policies that are inconsistent with current EU rules, or if the administration begins to advocate for reforms to weaken European integration.

If so, this could lead to political tension and elevated risk premia on European assets, given the risk that markets price a higher chance that progress on European integration comes to a halt.

Brazil and Mexico local markets have been in the spotlight for the past two weeks, as negative idiosyncratic developments drove significant corrections in both rates and FX. In Brazil, in a scenario where the BCB is hawkish at the June meeting, but not hawkish enough to validate the substantial premia for hikes embedded into the curve for the past weeks, we do think that DI rates overall could retrace lower, especially as our metrics point to a substantial clean-up in receiver positions held by locals, below average levels. As discussed in US Economics & Global Macro Strategy: US Inflation: After the Upswing, May 3, 2024, our US economists believe that rents, goods and financial services will decelerate and seasonality will pay back in 2H24.

As a result, US rates rallied. However, USD remains strong. The political uncertainty in Europe and ongoing unwind of LatAm carry trades are both contributing to the ongoing USD strength.

Overall we are comfortable with our stances and trades:

1) The outperformance of the US economy weighs on Asia FX: We run a carry trade portfolio with a long USD bias: Short TWD/INR and long USD/THB.

2) China should keep CNY stable for now but it could weaken in late 3Q24: Short CNH/ INR and long 6m USD/CNH 7.4/7.6 call spread.

3) Asia central banks are more cautious in cutting interest rates due to risks to FX: We like long duration selectively with a focus on relative value: Long 10-year G-Secs without FX hedge, pay 1-year HKD versus USD, pay 5-year SGD versus USD, 1s5s CNY NDIRS steepener and long onshore FX-hedged 1-year CGBs.

This week, we explore the topics of Chinese state banks' activities in CNY and a positioning overhang in some crowded EM trades post-election.

## Gauging Chinese state banks' activities in CNY

Where is the question coming from? CNY is facing two offsetting factors: On one hand, the overall balance of payments is weakening due to a widening services deficit, more repatriation of foreign companies' dividends, net FDI outflows and increasing errors and omissions. The balance of payments should point to a weaker CNY in basket terms versus its trading partners (see China's 3D Journey: The CNY Outlook in a New Equilibrium, December 17, 2023).

On the other hand, the PBOC is keeping a firm cap on the USD/CNY level using different tools including CNY fixing, FX intervention, window guidance and liquidity tightening. This has caused USD/CNY to be quite stable in the past 12 months.

Meanwhile, the PBOC FX reserves saw little change – they have been around US\$3 trillion for quite a long time. This would suggest that the PBOC hasn't tapped FX reserves to conduct FX intervention.

Media these days often report that SOE banks have been intervening in the FX market on behalf of the PBOC when CNY was under pressure. How big are those activities?

Twin surplus led to USD accumulation onshore in 2020/21: For context, China's trade surplus has increased significantly in recent years post-pandemic thanks to the demand from the western world and comprehensive supply chain in China. The annual trade surplus doubled from US\$430 billion in 2019 to US\$889 billion in 2022.

China also saw more than US\$100 billion of bond inflows in 2020 and 2021 thanks to index inclusion. For those years, China enjoyed a twin surplus in both the current account and financial account. In theory, CNY should appreciate or China's FX reserves should increase. Neither happened in those two years.

Most investors we talk with attribute such a discrepancy to USD buying from state banks. In other words, the amount of excess USD is kept in the private sector or outside official FX reserves. This can also be seen in the increasing onshore FX deposits in 2020/21.

Now the reverse seems to be happening – both China's current account and financial account are weakening a bit. The amount of USD sitting in the private sector or outside official FX reserves could be used to support CNY. To that extent, this supports media reports that the state banks have been in the market to buy CNY.

In order to gauge the size of the purchases, we went through the top 17 Chinese banks annual reports. Chinese banks report their FX assets and liabilities in their quarterly reports. As the annual reports cover the banks' offshore entities' assets, they should capture banks' selling USD/CNH offshore.

The latest reports cover the end of 2023, which means that analysing the data in 2H23 could give a clue to the size of purchases, given the pressure on CNY in 2H23 and the PBOC's effort to stabilise the currency.

FX assets versus liabilities: Chinese banks' liabilities are composed of deposits and interbank borrowing, which account for over 80% of the funding source. On the asset side, there are three main items: investment, loans and interbank lending. Investment is usually in USD assets such as overseas assets or USD bonds issued by Chinese corporates. Interbank borrowing/lending is shorter-dated instruments. The top 17 banks' overall FX assets are US\$1.6 trillion versus US\$1.56 trillion in liabilities.

In a banking system, loans create deposits, which are the major driver of Chinese banks foreign assets and USD deposits. Meanwhile, given the strong trade surplus, the increase in onshore FX deposits also leads to increasing FX assets. This was seen in 2021.

Loan demand started to weaken in 2022: When USD interest rates rose in 2022 and 2023, the demand for FX loans started to weaken and such a trend continued into 2023: FX loans decreased from US\$678 billion in 1H22 to US\$495 billion in 2H23 ( Exhibit 43 ).

More FX investment: Given that the pace of FX deposits decline was much slower than the decline in FX loans ( Exhibit 45 ), Chinese banks had to deploy those FX deposits into FX investments (mainly USD instruments) in order to maintain their interest rate margins. This led to an increase in FX investment portfolios in 2022 and 2023: FX investment increased significantly from US\$476 billion to US\$549 billion during 1H22 and 2H23.

Our credit strategist expects such trend to continue, especially as investors would have an appetite to buy FX investments, in particular USD fixed income products such as MBS and

So far, the data analysis above shows no sign of Chinese banks selling USD to buy CNY. If we assume that any FX intervention involves selling USD assets to buy CNY, Chinese banks' FX investment book should have decreased. To the contrary, it increased. Meanwhile, banks' FX cash (we put in the 'other' category in the banks' assets chart) has been around US\$121 billion throughout 2023, indicating no usage of FX cash as well ( Exhibit 46 ).

Sizeable decline in interbank lending in 2H23: Looking into the change of interbank borrowing/lending, we find something interesting: interbank lending decreased by US\$58 billion in 2H23 while interbank borrowing increased by US\$2 billion. According to the PBOC definition, the interbank borrowing/lending refers to behaviour of banks to adjust the surplus and shortage of FX positions, which belongs to the short-term FX financing market.

Chinese banks could have sold around US\$60 billion USD/CNY in 2H23: In other words, the interbank borrowing/lending could also be seen as FX cash and the banks could buy or sell it on a daily basis. Based on what we discussed above, Chinese banks could have sold around US\$60 billion USD/CNY in 2H23. More interestingly, 97% of the change was driven by the top two state banks.

As a disclaimer, the analysis above is based on the data available from annual reports and its conclusion might not be correct given that it is our interpretation of the data rather than any concrete evidence. In addition, some of the intervention is in the derivatives space, where the notional could be huge but the NPV is very small. None of that could be tracked in banks' annual reports. This makes the estimation quite difficult.

Overall, our message on CNY remains the same: The balance of payments is weakening a bit, which implies a weak CNY. However, the PBOC has been able to keep it stable since mid-2023 for various reasons. If our estimate of state banks' selling is a ballpark number of FX intervention, the intervention pressure isn't high, in our opinion. This is consistent with our view that the PBOC has the ability and willingness to keep CNY stable for now.

However, as we argued in Asia Macro Strategy Mid-Year Outlook: Stress Testing Our Three Themes, June 3, 2024, it would make sense to us if the PBOC reduces the pressure a bit by letting CNY weaken versus USD should USD be strong into the US election, given the potential tariff risks. This would avoid a more dramatic move in early 2025 should tariffs rise. The risk to USD/CNY in 3Q24 is on the upside, in our view.

## The positioning overhang in Mexico and South Africa

The original article was published in EM Strategy: The GBI-EM Investors' Special Lowdown: The Positioning Overhang in Mexico and South Africa, June 11, 2024.

Three major elections: In recent weeks, three major elections, namely Mexico, South Africa and India, surprised the market in terms of their result and market impact. Investors have asked us how EM real money investors, particularly GBI-EM investors, could react to these elections and whether the positioning overhang could cause further volatility.

In this note, we will answer questions such as:

• How important are GBI-EM investors for EM local markets?

• How were they positioned before the election?

• How long would it take for them to cut risks from OW to neutral?

How important are GBI-EM investors? For context, GBI-EM investors are the material players in EM local markets. They account for a decent size of foreign ownership of EM local markets.

Taking Mexico, South Africa and India as examples, the foreign ownership of MBonos, SAGBs and IGBs is 30.5%, 24.6% and 2.0%, respectively.

In USD terms, it is US\$80 billion for Mexico, US\$48 billion for South Africa and US\$29 billion for India. GBI-EM investors hold US\$300 billion AUM (JP Morgan has US\$220 billion as the official number but we think there would be investors who won’t tell JPM about their full AUM).

Hence, GBI-EM investors hold US\$43 billion in Mexico, US\$30 billion in South Africa and US\$9 billion In India based on our proprietary database. This accounts for 60%, 60% and 30% of foreign ownership of Mexico, South Africa and India, respectively.

This shows that what these investors are going to do in both duration and FX could have a material impact on the market.

## Mexico

• GBI-EM investors were quite OW duration into the election while their FX exposure was less so, almost neutral versus history (see also EM Strategy: The GBI-EM Investors' Lowdown, May 3, 2024).

• In portfolio contribution-to-duration terms, it is 0.19 OW versus the benchmark – the biggest OW in the GBI-EM portfolio.

• In Mexico’s stand-alone duration terms, it is 6.51 versus 4.56, or 1.95 year duration OW.

• In overall DV01 terms, it is US\$8.4 million DV01.

• The daily DV01 trading volume of MBono and TIIE is US\$500k and US\$1 million,

respectively.

• If we assume that 70% of the daily flows are in one direction, foreigners account for half of those flows and GBI-EM accounts for 40%, it would take 40 trading days to square the duration exposure from OW to neutral (i.e., cut the risk consistently every day).

• For FX, our LatAm strategists think that overall positioning, which includes both real money and fast money, has turned neutral from being long, based on options data, although the position hasn't turned short yet (see Mexico Local Markets Strategy: MXN Status Report, June 5, 2024, and Mexico Economics & Strategy: Revisiting Backstops, June 3, 2024).

## India

• GBI-EM investors have pre-positioned themselves ahead of index inclusion starting from June 2024 as they are now almost 3% OW versus the benchmark before the inclusion.

• As India will be 10% of the index by March 2025 versus the current holding of 3%, we doubt that investors would cut any positions after seeing the election result.

• They might slow down the pace of purchases given their existing positions in duration, but we doubt that they will stop investing in G-Secs because a sustained pause in increasing positioning in the next few months could cause them to be UW India when the index inclusion is happening.

• For INR, we think that political uncertainty could have some negative impact on portfolio flows, particularly around equity inflows.

• For context, India saw monthly inflows/outflows of equity markets in the size of US\$3-4 billion. The biggest outflows were in 2021 when India saw over US\$20 billion outflows within 12 months.

• In the scenario that outflows happen, the RBI has both the willingness and ability to defend INR given its US\$650 billion FX reserves.

• In this sense, India should see the least effect from political uncertainty compared to Mexico and South Africa.

## Conclusion

Our previous analysis suggested that it would take roughly three months for GBI-EM investors to cut their overall duration exposure from a very OW level to neutral (see How Long Does it Take for GBI-EM Investors to Cut Their Duration? November 3, 2023).

The country-level analysis points in the same direction: it would take 40 and 36 trading days for investors to cut their duration OW in Mexico and South Africa to neutral, respectively.

To be clear, we don’t think that GBI-EM investors will necessarily do so because cutting duration to neutral would be a big decision which would require some significant change in either the external environment (for example, the Fed and UST yields) or internal factors (such as politics, inflation and fiscal policy).

However, the analysis provides context for the potential positioning overhang should market conditions continue to worsen.
