# Singapore Equity Strategy Mid-Year Outlook | Asia Pacific

# Well Placed to Thrive

Singapore appears well placed as rates stay higher for longer, and we prefer high-yield/low-risk stocks levered to the key themes of AI and Restructuring.

Imminent start to shallow rate cuts: US interest rates are likely to begin falling within the next six months, and markets expect a shallower rate cut cycle than they did before, and for 10-year government bond yields to stay persistently high. A higher-for-longer interest rate outlook bodes well for Singapore equities, which historically outperforms other equity markets across APxJ when US 10Y bond yields are elevated. Beyond the next six months, the global macro outlook for 2025 seems highly uncertain, contingent on unknowns such as the outcome of US elections, and with the market outlook on interest rates continually in flux.

Well placed for uncertainty: Singapore appears well placed to thrive amid a cloud of uncertainty, and we think will perform well in both the Upside and Downside scenarios laid out by our global economics team. In its downside scenario, prolonged inflation pressures lead to higher US interest rates – a positive for Singapore. In its upside scenario, higher China growth leads to, again, higher US interest rates – doubly positive for Singapore. For a variety of potential global outcomes, Singapore offers cheap optionality, given low investor positioning and below-trend multiples (12x P/E, 5% DY). Proposals for increased market participation from growing private, pension and sovereign capital in Singapore are being reviewed by authorities, which if implemented could boost market liquidity and valuation multiples. Our revised MSCI Singapore Index target implies 8% potential upside and 13% total returns over the next 12 months.

Key themes – AI and Restructuring: Within Singapore, we find stocks positively aligned with critical global investment themes such as AI tech diffusion in the form of data centre operators and their power providers. Corporate restructuring is another key theme in place within Singapore that could be a major source of alpha not to be missed. Past examples have delivered \~100% in total returns, and several companies – mostly Temasek owned – are in the midst of their transformations.

Alpha opportunities in quality names: We recommend positioning in sectors and stocks that have positive exposure to the above-mentioned themes, and offer highyield/low-risk characteristics we described in our 2024 outlook. Surviving larger-cap members of the MSCI Singapore index, which was narrowed to a more concentrated group of 16 following recent index rebalancing, are also likely to benefit from rotational allocations. This includes stocks in the banks sector, which we continue to prefer given their strong dividend profiles and resilience to high interest rates. Our Singapore Focus List comprises: DBS, OCBC, ST, SCI, GENS, three of which feature in the ASEAN Best Business Models list.

## Investment Summary

## Well Placed to Thrive

Globally, all is well for the next six months: Higher economic growth, continued disinflation and the start of DM policy rate cuts outline the global macroenvironment for the next six months – a conducive backdrop for risk assets, in particular Europe and Japan equities, according to our Global Strategy team. Singapore's externally dependent economy is set to benefit from a better global growth outlook, as interest rates embark on a steady decline while the SGD currency remains stable (see section: Macro Backdrop: Growth Beta ). Beyond that, however, the outlook in 2025 seems considerably more uncertain, as much is contingent upon the outcome of the US elections, and as the market outlook on interest rates remain in flux.

Singapore to thrive amid uncertainty: Singapore equities offer cheap optionality with the potential to outperform in a variety of scenarios. In our Global economics team's Downside Scenario, persistent inflation in the US leads to push rate cuts to Jun-25 – and Singapore tends to thrive in a higher-for-longer rate environment (see section: Imminent Start to Shallow Rate Cuts ). In its Upside scenario, the team describes a stronger-than-expected recovery in China's nominal GDP growth, leading the US to delay rate cuts to the end of the year to combat higher imported inflation. Given extensive Singapore-China business linkages, the combination of higher China growth and higher US rates is doubly positive for Singapore equities, in our view (see section: Key Risks to Our View ). Meanwhile, active fund positioning in Singapore appears light, while stock valuations are at an attractive starting point with below-trend P/E multiples, and above-trend dividend yields averaging over 5% (see section: Compelling Valuations ). Furthermore, Singapore is studying proposals to boost the local stock market by increasing market participation from the growing pools of private, pension and sovereign capital that have been accumulating. While not in our base case, any effort to boost market liquidity will likely enhance the market's multiples.

Alpha opportunities in quality names: Our Singapore Focus List features the top five stocks (DBS, OCBC, ST, SCI, GENS) we think investors should be positioned in over the next 12 months, three of which are included in Morgan Stanley's ASEAN Best Business Models list of top 10 quality stocks to own for a two-year buy and hold portfolio (see section: Best in ASEAN ). Our selection criteria ( Exhibit 19 ) includes alignment with critical investment themes that are global, such as AI tech diffusion (see section: AI Tech Diffusion: Singapore Beneficiaries ) as well as rather uniquely local, such as corporate restructuring (see section: Restructuring: Singapore Corporates Reinventing Themselves ). All five stocks are surviving members of the MSCI Singapore Index, which we believe will benefit from rotational flows from off-index alternatives, following the stock deletions announced in the recent May-24 index review (see section: Index Rebalancing: Stronger after Losing Weight ). Our preferred sector remains banks, which has, and will likely continue to, fare well as US 10Y yields stay above 4% for the rest of the year (see section: Stock and Sector Preferences ).

## Imminent Start to Shallow Rate Cuts

Rate cuts are coming, or maybe not – wait, yes they are: In the last 12 months, market expectations for the number of policy rate cuts to be delivered by the US Fed has swung back and forth from four to two, to six to one. Global equity markets have whipsawed in unison, but Singapore equities as a whole have held relatively steady. Over this period, the MSCI Singapore Index traded at ±7% vs. MSCI APxJ Index's ±10%, and MSCI All Country World Index's ±12% ( Exhibit 2 ), despite having a more concentrated composition of stocks. Our US Economics team currently expects US Fed to deliver three rate cuts in 2024, and four in 2025 – a shallower rate cut cycle than previously expected as US inflation has proved sticky. It sees the first US rate cut arriving imminently in September, and markets expect chances of that happening to be 50:50 ( Exhibit 3 ). While we are most certainly nearer to a rate cut cycle than we were six months ago, it appears markets are more uncertain on exactly when, and in the meantime Singapore's high dividends and defensive qualities will likely prove valuable.

Singapore to do well as 10Y bond yields stay high for longer: So far this year, US 10Y bond yields have remained at elevated levels of above 4%, a trend likely to persist for the rest of the year, based on projections by our global interest rate strategy team. This bodes well for Singapore equities, which have a tendency to outperform other equity markets across APxJ when real and nominal US 10Y bond yields are high ( Exhibit 4 ).

## Compelling Valuations

Attractive entry point: Positioning in Singapore among active funds appears light given outflows since 2022 ( Exhibit 7 ), while valuation multiples appear attractive relative to history as well as peers in the region.

High dividend yields: Singapore's >5% dividend yield screens as relatively high among developed markets globally ( Exhibit 8 ), in what is likely to be a low-return environment in APxJ over the next 12 months. High and sustained growth in dividends, we believe, can meaningfully bolster portfolio relative total returns for investors, when APxJ equities are projected to be broadly rangebound through June 2025.

Low price to earnings: The MSCI Singapore index trades at a 12x P/E – well below trend and below levels seen in other major DMs ( Exhibit 9 ) and markets across ASEAN ( Exhibit 10 ). Our base case incorporates the conservative assumption that Singapore's P/E multiple stays at 12x, while EPS continue to grow, at an average pace of +8% p.a. in 2024- 26 (2ppt above consensus' +6%). ## Restructuring: Singapore Corporates Reinventing Themselves

Led by Temasek-owned stocks: Stocks substantially owned by Singapore sovereign wealth fund Temasek Holdings account for more than half of the constituents in the MSCI Singapore Index by both count and market cap. The share price performance of these Temasek-owned stocks has had some bearing on government revenue since 2016, when Temasek was included in the Net Investment Returns (NIR) framework. In recent years. we have noted accelerated efforts to maximize shareholder returns from Temasek-owned stocks, through divestments (e.g., NOL in 2016), privatizations (e.g., SPH in 2021), and corporate restructuring (e.g., CapitaLand and Sembcorp Industries in 2020-21).

• SembCorp Industries (Jun-20): SCI in Jun-20 announced it would spin off its 61% stake in SembCorp Marine (SMM, renamed Seatrium) via a distribution in specie, after a 5-for-1 rights issue by SMM. Following the spin-off, SCI shifted its focus to its remaining energy and urban businesses, and growing its sustainable solutions business. From the announcement, the stock delivered 191% in total returns over the year, outperforming the MSCI Singapore index by 174ppt ( Exhibit 11. ).

• CapitaLand (Mar-21): CapitaLand in Mar-21 announced it would effectively give out a 30% special dividend while selling its development business to Temasek. The listed entity that remained was CapitaLand Investment (CLI) – a streamlined, capital-efficient listed real estate investment manager, and one of the largest globally by AUM. Restructuring led CapitaLand to deliver 74% in total returns over the year, outperforming the MSCI Singapore index by 77ppt ( Exhibit 12 ).

Watch for more: Metamorphosed into more competitive form, these companies still have more benefits to reap from their restructuring exercises. Beyond that, we expect to see more corporate restructuring from Temasek-owned stocks. Keppel is transforming into an asset manager, Singtel has been monetizing assets and growing its data centre business as part of a strategic reset, and DBS has been acquiring overseas operations, and had considered spinning off its remittances business. Restructuring amongst non-Temasek companies have also been evident (and we will also likely see more), as companies evolve to adapt to a changing environment and generational shifts in leadership.

## Singtel: Growing Data Centres

Large and growing data centre business: Singtel is committed to growing its S\$5.5bn Regional Data Centre (RDC) business, and plans to deploy more than 100MW of nextgeneration sustainable AI-ready data centres within the next three years.

Securing Power: Singtel has been able to establish strategic partnerships with local power players. For example, in Indonesia it is partnering with Medco Power, and in Thailand with GULF.

Getting AI-ready with NVIDIA: Singtel has joined the NVIDIA Partner Network Cloud Programme and will bring NVIDIA’s full-stack AI platform to enterprises across the ASEAN region, aiming to launch GPUaaS in 3Q24 powered by NVIDIA's chips.

## Sembcorp Industries: Powering Data Centres

AI needs power: AI and data center demand will likely tip ASEAN electricity markets into tightness for much longer than most expect, especially Singapore and Malaysia.

SembCorp provides power: SembCorp operates the largest solar energy portfolio in Singapore, putting it in a solid position for negotiating and contracting renewables power purchase agreements, especially given a growing emphasis on green electron sourcing. SembCorp has signed power contracts with data centre operators Equinix, as well as ST Telemedia for their data centres in Singapore, ASEAN's largest data centre market.

This is nearly twice as large as the smallest constituent prior, and nearly 50% larger than the smallest constituent in each of the Developed Market country indices in the region. Potential for a National Agenda: Singapore is studying proposals to boost the local stock market, which has been struggling with low trading liquidity and delistings frequently outnumbering listings. These include suggestions to: 1) mandate stock market participation from private capital inflows into Singapore; and 2) allowing pension and sovereign money to be invested in the stock market, as seen in Australia or Thailand, according to a May 6, 2024 Financial Times article. Authorities from Singapore's EDB, MAS and MTI are currently reviewing these proposals, set out in a document from the Singapore Venture & Private Capital Association (SVCA), which includes state funds GIC and Temasek, and buyout groups Warburg Pincus and KKR.

Vibrancy = Liquidity Premium: Efforts to boost Singapore stocks' trading liquidity bode well for their valuation multiples, which lag comparable peers listed in more liquid markets such as Australia and the US. A more vibrant stock market is better equipped to attract new listings, which could be large and liquid enough to be included in benchmark indices (e.g., MSCI Singapore index). A deeper and broader market, in turn, enhances the liquidity premium of the stocks within, creating a potential virtuous cycle.

## Key Risks to Our View

US rate cut cycle: Our base case incorporates an imminent but relatively shallow (compared to our earlier assumptions) rate cut cycle in the US beginning in September, but our Global economics team's Upside and Downside Scenarios see US rate cuts being pushed to the end of 2024 and mid-2025, respectively. An even higher-for-longer rate environment in both scenarios likely poses upside risk to the relative performance of Singapore equities, which are heavily skewed toward banks with positive rate exposures. On the other hand, an earlier-than-expected start to the rate cut cycle that may be positive for potential absolute returns, we believe is quite the reverse for potential returns relative to other markets.

China domestic demand: A stronger recovery in China's nominal GDP growth, as policymakers push to rebalance toward consumption leading to faster and more sustainable reflation, will likely be broadly positive for corporate earnings revisions in Singapore, which has significant trade and business linkages with China. A more resilient Chinese economy could feed into higher imported inflation in the US, driving US interest rates to remain higher for longer. The combination of higher China growth and higher US rates is doubly positive for Singapore equities. On the flipside, the risks of China potentially falling deeper into a debt-deflation loop will likely weigh on potential returns in Singapore.

Trade tensions: Recent developments suggest that trade actions are broadening out across geographies, alongside the persistent rise in excess capacities in China’s manufacturing sector. A meaningful rise in trade tensions could curtail the global capex cycle, leading to a weaker exports outlook in Asia and potentially worsening China’s debtdeflation loop,a key risk flagged by our Asia Economics team. The externally dependent economy of Singapore will likely be negatively affected, as will its corporates' earnings and dividends.

Corporate restructuring: Major restructuring moves by Singapore-listed companies have grown in frequency in recent years. While these mostly have positive implications for the affected stocks, they can be negative as well, if the restructuring efforts are deemed to be value-destructive by the market.

Government policy changes: Looser- or tighter-than-expected fiscal, monetary and immigration policy changes could have a better- or worse-than-expected impact on the GDP growth trajectory on which we premise our corporate earnings growth forecasts. Sector-specific changes in regulation could be meaningfully positive (e.g., raising the gearing limit for REITs in 2020) or negative (e.g., capping dividends for banks in 2020). Singapore's policymakers have been quick to respond to a constantly evolving macro situation, and the changing policy environment could pose significant upside and downside risks to our view.

## GDP Growth

Better than we had expected: As a small, open economy, growth prospects in Singapore are firmly anchored toward external developments. As compared to our 2024 outlook, our global economics team is now projecting global growth to be stronger at 3.1% vs. 2.8% previously. With better growth in its trade partners, we also lift Singapore's GDP growth to 2.2% vs. 1.6% previously.

Singapore is syncing up with the rest of the region: Until a couple of months ago, Asia’s growth cycle was asynchronous and largely supported by domestic demand in select economies with structural stories. But a better-than-expected recovery in trade has meant that external demand will now complement domestic demand and will lift the tradedependent economies too. Indeed, Asia's growth will be on a stronger footing,

underpinned by more drivers and more geographies. More economies in the region will be posting a healthy growth trend and Singapore will be one of them too.

External demand strength and its spillovers to domestic demand: In a way, the liftoff in growth is entirely down to a better external demand outlook and how this will spill over to domestic demand. As external demand improves, we expect that it will have its usual spillover effects to domestic demand via stronger capex as well as better labor market conditions, which will support consumption. To be sure, this has not yet panned out in the 1Q24 data, as non-oil domestic exports are still contracting by 3.4% Yoy as compared to - 1.3% YoY in 4Q23. But forward indicators are looking up – as evident in the pick-up in exports growth across North Asia as well as the sustained improvement in PMIs on a yearto-date basis. Our favourite indicator to assess the near-term outlook for the trade cycle is the ratio of global PMI new orders to inventory. While the initial improvement was driven by inventory, the upturn in the past few months has been driven by a stronger trend in new orders.

External demand recovery should be sustained with a better DM capex trend: Fundamentally, Asia’s trade cycle has been highly correlated to the global investment cycle, particularly the US capex cycle. Over the next 18 months, we expect Asia’s exports to be on a gradual recovery path – our global economics team is projecting a modest improvement in the investment cycle for DM, particularly the US. Considering that 30% of Asia’s goods exports are tech, the US IT capex cycle has direct implications for the region’s tech goods exports.

## Inflation

Steady progress on the disinflation front: Headline CPI inflation moderated to 2.7% YoY in March 2024, while MAS core inflation was a tad higher at 3.1% YoY. Monetary policy has clearly done its job as import prices have been contracting on a year-on-year basis in 12 of the past 13 months. Meanwhile, tradables inflation has moderated to levels that are consistent with pre-Covid trends. Hence, while inflation does still sit above the central bank's comfort zone, it is clear that it is the non-tradable sector that is keeping it that way. Even then, signs point toward a further moderation as labor market indicators have been a bit softer, with unemployment rates rising a tad, while wage growth fell to 2.6% YoY in 4Q23 from 7% YoY in 2Q23.

## Interest Rates and Currency

No rush to tighten, no rush to ease: With this inflation backdrop, it should be the case that the MAS will not need to tighten monetary policy. Indeed, the MAS has also been keeping the status quo on its policy parameters and continues to sound confident about the downward descent in inflation. Conversely, we also think that it is in no rush to ease policy either. Monetary easing is usually prompted by a sharp downturn in the global economy, but that is not our global economics team's base case.

Trade tensions are a key risk: Recent developments suggest that trade actions are broadening out across geographies. This reflects concerns over the persistence of China’s excess capacities – something that we have highlighted in previous research. If trade tensions do rise meaningfully from here, we will be watchful of the indirect effects on financial conditions, corporate confidence and capex. Shifting investment flows from supply-chain diversification efforts may provide a partial offset, but the lower demand would still be a net negative.

## Communication Services / Utilities: AI Beneficiaries (+)

Both Singtel and SembCorp Industries could benefit from growing AI tech diffusion, and will likely be resilient in an environment of high bond yields.

With residential developers fully removed from the MSCI Singapore index by June, and US Fed rate cuts potentially nearer in the future than six months ago, we see less reason to underweight the real estate sector. ## MSCI Singapore Index Target: 1,550

We roll forward our MSCI Singapore index target from December 2024 to June 2025, and raise it by 7% to 1,550, representing 8% upside potential. This is based on our expectations for EPS to rise 7% in 2024, and 7-9% p.a. in 2025 and 2026; and a below-trend target forward P/E of 11.8x to factor in a lower earnings growth outlook, albeit from a higher base.

Base case – 1,550 (8% upside): In our base case, we assume the P/E multiple remains at \~12x, 1.0 standard deviations below the historical five-year average of 13.4x. We believe a below-trend P/E multiple appropriately reflects a more normalized pace of forward earnings growth following elevated levels in 2022-23 due to low-base effects from Covid. In this scenario, corporate earnings grow 7-9% p.a. in 2024-26, and the MSCI Singapore index trades to 1,550, 8% above current levels. This is 7% higher than our earlier Dec-24 target of 1,450, as we have rolled over a largely unchanged target multiple to a higher EPS. Our 2025 EPS is 3% higher as we factor in positive earning revisions at the stock level.
