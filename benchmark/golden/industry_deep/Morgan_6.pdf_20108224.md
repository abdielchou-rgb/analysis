# Venture Vision: Do We Need 3000 AI Start-Ups?

No – the power law will take care of that. But even vs. the Internet, AI has 2x as many start-ups and 3x the real dollar funding, with faster valuation expansion than public comps. At the same time foundation models on which start-ups are built are evolving faster than start-ups can pivot.

Last week we published on AI oversupply risks by vertical on a 1 and 3 year view.

TLDR: The part of the AI stack arguably most at risk of oversupply – and not discussed in last week's report – is venture-backed start-ups. This is not surprising: most value in tech cycles tends eventually to accrue to the venture-backed application layer. The rush to find this cycle's AI power law winner is therefore understandable. Yet, versus prior general purpose disruptive technologies, the number of start-ups is around double that of Internet or Mobile during their venture ramp-up. While valuations are lower on average than during the dot-com bubble, the amount of funding in real terms is respectively 3.5x and 5.4x greater for AI than in comparable windows for Internet and Mobile start-ups. AI now represents 20% of all venture/growth funding and nearly 50% of unicorn raises in FY24 to date.

Consequently, if AI resembles anything like the winner-takes-all/most paradigm of internet market share, the scale of investor disappointment could be multiple times greater this cycle. Investors are already becoming more discerning. Successful graduation from Seed to Series A funding within 2 years has fallen from 37% to 12% since 2022. Moreover, with each OpenAI launch event (noted here in November with Custom GPTs) or yesterday's GPT-4o announcements on voice assistants (a segment in which 206 venture-backed companies raised \$700m over the last decade), investors are being reminded with increasing regularity of the challenges of building on top of increasingly cheap, general-purpose and rapidly evolving models. TCP/IP and iOS/Android – despite continuous evolution – were relatively stable foundations on which start-ups could build. AI's foundations are far less so.

Internet saw key Enabler valuations expand faster than privates: Four years into the internet wave, in 1998, public telcos still dwarfed the internet disruptors. In 1998 Amazon’s market cap was sub-\$10 billion (it took until early 2003 to get back above this level) vs AT&T’s \$150 billion and Cisco’s at \$80 billion. Google was valued at less than \$10 million. The following year in 1999, the top-10 public companies featured multiple Internet enabler/adopters (see table below). Sun Microsystems – creators of Java – peaked at a \$200bn valuation before eventually being sold 97% lower. Compounded over the long term, AT&T is now 37% below its dotcom peak, Cisco 65% lower. Cisco's forward PE peaked at 106x on a market cap over \$500bn; the stocks is now trading at 13x forward and sub \$200bn.

AI is seeing private valuations expand faster than key Enabler stocks: In many respects – despite consistent comparisons to dotcom – the public/private dynamic in markets now feels very different compared to the period of Internet start-up funding. NVIDIA peaked at 62x forward earnings a year ago (and has now de-rated to close to half this level) but the shares have rallied c220% since on strong upgrades. Many public AI enablers' balance sheets are net cash; a luxury the telcos and internet enablers did not have in 2000. Broader than just NVIDIA, public market AI Enabler stocks have rallied; some with clearer earnings upgrade exposure than others. ## The Fintech Radar (12 May 2024)

• #1 Monzo secures £340m in new funding: Following press speculation last week, Monzo has confirmed it has raised £340m in new investment, in a round led by new investor CapitalG (Alphabet's growth fund), which has previously backed the likes of Stripe and Airbnb. Other investors such as Google Ventures, HongShan Capital, Passion Capital, and Tencent also participated in the latest funding round. This values Monzo at a pre-money valuation of £3.6bn, or c. £4bn post-money. The new investment will help fuel Monzo's ambitious growth plans, as it looks to grow its already large customer base of 9 million (up 2 million last year alone). In an interview with CNBC, CEO TS Anil stated that the firm plans to use the cash to build new products (notably pensions and mortgages) and fuel its international expansion. According to CNBC, the US market remains a priority, and a portion of this cash will be allocated towards helping Monzo expand into the US. The company recently (October 2023) hired Conor Walsh, a former executive at Block’s Cash App division, as its new US CEO.

• #2 iwoca secures £270m in debt funding: This week (7th May), UK-based SME lender iwoca has announced that it has secured a new £270m debt funding package, taking the total gross investment in the business to over £1bn. £150m of this was provided by Citibank and Insight Investment to support the company's growth ambitions in Germany, while £120m was committed by Barclays and Värde for the UK business, as it seeks to cater to the increasing demand for SME finance. This new investment follows £200m of funding from Barclays and Värde in October 2023, and £170m from Pollen Street Capital in January 2023. Since launching in 2012, iwoca has provided £3bn in loans to SMEs in the UK and Germany in need of working capital. According to the company, this latest funding comes as data shows more high street lenders are reducing funding to SMEs. iwoca's latest research finds that c. 76% of SME finance brokers report that high street banks are reducing SME funding, despite demand for finance increasing (c. 86% expect demand to increase over the next 6 months).

## Real-time Valuation Monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
