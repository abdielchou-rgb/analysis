## Market Overview: Idiosyncratic

Our positive stance in frontier local markets is paying off. We have been highlighting that frontier local markets are once again mostly uncorrelated to short-term global financial stresses. This is because many frontier economies have received substantial support from multilateral development banks, while a number are now reaping the benefits of implementing the crisis response toolkit of tightening monetary policy substantially and allowing exchange rates to adjust. Furthermore, global themes of lower inflation, approaching DM central bank easing and resilient growth should bode well for frontier markets. We expect these themes to continue playing out even as we anticipate the re-opening of key frontier local markets in Egypt and Nigeria.

We expect frontier local markets to deliver strong returns this year. Only two frontier local markets within our coverage delivered negative returns over the past month - Egypt and Nigeria. We expect both EGP and NGN will soon join the postive return party as policymakers continue to deliver on reform that improves the functioning of their foreign exchange markets, attracts external financing and tightens monetary policy sufficiently (see more below and in country pages). Elsewhere, we carried out some housekeeping in the last compass and took profits on our long-standing short USD/KZT and long USD/KES positions. However, we have since entered a new long infrastructure bond trade in Kenya, as the country<sup>’</sup>s external position continues to improve.

Frontier markets are back to being a group of very different idiosyncratic themes. After a number of years being dominated by the debt restructuring narrative, frontier markets are swiftly moving towards a more diverse set of idiosyncratic drivers, as it was prior to COVID-19 and the Russia-Ukraine conflict. We provide a quick summary of key and relevant developments in frontier local markets below:

Egypt: USD/EGP NDFs are now pricing a much lower spot rate over coming months due to larger-than-expected external funding and a likely announcement of a revamped IMF programme in coming weeks. The initial US\$10bn of funds from the UAE (for purchase of land and real estate) should give the CBE enough firepower to clear most of the FX backlog and manage the transition to a more flexible FX market.

Nigeria: Despite a few false starts due to the sequencing of FX and monetary policy reforms, we expect NGN to eventually become an attractive investment proposition in coming weeks. The central bank has unwound previous reforms which hindered the functioning of the FX market. Tighter monetary policy and short-end T-bill rates in the mid 20% should see an increase in investor appetite.

Kenya: We are long KENIBs as the central bank only just concluded its tightening cycle and as the external environment has become more favourable to the Kenyans. USD/KES is back to being slightly overvalued due to substantial appreciation related to portfolio inflows. The CBK has now stabilized the exchange rate.

Zambia: The kwacha has had an even larger appreciation against the US dollar over recent weeks as the combination of BoZ selling intervention and substantial tightening of monetary policy conditions led to an improvement in confidence. Also, the potential resolution to the impasse between the official creditors, commercial creditors and the Zambian government, should help boost confidence.

• Pakistan: The dust may be settling after national elections held earlier this month, but political risks still remain somewhat elevated. This is because while the

incumbent PML-N and PPP parties appear to have formed a coalition (both did not win the majority of seats by themselves), further infighting about key positions in the government could delay prospects of a new IMF deal.

Our net risk-reward score remains in positive territory, though it has deteriorated since our last Compass. The top-three ranked Frontier countries in the scorecard are now GHS, UZS and NGN, while the lowest-ranked frontier countries are ZMW, LKR and UAH (Figure 2Lates rnkigof /wd c). The NGN reward score has fallen back down from its January highs, after a normalisation in carry: as measured by 3m NDF implied yields, NGN<sup>’</sup>s carry fell from a peak of over 250% (which it reached on 31 January, after having been persistently above 50% since October 2023) to more pedestrian levels in the 10%-20% range. GHS, meanwhile, has seen a steadily rising reward score. In Ghana<sup>’</sup>s case, the gain since our last Compass has come from a positive terms-of-trade shock, driven by soaring cocoa prices. Since the start of 2024, cocoa futures prices have risen over 60%.

Likely prior actions are being taken ahead of resumption of the stalled IMF programme although waiting for a restoration of a functioning FX market. The past few weeks have given indications of a clear re-engagement of Egyptian authorities with the IMF. The CBE hiked the policy rate by 200bp at its first MPC meeting, while the IMF has also confirmed, following its latest mission that it has made excellent progress with authorities on discussions for a comprehensive policy package needed to reach a staff level agreement (link). The government also announced a 15% cut for the FY24 investment budget (current year) together with the removal of tax exemptions for SOEs and the plan to bring all their spending within the government umbrella within five years. We think the policy package will remain anchored on tighter monetary policy, as we now expect another 200bp hike once FX adjustment and market functionality is restored, as well as tighter fiscal policies. The recent mega-real estate deal for US\$35bn would likely be helping to unify rates.

FX market reopening path remains somewhat uncertain. We now expect authorities to manage the process of re-opening by allowing some FX flexibility while at the same time providing dollar liquidity to help clear the backlog, thus reducing the potential for an unbounded upward spiral. We maintain our end-1Q24 USD/EGP forecast of 38.50 for now as the recent mega real estate deal could help clear the backlog.

Continued monetary tightening and improved FX liquidity along with base effects should continue to aid disinflation. As much as we believe an official rate adjustment could lead to some immediate one-off increases, we also believe that restoring a functioning FX market would be highly beneficial for inflation dynamics.

After a deep contraction in DomRep’s GDP early last year, the economy has since accelerated sharply, with 4Q GDP growth hitting 7.7%q/q, saar. Even the slowermoving over-year-ago growth rate picked up substantially by the end of last year, sitting at 4.7% in December, just a whisker away from potential growth (5%). We expect momentum to continue in 1H24, paving the way for a 6%y/y expansion in 2024. Going into next week<sup>’</sup>s BCRD meeting we see no need to change our view for the rate to remain steady at 7% at least until June, when we expect rate cuts to resume. From a growth perspective, earlier easing is still working its way through the system and propping activity; credit continue to far outpace nominal GDP in terms of growth rates. In turn, inflation remains well under control, dipping further below the 4% midpoint target in January. Against this backdrop, BCRD remains squarely focused on external conditions, what is to say, on Fed policy. The spread with the US is already low, and earlier easing led to pressure on the currency. With domestic conditions doing fine, we expect BCRD to sit on the sidelines with a wary eye on US monetary policy before resuming a (incomplete) convergence of the policy rate to neutrality.

We hold Jan26 CB bonds, FX unhedged. In FX, after the December and January DOP sell-off, we have seen a stable currency even amid broad dollar strength and US yields rising. This is likely to continue amid BCRD cautious pause. Terms of trade and remittances remain supportive, allowing for some correction in the current account deficit. GBI-EM DomRep bonds are stronger on the year, outperforming in EM, highlighting the diversification potential of some of these stronger frontier names. Yields in real terms remain appealing, and we continue to hold Jan26 CB bonds.

## Frontier market risk-reward scorecard

Our net risk-reward score remains in positive territory, though it has deteriorated since our last Compass. The top-three ranked Frontier countries in the scorecard are: GHS, UZS and NGN. The lowest-ranked frontier countries are ZMW, LKR and UAH.

The NGN reward score has fallen back down from its January highs, after a normalisation in carry. Measured by 3m NDF implied yield, NGN<sup>’</sup>s carry fell from a peak of above 250% (reached on 31 January) to more pedestrian levels in the 10%- 20% range.

GHS, meanwhile, has seen a steadily rising reward score. In Ghana<sup>’</sup>s case, the gain since our last Compass has come from a positive terms-of-trade shock, driven mainly by soaring cocoa prices. Since the start of 2024, cocoa futures prices have risen over 60%.
