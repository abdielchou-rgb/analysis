# Agricultural Commodities Update

## February WASDE data wrap

At face value, the USDA<sup>’</sup>s February WASDE was bearish CBOT Soybeans; neutral/bearish CBOT Corn and CBOT Wheat; and bullish ICE #2 Cotton. The focus remains on South American weather, particularly on Brazilian safrinha corn production potential. Although the USDA has slightly reduced its estimate for Brazil<sup>’</sup>s soybean supply, significant reductions in the future iterations could be expected as the early harvest yields continue to disappoint.

0 US ending stocks increased on the month for soybean, corn and wheat, surpassing the trade expectations, driven by cuts in US soybean exports and lower consumption estimates for wheat and corn. CBOT grains, meals and beans finished the session down on the day.

Key upcoming events to monitor include the USDA<sup>’</sup>s Outlook Forum on February 15th and 16th and the release of the Malaysian Palm Oil Board January fundamental update on February 13th. Expectations call for a 9% MoM reduction in inventories due to decreased production amid wet weather.

Trade recommendations: We stay long the agri complex via an index. Our price forecasts sit above futures curves, and markets are discounting supply-side and geopolitical risks, which remain heightened in our view.

USDA world corn inventories declined by 1% on the month to 322 million tonnes, with decreases in Brazil, Mexico, and Serbia production partially offset by increases in India and Turkey. US exports remained constant at 2.1 billion bu, contrary to trade expectations. While, Ukraine saw a 2 million tonne increase in exports. EU imports were revised downward by 0.5 million tonnes, but still remained relatively high, while China<sup>’</sup>s imports remained unchanged (5 million tonnes above government<sup>’</sup>s estimates).

In Brazil, although safrinha plantings are progressing well, lower corn prices may deter farmers from planting, as evidenced by lower-than-expected seed sales. USDA has lowered Brazil<sup>’</sup>s corn planted acreage to 21.9 million ac (down 0.5 million ac) prompting production cuts of 3 million tonnes to 124 million tonnes while exports were further reduced by 2 million tonnes to 52 million tonnes. CONAB's fourth assessment for the 2023/24 crop revealed a 3.3% decrease in total corn crop production to 113.7 million ac, leaving the gap between the two data releases at over 10 million tonnes. The downgrade in the production was mainly on 3.3% MoM decline in safrinha corn crop planted area to 15.9 million ha.

Global soybean ending inventories increased by 1.2% MoM to 116 million tonnes, driven by higher beginning stocks, primarily from Brazil. US exports declined by 2% MoM to 1.72 billion bu compared to the trade expectations of 1.742 billion bu, while China<sup>’</sup>s imports were kept unchanged. Brazilian old crop was increased by 2 million tonnes to 162 million tonnes and new crop was decreased by only 1 million tonne to 156 million tonnes, against the trade expectations of 153.7 million tonnes. While, Brazil<sup>’</sup>s CONAB lowered its production projections for 20234/24 soybean crop by 3.8% to 149.4 million tonnes, reflecting 3.4% contraction in yield estimate. The soybean is 16% harvested as of late last week as per AgRural, compared to 9% last year and an average of 10%. While the harvesting pace remains strong, early crop yields are disappointing due to adverse weather conditions from October to November. The yield of later-developing soybeans is anticipated to be higher, but this remains uncertain until harvest.

Global wheat ending stocks saw a slight decrease to 259 million tonnes. India<sup>’</sup>s consumption increased by 1.1 million tonnes while the production was kept constant, despite extreme heat affecting key growing regions. Argentina<sup>’</sup>s production and exports were raised by 0.5 million tonnes each. Brazil<sup>’</sup>s production was lowered by 300k tonnes to 8.1 million tonnes, while, CONAB increased its production estimate by 2 million tonnes to 10.2 million tonnes. EU wheat imports and feeding increased by 2.5 and 1 million tonnes, respectively. Despite ample inventories in the EU and Russia, stocks remain fairly tight in the US and Canada. Furthermore, the wheat planted area is expected to decline in EU, US and Ukraine for next season.

Global cotton ending stocks decreased by 0.8% to 83.7 million tonnes, driven by lower beginning stocks and production estimates. Global consumption remained relatively stable, with increases in China and Vietnam offsetting declines in the US, Turkey, and Thailand. US cotton exports were increased by 1.7% MoM to 12.3 million bales as US exports sales continue to improve with total commitments at 10 million bales compared to 9.7 million bales last year. US ending stocks currently stand at 2.8 million bales, the lowest since 2016/17.

When the z-score (indicator of momentum) reached 0, known as switch point, it often triggers a change in signal from buy-to-sell, on sell-to-buy received by stochastic price/trend following trading models

This can drive heightened price volatility in the underlying commodity price when there is a large presence of CTA/trend-following trading models
