## Overseas exposure of Japanese companies is around 40- 50%; rising in recent years even for non-manufacturers

Overall, Japanese companies generate 56.7% of revenues from the domestic market, and 43.3% from overseas ( Exhibit 3 ). Among overseas markets, the highest exposure is to US & Canada, at 16.5%, followed by Asia-Pacific (excluding Japan, China) at 11.4%, developed Europe at 6.9%, and China at 4.4%. Exposure to EEMEA and Latin America is limited.

Looking at historical trends, exposure over the past 10 years or so has trended in the mid-50% range for the domestic market and the mid-40% range for overseas ( Exhibit 4 ). In 2024, the figures are, respectively, 57% and 43%, but if we adjust our coverage in line with 2023 (greater skew towards large caps), the figures become 52% and 48%. Exposure to specific overseas markets in 2024 is 18% for the Americas , 7% for Europe, 11% for APAC (ex Japan, China), and 4% for China.

Exhibit 4: Global exposure trends

![](images/8f53fce8eeabefd73e70ee270cce5e1f30e23213ee9416db6f05c74658cd33fb.jpg)

Exposure within the manufacturing sector has trended around 40% for Japan and 60% for overseas ( Exhibit 5 ). In 2024, the figures are 42% and 58%, but overseas exposure rises to 64% if the coverage is adjusted in line with 2023. In terms of specific overseas markets, the highest is for the Americas, at 23%, followed by APAC (ex. Japan, China) at 16%, and developed Europe at 9%.

For non-manufacturers, exposure to overseas markets is still below exposure to Japan, but interestingly it has been rising gradually, from 22% in 2016 to 29% in 2023 ( Exhibit 6 ). Although it falls to 23% in 2024, adjusting our coverage to exclude names not covered in 2023 results in overseas exposure of 27%. The rise in the overseas ratio mainly reflects a higher exposure to the Americas.

## Exposure by sector

In this section, we examine exposure to each country/region by sector, based on our F2024 revenue estimates.

Overseas ( Exhibit 7 ): Exposure is highest for mining, transportation equipment, rubber products, machinery, and precision instruments, all at 70% or above. Sectors with low exposure (single digits) include electric power & gas, land transportation, warehousing & harbor transportation, and real estate.

US ( Exhibit 8 ): Exposure is high for transportation equipment, pharmaceuticals, rubber products, other products, and machinery. The ratio in the transportation equipment and pharmaceutical sectors is especially high, at 30%+. Retail exposure is 22%, the only nonmanufacturing sector to have exposure that is higher than the overall figure of 16%.

Developed Europe ( Exhibit 9 ): Exposure is high for other products, pharmaceuticals, precision instruments, electric appliances, and rubber products. Among nonmanufacturers, exposure is relatively high for mining (12%), food (9%), and wholesale (9%).

EEMEA ( Exhibit 10 ): Exposure in the mining sector is conspicuously high, but limited elsewhere. INPEX (1605) has a considerable impact on the mining sector.

China (including Hong Kong, Macau; Exhibit 11 ): Exposure is highest for textiles & apparel, followed by electric appliances, precision instruments, and non-ferrous metals. Toray (3402) has a considerable impact on the textiles & apparel sector. Revenue exposure in the transportation equipment sector is not especially high, at 4%, because auto makers operations in China are conducted through JVs.

APAC (ex Japan, China; Exhibit 12 ): Sectors with high exposure are mining, chemicals, transportation equipment, and electric appliances.
