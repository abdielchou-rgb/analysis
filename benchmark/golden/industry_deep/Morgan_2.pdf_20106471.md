# AI: Adopters Are Adopting

We have argued that 2024 will be the "Year of the Adopters". The market is still treating this thesis as a "show me" story; Enablers are still driving returns. Yet, be it Consumer or - more importantly - Enterprise adoption, monthly data points we track suggest AI adoption is continuing at pace.

"Once an innovation delivers a competitive advantage everyone must either adopt it, leapfrog it, switch focus or lose market share and eventually go bust." Mustafa Suleyman, Co-Founder of DeepMind.

This comment neatly encapsulates the thinking and urgency behind our call for 2024 being the "Year of the Adopter". No one individual datapoint can answer the question of where we are in this journey, but by joining various consumer and enterprise dots, we are able to gauge direction of travel and momentum:

• First movers are causing divergences: There are a widening array of consumer-facing chatbots (ChatGPT, Bard/Gemini, Claude, Perplexity etc) and yet OpenAI's lead with consumers remains commanding...albeit yet to make new highs after the Christmas break ( Exhibit 7 ). Developers present the best case study for early adoption. The prospect of >50% productivity gains is driving ever increasing traffic to Github Copilot even as Stack Overflow responded by integrating its own AI assistant in 4Q23 ( Exhibit 1 ).

• Open source model listings surpass half a million: From a more enterprise perspective, models listed on Hugging Face - an open source model repository - have surpassed 500,000 and datasets used for fine-tuning them have surpassed 100,000. The people or companies downloading these models are likely using them as starting points for their own domain-specific models, fine-tuning of which requires GPU capacity ( Exhibit 1 ).

• Open source model downloads by Adopters rising 120% YoY: While anyone can download these models we consider the direction of travel as somewhat of a proxy for enterprise trial-and-error and/or Adoption. We cannot deduce when prototype moves to production. Nonetheless, model downloads are running at 120% YoY, but of which the top 10 models are seeing the lions' share of downloads (>53% share) and running at a 300% YoY download growth rate ( Exhibit 16 ).

• Text models now less than 50% of models vs multi-modal: Multi-modal models (i.e. those capable of ingesting and outputting a combination of text, image, audio and/or video) now make up half of the number of models listed available for download by Adopter companies, from >15% two years ago ( Exhibit 18 ). Their applications range from disruption to the music industry, applications in autonomous driving, machine vision in healthcare, targeted advertising, social media influencing or even humanoid robotics.

## The "Year of the Adopter" Thesis Recap

The current technology shift is progressing faster than any before it. Companies that best adopt new technology to scale their businesses while maintaining robust barriers to entry accrue most value ( Exhibit 1 ). In this context, we draw investors and corporates to three main conclusions from our inaugural mapping exercise:

1. 2024 – the year of the Adopters: The Enablers gained >\$6trillion of market cap in 2023. Our analysts see less upside for these names in the near term. In 2024, we argue that investors should focus on the Adopters, a group that rose only c6% in 2023. If our Adopter companies were to lever up to 2x Net Debt/EBITDA for incremental AI investment, they could spend >\$3 trillion on the Enablers' hardware and software; 39% of the AI Enablers' 2024 estimated revenue. Adopters hold the keys to their own product ramp as well as fundamentals of the Enablers.

2. AI rate of change will be important for re-ratings: In 2023, the Enablers where AI was considered Moderately important to the stock debate increased their total market cap by c28%. If this group were to continue to mix shift towards Core To Thesis materiality, it could add another 41% or \$900bn to market caps of this group based on 2023 performance.

3. Companies enumerating AI spend or gains tend to be rewarded: During 2023, only 15% of 316 companies over \$10bn market cap gave quantified AI disclosure (i.e. dollar spend, revenue impact, percentage productivity gains). While this may not be entirely attributable to the AI impact, those that did give disclosure outperformed those that didn't (here).

## Data Show Rapid AI Adoption

There is no one catch-all metric for tracking enterprise adoption of AI. We favour a combination of the below to track progress through the "Year of the Adopter":

• (1) Search interest by individuals (consumers & in enterprise) - whilst harder to disaggregate casual users from students from employees using it to help gain a productivity advantage at work, the data are nonetheless useful to show direction of travel from a high level perspective. We show this in more detail in our Emerging Theme Tracker.

• (2) Quantity of foundational models - this can be tracked by models listed and downloaded as well as web traffic to major model platforms. We can also glean detail from the number of datasets uploaded and downloaded from which models can be fine-tuned.

• (3) Quality & capabilities of foundational models - the convergence between open source and proprietary foundational model capabilities (shown below) means the landscape of foundational models on which Adopters are building new products is continuously evolving from a quality and cost perspective. Adopters are arguably best off diversifying their AI product build across both open and closed models.

• (4) Earnings calls or newsflow quantification - this has continued to ramp up through FY23 results in January/February and we are tracking the quantified uplifts to both revenue and - more frequently - cost implications of AI Adoption for enterprise.

• (5) CIO survey - As our latest survey shows, 29% of CIOs foresee this adoption spend coming from net new budget. And while 6% of CIOs have delayed their AI product launch expectations beyond 2H24 relative to the prior survey, AI ranks as the top priority for CIOs.

## Pushback to the above data include the following:

• These data include universities, students and individuals experimenting with AI models. This can give an overinflated view of enterprise adoption.

• The ebbs and flows between open and closed model preference by enterprise adopters (due to cost or quality of foundational models) may give a false impression of speed of adoption ( Exhibit 15 ).

• There will be a long tail of models which will see very few downloads and are therefore noise rather than signal when it comes to the true picture of Adoption.

• By only using the data from one vendor - Hugging Face - growth in models listed will also reflect their own market share gain or loss as a variable not easily extractable from these data.

• For inference/hyperscaler stocks (rather than GPU players) it could be a false or misleading correlation. The vast majority of what makes up cloud generate AI revenues is inference against the existing models; nothing to do with downloads from Hugging Face.

Exhibit 15: Open source model quality converging with closed models (5-shot MMLU Performance)
![](images/3bc7353e6c5087c652ed0b0bf8fa1219673c53ac1a1103701026c13c73402a18.jpg)
Source: Hugging Face, X, Ark, Morgan Stanley Research

While justified, these push-backs can largely be addressed by focussing on the top-10 models listed and downloaded on Hugging Face. The top 10 open source models represent >50% of downloads and this proportion is rising. Moreover, trial-and-error can be argued to be broadening given the number of models downloaded per visit to the site has more than doubled from 1.5 a year ago to over 3 now. The growth in these aggregate top 10 models is also growing at >300% YoY versus a more modest 120% YoY growth in downloads for the top 100 models.

## (3) Applications are Broadening (Enterprise Adoption)

Not only have the number of models available for download been increasing exponentially, the mix of models which can be leveraged by adopter companies have broadened similarly rapidly.

As recently as 2022, over 70% of models available for download involved text classification, summarisation, generation or translation. The early signs of diversification came in text to image applications. Consumer facing bots leveraging these models include DALL.E, Midjourney or Stable Diffusion. Their output is already regularly applied in the fields of marketing and advertising, the creation of personalised targeted ads for social media and even social media influencing.

Multi-modal models (i.e. those capable of ingesting and outputting a combination of text, image, audio and/or video) were a rarity in both the proprietary and open source fields in 2021/22. These types of models now make up as much as half of the number of models listed on Hugging Face. Their applications range from disruption to the music industry, applications in autonomous driving, machine vision for healthcare applications or humanoid manufacturing robotics.

Exhibit 18: Breadth of absolute and relative number of models by modality is expanding rapidly
![](images/991b84091808745063ae82cc90a36d03be866fc7c3e312efd56b6bd1e8113313.jpg)

Our point here is that the menu of options that Adopter companies now have readily and cheaply available to them to explore both revenue-uplift and cost-saving opportunities continues to broaden. Not all of these applications will manifest in an AI tool with positive ROI for adopter companies. The success or otherwise of this adoption will become clearer over time as we are able to track the operational gearing of adopter companies.

## (4) Earnings calls quantification (Enterprise Adoption)

As we have previously show (here and here), companies that enumerate their spend on AI or ideally the upside they expect on top- and bottom-line from AI adoption, tend to outperform those that do not. Consequently, we have been tracking companies and the announcements made across all aspects of their businesses. The majority of announcements show the following patterns:

• They overwhelmingly focus on productivity and cost savings rather than revenue upside from new products or better cross-selling.

• The most frequently cited efficiency and cost gain mentioned by companies is in developer coding efficiency, customer service cost saving or working capital management.

• The majority of figures cited by companies are double digit gains relative to the status quo for that business. This is also evident from academic research across various professions undertaken in the past 12 months.

• Overwhelmingly, willingness to discuss and quantification of AI adoption is highest within the pharmaceutical and medtech spaces above any other Adopter sector.

## (5) CIO Survey findings (Enterprise Adoption)

In 2024, Artificial Intelligence emerges as #1 on the CIO priority list (\~13% of CIOs citing it as a top priority). DW/BI/Analytics (8.3% of CIOs citing it as a top priority) comes in at #2 in 4Q, a meaningful jump from #5 in 3Q. Security Software (8.0% of responses), Digital Transformation (8.0% of responses), and Cloud Computing (7.3% of responses) round out the top 5 CIO priorities. Artificial Intelligence/Machine Learning realized the largest QoQ increase in prioritization (+330 bps), and the highest net prioritization score in the history of our survey.

An increased proportion of CIOs are indicating that recent innovations in AI and Large Language Models are impacting IT investment priorities.
