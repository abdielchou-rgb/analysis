## Investors Could Be Discussing Below-Target Inflation Soon

The surprisingly high and way-above-central-bank-target inflation of the past two years came about through a "perfect storm" combination of pandemic-related reduced supply of goods and services, overly long-lasting extremely accommodative central bank policy, and heightened demand from post-pandemic reopening and overzealous fiscal support.

Each of those factors faded quickly over the past year and, by some measures, have reversed course. Very little evidence suggests supply chain issues remain, either on the goods or services side. Extremely accommodative monetary policy moved quickly to restrictive policy. And the stance of fiscal policy changed from accommodative to neutral and, on some accounts, restrictive.

Look at US monetary policy, for example. The Fed has kept its target rate range at 5.25- 5.50% over the past year, since July 2023. In the same year-long period, US core CPI inflation fell by 1.4pp, from 4.7% to 3.3% Y/Y. That suggests Fed policy actually tightened by 140bp in real terms over the past 12 months. In other words, the Fed took its real policy rate to levels not seen since the early 1980s just over the past year (see Exhibit 1 ).

Source: Morgan Stanley Research, Federal Reserve, FRB New York, BEA, Bloomberg \* Projection assumes r\* remains unchanged from its last level through the projected horizon.

For the "long and variable lag" monetary policy acolytes, worries should mount. Our economists see US core PCE inflation falling back to 2% in the second half of 2024 on 3- and 6-month annualized rates of change. A closer inspection of their forecasts shows that both 3- and 6-month annualized rates of core PCE inflation actually dip below 2 percent (see Exhibit 2 and Exhibit 3 ).

Today, monetary policy looks positioned for about half the restrictiveness - in real terms - as under Chair Volcker (see Exhibit 1 again), but only for a third of the inflation of the 1980s (\~9% back then vs. \~3% today). That alone should get investors nervous about the Fed's restrictive monetary policy overstaying its welcome.

Couple that thought with our economists' inflation forecast for sub-2% core PCE inflation on 3- and 6-month annualized rates of change, and you have the makings of a belowtarget disinflation narrative. Don't forget the dramatic swing in the fiscal impulse in cash flow accounting terms - the cash flow deficit is down 19% over the past 6 months and down 49% over the past 3 months vs. last year (see Exhibit 4 and Exhibit 5 ).

Still, investors may recall the Fed's perceived policy making modus operandi weighs realized data more than forecasted data. As such, we expect investors will increasingly embrace the nascent US yield curve steepening trend we flagged in Time to Enter UST Steepeners more than US duration longs.

## What is Going On with USD/JPY?

USD/JPY has been deviating from our medium-term outlook of late (3Q24 at 149 and 4Q24 at 146), although things have actually gone more or less as we had envisaged with regard to both economic data and market pricing, with the latter now pointing to a fair value for USD/JPY at around 145 (see Exhibit 6 ).

We presented a detailed analysis of possible reasons for this “anomaly” in JPY | Strong Demand For Carry, but our basic conclusion was that expectations of the Fed taking a “higher for longer” approach were fueling demand for JPY carry trades.

We had indicated in our Mid-Year Outlook report that we expected strong demand for JPY carry trades to keep USD/JPY higher than warranted by the envisaged Fed rate-cut trajectory (see Exhibit 7 ). But it appears as though we had significantly underestimated just how strong such demand might be.

Market pricing for 2H 2024 has, in fact, barely changed despite the recent weakness of economic indicators (see Exhibit 80 ). This has contributed to keep USD/JPY implied volatility low, which is serving to make carry appear all the more attractive on a riskadjusted basis.

Markets are as yet only pricing in around five Fed rate cuts within the next year, but our economists expect to see a total of seven. We thus see ample scope for a deeper rate cut trajectory to be priced in if the Fed ends up using its July FOMC meeting to start laying the groundwork for a September commencement of easing.

Risk-adjusted expected carry is liable to plunge if volatility rises/carry decreases in reaction to expectations of the Fed turning more dovish. Uncertainty around a possible MoF intervention also pushes up the near-term expected volatility. This causes at least somewhat of an unwinding of carry trades, such as was previously seen in 2H 2023 and before that in 2H 2022.

To summarize, we are sticking with our medium-term forecasts for USD/JPY in the belief that (1) fundamental narrative remains essentially unchanged, and (2) current divergence from fair value is indeed a predominantly “speculative” phenomenon rather than a structural issue.

## Is a Weaker USD Sustainable?

Global currencies have all had a pretty good run against USD so far in July, with USD one of the weakest currencies in the world. EMs in particular are outperforming, though GBP, JPY and AUD all feature in the top ten returning currencies MTD. Only NOK has weakened vs USD.

US data have been unquestionably soft, across both prices and real activity, and a 25bp cut from the FOMC is now fully priced for Sept. 10y UST yields continue to fall. Notably, USD has weakened even as the market has judged a rising probability of a Republican win in November ( Exhibit 12 ), something that ought to put upward pressure on USD, at least according to our view on the effect Republican policies would likely have.

However, at the same time, developments in the major economies of the rest of the world hardly point to a strengthening of currencies vs USD either.

This suggest to us that a global move lower in bond yields is likely more sustainable than the move lower in USD.

In EM in particular, we continue to think that the better trade is to position for lower yields rather than stronger currencies.

It's clear that global growth is slowing: Economic surprise indices have been trending lower in unison since April ( Exhibit 10 ). Most recent data from China showing a weak credit impulse, weak imports, continued deflationary pressures and a pull back in PMIs paint a weak domestic demand picture. Rate cuts are likely and we see more USD/CNH upside. This week's ECB meeting may be fairly uneventful, but some dovish commentary could come on the back of growth tracking weaker in Q2 versus ECB expectations (MS tracking at 0.2% vs 0.4% expected by the ECB).

With activity data also suggesting a slowing US economy, global growth is decelerating, which is not typically an environment where the USD weakens meaningfully.

Global inflation is also soft: US and EM core CPI continue their march lower, with US data turning sharply in the past few months ( Exhibit 11 ). Fed commentary has turned more dovish, but the same should be the case over time in the rest of the world where inflation is generally heading down as well. Across EM, core inflation readings for June have been soft, maintaining recent trends.

These global inflation trends combined with recent USD and UST price signals, should help to give central banks space to adjust their commentary in a more dovish direction.

More dovish central banks outside of the US should help to prevent rate differentials from moving in a USD supportive direction for much longer.

The prospect of easier monetary policy may well take the edge off the growth weakness for markets, as investors should start to assume any growth weakness will be moderate and short-lived. This could support risk appetite and risk sensitive currencies. But for a sustained weakening in USD, we need rate differentials to move in a weaker USD direction. This is not guaranteed, and in fact we see UST-Bund yields moving in a USD supportive direction over time with ECB pricing for 2025 too hawkish in our view.

Recent news on the wage front has been generally encouraging, with BoJ branch managers reporting at their quarterly meeting that wage hike momentum has started to build even for those smaller firms that were not represented in the spring labor management pay negotiations, and the May Monthly Labour Survey results showing significant growth in scheduled cash earnings for continuously sampled establishments.

However, we are mindful of the potential for the 2-5y sector to underperform if the BoJ does end up hiking this month as envisaged by our economists or even just if speculative reports during the lead-up to the July 30-31 meeting cause the banking sector to limit its buying.

Continuing cheapness of the 10y sector on the curve is likely a reflection of (1) expectations that BoJ absorption will be dialed back significantly and (2) only limited demand from domestic investors. A continuing equities rally will have left pension funds needing to rebalance out of stocks into domestic bonds, and a recent media report suggests that portfolio adjustments are now being made on a weekly rather than monthly basis (in which case we might not need to wait until almost the end of July for such flows to have an impact).

With the external climate and the prospect for pension fund portfolio rebalancing looking conducive for a flattening of the curve, we see a risk of the long-term sector strengthening in the near term and maintain long 20y JGBs on 5s20s30s fly.

## G10 | Unpacking the G10 snooze

Excitement is probably not a word many would use to characterize G10 currency markets this year. We provide some explanations on what may have contributed to the relatively muted trading environment. Potential drivers include (1) elevated G10 correlations to the broad USD; (2) limited volatility in terminal rate expectations of G10 central bank cutting cycles; and (3) relatively muted global flow data.

## Europe

## EUR | The beach is overrated

The quantity of European growth has improved relative to consensus and ECB expectations. Growth improvement coupled with cautious ECB tones on rate cuts has breathed new life into EUR-bullish views.

But the quality of growth is important too. The engine of Europe's recent recovery has been led by low-productivity sectors like tourism. 'Beach disease', or the concept that a booming tourism sector can crowd out other more productive sectors and generate more long-term harm than good, should be rising as a market discussion.

The quantity of growth matters for driving the output gap and where pricing for the ECB's terminal rate should settle relative to neutral. But the quality of growth, which helps to drive changes in the potential growth rate and thus neutral rate, matters for where markets price the neutral rate itself. Growth converging towards a lower trend level is hardly auspicious for EUR returns.

Investors have been squarely focused on French politics given the election. But we think that investors are meaningfully underpricing political risks in France's neighbour, Germany, where the combination of concerns about the federal budget and local elections in September may raise political risk premia in the currency bloc.

Short EUR/USD hedges a lot of different risks, from global stagflation, to geopolitics, to US outperformance. From a purely political perspective, investors may be underpricing European political risk premia (which is EUR negative) as well as US election risk premia (which is USD positive). We continue to see scope for additional risk premia to push EUR/USD below 1.05.

## EUR | ECB Preview: Watch rate differentials

With markets pricing in a 0% chance of a July cut from the ECB, the messaging will be closely watched. That said, as our economists have laid out in the latest ECB

preview, most of the recent data have been coming in in-line with the ECB's expectations. As such, the ECB seems likely to reiterate its data dependency and meeting-by-meeting approach.

While we expect minimal EUR reaction on the back of this meeting, we continue to see more scope for rates to diverge between the US and the euro area, with growth and inflation differentials likely pointing to a weaker EUR relative to USD and GBP. We like to position for this via short EUR/GBP, a trade that benefits from positive carry and remains an attractive hedge for political risk in Europe, in our view.

## NOK/SEK | What was that?

Nothing to worry about, in our view. We think the Norwegian CPI miss that induced a rather material sell-off in NOK/SEK ultimately doesn't, or shouldn't, change the story for the cross. Relative to recent history, the size of the surprise was not extraordinarily large. More importantly in our eyes, however, is where we stand and where we are going in these two economies.

We think the dichotomy is evident in the central banks' respective policy forecasts. If the forecasts are correct, not only will we have to wait for convergence of policy between the neighbor states, but we will first have to go through even greater divergence.

Instead, this week's sell off appears to have been amplified by market dynamics: specifically, low July liquidity. Hence, with the drivers still largely intact, positive carry, limited risk-sensitivity, and now even more attractive entry levels, we continue to recommend NOK/SEK longs.

## Japan

## JPY | No changes to our FX view

USD/JPY has been deviating from our medium-term outlook of late, with USD/JPY trading around 159.00, while we see it set to decline to 146 towards 4Q24. While the basic narrative of the Fed rate-cutting path remains in play, the demand for JPY carry trades exceeded our expectation. However, we believe that carry trades would be unwound quickly once the market starts to price in sharper Fed ratecutting cycle within a short period of time, as expected volatility increases while long-term carry decreases.

We are sticking with our medium-term forecasts for USD/JPY in the belief that: (1) fundamentals remain essentially unchanged; and (2) current divergence from fair value is, indeed, a predominantly “speculative” phenomenon rather than a structural issue.

Watch for 'beach disease' discussions… 'Beach disease' is an economic concept similar to 'Dutch disease'. The idea in both cases is that an economic orientation towards tourism and petroleum, respectively, raises exports and boosts the currency, leading to reduced export competitiveness abroad and import substitution due to the currency appreciation.

In the case of tourism, research on whether it directly raises the exchange rate is debatable. In the case of Europe we're skeptical that the uptick in tourist flows will be sufficient to raise the value of EUR. FX turnover in EUR exceeds US\$2 trillion per day after all, and tourism flows (like the trade balance in general) are far more sensitive to the level of the exchange rate than capital flows.

...and what it means for pricing in a lower neutral rate: But the pivot towards the 'easy money' of tourism as an engine of growth does raise structural concerns for Europe. Strong tourist flows and the resulting income generation may attract entrepreneurs to the tourism sector instead of investing in higher-productivity areas. Tourism may not raise EUR, but it could raise local prices, crowding out locals and eroding their consumption, and raising wages to levels well in excess of their productivity levels.

Over time this pattern would suggest that potential growth and the neutral rate would be lower, not higher.

Taking these two points together, the quantity and quality of growth both matter for market pricing of the ECB's cutting cycle's terminal rate. The terminal rate of the cutting cycle is a function of the market's pricing of both the level of the neutral rate and whether the terminal rate should be at, above or below neutral.

The quantity of GDP growth relative to potential, generating a positive or negative output gap, explains where the terminal rate relative to neutral should settle. If the quantity of growth is too low, disinflationary pressures suggest a greater risk that the terminal rate falls below neutral.

But the quality of growth helps to explain where pricing for the neutral rate itself should be. High productivity growth suggests a higher neutral rate while lower productivity growth suggests a lower one all else equal (and assuming no change in the stock or composition of the labor force).

To this end, we think that investors' optimism about the quantity of GDP growth, when driven by concerningly low-productivity sectors, should begin to reverse, supporting EUR/ USD downside. We maintain our short EUR/USD expression via long put options. We also maintain short EUR/GBP.

## Further progress toward 2% = Tighter distributions

Beyond the removal of event risk, the catalysts for this week's move lower in volatility pricing were June's CPI and PPI prints. Core CPI came in below expectations (6bp vs. BBG 20bp), with:

• A notable shift lower in both rent of shelter and owners' equivalent rent

• A continuation in May's weak trimmed mean and median prints

• A slowing in core services ex-shelter

Borrowing Governor Waller's framework of grading CPI (see quote below), we think the above factors mean the June print is pushing an A+. If the FOMC needs to see three solid CPI prints before getting the confidence they need that inflation is lessening, we think the past two CPI prints will have left the committee with an optimistic outlook on achieving their price target ( Exhibit 112 ).

Governor Waller (May 21, 2024): "Last week's report on consumer price index (CPI) inflation in April was a welcome relief after three months without progress toward 2 percent. That said, the progress was so modest that it did not change my view that I will need to see more evidence of moderating inflation before supporting any easing of monetary policy. If I were still a professor and had to assign a grade to this inflation report, it would be a C+—far from failing, but not stellar, either."

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Definition of terms

Buy/Long: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be positive over the relevant time period.

Sell/Short: The analyst expects the total or excess return (depending on the nature of the recommendation) of the instrument or issuer that is the subject of the investment recommendation to be negative over the relevant time period.

Selling protection or Buying Risk: The analyst expects that the price of protection against the event occurring will decrease over the relevant time period.

Buying protection or Selling Risk: The analyst expects the price of protection against the event occurring will increase over the relevant time period.

Pay: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will increase.

Receive: The analyst expects that over the specified time period the variable rate underlying the swap agreement that is the subject of the investment recommendation will decrease.

Unless otherwise specified, the time frame for recommendations included in the Morgan

Stanley Fixed Income Research reports is 1 - 3 months and the price of financial instruments mentioned in the recommendation is as at the date and time of publication of the recommendation.

When more than one issuer or instrument is included in a recommendation, analyst expects one part of the trade to outperform the other trade or combination of other trades included in the recommendation on a relative basis.
