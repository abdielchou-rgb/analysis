## March 26

USTs twist flatten amid strong 5y auction; EUR/CHF hits high since June; BoE's Mann pushes back on market-implied cuts; OATs track core duration amid French deficit data; USD/JPY near local highs, MoF remarks continue; COP advances after holiday; DXY at 104.30 (+0.1%); US 10y at 4.232% (-1.4bp).

• Treasuries twist flatten after a strong \$67bn 5y UST auction comes 1.0bp through; focus turns to likely earlier than usual month-end activity given a holiday-shortened week in the US.

• EUR/CHF (+0.4%) advances to its highest level since June as the franc's appeal as a funding currency increases, following the surprise SNB rate cut last week.

• BoE MPC Member Mann warns markets are "too complacent," in reference to the series of rate cuts priced in this year, and says markets are "pricing in too many cuts."

• Despite outperformance of peripheral duration, OATs fail to richen against core after Insee reports a higher-than-expected French public deficit in 2023.

• USD/JPY (+0.1%) cautiously approaches local highs even as Japanese Minister of Finance Suzuki reiterates the MoF is watching FX moves with a high sense of urgency.

• COP (+1.3%) significantly outperforms as markets react to the BanRep meeting on Friday after a local holiday Monday.

## Japan:

JGBs essentially traded rangebound and the 10y sector underperformed on the curve. Long-end JGBs traded relatively resiliently despite the 40y JGB auction on Wednesday. As a result, the 10s30s curve retraced some of its steepening move from Monday (-1.0bp d/d). 5y JGBs also traded relatively firmly, supported by demand to buy the dip; 5y JGBs rallied by 0.5bp d/d, to 0.375%, on a simple yield basis. We believe the 40y JGB auction will be digested relatively smoothly due to demand from the pension fund community related to month-end rebalancing, as well as demand from the lifer community to invest surplus cash into the fiscal yearend.

TONA OIS broadly outperformed cash JGBs, as demand to square short positions was strong. There was strong receiving pressure in front-end TONA OIS despite the elevated level of USD/JPY. XCCY basis curve remained under steepening pressure, with strong receiving demand at the 1y tenor.

During Tokyo trading hours, USD/JPY traded rangebound. Early in the Tokyo morning, Japanese Finance Minister Suzuki discussed the recent FX move. He said the Ministry of Finance will take appropriate action against excessive FX moves. With that said, the reaction in the FX market appeared to be limited. Into the London morning, USD/JPY ground lower toward 151.20. During Tokyo hours, other CCY/JPY pairs were essentially range-bound but began to rally from the London morning as the equity market recovered; EUR/JPY ground higher toward 164.50.

## Europe:

CHF lagged and extended losses against both USD and EUR given the attractiveness of using CHF as a funding currency following the SNB rate decision last week. EUR/ CHF (+0.4%) reached its highest level since June of 2023, just below 0.9800 in the NY afternoon.

European duration opened lower and eventually Bunds traded sideways and richened slightly in a quiet London session with respect to data releases. US durable goods orders were stronger than expected and drove US and European rates lower. Overall, 10y Bunds rallied 2bp, to 2.35%. A higher-than-expected French deficit for 2023 was confirmed when Insee reported the public deficit reached 5.5% of GDP in 2023 (2022: 4.8%). 10y OATs failed to richen relative to core, even though the 10y BTP-Bund spread tightened by 2bp. Core ASW traded sideways and closely relatively unchanged. The EUR 2s10s swap curve flattened by 1bp, to -54bp, while 10s30s closed flat.

## Dollar Bloc:

The US dollar held steady (DXY USD Index: +0.1%) in a relatively muted trading session. Canadian front-end yields climbed higher (2y: +3bp) in the aftermath of remarks from BoC First Deputy Governor Rogers on structural productivity issues in

Canada. Her comments highlighted how productivity gains may shield the Canadian economy from a future where inflation “may be more of a threat than it has been over the past few decades.” A compression in US-Canadian yield differentials supported CAD. USD/CAD closed unchanged and CAD outperformed the rest of G10 against USD.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies strengthened broadly but moves were modest. USD/CNH continued to move lower, by 0.1%, to \~7.25, after the PBoC reinforced its support for RMB and lowered the USD/CNY fixing further to 7.0943. This diverged from USD/CNY, which gained 0.1%, but remained below 7.22 as media reports suggested Chinese state banks had increased USD sales after USD/CNY breached 7.21. KRW led gains in the region, up 0.2%, to 1,340, amid outperformance in the local equity market (KOSPI: +0.7%) and nearly US\$520mn of foreign inflows. SGD and INR gained 0.2%, as USD/ INR fell back below 83.4 in its first trading session since Friday. MYR gained 0.1%, to 4.72, after the central bank stressed optimism on both the currency and economic outlook. TWD was an outlier, as USD/TWD rose 0.2%, back near the 32.0 level, as the local equity market underperformed (TWSE: -0.3%) and foreigners withdrew funds for the second session.

Most AxJ rates sold off following the UST moves on Monday. Taiwan rates underperformed the most among peers. 10y TWD NDIRS yields surged another 9bp, to 1.89%, the highest since at least 2014, as the curve bear-steepened. Given the UST move, Singapore rates also underperformed; yields rose \~4bp on the swaps curve. In Korea, 10y NDIRS yields rose 2bp and the 2s10s curve bear-steepened by \~1bp, as Korean inflation expectations for March ticked up for the first time since October, to 3.2%. In Hong Kong, swap yields rose \~2bp across the curve from the 2y tenor. In China, NDIRS yields rose by \~1bp while CGB yields had limited changes. India and Thailand were two exceptions. India and Thailand yields fell by \~2bp across the swaps curve. It was the first trading session of the week for the former after a local holiday on Monday.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mixed. COP (+1.3%) significantly outperformed as markets reacted to the BanRep meeting on Friday after a local holiday Monday. CLP (-0.2%) and BRL (-0.2%) weakened marginally, with few local drivers. Most rate curves in the region ended higher; CAM sold off 4bp across the curve. DIs underperformed as markets perceived that the BCB minutes and Brazil's higherthan-expected CPI data raised the odds for a deceleration in the BCB’s cutting cycle (January 2026s: +7bp). Most TIIEs ended unchanged, while the very front end of IBR curve outperformed (1y: -2bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• BoE MPC Member Mann warned markets are "too complacent" in reference to the series of rate cuts priced in this year and said markets are "pricing in too many cuts"

• BoC First Deputy Governor Rogers discussed the structural productivity challenges the Canadian economy is facing. She argued an improvement in productivity may shield the Canadian economy from a future where inflation “may be more of a threat than it has been over the past few decades"

## Emerging Markets

• BNM Deputy Governor Marzunisham reiterated MYR is undervalued based on the central bank’s internal studies. He expressed optimism MYR will appreciate toward the end of the year, driven by structural reforms and positive growth prospects, and is projected to be between 4.0% and 5.0%, buoyed by household spending and private consumption

• PBoC Governor Pan said China’s property market is showing positive signs, with a solid foundation for long-term stable development and a limited impact on the financial system. Regarding financial stability, Pan mentioned overall government debt is at low and moderate levels compared to international standards and local government debt risks are being solved. Additionally, he stressed China will continue to open the financial sector, which is vital for serving the real economy and improving China’s international competitiveness. This came after China’s Vice Commerce Minister announced China will fully guarantee national treatment for foreign companies

• BoK Board Member Suh, whose term is due to end in April, called for measures to prevent a rise in household debt and house prices as monetary policy normalizes. She highlighted tougher regulations on debt-to-income ratios applied in the process of reviewing household loan applications could be among measures to safeguard the financial balance

• NBH lowered its base rate by 75bp, to 8.25% (C: 8.25%). The decision was unanimous, although 50bp and 100bp of rate cuts were also discussed by the MPC. The statement emphasized the increase in risk premia of Hungarian assets and FX weakness to justify the slowdown compared to February. Deputy Governor Virag signaled further slowdown in the pace of rate cuts in 2Q24 and very cautious monetary policy in 2H24 will be needed. According to Virag, base rate expectations for 6.5-7.0% by the end of 1H24 are justified, which is slightly higher than the previous 6%-7% range (for more, read here)

• Minutes from the March BCB meeting showed policymakers preferred to have more flexibility in coming meetings as higher services inflation and other risks have raised uncertainty around the path for the last leg of inflation. Although they did not close the door to a continuation of the 50bp cutting pace at the June meeting and said their baseline scenario has not changed substantially (for more, read here). Markets have currently priced in a deceleration in the cutting pace with -27bp priced for the June meeting

## Developed Markets

• In Australia, the Westpac Consumer Confidence Index fell by 1.8% m/m in March (P: 6.2%). Unemployment expectations increased marginally but remained stable. Views on current household finances fell and forward expectations ticked to the lowest level since November 2023. Housing sentiment remained mixed – price expectations fell, while the share who thought it is the right time to buy increased by 4.9% m/m (P: 3.1%). Spending intentions decreased, arguably reflective of continued thrift behavior, evident in the consumption profile. Our economists note an improvement in consumer sentiment can be expected (real incomes rise) but will be weighted in the second half of CY24 (for more, read here)

• In Germany, Consumer Confidence improved to -27.4 in April (C: -28.0; P: - 28.8)

• In Spain, final 4Q23 GDP came in at 0.6% (C: 0.6%; 3Q23: 0.4%), in line with the preliminary estimate

• In the US, the Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index was little changed at 104.7 in March (C: 107.0; P: 104.8), after a downward revision to the prior month. The headline has been rangebound since December 2021 and neither sent a strong signal of acceleration or deceleration. The present situation rise was due to improved labor market conditions. The labor market differential rose as a net 32.2% (P: 30.1%) of consumers viewed jobs as plentiful and did not signal any deceleration in the labor market

• In the US, the Richmond Fed Manufacturing Index fell to -11 in March (C: -5; P: -5). The ISM-adjusted measure, which combines survey components fell to 45.5 (P: 49.3), but our economists maintain their March US ISM Manufacturing PMI forecast at 49.0

## Emerging Markets

Department Store Sales rose 7.2% y/y in February (P: 0.7%) and Discount Store Sales surged 21.0% y/y in February (P: -9.2%). Consumer Confidence fell to 100.7 in March (P: 101.9), but Inflation Expectations ticked up to 3.2% y/y in March, the first rise since October 2023

• In Hong Kong, the Trade Balance swung to a deficit of HK\$41.7bn in February (C: -HK\$31.0bn; P: HK\$3.6bn). Exports fell 0.8% y/y in February (C: 2.0%; P: 33.6%) and Imports declined 1.8% y/y in February (C: -1.0%; P: 21.7%)

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.

The CBC unexpectedly delivered a rate hike on March 21, against ours and consensus views of no change. Both the growth and CPI inflation forecasts were raised on strong exports and renewed concerns on inflation ahead of electricity price hike. However, this likely was a final adjustment.

## Turkey Economics and Strategy: CBT Review: Getting Ahead of the Curve

## 21 Mar 2024

The CBT delivered a surprise 500bp hike (to 50%) today. The increase in the width of the interest rate corridor implies an even bigger, 650bp, hike in the interbank rate. We expect the decision will ease the pressure on the currency and reserves, and help improve inflation expectations. In credit, we now recommend 10s30s flatteners.
