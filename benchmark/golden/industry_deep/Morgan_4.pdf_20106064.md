China IT Services and Software I Asia Pacific

# 2024 outlook: A chilly year for enterprise IT spending

Against the backdrop of ongoing 3D issues (debt, deflation, demographics), our industry view is still Cautious. We see two themes to show relative standouts: 1) overseas pioneers, and 2) sustainable market share gainers. We think the expectations gap in the Al value chain will offer opportunities.

With macro developments pointing more negatively than when we published our industry downgrade (lackluster monthly data and a lack of reflationary policy), we reiterate our Cautious industry view and continue to believe the 3D issues in China will act as a major hurdle to enterprise IT spending, which is late cycle in nature. Painful digestion is still being processed given consensus forecasts and valuation have yet to reflect fundamentals. We see downside earnings risk in the 4Q23 earnings season and highlight that 1Q24 will face a very high base, particularly in the cybersecurity group. In this tough market environment, we identify two themes to find relative standouts.

1. Going overseas. Overall, Chinese players are currently not competitive in the global market. With more Chinese enterprise customers going abroad, they are attaching a higher importance to overseas markets. We see less price competition, better willingness to pay, and higher acceptance of the public cloud/subscription model overseas. Advantages include: 1) home customers, 2) attractive pricing, which is already much higher than domestic levels, and 3) leading technology in some niche areas. Among players with relatively large overseas exposure, we are bullish on Wondershare (initiated with OW), Tuya (OW) and GDS (OW). We also recommend monitoring the progress of Agora (EW) and Wangsu (EW).

2. Sustainable share takers. Market share becomes a more critical revenue growth driver when TAM slows down, and we like players who can gain share, though not via a price war. Kingdee (ow) is a key beneficiary of ERP localization with an advantage in cloud products. Beisen (ow) will likely consolidate HCM market share left by fading startups and global players. CSI (ow) should benefit from Huawei's recovery in multiple business lines. We recommend monitoring Baosight (uw→EW) on parentco's M&A, and VenusTech (OW) on its partnership with China Mobile after the cybersecurity market moves past high comps after 1Q24.

Trading opportunities from expectations gap in Al value chain. After the roller coaster performance of Al related stocks in 2023, we are entering the stage where earnings delivery will be tested. For Al applications, we think current market expectations and valuations will start to become acceptable for Kingsoft Office (uw→Ew) but more reasonable for Wondershare. For IDC, expectations are low due to pool cloud recovery. We think the potential demand rebound from hyperscalers will be a call option for IDC players, likely in very late 2024.

# Macro implications: Will 2024 be a better year than 2023 for the software group?

We lower our 2024 industry growth outlook; we are 9-10% below consensus for 2024 revenue: After the fourth year of overall disappointing fundamental performance (though some stocks did well on thematic trades), we have lowered expectations for the industry from a top-down perspective going into 2024. This is supported by:

1. The removal of the low base effect in real GDP growth;

2. Policy remaining defensive to prevent systematic risk rather than being sufficiently reflationary, which means the potential resolution of the debt-deflation issue could take 2-3 years;

3. Monthly high frequency data towards the end of 2023 worsened, including PMI CPI/PPI and consumption.

The second and third points are incrementally negative compared with our last China ClO survey in late September 2023 and our industry view downgrade in early November 2023

Both our industry growth forecasts in 2023 (6% MSe vs. 11% consensus) and 2024 (16.5% MSe vs. 21% Cons) are much lower than consensus. Combining the two year growth gap, our 2024 absolute industry revenue forecast is 9-10ppt below consensus. From a bottomup perspective, we think consensus downward revisions for most names are still in process. We believe the risk to our forecasts is skewed to the downside.

We highlight the deflation risk to sector growth by referring to the case in Japan, where we saw almost no IT spending penetration improvement for many years before Covid-19, despite much higher labor cost than China. The overall software and IT service sector is likely to just demonstrate its cyclical nature rather than deliver secular growth in a deflationary environment.

Compared with China, Japan was and is still facing more serious labor cost challenges, with its working age population declining from 85mn to 74mn in 2002-22. However, its IT spending penetration (implied by IT markets as a percentage of GDP and IT markets as a percentage of corporate revenue) has stayed largely stable at 2-2.5% and 0.7-0.9%, respectively, in the past 20 years. Japan's IT markets only recorded a 1% CAGR in spending in that period. The slow growth of nominal GDP at a 0.3% CAGR in 2002-22 led to slow growth in IT markets as well. The secular growth of digitization over a long time frame can't offset the impact of macro environments, especially deflationary, in our view.

We believe overall industry competition will remain intense due to the shrinking pie, but will not worsen to a price war: That's because:

1. Most players cannot afford to gain market share by significantly hurting profitability; and

2. We expect to see more exits of long tail players.

Overall competition on the latent side should further subside in 2024, except for certain cutting-edge areas like Al. Stringent headcount control and rising focus on profitability are becoming software company management consensus, and will likely sustain going into 2024. We think industry profitability will improve across the board at a pace faster than revenue on operating leverage and a very low base, but 2024 PE multiples are still quite high for most names.

Multiple wise, we see a mixed impact ahead. On the positive side, our economics team forecasts US interest rates to start to declining in mid 2024, and China should stay relative loose on monetary policy. On the negative side, the market will gradually step out from the excitement of Al technology and expect real financial contributions, and we believe stock multiple will follow the classic hyper cycle curve for new technology. We keep our view that it will take a very long time to see Al enterprise application monetization in China due to relatively low labor cost and a lack of high-performing

chipset supply. The previous series of thematic trades in the A share market took multiples to an unjustified level and there is still room to correct, in our view. The upside risk is sustained thematic trades in te software sector driven by retail investors when the overall market performance is under pressure.

Going overseas

Less price competition, greater willingness to pay, and higher acceptance of public cloud/subscription models: Overall, Chinese software and IT service players are currently not competitive in the global market owing to disadvantages in technology (compared with US software players), cost (compared with Indian IT service players), and the previous three years of Covid disruption (which prevented firms going abroad to set up teams or marketing).

Most of the players in our coverage have small revenue exposure to the global market. However, with the slowing of the domestic market and the forays abroad of enterprise customers to explore new growth opportunities, Chinese software players are attaching higher and higher importance to the global market. They see less price competition, greater willingness to pay, and higher acceptance of public cloud/subscription models in the global market. They would like go to ASEAN as the first stop in globalization, followed by the Middle East, other parts of Asia, and the EU.

## The advantages of Chinese software/IT service players in the global market include:

1. Home customers going abroad

2. Lower pricing than global peers, which is already much higher than their domestic prices;

3. Leading technology in some niche areas.

So far technology restrictions from the US on software is not a big issue for Chinese software players except for Al-related areas, given their greater focus on enterprise applications. But most them have low intention to go to the US market in view of relatively high geopolitical risk.

## Market share gainers

With growth slowing down across most software/IT service submarkets, market share becomes the critical driver behind relative standouts. However, gaining share is not easy in the sticky enterprise software market and additional market share at the cost of price competition could be even worse. We highlight the following companies' potential in gaining share through a disciplined pricing strategy in their submarket.

Kingdee (ow): Leading cloud product and the ERP software localization trend are the drivers behind its market share gain, at the expense of global players. Our AlphaWise China CIO survey indicated an inflection point of SAP's market share in China after multiple years of solid performance. We also collected evidence that SAP's Chinese partners like SiE and Hand are both suffering from poor growth in their pan-ERP business. Kingdee's previous heavy investment in cloud and the large enterprise market should pay off with meaningful share gain. Meanwhile, we are not concerned about the potential competition from metaERP, given it is very focused on the high end (not Kingdee's addressable market) and still far from becoming a standard product (deep customization IT service model). We expect Kingdee to sustain high-teens revenue CAGR in the next 2-3 years, compared with overall ERP market CAGR at \~10%.

Beisen (ow): Beisen is well positioned to consolidate share in China's HCM (Human Capital Management) markets from fading start-ups as well as foreign players. We believe the recent completion of the selldown from its pre-IPO major shareholders removed a major overhang for Beisen's share price and paves the way for valuation recovery.

## Al development

Different from our US Software team's positive industry view, helped by GenAl, we remain very selective in this supply-side technology innovation cycle in China. We highlight a few comparisons:

• From an addressable market perspective, labor cost in China is around one-fifth of the US level on average; from the cost perspective, computing power costs are similar globally, or even more expensive in China in view of the ban on shipment of high-performing GPUs. Putting them together, the commercialization of Al enterprise software in China will take more patience.

• Willingness to pay for productivity software: we use leading office software in respective markets as an example, the ARPU for WPS in China is around 10% that of Microsoft 365.

• Al product gap: It took four months for Baidu's Chatbox to reach 10Omn users vs. two months for OpenAl's ChatGPT, though ChatGPT accomplished global user education first, and still cannot enter China.

• With regard to expectations, we see revenue beat and raise for US Al beneficiaries, while we believe current consensus remains high for most Chinese companies.

• In our AlphaWise 4Q23 US CIO Survey (conducted during October through December 2023), Al rose to the top of the ClOs' priority list. This compares to the #3 priority ranking for Al in the China 2H23 CIO Survey.

• After a big rally in 2023, the top 5 most expensive US software stocks are at trading at 17.4x one-year forward EV/sales, all with >30% 2022-24e sales CAGR. This compares with Kingsoft Office at 18x one-year forward EV/sales multiple and 22% 2022-24e sales CAGR.

• The last big technology upgrade in the US software market was cloud computing, which led to the prosperity of SaaS for more than ten years. The SaaS players are still suffering in China's market after 10 years.

However, these points do not mean that we cannot find investment ideas along the Al value chain in China this year. We believe some pioneering B2C Al software (especially tool software, like WPS Al and Wondershare's Filmora) will start to bring the benefits of Al to user base and lift ARPU starting from 2024, and we see a lot of beat-or-miss opportunities. For data centers, we see "option value" in domestic hyperscalers' Al computing power deployment given very low market expectations after the cloud recovery missed.

Wondershare: Wondershare is a pioneer in adopting generative Al functions in its existing product. In its latest Filmora update, it offers users a wide range of Al functions in the cost of Al credits. It could further promote its paying subscription because those users have access to unlimited Al credits. On top of that, it has also launched the new product Virgo, which is a meta human product allowing users to create short videos easily. We believe Wondershare's access to global Al infrastructure and early integration into the technology could allow it to become a key beneficiary of Al application development.
