## Key Points

Reflation in 1Q24 was substantial, but weaker prints are coming. We go over four main points that underpin our call:

1. Reflation in 1Q24 was narrow and mostly explained by an acceleration in goods and financial services prices. We expect both components to decelerate from here.

2. Leading indicators point to weaker rents inflation in the second half of the year.

3. Our analysis suggests that residual seasonality contributed to the unexpected strength in 1Q24 inflation, implying payback in 2H24.

4. Auto insurance inflation, a key component for CPI, will start its descent in 2H24.

## Reflation in 1Q24 was substantial, but weaker prints are coming

We expect deceleration ahead, with slowdown in the monthly pace of core PCE inflation, especially in 2H24. We see sequential rates broadly aligned with the Fed's 2% inflation target by the end of the year, bringing annual core PCE inflation to 2.7% 4Q/4Q in 2024.

## Goods prices have more room to normalize

The relative price of goods vis-à-vis services is still above the pre-COVID trend and we expect convergence to normal levels in the future (left). ## Rents inflation is key and leading indicators point to deceleration in 2H24

Core PCE is currently running at 2.8%Y, 80bp above target. More than half of the distance to target is explained by rents inflation (left). But we expect deceleration. The New Tenants Rent Index (NTRR) came in weak in 4Q23 and 1Q24 – both prints below 1%Y (right). The NTRR leads rents CPI by about 3 quarters and is a useful explanatory variable in our models.

## Residual seasonality contributed to the strength in 1Q24

Residual seasonality may be the dark horse behind the upswing in 1Q24 inflation. Seasonal factors did not pick up typical price increases during the first quarter of the year – especially in January. We find that: (1) there is evidence of residual seasonality 1Q24, (2) residual seasonality has become more important after COVID, and (3) the bias might have been even more important in 1Q24.

## Auto Insurance: Main Driver of CPI core services ex-housing

Auto Insurance is a key component explaining core services ex-housing CPI inflation (left). It is less important for core PCE because: (i) it carries a lower weight in the index, and (ii) PCE uses PPI inputs for car insurance which have been weaker compared to CPI (right). We expect deceleration in car insurance CPI, narrowing the CPI-PCE wedge.
