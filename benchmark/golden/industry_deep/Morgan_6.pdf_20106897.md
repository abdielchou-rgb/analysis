## March 6

USTs grind richer, curve continues to bull-flatten; Fed's Powell delivers balanced testimony; Resilient risk demand, lower yields weigh on USD; BoC holds firm, CAD gains; Prospect of more PBoC easing sends China yields lower; EUR gains before ECB; DXY at 103.37 (-0.4%); US 10y at 4.102% (-5.0bp).

• US Treasuries grind richer through Powell's testimony and extend to session highs alongside a reemergence in regional bank stresses; the curve continues to bull-flatten.

• Fed Chair Powell delivers balanced prepared remarks: "We believe that our policy rate is likely at its peak for this tightening cycle. If the economy evolves broadly as expected, it will likely be appropriate to begin dialing back policy restraint at some point this year."

• Resilient risk demand and lower Treasury yields translates into broad US dollar weakness; the DXY USD Index (-0.4%) settles at its lowest level since early February.

• CAD (+0.6%) gains against USD after the BoC keeps its policy rate at 5.00% (C: 5.00%) and maintains the same policy guidance from its previous meeting.

• China rates lead a rally across AxJ rates as the PBoC notes it is still possible to cut the reserve requirement ratio further; 30y CGB yields fall 4bp, to 2.43%, new lows since 2004.

• EUR (+0.4%) gains against USD ahead of the March ECB meeting, where focus will be on the new set of inflation and growth projections.

Labor market data, which consisted of the February ADP employment report and JOLTS Job Openings for January, were largely in line with expectations. This left Fed Chair Powell’s testimony to the US House Financial Services Committee the marquee risk event for the NY morning.

Powell’s prepared remarks conveyed a very similar message as the January FOMC meeting. He stated “we believe that our policy rate is likely at its peak for this tightening cycle. If the economy evolves broadly as expected, it will likely be appropriate to begin dialing back policy restraint at some point this year.” An incremental development was willingness he signaled for a re-proposal of the Basel III framework, Powell described re-proposal as a “very plausible option.”

Powell’s testimony was quickly overshadowed by the re-emergence of regional US bank stresses as media reports suggested a bank had sought a cash infusion. Already richer, US Treasuries extended to session highs as risk premia for tail-risk emergency cuts found renewed viability. Although terms of a deal were announced, the long end (30y: 4.24%, -5.2bp d/d) remained resilient as the curve continued its bullflattening momentum.

Uncertainty proved isolated to regional banks (KRX: -0.44%) as US equities remained resilient at the index level (S&P 500: +0.51%). Both investment grade and high yield CDX indices more than retraced the minor widening from the prior session and settled at new 52-week tights, at +50.6bp and +332.5bp, respectively. Resilient risk demand and lower Treasury yields translated into broad US dollar weakness; the DXY USD Index (-0.4%) settled at its lowest level since early February. CAD (+0.6%) gained against USD after the BoC keeps its policy rate at 5.00% (C: 5.00%) and maintained the same policy guidance from its previous meeting.

After a decline to the lowest level (\$439.8bn) since June 2021, utilization of the Fed’s ON RRP facility has increased modestly, to \$456.8bn. The number of counterparties who used the facility fell to 68, the lowest since mid-January (65).

## Japan:

Despite the broad rally in USTs overnight on Tuesday, which followed weak US ISM Services data, JGBs sold off on Wednesday as a media report suggested one major market participant expects NIRP removal at the March BoJ meeting. Front-tomedium-term JGBs particularly underperformed on the curve, as market participants priced in a more hawkish normalization cycle; 5y JGBs sold off by 1.5bp d/d to 0.370% on a simple yield basis. Long-end JGBs were supported despite the 30y JGB auction on Thursday. We expect the 30y auction to be digested relatively smoothly, as lifers have ability to deploy surplus cash by the fiscal year-end; 30y JGBs rallied by 1.0bp d/d to 1.75%. In the Tokyo evening session, a media report that suggested the possibility of NIRP removal at the March meeting dragged JGB futures 10 ticks lower.

TONA OIS twist flattened alongside JGBs but continued to outperform JGBs, as market participants increasingly priced in the risk of removal of the overshoot commitment as well as less demand for collateral if the one-tiered system will be introduced post-NIRP removal. XCCY basis bull-flattened, led by the long-end.

During Tokyo trading hours, USD/JPY was range-bound \~150, despite decent selling pressure in JGBs. Other CCY/JPY pairs essentially traded sideways given limited catalysts. The environment changed from the London open, after a media report suggested at lease one BoJ board member will support NIRP removal at the March

BoJ meeting. USD/JPY fell suddenly to 149.30, while other CCY/JPY pairs also instantaneously fell. However, the initial JPY strength was quickly retraced by decent demand to buy the CCY/JPY dip. Another media report suggested there is not consensus among BoJ board members about the timing of lift-off, although they will likely be more confident about stronger wage growth. This seemingly contributed to the recovery in CCY/JPY pairs.

## Europe:

The UK budget did not contain any significant surprises, and GBP (+0.2%) strengthened in line with the rest of G10. EUR (+0.3%) reached an intraday high above 1.0900 during the NY afternoon.

European duration was under pressure in the London morning and partially retraced the rally from the prior session. There was not a clear catalyst, aside from strong German exports and imports. Price action subsequently reversed and followed the move in Treasuries. Overall, 10y Bund yields closed unchanged at 2.32%. In swaps, 2s10s was little changed while 10s30s steepened over 1bp and 5s10s30s richened by 2bp. Strong performance in risk assets helped tighten the 10y BTP-Bund spread by 6bp to 132bp. Core ASW resumed its path lower (Bund ASW: - 0.5bp), apart from Schatz, which closed wider by 0.6bp, despite cheapening for most of the London session.

## Dollar Bloc:

The DXY USD Index reached its lowest level since early February, below 104 in the NY afternoon, after Fed Chair Powell reiterated the Fed expects to cut rates later this year. Risk-sensitive currencies outperformed in the G10, and AUD rose as much as 0.9% amid the broad demand for risk assets, reflected in equities and oil prices. 4Q23 Australian GDP had limited impact on the currency as the data was in line with expectations. USD/CAD (-0.6%) fell and front-end GoC bonds underperformed after the BoC retained the same policy guidance at its March meeting as in January. Some market participants had expected an incremental change in tone towards a potential policy shift in April.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies were stable to stronger as risk appetite recovered. CNH relatively underperformed, \~0.1% weaker as the PBOC said there is still monetary policy room. IDR outperformed and retraced losses from the prior two sessions and rebounded 0.4% to 15,705; INDOGBs rallied for the first time since February 23. THB gained 0.3%, aided by a steady rise in gold prices, which reached a record high of \~2,131 intraday. PHP rose 0.2% to 55.87 after the BSP governor noted it is unlikely rates would be cut soon.

AxJ rates rallied broadly and bull-flattened, following the overnight trend in USTs. China rates led the rally as the PBOC noted it is still possible to cut the reserve requirement ratio (RRR) further. 10y CGBs rallied 5bp; the rally in 30y CGBs resumed (4bp) after a pause on Tuesday. The 1s5s CNY NDIRS curve bull-flattened by 2bp. Korea rates also outperformed, as 10y NDIRS yields fell 5bp and the 2s10s curve bull-flattened 2bp. The UST trend outweighed the slight upside surprise in Korea’s February headline inflation. Hong Kong front-end rates underperformed as HIBOR stabilized; 1y HKD swap yield rose 1bp. In India, 10y G-sec rallied by 1bp and the NDOIS shifted lower by 2bp.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mostly stronger, driven by broad USD weakness. COP (+0.6%) led peers, aided by the rise in oil prices (+1.00%); MXN (+0.4%) and BRL (+0.3%) strengthened with few local developments. CLP (-0.4%) losses extended despite no major local drivers. Rates ended mixed. DIs ended higher in the front end (January 2026s: +3bp) and TIIEs gained mainly in the long end (10y: -4bp). The CAM curve outperformed; the front end rallied (1y: -7bp) and the curve bull-flattened (1s10s: -5bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The Bank of Canada left its policy rate unchanged at 5.00% (C: 5.00%) at its March meeting. The Bank drew attention to recent developments in inflation but cautioned it remains concerned inflation could still prove more persistent-than-expected. It reiterated a desire for "further and sustained easing in core inflation." BoC Governor Macklem reiterated it is "still too early to consider lowering the policy rate." The discussion focused on how long the policy rate should stay at its current level before the first cut and developments in core inflation remain key. Our economists do not believe the bank is in a rush to cut rates, given a focus on sustained declines in core inflation. Our economists expect the bank will continue its "higher-for-longer" approach and be patient in determining its next move. Our economists expect the first 25bp rate cut in July 2024; however, they acknowledge risks of an earlier start in June should the pace of progress for inflation prove faster-than-expected

• Fed Chair Powell stressed patience in his Humphrey Hawkins testimony to the House Financial Services Committee. Our economists believe the Fed remains on track to cut “at some point” this year, more flexible than the previous “mid-year” message. Inflation does not need to be at 2% before cutting, but the Fed needs “a bit more evidence.” Our economists believe core PCE at 2.5% y/y is a good level to signal a June cut (for more, read here)

• The Federal Reserve’s Beige Book stated “economic activity increased slightly,” based on data collected on or before February 26. This was a slightly better tone than the last Beige Book for early January (“little or no change in economic activity”). Employment rose at a “slight to modest pace” as labor market tightness “eased further.” “Nearly all” districts had improved labor availability and retention. It was easier to find qualified applicants. This tone echoed Fed Chair Powell’s, who said the labor market is coming into better balance but contrasts with the surge in payrolls in the last employment report. Price pressures “persisted” but “several districts reported some degree of moderation”

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
