# Global Macro Commentary | North America

## June 3

USTs rally after soft ISM data; MXN weakens alongside preliminary election results; JGB super-long sector sells off; AxJ FX rise amid rally in AxJ equities; Fed's Kashkari says rates will stay on hold for an “extended period”; Brent crude oil falls; DXY at 104.1 (-0.6%); US 10y at 4.388% (-11.0bp).

• USTs bull-flatten as soft ISM Manufacturing data provide additional confidence in decelerating inflation and reaffirm trends of slower growth amid a tight labor market.

• MXN (-3.8%) weakens, largely underperforming all major currencies, as market participants digest preliminary election results of a landslide win for the ruling party.

• JGBs twist-steepen with the super-long sector continuing to underperform given a lack of demand due to uncertainty around the BoJ’s policy rate and QT; 40y JGB rises 3.5bp on a simple yield basis.

• Most AxJ currencies outperform and strengthen against the USD amid a broad rally in Asian equities (KOSPI: +1.7%; SENSEX: +3.4%; TAIEX: +1.7%).

• Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari says on a podcast recorded last week that rates should remain on hold for “an extended period of time” until policymakers get “a lot more data” to convince them inflation is on its way down.

• Brent crude oil futures (-3.7%) close below \$80 for a barrel for the first time since February after OPEC+ decision to extend and then phase out its production cuts.

## Developed Markets

## United States:

Overnight, USTs continued to fall alongside global duration with soft data in Europe and declines in oil prices. The rally accelerated both in USTs and European duration with the release of weak US economic data in the NY morning.

US ISM manufacturing data for May showed softness and fell to 48.7 (C: 49.5; P: 49.2). The decline was led by new orders, which had in recent months led improvement in the ISM index, signaling weakening demand and economic slowdown. Additionally, the Prices Paid Index fell to 57.0 (C: 59.0; P: 60.9), which confirmed the deceleration in inflationary pressures. The one notable upside was strength in the Employment Index, which rose to 51.1 (C: 48.5; P: 48.6) and continued to signal a tight labor market.

Many market participants believe the weak ISM Index supports the ability of the Federal Reserve to cut rates this year. Treasury yields fell \~5-6bp across the curve immediately following the data release. Incorporating the soft data, the latest Atlanta Fed 2Q24 GDPNow estimate was revised to 1.8% q/q, down from 2.6% on May 31. US equities and the US dollar followed Treasury yields lower after the data. STIR also repriced \~5bp lower, and by the end of the NY session, market pricing implied \~1.7bp of cuts through the end of this year. USTs bull-flattened and closed on Monday with 2y yields down 6bp and 10y yields down 11bp.

In funding markets, the Fed's ON RRP dropped below \$400bn for the first time since mid-April as the front end normalized after month-end activity in the repo market.

Though equities initially fell alongside Treasury yields, into the NY afternoon they pared their losses and the S&P 500 (+0.1%) ended slightly higher.

After Sunday’s OPEC+ meeting, Brent crude oil futures (-3.7%) closed below \$80 a barrel for the first time since February. Over the weekend, OPEC+ unexpectedly rolled out a plan which extended the “voluntary” production cuts from key members such as Saudi Arabia and Russia. However, these cutbacks would be phased out starting in October, which was earlier than many market participants expected and hence sent prices lower.

## Japan:

JGBs opened the Tokyo session richer, following the rally in USTs on Friday after US PCE data confirmed the deceleration in inflationary pressures in April on the heels of strong data in 1Q24. However, in the late Tokyo morning, the super-long sector sold off. Given the continued uncertainty around the BoJ’s policy rate and QT, Japanese real money market participants remain reluctant to buy the super-long sector, which continued to underperform in the Tokyo afternoon. The 10y sector outperformed ahead of Tuesday’s auction. By Tokyo close, the JGB curve twiststeepened; 2y JGB was flat at 0.4%, 10y JGB rallied 1.0bp d/d to 1.06%, and 40y JGB sold off 3.5bp d/d to 2.41% on a simple yield basis. For Tuesday’s 10y auction, we expect smooth digestion, supported by demand to square short positions; however, we do not expect market participants from the banking sector will rush to buy JGBs given lingering concerns for a cut in the BoJ’s bond purchases in the near term.

The TONA-OIS curve bull-steepened and outperformed JGBs in the short-end and super-long sectors, while 10y ASW richened. XCCY basis tightened, led by the belly of the curve.

USD/JPY traded in a range of 157.00-157.50 throughout the Tokyo session despite the rally in UST yields on Friday. While data released in Japan were mixed with sluggish 1Q capital spending, but solid manufacturing PMI, the demand for JPY carry trades amid the low FX volatility regime prevented USD/JPY from trading lower. USD/JPY traded at 157.4 into the London morning, but ground lower toward 157.00 as UST yields rallied. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY. EUR/JPY rose to 170.70 into London morning, but ground lower toward 170.20 following weak European PMI data.

## Europe:

In G10 FX, the biggest mover was SEK, which gained 1.1% despite its habitual sensitivity to US equities which sold off during the London session. Its neighbor, NOK, only gained 0.1% after roller-coaster price action in oil and gas, which ultimately pulled NOK in separate directions, and kept it largely unchanged. Interestingly, CHF rallied 0.7% as markets continued to pare CHF shorts, and was supported by the soft ISM data in the US. EUR gained 0.3% as it begins to approach the 1.09 mark. Sterling also gained 0.3% despite equity weakness.

European duration rallied, and the 10y Bund completely retraced last week’s selloff and fell 8bp to 2.58%, alongside the move lower in Treasury yields. Oil sold off 3% during the London session, and US ISM Manufacturing and Prices Paid for May were below consensus expectations, which supported duration. The belly of the curve led in the rally, and 5s30s bull-steepened 2bp (2s5s10s: -3bp). In money markets, greens rallied 8bp. In EGBs, peripheral spreads tightened (10y BTP/Bund: - 1bp) while 10y OAT/Bund spreads were unchanged on Monday despite S&P’s downgrade of France over the weekend. Core ASW stabilized in the rally.

## Dollar Bloc:

The main catalyst for G10 FX came out of the US on the back of weak ISM manufacturing survey data in May. Not only did the headline figure drop (as opposed to the consensus-expected marginal rise), but the sub-indices also pointed to weakening demand and slowing inflationary pressures. With that, USTs rallied, sending USD yields lower, and equities sold off. The effects were felt broadly for the US dollar, and the DXY USD Index fell 0.6%. CAD (+0.0%) was pulled in opposite directions as the US dollar weakness was likely counterbalanced by oil weakness and expectations of a BoC cut. Big moves were found in USD/JPY, which fell 0.8%, given its high sensitivity to US yields as of late. AUD gained 0.3% despite equity weakness.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies strengthened against USD amid in-line US inflation, softer US personal spending, and a broad rally in Asian equities. KRW, INR, and IDR were the major outperformers in the region. KRW led peers higher due to its high beta to USD and strong Korea PMI in May. USD/KRW declined 0.7% to 1,376 alongside outperformance in the equity market (KOSPI: +1.7%). After exit polls from the elections in India were released, INR initially saw the largest intraday rise since November 2022, to 83.00 per USD. In the Tokyo afternoon, INR (+0.4%) partially retraced gains to 83.16 per USD alongside a surge in the local equity market (SENSEX: +3.4%). IDR appreciated 0.1% to 16,230, partially driven by accelerated core CPI in Indonesia in May. TWD was relatively steady at 32.41, despite a strong performance in the equity market (TAIEX: +1.7%). CNH and CNY were largely unchanged given that strong Caixin Manufacturing PMIs had limited impacts on the market. THB and MYR were closed for local holidays.

AxJ rates outperformed broadly following the rally in UST on Friday. In China, the 1s10s CGB yield curve bull-steepened 1bp given a report backed by the PBoC and released on Friday, which reiterated warnings that the PBoC could step into the market to sell bonds if demand continues to rise. Meanwhile, the CNY NDIRS curve shifted lower by 2bp. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-flattened 3bp, while the 3s10s KTB yield curve twist-flattened 8bp. Hong Kong and Singapore swap curves shifted lower by 7bp and 4bp, respectively. In India, the 2s10s NDOIS curve bullflattened 2bp, while 10y G-sec yields fell 3bp to 6.96%. In Indonesia, the 1s10s IndoGB curve bull-flattened 2bp.

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
