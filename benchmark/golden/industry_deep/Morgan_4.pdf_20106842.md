# Current Quarterly Roll Environment

## Overview

The US Mar-Jun equity futures rolls are trading at relatively expensive levels compared to historical quarters (Figure 1). The S&P 500 roll is trading at its highest roll cost in 6 years, excluding the December rolls that are subject to year-end seasonality due to G-SIB dynamics. However, this seems reasonable given that investor positioning appears fairly elevated and markets are trading at all-time highs. On the positioning side, we note asset managers<sup>’</sup> net futures positioning is at record highs (Figure 2) and increased \~ \$60Bn since mid-December, and systematic investors have re-levered over the past few months (see the Positioning section for more details). The roll spreads have moved significantly higher over the past month as markets rallied (which drove the roll fair value higher, Figure 4The S&P50rol mvdig nFbuay tsp  c. f ), while implied financing rates also richened moderately given the strong demand for equity financing (Figure 3S&P 503Mfinacg spredm htoly v,u w -).

Our base case is for the S&P 500 roll to richen moderately further into expiry this quarter given long positioning, however we wouldn’t take large active timing tilts due to asymmetric market risks.

In a status quo environment (i.e. equity markets holding steady or continuing to rise), we would expect the roll to moderately richen into expiry given significant long positioning. Even though the roll is already quite expensive, it could be pushed higher as long rollers come looking for liquidity over the next two weeks. The Mar<sup>’</sup>24 contract has been richening recently (e.g. the Mar<sup>’</sup>24 AIR TRF rallied from \~40bps to \~55bps over the past 2 weeks), which could indicate stronger outright long futures demand that could be propagated to the roll spread as the March positions are rolled over the next couple of weeks. Our roll trend model also predicts a modest richening trend into expiry given the increase in positioning (see below). But we wouldn<sup>’</sup>t expect a large upside move since year-end/G-SIB dynamics aren<sup>’</sup>t at play this quarter, long positioning isn<sup>’</sup>t extreme, and cash-rich buyside investors are likely to step in to monetize the rich implied financing levels, adding supply.

However, we believe that the risk/reward for equities is poor following the strong recent rally, with the market priced for perfection, equity market internals unhealthy given extreme concentration, elevated investor positioning, expensive valuations, upside risks to inflation, and high geopolitical tail risks (see Global Asset Allocation). In other words, we see a higher likelihood that the next few % move from here is to the downside rather than to the upside. This suggests an asymmetric distribution for roll spread levels, since spot weakness, if realized, would weigh on the roll in terms of both price (due to lower fair values) and likely roll cost (due to reduced futures demand increased supply). However, with few near-term catalysts expected that could force such a repricing, a sell-off may not develop soon enough to impact the roll.

As we discussed in recent quarters, with short-term rates elevated, the roll level has strong spot dependency. Higher (lower) spot prices mechanically translate into higher (lower) roll fair values (e.g. see Figure 44) since the roll spreads price in the interest cost of financing a larger (smaller) contract notional value, respectively. For example, it<sup>’</sup>s possible for the roll to simultaneously increase in price but cheapen in terms of implied financing rates on a spot rally, if the roll spread increases by less than its fair value. Similarly, a hawkish (dovish) repricing of the upcoming FOMC meetings could push the roll spreads higher (lower) since fair values move in-line with rates, even though the roll could cheapen (richen) vs. fair value, respectively.

## Roll Trend Model<sup>1</sup>

Our roll trend model predicts a moderate richening trend into expiry of \~0.2bps/day (\~8bps in total over the two weeks into expiry). Within the model, positioning variables improved vs. last quarter: dealers<sup>’</sup> net futures positions became shorter over the past quarter (by \~6% of OI), while S&P 500 tracking ETFs had slight net inflows over the past couple of months. Risk aversion/sentiment and funding indicators showed mixed changes vs. last quarter with the VIX increasing modestly to an average of \~14 in the lead up to the roll vs. \~13 last quarter, and Financials<sup>’</sup> CDS levels decreasing moderately q/q.

## Dec-Mar Roll Recap

For the Dec<sup>’</sup>23/Mar<sup>’</sup>24 US equity index futures rolls, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 exhibited a richening bias, moving significantly higher through the highest volume days (but cheapening the last few days into expiry), and the Russell 2000 exhibited mixed trends, in-line with our predictions. Spot levels rallied significantly over the roll period, while rates held relatively steady, resulting in fair values and roll spread prices generally moving higher. Meanwhile, the MSCI EM roll initially cheapened significantly as the roll first became active but generally richened thereafter, and the MSCI EAFE roll exhibited mixed trends and traded sideways through the peak volume days. See last quarter<sup>’</sup>s Roll Recap for more details.

## Russell 2000 and Nasdaq 100 Rolls

The Russell 2000 roll is trading rich to fair value and expensive compared to rolls over the past few years, but cheaper than in December. Last quarter, the Russell 2000 roll exhibited mixed trends into expiry. Russell futures positioning appears to have moderately improved over the past quarter, though likely remains weaker than for the large cap US contracts. We therefore believe the Russell 2000 roll is slightly biased to richen into expiry. However, with the roll trading rich on a historical basis, positioning only moderately improved, and the Russell still trading just a couple of % above key momentum levels that could trigger de-leveraging by systematic strategies, we would keep active timing tilts relatively small.

The Nasdaq 100 roll is trading rich to fair value and above its average roll cost in the last few years, but below last quarter<sup>’</sup>s VWAP roll cost. Last quarter, the Nasdaq 100 futures roll showed a significant richening bias over the roll period (in line with our expectations). Positioning remains strong in Nasdaq futures, so we expect the Nasdaq roll to richen into expiry again this quarter, and recommend rolling longs early/shorts late.

## MSCI EM and MSCI EAFE Rolls

The MSCI EM roll is trading cheap to fair value, but the roll has barely started to trade and bid-offer is wide, so it may still have a significant adjustment to a clearing level once the roll becomes active this week. Last quarter, the MSCI EM roll initially cheapened significantly as the roll became active, and despite a moderate richening trend through expiry week, remained cheaper than its starting level. EM futures positioning appears to have deteriorated over the past quarter, so we believe the MSCI EM roll is biased to cheapen into expiry.

The MSCI EAFE roll is trading cheap to SOFR fair value. However, it has barely started to trade and bid/offer remains wide, so there could be a sizable price adjustment to a tradable level once the roll spread begins actively trading later this week (as has occurred frequently in the past). The roll faces dividend risk and we would expect the roll to trade significantly cheap vs. fair value this quarter due to dividend concentration, since the futures embed lower tax withholding rates than those assumed by MSCI, which are used in our fair value calculation. Last quarter, the MSCI EAFE futures roll was fairly well-behaved and exhibited mixed trends, as we predicted. Investor positioning in EAFE futures appears moderately improved vs. last quarter (see the Positioning section below), so we believe the roll is slightly biased to richen into expiry this quarter (once a tradable level is established).

## Rates Outlook

We see interest rate risk over the upcoming roll period given uncertainty on the timing of the first Fed rate cut, and potential for incoming data releases (e.g. NFP on 3/8, CPI on 3/12) or Fedspeak to shift expectations, but the March FOMC meeting falls after futures expiry.

Rates markets are currently pricing in an expectation that the Fed will hold rates steady for the March meeting, and a \~25% chance it will cut in early May; however, market pricing has moved sharply over the past 2 months, at the start of the year pricing in a full cut in each of the March and May meetings, to now pricing only around a quarter of a cut total by May. Our US economists note that the minutes to the January FOMC meeting reinforced a patient approach to cutting rates, with participants noting that risks to achieving their employment and inflation goals are coming into better balance, but they are still highly attentive to inflation risks (see here). Additionally, since then the January PCE was released, with Powell<sup>’</sup>s preferred super-core PCE increasing 0.6% in January (+3.5% y/y), giving further support to the Fed<sup>’</sup>s reluctance on rate cuts. Our economists also note that the participants had contrasting views on financial conditions, with several participants noting that financial conditions could become too easy, and both the staff and the Committee sounded a little more concerned about the financial health of the household sector. As a result, the Fed is likely to remain data dependent moving forward.

Thus, we see interest rate risk this quarter, given the possibility for incoming data releases (e.g. NFP on 3/8, CPI on 3/12) or any of the Fedspeak in the run up to the March 20<sup>th</sup> FOMC meeting (e.g. Powell testifying before Congress 3/6-3/7, Kashkari speaking 3/6, Mester on 3/7, Williams on 3/8) to shift expectations on the timing and extent of rate cuts. For US CPI, our economists look for 3.0% oya (+0.3% m/m) on headline CPI and 3.7% oya (+0.3% m/m) for core CPI. In addition to their impact on rates expectations, these datapoints/speeches could impact risk sentiment and drive market volatility, and thus impact the roll as investors rush for liquidity to implement new directional positions.

As discussed in past quarters, we switched our roll cost calculations to reference SOFR rates due to LIBOR<sup>’</sup>s cessation as a benchmark – see our Roll Outlook note from December 2021 for details/implications.

## Dividends

The S&P 500 roll faces limited dividend risk this quarter as there are few payments that go ex around March expiry that remain unannounced.

We estimate \~19.16 index points (\~38bps) of dividends on the S&P 500 between the March and June expiries, modestly above the quarterly dividend futures that are trading at \~18.95ip. S&P 500 dividend news has been positive overall through the 4Q23 reporting season, with \~100 companies announcing hikes (vs. just 2 ordinary dividends cut), new large dividend initiations from META and BKNG, and the index<sup>’</sup>s annualized dividend run rate rising by \~0.7ip. However, as we discussed in a recent S&P 500 Dividend Weekly (here), a partial offset to this is the cuts to several commodity companies<sup>’</sup> variable dividend programs, including PXD, DVN, EOG and NEM.

Of the 19.16 expected dividend points over the upcoming quarter, 16.7 index points (\~33bps) have yet to be announced, but just \~0.9 undeclared index points (\~2bps) are expected to go ex by March expiry or within a week afterwards. However, we see limited timing risk surrounding the largest cusp dividends that remain to be announced. AVGO, historically a source of uncertainty due to a large dividend that varied between falling pre/post expiry, is currently projected to have an ex-date on Mar 19th, and since 2020 they have established a pattern of their ex-date falling on the 21st (or the prior business day if the 21st falls on a weekend). JCI poses some risk given that historically they have paid on March expiry, but this year its dividend is forecasted to fall postexpiry. Other large cusp dividends pose limited timing risk: PM, MDT and VICI have fallen post March expiry for the last few years. Additionally, \~2 points (\~4bps) of dividends are expected to go ex in the week on each side of the June expiry, but these dividends present considerably less risk as they are unlikely to be announced during the upcoming roll. Table 1 lists the largest undeclared cusp dividends around each of the next two quarterly expiries.

There are a few dividend increase announcements that could fall into the roll period (e.g. NXPI), however these are relatively small dividends and thus do not present large risk to the roll. There are a number of large dividend payers expected to announce increases to their quarterly distributions during the upcoming quarter (e.g. AAPL, UNH, PG, IBM, QCOM, JNJ, etc.), but these are all expected to be announced after March expiry and thus shouldn<sup>’</sup>t materially impact the roll.

## Roll Dynamics

The Russell 2000 roll is trading rich to fair value and expensive compared to rolls over the past few years, but cheaper than in December (Figure 19). The roll is trading \~27bps rich relative to SOFR-based fair value, and has generally richened over the past couple of weeks (Figure 18). Last quarter, the Russell 2000 roll exhibited mixed trends, initially richening at the start of the roll period, but then generally cheapening into the highest volume days, before richening again the last day before expiry. Russell futures positioning appears to have moderately improved over the past quarter as dealers moved from flat to significantly short, though likely remains weaker than for the large cap US contracts (see the Positioning section below). We therefore believe the Rusell 2000 roll is slightly biased to richen into expiry. However, with the roll trading rich on a historical basis, positioning only moderately improved, and the Russell still trading just a couple of % above key momentum levels that could trigger de-leveraging by systematic strategies, we would keep active timing tilts relatively small.

The MSCI EAFE roll faces dividend risk due to seasonal dividend concentration. ## Appendix 1: Basic Roll Concepts

The implied funding rate (IFR) is the breakeven funding rate for selling the roll, buying the index at front-month expiration, and selling the index at back-month expiration. If the calendar IFR is historically low compared with the three-month forward financing rate, one considers the roll to be cheap (the relevant three-month Eurodollar future is generally used as a proxy for the forward funding rate).

The timing and amount of dividend payments influence the roll cost. An increase in expected dividend payments between front-month and back-month expirations should cause the spread between front- and back-month futures to narrow (the roll to cheapen). “Cusp dividends<sup>”</sup> are undeclared dividends that are expected to fall close to expiration of the second-month contract, but have the chance of being paid before or after the expiration.

Changes in short-term funding rates. An increase in funding rates generally causes futures prices to rise relative to cash prices because it increases the cost of carry. Thus, the roll cost reflects changes in the shape of the short end of the yield curve. Because the back-month contract has three months more of carry in its basis, changes in funding rates affect back-month futures more than front-month futures. As a rule of thumb, for a parallel change in funding rates:

Change in Roll Cost = (Index Level) x (Change in Funding Rate) / 4

For example, if short-term rates increase by 25 basis points and the underlying index is trading at 4200, we would expect the roll cost to increase by approximately 2.0 index points: 4200 x 0.0025 / 4 = 2.625

Supply and demand for roll ultimately determine the discrepancy between the roll cost and the roll fair value. The roll may trade rich or cheap depending on the demand for hedging, overall equity allocations, pricing and availability of competing products (e.g., equity swaps, ETFs, algorithmic trading), etc.
