# ASEAN Economies Present a Sizeable and Diverse Opportunity Set

## Derrick Kam

ASEAN in a global and EM context: From a macro perspective, discussions with investors on ASEAN tend to veer towards two main topics. For equity investors, the starting point of the conversation is almost always around growth, while for fixed income investors it is usually macro stability and more often than not the inflation outlook. On both fronts, we think ASEAN economies are well-placed in a global and EM context as they tend to enjoy relatively robust growth rates with fewer of the inflation fears that tend to affect other EM economies.

Sizing up the ASEAN growth opportunity: Within ASEAN, we cover five economies: Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore and Thailand. Collectively, these economies have a combined GDP of US\$3.5 trillion, as estimated by the IMF as of 2024, and they are home to 512 million people, as per UN statistics. This compares with Japan's US\$4.1 trillion GDP and US\$1.8 trillion in Korea. From just US\$492bn in 2000, combined GDP has grown around 7x for ASEAN economies under our coverage.

Comparatively strong growth dynamics within EM: On a real GDP growth basis, ASEAN also compares well with other regions in EM. For instance, real GDP growth in ASEAN has consistently outpaced that of CEEMEA and Latam for much of the past two decades. Between 2000-23, real GDP growth in ASEAN averaged 4.6% compared with 3.9% in CEEMEA and 2.4% in Latam. On a forward-looking basis, we expect ASEAN economies to generate an additional \~US\$250bn of nominal GDP every year between now and 2028, as per IMF estimates.

## Still a strong structural growth story

Structural positives still hold: The medium-term growth outlook remains strong for ASEAN. Indeed, the IMF projects that ASEAN will grow by an average of 4.7% over 2024- 29 compared to 3.4% for CEEMEA and 2.1% for Latam.

There are a number of factors which help anchor the strong growth outlook, in our view.

First, over the coming decade, the UN projects that demographic trends across ASEAN are still going to be positive in three out of the five that we cover – namely Indonesia, Malaysia and Philippines. Given that demographic trends tend to weigh on potential growth, this positive demographic picture suggests that the medium term structural growth opportunities still abound within ASEAN.

Second, the market has increasingly viewed ASEAN as a favorable investment destination for FDI flows, especially relating to supply chain diversification efforts.

In addition, as part of a complementary regional growth strategy, we have seen ASEAN governments collectively implementing reforms to promote an integrated marketplace. In the first phase, this has already been completed for goods, and in recent years the conversation has increasingly shifted towards the liberalisation of services trade. These efforts will likely complement those at national level, where policy-makers in each of the economies are also pursuing growth strategies.

To be sure, there are still cyclical challenges ahead: The current strong US dollar environment is one such cyclical challenge. While macro and external stability factors are now much more in check and FX reserves have been built up, currencies across ASEAN have come under intense depreciation pressures in recent months. The repricing of the Fed policy path has meant that we too have had to consider changes to central bank paths in ASEAN. For instance, we have pushed back our expectation for the start of the first rate cut in Indonesia and Philippines to 2025. We are also closely watching domestic politics and how this could influence fiscal policy. Finally, given that most economies in ASEAN are in the middle-income group, one longer-term concern that investors may have is whether these economies can break out of the so-called middle-income trap.

# Equity Strategy - A Low-Beta Safe Haven within EM

Jonathan Garner, Kristal Ji, Daniel Blake

From an equity strategy perspective it can often seem that ASEAN stocks are overlooked in the broader Asia / EM context. However, it is important to understand that considered as a whole, ASEAN has some unique cyclical characteristics and looks attractively valued.

MS analysts cover 126 stocks in ASEAN but it is fair to say that the region is often overlooked by investors. This is particularly so currently, as two major markets - Japan & India - are enjoying secular bull markets whilst a third - China - is deep in a structural bear market. Lacking scale (ASEAN is just 5% of the combined MSCI Asia / EM equities universe) or a single national geographic or pan-regional thematic, our experience indicates ASEAN stocks tend to find their way into portfolios on their merits alone. And there is something for every type of investor in ASEAN, from high growth / high valuation stocks in Indonesia to stable utility dividend streams in Singapore, from asset-light Internet business models early in their corporate life cycle, such as Grab and Sea Limited, to established consumer brands like Thai Beverage, a wide range of financial stocks, such as DBS and Bank Central Asia, and asset-heavy refining and utilities firms, including Thai Oil and SembCorp.

## ASEAN as a Counter-Cyclical / Low-Beta Safe Haven Play

A key feature of ASEAN equities is that due to the high weight in non-cyclical sectors and higher-than-average dividend yields versus the EM index they have historically been a relatively low-beta safe haven play.

These key attractive features of ASEAN equities are illustrated in Exhibit 11 and Exhibit 13 .

Exhibit 10 shows that ASEAN last outperformed overall MSCI EM during the major bear market of 2022 but has lagged somewhat, as EM and global risk appetite recovered in 2023 and year to date. This has also been affected by the drag from local currency weakness given elevated rates and USD strength. ASEAN also outperformed in the early 2010s during the Eurozone sovereign debt crisis as well as in the early phases of multipolar world dynamics in 2018.

Exhibit 11 shows that Asean's dividend yield of 4.0% is well above that of overall MSC EM at just 2.8%. Indeed the spread is well above the long-run average and the highest in a decade.

FactSet, a third party vendor, developed FactSet's SFDR Principal Adverse Impacts (PAI) data speci cally to support compliant Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR) reporting. FactSet collects PAI metrics from publicly available company disclosures, then standardizes the company-reported information to allow for easy comparison across companies. Speci <sup>fi</sup>cally, FactSet converts nonconforming data points to standardized units as required by SFDR, like gigawatt hours, metric tons of CO2e, Tons and anti-corruption <sup>fi</sup>nes paid in EUR.

The Holistic Equal Representation (HER) score, a Morgan Stanley Research proprietary framework, systematically ranks companies on their level of gender diversity representation: the percentage of women who are (1) board members, (2) executives, (3) managers, and (4) employees. For each metric of representation, we calculated monthly region and sector (cohort) neutral z-scores which were then combined at an equal weight (25% each) to formulate HER Score. By using z-scored values, we are able to identify companies that are above or below their cohort mean. If any of the 4 metrics are missing for a company, the z-score for that metric is set to 0. Companies that rank the highest within their cohort will have the largest z-score and the those that rank the lowest will have the smallest (i.e. negative) z-score. Companies with the highest (lowest) z-scores across all 4 metrics will have the highest (lowest) HER Score. In Japan, we z-scored the equal weighted average of the raw metrics of representation by sector. We show which tertile each company is in for workforce gender diversity, relative to their regional sector peers.

Sustainable Solutions Interactive Tool, a Morgan Stanley Research proprietary tool, maps historical and projected revenue and capex exposure of stocks to sustainability themes. ## Ayala Land – Real Estate

## Primary Analyst: Wilson Ng

Key Competitive Advantages: Ayala Land (ALI) is the leading residential developer in the Philippines, with an established track record and brand equity unparalleled by peers. Downstream, ALI owns and manages Philippines’ first and largest REIT, Ayala REIT (AREIT PM, non-covered), which serves as an offtake vehicle for ALI to recycle capital from stabilized developed assets into new investments, supporting higher returns over a more efficient capital structure.

Long-Term Drivers: ALI is poised to benefit from a fast-growing urban middle class population in need of organized housing in the Philippines. Boasting one of the strongest demographic profiles in the region with a relatively young and growing population, the Philippines' workforce is on track to substantially outgrow other ASEAN countries through 2050 (according to Asia Development Bank [ADB]), along with its GDP and GDP per capita. This suggests a long runway for growth in the economy and housing demand – key drivers of ALI’s earnings and profitability over the long run.

Risks to Competitive Advantages: Property cooling measures by regulators in the event the housing market becomes overheated and household leverage grows to excessive levels could affect ALI’s sales volumes and margins.
