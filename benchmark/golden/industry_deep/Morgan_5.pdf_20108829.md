# Edge AI: The Catalyst We Needed

As we argued in our Edge AI primer, Apple entering the Edge AI race has been the catalyst we needed for investors to take notice of this sub-theme. Edge AI stocks are now outperforming Powering AI names, which had driven most AI alpha generation in 1H24.

For context, see:

• Thematics: Tech Diffusion: Edge AI – Growing Impetus (7 Nov)

• Global Technology: AI PCs To Usher In The Next Leg Of PC Market Growth (21 May)

• Apple, Inc.: A More Intelligent Apple Makes Its Debut (11 Jun)

• Global Technology: AI Supply Chain – Apple WWDC implications (17 Jun)

Since publishing our Edge AI primer \~7 months ago, Edge AI has quietly been gathering momentum, waiting for a 'mass-market catalyst'. As we, and our global tech colleagues, highlighted in our initial work, Edge AI has implications across a broad range of uses, but consumer products will be the first to move the needle. We now believe this is in motion, with Apple and Samsung having dedicated AI strategies for smartphones. Separately, Lenovo/Dell and other global PC OEMs are investing in AI PCs. In addition to the Edge AI Six, our global tech analysts have highlighted a range of exposed companies across the tech stack. Similar to the Gen AI infrastructure, the exposed value chain is expanding.

## Edge Al – What's New?

Smartphones: Apple's developer conference was, in our view, the catalyst to bring attention to the Edge Al story, with the stock rallying +10% following. Apple's new Siri feature will be powered both on-device and by cloud. Phones without sufficiently good processors will not get the new features – in our view improving replacement cycles.

PC/Notebooks: Microsoft's Copilot will be available on-device and PC's with sufficient computing power are being launched in late 2Q24. In our US Tech Team's quarterly CIO survey, the percent of ClO's evaluating PCs optimized for GenAl is now at 50%. Two quarters ago this sat at 15%.

The market reaction supports our view that investors are warming to the opportunity that Edge AI can be successfully monetised. We believe corporates exposed to Edge AI have an opportunity to meaningfully improve their business, using three key levers. Strategies around Edge AI focus on 1) accelerating replacement cycles of devices, 2) commanding premium pricing of devices and 3) facilitating new revenue opportunities of on-device capabilities. ## Here Come the Killer Apps

Exhibit 6: In our view, Apple Intelligence and Microsoft Copilot give customers new impetus to upgrade to AI enabled devices
![](images/87025feaf898e4399a1e71d1371498be0d52584675ce870374377f75f936c74f.jpg)

In the last month, there have been two critical developments we believe investors should be aware of.

1. We now believe the 'Killer App' question has been answered by Apple Intelligence and Microsoft Copilot confirmed to be run on-device and requiring high-end hardware.

2. There is a broadening out in focus, from Edge AI OEMs and chip providers to the wider supply chain.

We believe the question around 'Killer Apps' has been answered. The key, and valid, question investors have raised in the Edge AI debate is "Why should consumers pay to get Gen AI directly on their device when the cloud can perform the same functionality?". An upgraded Siri and Microsoft Copilot satisfies this, in our view:

• A Gen AI powered Siri is the closest we have come to a virtual assistant akin to what our US Tech team laid out in their 'Building JARVIS' report. A virtual assistant that can perform tasks for the user is, in our view, a big step towards the 'Killer App' that investors have felt was lacking. Albeit, it will take time for this to be integrated into third-party apps.

• For PCs, there is now sufficient evidence that Microsoft Copilot can run partially on-device. Given the productivity benefits and CIOs' willingness to pay for AI PCs (see details here), we believe that Copilot satisfies the investor need for a Killer App to become incrementally bullish on the Edge AI story.

The broadening out to Edge is now in focus. When we originally published our note, we focused on the immediate impact to consumer device OEMs and the chips powering the devices. Since then, in parallel with increasing interest, there has been a broadening out in the investment debate: processor architecture, memory and device assembly, and now increasingly rolled up within the Edge AI story. In our view, this resembles the broadening out of the Gen AI enabler story from six months ago, providing investors with a broader way of playing the theme.

## AI PCs Making an Entry

For further reading, please see our US Tech Hardware analyst Erik Woodring and Greater China Tech Hardware analyst Howard Kao's report on AI PCs Growth Opportunity.

We believe new AI PC launches can help catalyse the commercial PC refresh and accelerate PC unit growth in 2025. With the launch of the first 40+ TOPS AI PCs in late 2Q24 (driven by Windows on Arm), Microsoft's Copilot AI assistant finally available ondevice, and new (more powerful) silicon launches expected later this year and into 2025, the AI PC revolution is upon us. We believe that the commercial PC market will be the initial adopters of AI PCs, given 1) they are primarily being marketed as productivity tools, and 2) the initial >\$1,000 price point, and that AI PCs – alongside a commercial PC installed base that is 13% larger than pre-COVID and due for a refresh ahead of Windows 10 end of life in October 2025 – can serve as a key factor accelerating PC market growth in 2H24 and 2025. In fact, according to our latest IT Hardware CIO survey, 75% of US and EU CIOs are evaluating and/or planning to evaluate AI PCs today, up from just 40% in 3Q23, and are, on average, willing to pay 6% more for AI PCs. Over time, we would expect consumers to adopt AI PCs as they proliferate to lower price points.

## Edge AI Thesis Revisit

For our full primer on Edge AI, please see: Thematics: Tech Diffusion: Edge AI – Growing Impetus (7 Nov 2023).

What is Edge AI? Edge refers to the field of running AI algorithms locally on hardware devices rather in centralised data centres in the cloud. It simply refers to inference or computation at the "edge" of a network, by definition closer to the point at which data is generated. This approach can help to save costs, reduce latency, rationalise bandwidth, enhance privacy, and allow for real-time processing at the point where data is collected. Every day, examples of Edge AI include facial recognition on a smart phone, voice recognition in smart speakers and anomaly detection in industrial equipment.

Is this a new phenomenon? Edge AI is not new. "The Edge" has historically been loosely interchangeable with the "Internet of Things" or "Industrial IoT", which has been through its own hype cycles over time. However, with the advent of Generative AI, the impetus for device upgrades to enable greater computational power natively on consumer hardware is accelerating and spanning beyond often narrowly used smart speakers, for example. As demonstrated by our European semis analyst, Lee Simpson, in his work on "TinyML" – a field of machine learning where models are small enough to be implemented on microcontroller units (MCUs) – Morgan Stanley Research into Edge AI has been gathering pace. Our November primer serves as a primer for generalists encountering the topic for the first time.

What are the benefits of Edge AI? In the context of this note, we focus on edge computing on battery powered devices (i.e. not mains powered). This can be a smartphone, smartwatch, drone – anything with the capability to process data. Doing ondevice processing – edge computing – offers different advantages to processing data in the cloud. We believe these advantages will fuel new applications in technology hardware. In our view, the key advantages to edge computing vs cloud computing are as follows: (1) personalisation; (2) low cost per query; (3) availability offline/on-demand; (4) low latency versus data centres; and (5) privacy and security with anomaly detection.

![](images/3f1d6c2c5c7c1aff82a6b5ead5fcd5ffa491770d0e5812c8faa4a403992853f7.jpg)
Source: Morgan Stanley Research

However, running AI models directly on device offers a set of challenges versus processing models in the cloud.

1. Battery & power consumption – Tests indicate that running AI models on device can require 30-50x as much of the battery. How limited is the device's battery without applying high-level AI models? We assess the battery life, and relative importance to the device of having more/less. Enablers in this category either A) expand the battery capacity or B) reduce the power consumption of components.

2. Processing power & memory – How processing and memory constrained is the device? We assess the current processing capacity and potential for increasing it to facilitate more complex models. Enablers in this category improve the processing power of the device.

3. Form factor – How size constrained is the device? What about the durability of the device and/or the harsh conditions to which an edge device could be subject (think of an oil & gas drill)? We assess whether the products' volume/weight limitations and durability are a barrier to enabling AI functionally. Enablers in this category allow devices to free up space for upgraded hardware.

4. Application use cases – What is the device used for? How central is it to AI applications? What is the proverbial ROIC for running inference at the edge on this device? For smartphones, this category isn't a constraint – it is clear why consumers would use a smartphone with Gen AI. But for wearables, it is a constraint. Similarly, we view the AI use cases for drones as less well established at this point in time. Enablers in this category are leveraging developer kits for building killer apps for constrained devices.

Embedded in the constraints lies the investment opportunity. We believe companies that can solve for these constraints will help AI models diffuse into objects and devices in different ways than if models were accessed purely via the cloud.
