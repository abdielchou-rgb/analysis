# What to Buy & Sell

The market is fidgeting to find the "next big theme" despite the current ones (1) having attractive fundamentals, (2) performing in line with prior multi-year themes, (3) seeing upgrades to consensus estimates and (4) remaining reasonably valued relative to growth expectations.

Don't sell secular themes early: We showed in this report that themes leading the market average a 66% return in their first calendar year of being 'live'. These themes typically follow-through to deliver 17% in calendar year 2 but with a wider spread. By contrast the S&P500 has returned 11% annually over the last decade. Amongst the three most popular themes in the market - Obesity, AI and Defence - the former is leading the pack. However, the fundamentals of these themes - shown in greater detail below - more than justify their valuations in our view. History tells us not to sell secular themes too early, particularly when earnings upgrades are being delivered, as they are for each of these themes.

• Obesity leaders have indeed re-rated strongly over the past 2 years, but continue to show best-in-class revenue and EBITDA growth relative to any other theme.

Does China Tech look appealing?: Whilst not a theme, per se, it is nonetheless captured by MSCI's thematic categorisation. With 10% sales growth, the analysis above would suggest that a valuation multiple c3x higher (at 6x EV/Sales vs c2x) would be more reasonable. This is particularly true as the companies in this grouping see EBITDA upgrades but with the stocks continuing to be de-rated YTD. While optically interesting, we note the latest views from our China team on market conditions: Tech and supply chain self-sufficiency has been elevated to national strategy in July’s Third Plenum. Whether more structural reforms and reflationary measures could be announced/executed to help reflate the economy remains critical. With rising protectionism and China's 3-D challenges, catalysts are lacking for any inflection at this point. From a bottom-up perspective, the recent downbeat outlook on profitability from PDD gives us pause.
