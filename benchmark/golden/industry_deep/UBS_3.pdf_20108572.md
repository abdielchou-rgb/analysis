# China Refining Sector APAC Focus: New capacity launch could lag expectations, supporting industry fundamentals

![](images/b8b2d99761113b44bdd9f86ad2e298248d19e9214704685b728d657fa6833ff0.jpg)

We expect China's refining capacity growth to slow to 1-2% in 2024-26, and the Yulong refining project – the largest new refining project in Asia this year – to begin mass production no earlier than end-2024, later than mainstream institutional forecasts (IHS, Baiinfo). Leveraging UBS Evidence Lab satellite imagery, we find the project's aromatics and ethylene downstream units remain under construction. The progress of Huajin Aramco Petrochemical Company (HAPCO) is also behind our expectation, which we believe could support China's aromatics and ethylene fundamentals. We most prefer coal/gas-based olefin stocks with cost advantages (Baofeng, Satellite), and like beneficiaries of improving sector fundamentals (Sinopec, Hengli). We downgrade Eastern Shenghong to Neutral.

## Yulong and HAPCO projects may launch slower than expected

Per UBS Evidence Lab's satellite imagery in March, the Yulong project's crude distillation units (CDUs) are largely complete, but its aromatics and ethylene downstream units are still being built. We therefore expect the project to begin large-scale operation no earlier than year-end. We also push back our forecast of when HAPCO will be launched to 2026. As such, we lower our domestic refining capacity growth forecast for 2024 to 1.2% (vs 2.3% demand growth), and expect refining fundamentals to remain intact in H224, given the Yulong project's low oil product yield and the larger second batch of oil product export quotas this year, which should ease domestic supply pressures.

## Fundamentals to improve for ethylene and remain strong for PX

We expect Yulong project's ethylene/aromatics' capacity to launch later than the market expects, and forecast the effective capacity growth of ethylene in China at 4.6%, well short of the planned 18%. IHS forecasts 2024E global ethylene supply growth to slow from 4.3% in 2023 to 2.6%. We also expect naphtha-based ethylene to turn from losses to Rmb100/t gross profit in 2024-25, PX gross profit to stay above Rmb700/t, and downstream polyester fibre gross profit to turn from losses to Rmb100/t in 2024-25.

## Q: Refining: will the Yulong project impact industry fundamentals in H224?

Unlikely. We do not foresee the Yulong project impacting the Chinese refining sector's solid profitability in H224. 1) The oil products yield at the Yulong project is set to be low at under 40%. We estimate the project will account for just 0.2-0.5% of China's total gasoline/diesel/kerosene output in 2024. 2) We expect the second batch of oil product export quotas, which is 56% YoY larger, to help ease domestic supply pressures.We think the peak period for China's refining capacity expansion has passed and expect the capacity growth to slow to 1-2% in 2024-26E.

## Q: Olefins: can ethylene fundamentals improve in 2024?

Yes. Unlike consensus, we expect ethylene profitability to improve in 2024-25, mainly as: 1) Global ethylene supply growth is likely to slow down from 4.3% to 2.6%, compounded by potential shutdowns. 2) We forecast growth in China's ethylene effective capacity to come to 4.6% in 2024 (versus a planned 18%). 3) PMI and GDP growth showed signs of improvement in Q124, and potential ethylene facility maintenance in Q224 may ease supply pressure.

## Q: Aromatics: can PX profitability remain solid in 2024?

Yes. The Yulong project is the only PX capacity planned for 2024, and downstream PTA capacity expansion should underpin PX demand. YTD utilisation of downstream textile industry has stayed around 80%, and we expect fundamentals in the polyester filament space to improve.

The enterprise owns Yulong refining-petrochemical integrated project, which has a total designed refining capacity of 40mtpa. Ground was broken on the project in October 2020.The enterprise was granted 8.3mt of crude oil import quotas for 2024. ## Our major conclusions

1) In the refining zone, the 10mtpa/10mtpa atmospheric/atmospheric and vacuum distillation units have been largely built. Given the 8.3mt of crude oil quotas the enterprise has secured for 2024, we expect the Yulong project to begin selling oil products in H224.

2) Of the two 1.5mtpa aromatics complexes, one has seen faster progress in construction, while construction of the other remains in the early stage.

3) Of the two 1.5mtpa ethylene facilities, one set has been largely built and the other has entered the late stage of construction. We expect the facilities to begin trial production in H224. However, the downstream facilities ( eg. PP/PE production facilities) remain under construction.

4) We expect the project to begin mass operation no earlier than end-2024, as prior mega refining-petrochemical projects suggest it usually takes 3-6 months to launch CDUs and 6-9 months to launch ethylene/aromatics facilities.

# Q: Will refining, aromatics and olefin fundamentals stabilise or improve marginally in 2024?

UBS-S VIEW

We expect ethylene fundamentals to improve, and aromatics fundamentals to remain strong. Also, we expect the launch of the Yulong project to have a relatively limited impact on refining fundamentals in H224.

Refining: will the Yulong project impact fundamentals in H224?

We see no significant impact from the launch of the Yulong project on the domestic oil product market in H224. Per UBS Evidence Lab, atmospheric/ atmospheric and vacuum distillation units in the refining zone of the Yulong project are largely built. Given the 8.3mt crude oil quotas the enterprise has obtained for 2024, we expect it to begin selling oil products in H224. That said, we expect domestic refining profitability to remain solid in H224 for three reasons.

1) Yulong is designed with a low oil products yield: The EIAs for the Yulong project and other publicly available information indicates the project will have less than a 40% yield for oil products, which is relatively low vs more than 60% for Sinopec. ## Q: Will coal-to-olefins' (CTO) profit advantage continue to widen in 2024-25?

Yes. We estimate the CTO annual average cost advantage at Rmb1,940/1,500 in 2022/23. UBS expects Brent prices at US\$83/80/bbl and coal prices at Rmb850/770/t in 2024/25E. We therefore forecast the CTO cost advantage to gradually widen to Rmb2,100-2,300/t in 2024-25.

## Q: Olefins: can ethylene fundamentals improve in 2024?

Yes. Unlike consensus, we expect ethylene profitability to improve in 2024-25, mainly as: 1) Global ethylene supply growth is likely to slow down from 4.3% to 2.6%, compounded by potential shutdowns. 2) We forecast growth in China's ethylene effective capacity to come to 4.6% in 2024 (versus a planned 18%). 3) PMI and GDP growth showed signs of improvement in Q124, and potential ethylene facility maintenance in Q224 may ease supply pressure.

## Q: Can olefin capacity additions/stronger sector fundamentals drive a 50%+ earnings CAGR in 2023-25 for Baofeng?

Yes. We forecast an earnings CAGR of 60% for Baofeng in 2023-25, underpinned by the gradual launch of the 3mtpa olefine capacity at its Inner Mongolia plant in 2024-25. The olefin plant in Inner Mongolia has a higher cost advantage than its Ningxia plant, mainly due to: 1) lower transportation and labour costs; and 2) lower raw coal consumption per tonne. We expect the new capacity in Inner Mongolia to lift GPM from 30% in 2023 to 33%/36% in 2024-25E.

We expect Baofeng’s 2024-25E earnings growth to be driven by: 1) the scheduled launch of the 3mtpa olefin capacity in Inner Mongolia in October 2024; and 2) cost advantages, as per-tonne olefin unit consumption is lower than the industry average and could decline further at the Inner Mongolia plant, per management. UBS expects the coal price downtrend to continue in 2024-25E, which would be positive for coal-based chemical companies amid elevated oil prices. We initiate coverage with a Buy rating.

1) Based on per-tonne profit data for cracking facilities in the past decade, profits for ethane cracking and CTO facilities outperformed naphtha cracking facilities. 2) A large amount of new coal capacity has been approved since 2022 in China and coal prices have declined from a 2021 peak of over Rmb2,000/t. UBS expects coal prices to fall from Rmb967/t in 2023 to Rmb850/770/t in 2024/25E. 3)Baofeng's 3mtpa olefin project at its Inner Mongolia plant is on track for launch and ramp-up through 2024-25, including 1.5mtpa of polyethylene (PE) and 1.5mtpa of polypropylene (PP).
