## Japan Equity Strategy

## Monthly strategy: Yen weaker than ¥157/\$ negative for real wages, equity prices

Japanese equities have corrected since the start of April on the increased likelihood of US interest rates remaining high for long. Normally, yen weakness is positive for Japanese equities, but we think the reason that Japanese equities have fallen further than US equities is that, in addition to a sense of vigilance about exchange intervention, the market has become aware of the negative aspects of the yen<sup>’</sup>s depreciation. The near-term focus will be on the outcome for yen weakness accelerated after the BOJ<sup>’</sup>s April Monetary Policy Meeting (MPM) and the impact on equity prices via the effect it has on real wages and personal consumption. The modeling of our macro equity research team indicates that the yen<sup>’</sup>s weakness is positive for Japanese equities up to ¥157/\$ (the breakeven point at which real wages turn upward; an average FX rate for FY2024 ), but negative beyond that. While US interest rates increasingly look like remaining high for some time, J.P. Morgan<sup>’</sup>s forecast is not for the yen to weaken that far, and we expect real wages to start to rise modestly after some time lag. However, gains by consumption-related stocks are likely to be delayed by the tardiness of the recovery in personal consumption, and we expect overseas demand stocks to continue to outperform for some time. The probability of rises in interest rates is likely to increase at around ¥157/\$, so we reiterate our Overweight stance on financial stocks (banks, insurers). Real estate stocks are also likely to continue performing strongly. In this report, we outline three investment ideas: the J.P. Morgan Japan Core 30 Basket of Japanese companies likely to drive growth in Japanese equities and Japanese companies over a term of 5–10 years across the economic cycle, focus stocks related to consumption, and blue chips yet to see significant price gains.

## Summary

Yen depreciation is normally positive for Japanese equities, but since the start of April the Japanese market has fallen further than US equities because investors are increasingly conscious of the negative aspects of the yen<sup>’</sup>s weakness.

Yen weakness accelerated after the BOJ<sup>’</sup>s April MPM. The yen<sup>’</sup>s depreciation is positive for Japanese equities up to ¥157/\$, but negative beyond that. This is the breakeven point at which real wages do or do not turn upward (an average FX rate for FY2024; assuming WTI price of \$83/ bbl).

We expect the recoveries in real wages and personal consumption to be delayed because US interest rates remain high for long, and the shift from external-demand stocks to internal-demand stocks as the main market driver could therefore occur later.

We think upward pressure on yen interest rates will increase at around ¥157/\$, so the outperformance of financial stocks should continue.

Near-term focus on impact of excessive yen depreciation on Japanese equities: Japanese equities have corrected since the start of April on the increased likelihood of US interest rates remaining high for long. Yen weakness is normally positive for Japanese equities, but the declines in the Japanese market have been in line with or larger than those in US equities. We point out the need to be prepared for a return of the high-for-long interest rates of summer last year, and discuss the negatives for equity prices of the excessive depreciation of the yen (see report Japan equity strategy in preparation for return of the “high for long<sup>”</sup> scenario). The specific turning point was when the equity market reacted negatively as the USDJPY exceeded the ¥152/\$ that was seen as the government<sup>’</sup>s defense line, and we argue that there will be concern regarding the expected upturn in real wages if the currency pair rise past ¥157/\$(see report Is long-term yen depreciation positive for the stock market?). In Japan, we expect companies to make greater efforts to add value as full-year results reports peak in May, and with capital expenditure also turning upward we remain bullish on Japanese equities thanks to such structural catalysts. In the short term, however, we note the risk that Japanese equities will remain flat or correct to some extent due to US interest rates remaining high for longer.

Yen’s depreciation positive for Japanese equities up to ¥157/\$ (breakeven point for upturn in real wages), but negative beyond that: The near-term focus will be on the outcome for yen weakness accelerated after the BOJ<sup>’</sup>s April MPM and the impact on equity prices via the effect it has on real wages and personal consumption. Our macro equity research team thinks that the yen<sup>’</sup>s depreciation normally adds to the market<sup>’</sup>s resilience to downside risk, but that a weakening to above the ¥157/\$ (an average FX rate for FY2024) level noted above would cause import price inflation to fully offset the 3.6% increase in base wages indicated by shunto spring wage negotiations, thereby increasing the risk that real wages will not start to rise (Figures 1-3). Here we assume the WTI price remains at our forecast of \$83/bbl, but if it rises above this, then the breakeven point for the USDJPY would decline further. If the upturn in real wages that is priced in by the market fails to materialize, then there would be limited potential for gains by Japanese equities driven by internal-demand stocks. We think that ¥157/\$ and \$83/bbl constitute a red line that that the BOJ will not want crossed. The main scenario of our economics research team is for real wages to turn slightly positive and for a recovery in personal consumption after some delay.

Sector allocation: Under such a macro scenario, we reiterate our Overweight stance on consumer discretionary (cyclical, consumer-related stocks) and staples despite delays to the recovery in personal consumption, because we expect the FRB to cut rates and real wages to turn upward. Specifically, we remain Overweight on cyclical consumptionrelated sectors such as retail, transport, and leisure. On the contrary, we expect overseas-demand sectors, such as autos, machinery, and semiconductors, to continue to outperform, as the yen weakens further. These cyclical overseas-demand stocks have corrected significantly over the past month, and should find support from the yen<sup>’</sup>s weakness after pricing in results reports. We reiterate our Overweight stance on financials, as the yen<sup>’</sup>s weakness will put upward pressure on interest rates (we prefer insurers on a decline in BOJ purchases of JGBs and banks on the prospect of a rate hike). Among factors, we think the effectiveness of EPS revisions will increase during the results season, and the prospect of interest rates remaining high for long could mean an increased risk of the unwinding of the momentum trade. We expect the value factor to remain effective, thanks to the normalization of monetary policy in Japan.

## Level of breakeven exchange rate that would prevent upturn in real wages and equity market

Real wages unlikely to turn upward if average FY2024 exchange rate above ¥157/\$ The near-term focus will be on the outcome for yen weakness yen weakness accelerated after the BOJ<sup>’</sup>s April MPM and the impact on equity prices via the effect it has on real wages and personal consumption. Our macro equity research team thinks that yen weakness is normally positive for Japanese equities, as it adds resilience to downside risk. However, if excessive yen depreciation meant the exchange were to rise above an FY2024 average of ¥157/\$, there would be an increased risk that the rise in import prices would fully offset the 3.6% increase in base wages indicated by spring wage negotiations, making an upturn in real wages unlikely.

This is the breakeven point based on our modeling of the relationships between the USDJPY and import prices, and import prices and the inflation rate. Figure 1 shows change in the core CPI at different levels of FX rate. The row in bold is based on assumptions of a WTI price of \$83/bbl and an FY2024 average exchange rate of ¥155/\$, and shows that the core CPI would be boosted by 0.5ppt if the exchange rate were ¥155/ \$ and 0.8ppt if it were ¥157/\$. In its April 26 Outlook report, the BOJ<sup>’</sup>s price outlook was for a core CPI of 2.8% at end-FY2024 (Figure 2). Relative to the 3.68% increase in base wages indicated by spring wage negotiations, the breakeven is ¥157/\$, and if the yen weakens to above that then there would be no upturn in real wages (Figure 3).

## Rises in crude oil price assumptions cause the breakeven exchange rate for an upturn in real wages to decline

If the WTI price were to rise to above our assumption of \$83/bbl, the breakeven exchange rate would decline further. For example, if the WTI price rose to \$85/bbl, the breakeven exchange rate would be ¥151/\$, and if the WTI price were in reverse to fall to \$80/bbl, then real wages would turn upward at ¥149/\$. Based on the matrix in Figure 2, we think that to realize an upturn in real wages, ¥157/\$ and \$83/bbl represent a red line that the BOJ will not want crossed.

## Our main scenario for upturn in real wages

We forecast an end-2024 WTI price of \$81/bbl (fiscal year average of \$80/bbl) and an end-2024 exchange rate of ¥153/\$ (fiscal year average of around ¥152/\$), and therefore expect an upturn in real wages and a recovery in consumption (Figure 4).

Limited downward pressure on stocks even if wage growth doesn’t turn positive In terms of the impact on equity prices, in past periods of increases in real wages the growth has been driven by deflation, so there has been no clear outperformance by domestic-demand stocks (as shown in Figure 5, there have been rises during the past five periods other than those during the bursting of the bubble and under the Koizumi government). However, this time we think that positive real wage growth when there is inflation will drive the outperformance of internal-demand names. Even if the ongoing depreciation of the yen prevents real wages from turning upward, there is only limited upside for internal-demand stocks, as indicated by the only small gains they made despite the results for spring wage negotiations (Figure 6), but we nevertheless think that downside for indices will be limited.

## Japan equity outlook

Regarding the global economic outlook, which is the basis of the Japan equity outlook, the main scenario of our global economics research team is for a bullish risk that US latent growth rates remain high despite interest rates staying high for longer (probability of 30%), but the team ascribes the same probability also to a scenario for the economy to slump after interest rates have been kept high for long (Figure 8). We forecast three FRB rate cuts between July and year-end, and the Fixed Income Strategy research team forecasts a decline in the 10-year US Treasury yield from the current 4.6% to 4.0%. In Japan, we expect very little reversal in the yen<sup>’</sup>s depreciation despite expecting rate hikes in July and December (we forecast an end-2024 exchange rate of ¥153/\$; Figure 9). This macro scenario assumes that the yen remains weak despite two BOJ rate hikes and that the increase in import prices puts downward pressure on real wages.

Our macro equity research team sees a range of global risk factors, such as the strength of the dollar, rises in bond yields, increases in crude-oil prices, and increasing market concentration. So we expect change in the equity market, which over the past few months has seen expansion in share-price multiples, low volatility and tight credit spreads, while it has not factored in potential negative catalysts. There has been an increase in downside risk for the market due to a combination of inflation remaining high, adverse interest rate rises, and the macro situation, so we think more defensive positioning is preferable (please see Mislav Matjka<sup>’</sup>s April 22 report USD, bond yields, oil and concentration all showing elevated risks remains a problematic backdrop).

The near-term focus will be on the outcome for yen weakness accelerated after the BOJ<sup>’</sup>s April MPM and the impact on equity prices via the effect it has on real wages and personal consumption. The economic research team<sup>’</sup>s forecast for an upturn in real wages is for only modestly positive growth, but the main scenario is nevertheless for an upturn in real wages to be realized even if the recovery in consumption is tardy and growth in real wages is only very modest. The outlook for Japanese stocks remains the same (Figure 11).

## Sector allocation

We remain Overweight on consumer discretionary (cyclical, consumer-related stocks) and staples even though under a high-for-long scenario for interest rates the recovery in personal consumption will likely be delayed, because we continue to expect FRB rate cuts. Specifically, we remain Overweight on cyclical, consumer-related sectors such as retail, transport, and leisure (see our report Time to look at consumption names). At the same time, we expect overseas-demand sectors, such as autos, machinery, and semiconductors, to continue to outperform as we think the yen will depreciate further. Such cyclical external-demand stocks have corrected significantly over the past month. Once they have factored in earnings during the results season, we think they will find support from the yen<sup>’</sup>s weakness. The sector EPS revision indices point to upward revisions for both external-demand and internal-demand sectors, but the performance of internal-demand sectors has been inferior (Figure 15).

We remain Overweight on financials also, as the yen<sup>’</sup>s weakness will put upward pressure on interest rates.

Under a risk scenario of the yen weakening to above ¥160/\$ (annual average of ¥157/\$), FRB rate cuts would provide support to growth stocks such as semiconductors and telecoms, but the story of a recovery in personal consumption would recede, putting downward pressure on related sectors. The negative effect on overseasdemand sectors would be relatively smaller, so they should outperform for longer. The primary impact on financials would be positive, as the BOJ would be forced to accelerate monetary policy normalization in an attempt to halt the yen<sup>’</sup>s depreciation, so we remain Overweight on this sector.

Japanese investors<sup>’</sup> positions remained bullish on Japanese equities overall until the start of April, with buying focusing on electronics, precisions, financials, consumption, steel, machinery, and materials, and selling focusing on pharmaceuticals, food, utilities, construction, real estate, and telecoms (Figures 17-19, monthly QUICK survey).

## Style allocation

With respect to style, since the start of the results season the effectiveness of EPS revisions has increased, while there is an increased risk of the unwinding of the momentum factor due to changes in the macro situation that make it likely that interest rates will remain high for longer in the US. We expect the effectiveness of the momentum factor to decline in Japan also, although this will be impacted by the results of the magnificent seven. We remain Overweight on value, growth, and high dividend, and Underweight on small caps. In addition to the direction of interest rates, corporate action aimed at improving governance also supports value, while US rate cuts look likely to support growth. However, with the high-for-long scenario now looking more probable for US interest rates, we think it will take some more time for the effectiveness of growth to improve.

The performance of small- and mid-caps has lagged, but we think the recovery in domestic consumption will be some time in the future, and BOJ rate hikes would be a headwind, so we remain Underweight. We expect high dividend stocks to outperform thanks to an ongoing inflow of funds from individual investors.

## Analyst focus stocks in consumption-related sectors

Spring wage negotiations indicated strong guidance for wage increases, so real wages look increasingly likely to turn upward.
