## Developed Markets

## United States:

Once the highly anticipated annual US CPI revisions showed little change to the recent trend of inflation (core goods revised up, core services revised lower), appetite to take on risk in the Treasury market waned. With this came a softening in implied interest rate volatility, which offered little resistance to the grind higher in equities. Alongside the rise in equities, NOK (+0.6%) gains extended, after slightly stronger-than-expected January CPI data, as well as a rise in Brent crude oil prices. In line with the accommodative macro backdrop, both investment grade and high yield corporate credit CDX spreads tightened.

While UST yields ended the week higher by double digits across the curve, the majority of the rise took place on Monday as the bearish momentum from January nonfarm payrolls carried over into this week. Throughout the remainder of the week, US rates displayed a notable sensitivity to concerns around the extent of risks around US CRE exposures. A litany of Fedspeak also entered the fray but unfortunately for market makers, this Fedspeak was homogenous in nature.

The overarching message was one that acknowledged it would likely be appropriate to cut rates “later” or “at some point” this year. Yet, all participants sought “greater confidence” before making policy less restrictive and emphasized the now two-sided risks around their dual mandate. Remarks from Cleveland Fed President Mester summarized the policy stance well.

She said, “it would be a mistake to move rates down too soon or too quickly.” If the economy evolves as expected, she believes the FOMC “will gain that confidence later this year” and begin to move rates down. Her base case calls for rate cuts at a “gradual pace” and for three rate cuts this year. She added, “of course, if downside risks materialize, we would have the opportunity to move rates down more quickly, just as we raised rates more aggressively than usual to combat rising inflation. Or if inflation appears to be stalling at a level above our goal, we would have the opportunity to maintain a restrictive stance for longer.”

## Japan:

A continued rally in USD/JPY toward 150.00 seemingly encouraged bearish positioning in Japan rates and JGBs broadly sold off across the curve. Although BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida suggested a gradual rate hike path under the current economic and price outlook, the 5y JGB auction was weak with the lowest bid-tocover ratio (3.44x) since last August. Relatively weak demand for 5y paper led to a renewed sell-off across the curve. 20y JGBs particularly underperformed ahead of the 20y JGB auction next Tuesday; 20y JGBs sold off 3.5bp d/d to 1.505% on a simple yield basis. 30y+ JGBs traded firmly, supported by demand to buy the dip; 30y JGBs sold off 2.0bp d/d to 1.785%. TONA OIS bear-steepened and long-end JGB ASW outperformed as a result. XCCY basis tightened, led by the long-end.

During Tokyo trading hours, FX markets were muted ahead of the US annual CPI revision. USD/JPY hovered \~149.50, despite the further sell-off in USTs. Risksensitive currencies began to outperform from the London morning alongside continued strength in risk markets. During Tokyo hours, AUD/JPY traded sideways \~97.00 but ground higher toward 97.30.

## Europe:

NOK (+0.5%) gained against the US dollar, despite only modestly stronger-thanexpected m/m CPI data. Data above the seasonal average for January, which tends to be quite soft, supported NOK. Price action in the remainder of G10 currencies was relatively muted.

In the London morning, bearish price action in European duration continued and curves bear-flattened. The focus was on the annual US CPI revisions released early in the London afternoon. The revisions did not change the 3-month annualized pace for core CPI by December 2023 at 3.3%. Details showed upward revisions to core goods and downward revisions to core services into the end of the year. Our economists note this will not change the Fed’s perception of the softening trend in core PCE inflation. Initially, lack of an upward revision bull-steepened the US and European curves but the rally was short-lived. Reds sold off \~5bp and 10y Bund yields rose 3bp to 2.38% and curves bear-flattened. Core ASW closed mixed, Schatz led in the cheapening (-1bp), which steepened the Schatz-Bund ASW box. OATs outperformed and tightened slightly against Bunds, while the 10y BTP-Bund spread gradually widened to 158bp.

## Dollar Bloc:

The DXY USD Index was little changed at the end of London trading, after highly anticipated, but ultimately uneventful annual revisions to US CPI. NZD (+0.7%) outperformed after market participants discussed further RBNZ hikes to tackle inflation. AUD (+0.4%) gained against USD, alongside a rally in equities.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ markets were closed due to the Lunar New Year holiday. USD/THB rose \~0.2% to 35.88 after the BoT Assistant Governor said the central bank is prepared to lower rates if it is certain the economic downturn is long-lasting, rather than

cyclical. USD/INR (+0.1%) continued to trade in a narrow range, but rose slightly amid higher US bond yields and a stronger US dollar. USD/MYR fell 0.1% to 4.76 amid limited activity in equity markets. USD/SGD remained stable while the local equity markets underperformed.

Most AxJ rates remained little changed. India G-Secs were stable ahead of the RBI liquidity injection via VRR auction and eventually rose after the debt sale and 10y yield rose \~2bp to 7.10%. On the India NDOIS swap curve, the 10y yield rose 8bp. Thailand rates were little changed, just 1bp higher at 2.55%. Singapore rates also exhibited limited activity. China, South Korea, and Taiwan markets were closed.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mixed with few catalysts in the region. BRL (+0.7%) led peers ahead of the Carnival holiday (local markets will reopen at 12:00p ET on Wednesday); COP (+0.7%) and MXN (+0.4%) also ended stronger. CLP (- 0.5%) lagged peers and ended the week -2.3% softer. Rates ended broadly higher; TIIEs were the exception (5y: -2bp), in a continuation of the post-Banxico rally. The IBR curve steepened in the front-end (1s2s: +6bp), while the CAM curve had losses concentrated in the belly (5y: +6bp).

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
