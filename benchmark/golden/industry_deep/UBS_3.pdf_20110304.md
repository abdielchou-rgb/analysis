## Policy rate hike? Not next week

We do not expect a policy change at the next monetary policy meeting on 19-20 September. According to the latest Bloomberg survey of 53 BoJ watchers, all of them agree on the "no hike" call next week.

## But, the BoJ's intention looks clear

However, the message from two board members, Nakagawa and Tamura who spoke this week in separate meetings, was clear. The Bank's board members expect underlying inflation to rise further and reach a level consistent with the Bank's inflation target of 2.0% between October 2025 and March 2027, and the policy rate should be neutral by that time, if this outlook materializes. Tamura said the neutral rate is at least 1%. His view is probably the most hawkish among the board members, but we think recognition that the current policy rate at 0.25% is too low relative to the base case outlook of the economy and inflation, is the consensus among all nine board members.

We think it is reasonable to expect the next rate hike will be coming soon, which is in line with the consensus view among BoJ watchers. Indeed, 46 out of 53 (87%) expect the next rate hike to be between October and January next year. October or December (or later) depends on the market situation and political developments. We think October is still possible, but elevated market nervousness and political developments make us think that the risk is more skewed to December than before. We will update our BoJ policy outlook after seeing the press conference of Governor Ueda on 20 September.

## BoJ likely to stay on hold next week

As explained in our BoJ preview ("BoJ preview: Not next week"), we do not expect a policy change at the next policy meeting on 19-20 September. According to the latest Bloomberg survey of 53 BoJ watchers, all agree on the "no hike" call next week (Figure 1). Indeed, we see no reason for the Bank to raise the rate, which would surprise the market and the public again, especially when market sentiment is still cautious (Figure 2).

However, the message from two board members, Nakagawa (regarded as neutral) and Tamura (hawkish) who spoke this week at separate meetings, was clear. Median outlook of the board members expects underlying inflation to rise further and reach the level consistent with the Bank's inflation target (i.e., 2.0%) in the second half of the outlook period (i.e., between October 2025 and March 2027), and the policy rate should be a neutral rate by that time if that outlook materializes. To be sure, the board members totalling nine, including the Governor and two deputies, appear to have different degrees of confidence on the outlook and all members who spoke recently, except Tamura (see below), are avoiding mentioning the level of the neutral rate. Still, we see no clear dovish dissent to the basic outlook of the economy and inflation from any board members, although Nakamura and Noguchi dissented the rate hike in July.

Tamura said in the speech that the neutral rate is at least 1%. He also showed that current market pricing looks for just 0.5% to 0.6% at the end of 2025, and highlighted a risk of higher than desired inflation if the rate was hiked as current pricing shows. His view is probably the most hawkish among the board members, but we think the recognition that the current policy rate at 0.25% is too low relative to the base case outlook of the economy and inflation, is the consensus among members.

In all, we think it is reasonable to expect the next rate hike will be coming soon, which is in line with the consensus view among BoJ watchers. Indeed, 46 out of 53 (87%) expect the next rate hike between October and January (Figure 1). October or December (or later) depends on the market situation and political developments, especially on the timing of an expected snap election with the new Prime Minister. We think October is still possible, but elevated market nervousness and political developments make us think that the risk is more skewed to December than before. We will update our BoJ policy outlook after seeing the press conference of Governor Ueda on 20 September.

## Who will be the next Prime Minister?

On Thursday, the Liberal Democratic Party of Japan (LDP) officially announced its once-athree-year leadership election on 27 September. Nine candidates are running in the election. Initially, 11 or 12 names are raised as potential candidates but those who did not run could not secure 20 Diet politician supporters in the party, which is a key condition for running in the election. In this election, voting counts out of 734 with 367 votes from the Diet politicians and the same number of votes from around 1.1 million

There will be nine candidates for the LDP leadership election on 27 September

party members. If no candidate takes a majority, a run-off vote by 414 votes from the 367 politicians and 47 prefecture representatives will be held immediately.

According to the latest poll by NHK (Figure 3) , national broadcast, the top candidate is Ishiba (age 67) with 28%, followed by Koizumi (43) with 23%, and Takaichi (63) with 9%, and Kono (61) with 6% (others are less than 5%). However, the response is a bit different from LDP members; the ranking is the same but Koizumi (27%) is closer to Ishiba (29%). Since the 20 supporters would vote each candidate, it looks nearly certain that a run-off vote will be conducted. Current market consensus looks for Koizumi as the next Prime Minister, based on the latest Quick survey (equity investors), which showed 78% of 124 respondents expect him to be the winner. The Bloomberg survey of BoJ watchers also showed a 69% vote for Koizumi, followed by Ishiba with 23%.

According to the latest poll, Ishiba and Koizumi are the two top runners

On economic policy, Ishiba stated in his policy collection that "we will conduct economic and fiscal management that places the highest priority on overcoming deflation, and strive to realize a growth-oriented economy," which is similar to the current Kishida's basic policy stance. However, he has emphasized the importance of fiscal health in the past. Moreover, BoJ watchers ranked him top (39%) for the BoJ to continue policy normalization (the least favourable for policy normalization is Takaichi with 86%). These suggest the market perception on Ishiba is that he is hawkish in both fiscal and monetary policy. On the other hand, Koizumi said that he would keep Kishida's policy in principle while raising the reform of the employment system (dismissal regulation) as a key agenda. Neither candidate has mentioned monetary policy directly.

We see no significant economic policy change from these two candidates

Meanwhile, both favour going to the general election (a snap election) soon. The possible dates are 27 Oct, 3, 10, 17 and 24 November as national elections are usually held on a Sunday.

## Current account surplus: close to record high

On Thursday, MoF released the Current account for July. Seasonally adjusted data showed a ¥2.8 trillion surplus, spiked from ¥1.8 trillion in June. Excluding October 2023, it was the highest surplus since 1985. Main driver of recent strength is explained by primary income (both direct investment and portfolio investment), which more than offset deficits of goods trade (¥0.4 trillion) and services trade (¥0.3 trillion).

The balance of digital-related services posted a deficit of ¥0.6 trillion in July (it is often called "digital deficit"). The deficit has widened from ¥0.4 trillion a year ago partly due to JPY depreciation. Still, an absolute size is small, and unlikely to change the trend of overall current account balance.
