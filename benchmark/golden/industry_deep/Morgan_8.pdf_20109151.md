The main events of Wednesday’s session included global inflation data releases that mainly surprised to the downside and additional guidance from policymakers across the globe. China, Norway, and Brazil’s June inflation data were all below consensus expectations; Brazil swap rates rallied by double-digits, EUR/NOK surged 1.3%, and China short-term yields fell in response to respective weak data releases.

In terms of guidance from policymakers, overnight the RBNZ maintained its policy rate at 5.5% at its July monetary policy meeting but lessened its hawkish stance, with the announcement highlighting that the tight monetary policy may be feeding through to demand “more strongly than expected.” Fed Chair Powell continued his testimony to Congress and largely reiterated his tone from Tuesday, which many market participants interpreted as more dovish than prior remarks as he focused on the balance of risks between labor and inflation. On the other hand, remarks from BoE Chief Economist Pill leaned hawkish as he emphasized the persistence in inflation numerous times. The market-implied probability of an August BoE cut repriced to below 50% before partially retracing the move, with \~14bp of cuts currently priced for the meeting.

Treasuries traded in a fairly narrow range on Wednesday as market participants awaited Thursday’s June US CPI release. In the NY morning, attention was focused on Powell’s testimony before the House Financial Services Committee, but given that the sentiment was largely unchanged, price action in USTs remained subdued.

Powell continued to emphasize the dual-sided risks of the policy rate and, when asked, flagged that striking the proper balance between labor and inflation is the number one thing that keeps him up at night. He continued to recognize further progress on inflation while highlighting clearer risks to maximum employment. He reiterated that the Fed does not need inflation to be below 2% before cutting rates, while also suggesting that the neutral interest rate has risen. When asked about balance sheet run-off, Powell indicated that while the Fed has made “quite a lot of progress…we think we have a good ways to go."

In the NY afternoon, supply was at the forefront. Auctions continued to illustrate demand for USTs, with the \$39bn 10y reopening stopping 1bp through with a cut-off yield of 4.276%. Allotment to primary dealers fell slightly to 11.5% (P: 11.6%) as the increase in the allotment to direct participants to 20.9% (P: 13.8%) offset the drop in the allotment to indirect participants to 67.6% (P: 74.6%). While the well-digested 10y auction marginally supported longer duration, USTs ended the day relatively unchanged, with yields \~1bp lower across the curve.

Additionally, in the NY afternoon, there was the TIPS Treasury buyback operation for securities maturing in 2032-2054. Treasury purchased five CUSIPs and accepted \$53mn, but had \$728mn of offers. Breaks hit lower after the disappointing purchase size.

Usage of the Standing Repo Facility returned to near zero on Wednesday, which confirmed the \$100mn pickup on Wednesday was likely related to a small value exercise.

Equities rallied and the S&P 500 increased 1.0% to a fresh high above 5,600. While gains in the technology sector continued to lead the way, all sectors in the S&P 500 reported a d/d increase, with 80% of constituents up on the day.

According to the weekly EIA Crude Oil Inventory Report, US crude oil inventories fell by 3.44mn barrels for the week ending July 5, which was more than expected, while refineries operated at the highest utilization rates since the end of May. WTI crude oil futures rose 0.9% and closed higher for the first time in four sessions.

## Japan:

In the Tokyo morning, long-end JGB yields initially traded higher following the UST twist-steepening overnight. Media reports from Tuesday that some domestic market participants were calling for aggressive cuts in the BoJ’s JGB purchases continued to weigh on market sentiment. Meanwhile, short-end JGBs traded firm, especially in the 2y sector, and Wednesday’s BoJ-scheduled JGB buying showed that both the 1-3y and 3-5y sectors were on the stronger side. It seemed to us that a shortage of collateral inventory and foreign currency JGB ASW demand supported the short-end sectors. The momentum was largely unchanged in the Tokyo afternoon, but the selloff in the super-long sectors accelerated due to the concession efforts ahead of Thursday’s 20y auction. The JGB curve twist-steepened; 2y JGB rallied 1.0bp to 0.335%, 10y JGB sold off 1.5bp to 1.085%, and 30y JGB sold off 2.0bp to 2.225% on a simple yield basis.

TONA-OIS curve bear-steepened, but the long-end OIS outperformed JGBs. XCCY basis continued to tighten. The 20y sector’s auction is scheduled for Wednesday. We think demand to square positions should support the sector, and solid performance in the equity market could lead to rebalancing demand from real money market participants. That said, the sluggishness in the sector may not improve in the near term especially if the steepening in USTs continues.

USD/JPY continued to trade higher in the Tokyo morning. The combination of higher long-end UST yields and the rally in the equity market might have weighed on the Japanese yen. In the Tokyo afternoon, the USD/JPY traded in a narrow range, with limited catalysts. Other CCY/JPY pairs generally moved in tandem with USD/JPY, except for NZD/JPY. The RBNZ kept its policy rate at 5.5%, though its tone was less hawkish than prior meetings and implied earlier monetary easing. NZD/JPY fell from 99.0 to 98.4 in the London morning.

## Europe:

European currency performance was mixed. NOK underperformed meaningfully after June inflation data surprised to the downside relative to both consensus expectations and forecasts from the Norges Bank. EUR/NOK and USD/NOK surged 1.3% and 1.2%, respectively. GBP (+0.4%) outperformed as BoE policymarkers, including Chief Economist Pill, delivered a more hawkish message than market participants were expecting. Pill’s remarks underscored uncertainty around timing of the first rate cut, as he argued that inflation remains persistent in the UK. Market participants reduced the likelihood of a first rate cut in August, with \~14bp remaining priced in for that month's meeting.

## Dollar Bloc:

In the Dollar Bloc, the USD traded slightly weaker in the NY afternoon and DXY

USD Index fell -0.1%. AUD/NZD (+0.8%) reached its highest level since November 2022 after the RBNZ made a meaningful adjustment in its rhetoric of the July rate decision. The announcement mentioned that tight monetary policy may be “feeding through to domestic demand more strongly than expected," while the committee expressed more confidence that domestic price pressures will decline. AUD/NZD found resistance at 1.11. NZD fell -0.8% against the USD as market-implied pricing now attributes \~50% chance of a first rate cut at the RBNZ’s August meeting.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies traded mixed but in a narrow range ahead of US CPI data. USD/CNH rose 0.1% to 7.2930 amid a gradually weaker USD/CNY fixing, which was set lower at 7.1342, and soft inflation data in June. USD/CNY still hovered around \~7.2759, the top band of the allowed trading range. PHP outperformed the most in the region; USD/PHP slipped 0.2% to 58.34. USD/SGD declined 0.1% to 1.350, while the equity market advanced (STI: +0.8%) and recorded the highest level since 2019. USD/KRW rose 0.1% to 1,385 ahead of the BoK rate decision on Thursday, though the local equity market saw US\$320.8mn foreign inflows for a sixth straight day. TWD was the major underperformer in the region; USD/TWD picked up 0.1% to 32.59 amid the fourth consecutive session of foreign equity outflows, which amounted to US \$333mn on Wednesday. USD/THB remained steady at 36.38 after a five-session losing streak. INR and MYR were largely unchanged.

It was also a quiet session for rates in the AxJ region. In China, after its June CPI softened, a sign that the nation is still under deflationary pressures, the CNY NDIRS curve shifted lower by 1bp while the 2y short-term CGB yields fell 1bp to 1.63%. In Korea, 2y NDIRS rates rose 1bp to 3.19% while bond market moves remained subdued. India rates relatively outperformed; INR NDOIS curve shifted lower by 1bp, while 2y G-sec yields dropped 2bp to 6.92%. Thailand bonds also outperformed with a rally across the curve; the 2s10s ThaiGB yield curve bull-flattened 2bp. Meanwhile, the 2s5s THB NDOIS curve shifted lower by 2bp. Malaysia and Indonesia bond markets exhibited limited volatility and were relatively unchanged.

## Developed Markets

The Monetary Policy Committee “discussed the risk that [a decline in activity] may indicate that tight monetary policy is feeding through to domestic demand more strongly than expected." With regards to domestic inflation, the RBNZ still characterized it as remaining more “persistent,” but “growing excess capacity in the domestic economy provides greater certainty that [domestic inflation measures] will

sustainably decline"

• BoE Chief Economist Pill mentioned that inflation remains persistent in the UK, and emphasized the “uncomfortable strength” in services inflation

• BoE’s Catherine Mann mentioned that she wants to see a sustained slowing in services price inflation

• Fed Chair Powell's second day of testimony to the US Congress had the same tone as Tuesday's testimony. On inflation, he did not repeat that the Fed is “highly attentive to inflation risks” – language that the FOMC statements and Powell's speeches had included since the June 2022 FOMC statement. On the labor market, Powell said that the FOMC now also has to take into account its maximum-employment goals. Our economists note that Powell’s testimony is consistent with their forecast for a shift to a clearer easing bias in the July FOMC statement that sets up a September rate cut

## Emerging Markets

• CBC Governor Yang said the Taiwan central bank aims to keep inflation under 2% and noted that wage hikes have had a smaller impact on inflation. He added the authority currently has no plans for a rate cut, and reiterated that rate decisions are based not only on the situation in the property market but also on the environment at home and abroad

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
