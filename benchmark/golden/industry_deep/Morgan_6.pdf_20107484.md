## April 9

Treasuries grind richer on optimism for shelter reset; Gross reduction drives equity internals; USD lacks trend, CHF gains; CAD weakens against most G10 peers before BoC; MXN lags on soft Mexico CPI; Bostic reiterates baseline, signals flexibility; DXY at 104.11 (-0.0%); US 10y at 4.362% (-5.8bp).

• Treasuries grind richer through a poor \$58bn 3y UST auction ahead of March US CPI, optimism builds for a long-awaited eventual stepdown in shelter inflation.

• Reduction in gross exposure before CPI shapes equity market dynamics as muted index level performance (S&P 500: +0.14%) masks sharp moves in the momentum factor.

• US dollar struggles for direction, allowing CHF (+0.2%) to take the reins among safe-haven currencies.

• CAD matches US dollar weakness ahead of the April Bank of Canada meeting, where our economists expect the policy rate to remain at 5.00% (C: 5.00%).

• MXN (-0.5%) retraces from its strongest level against the US dollar since 2015, after Mexico CPI comes in lower-than-expected.

• Atlanta Fed President Bostic reiterates he expects one rate cut this year but signals openness for even later rate cuts, on economic resilience, or perhaps sooner cuts on signals of labor market pain.

## Japan:

JGBs traded relatively resiliently, particularly in the front-end of the curve despite the 5y JGB auction. The auction (new issue of JS167) was strong with a tail price of 99.96 (C: 99.94), given decent demand to square short positions as well as demand from the domestic community. Yet post-auction, JGBs sold off with strong selling pressure in the long-end of the curve ahead of the 20y JGB auction on Thursday. While there was demand to buy the dip in tenors less than 10y, long-end JGBs remained vulnerable; the 20s30s curve steepened by 2.0bp d/d. 30y JGBs sold off by 1.5bp d/d to 1.84% on a simple yield basis, while 5y JGBs ended the Tokyo session flat.

In the Tokyo evening session, a media report that suggested the BoJ may revise up its inflation outlook at the April monetary policy meeting quickly drove JGB futures 15c lower. TONA OIS sold off across the curve after further weak price action in USTs overnight on Monday and broadly underperformed JGBs, except for the longend. The XCCY basis curve twist-steepened, with tenors less than 10y better received.

Although JPY continued to underperform against most G10 currencies, USD/JPY was rangebound near the 152.00 level given concern about potential FX intervention by the Japanese Ministry of Finance. During the London morning, a media report suggested the BoJ may revise up its FY24 inflation outlook, following strong wage deals. The media report also mentioned the BoJ is likely to forecast inflation of \~2% in its first projection for the fiscal year from April 2026. This media report led to slight downward pressure in USD/JPY, which still hovered \~151.80 after the media report. Antipodean currencies outperformed amid the improved risk sentiment; AUD/JPY rose above 100.50.

## Europe:

G10 currencies closed the London trading session close to where they opened. EUR,

NOK, and SEK followed the round trip in the US dollar closely, and ended the London session flat. CHF (+0.2%) was the biggest mover in Europe as its safe-haven appeal grew once equities sold off. JPY showed the least sensitivity to the US dollar as USD/JPY (-0.1%) fell slightly during London trading hours to 151.67.

## Dollar Bloc:

The DXY USD Index sold off 0.3% from its peak during London hours alongside lower US yields but rose from its trough after an equity sell-off led to US dollar strength. There was lack of a clear catalyst for these moves. Ahead of their respective central bank meetings, CAD (-0.3%) weakened against the US dollar while NZD (+0.4%) gained.

## Asia:

AxJ currencies exhibited mixed moves ahead of March US inflation data. THB outperformed ahead of the BoT rate decision, 0.5% stronger to 36.45, alongside higher gold prices. USD/MYR fell to 4.75 after the BNM affirmed efforts from government-linked companies have strengthened MYR. The central bank also said Malaysia is not considering capital restrictions. KRW was the main underperformer in the AxJ region. USD/KRW rose 0.1% to 1335 alongside weakness in the local equity market (KOSPI: -0.46%). TWD remained steady at 32.12 while the local equity market surged 1.85% alongside US\$640.5mn in foreign inflows. CNY remained steady at 7.2340. IDR, INR, and PHP markets were all closed.

AxJ rates rallied broadly. In China, the CNY NDIRS curve shifted 1bp lower. The CGB curve bull-steepened; 2y and 5y CGB yields fell 3bp to 1.84% and 2.13%, respectively, while 10y CGB yields fell \~1bp to 2.28%. Both the Hong Kong and Singapore swap curves shifted 4bp lower. In Malaysia, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-flattened by 1bp, while 10y bond yields fell 1bp. Korea and Thailand were the outliers. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve rose 2bp, and 10y KTB yields rose 1bp to 3.50%. In Thailand, the 2s10s NDOIS curve bear-steepened 3bp; 10y swap yields rose 7bp while 3y ThaiGB yields rose 2bp to 2.19%.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mixed. BRL (+0.4%) and CLP (+0.3%) led peers, although there were few local drivers. MXN (-0.5%) weakened following lowerthan-expected CPI. This was evident in rates, where TIIEs outperformed core rates in the front-end (2y: -8bp). The rest of LatAm rates also generally rallied. DIs were 9bp lower in the front-end (January 2026s) and the IBR curve steepened (1s5s: +4bp) despite few local developments.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The ECB’s 1Q24 Bank Lending Survey showed broadly unchanged credit standards for enterprises over the past three months, while for households there was a marginal easing relative to the prior period. This is in-line with previous surveys which have shown the peak of the financial conditions tightening is in the past. Across countries, there was a visible easing in credit standards to households in France, other major countries were broadly unchanged. Banks expect credit standards will remain broadly unchanged over the next three months. Loan demand from households also remained broadly unchanged, after material declines for six quarters. Loan demand from enterprises normally follows households with one/two quarters of lags

• Riksbank Deputy Governor Floden claimed the small uptick in services inflation recently is not a major concern, as these are lagged effects of increased administrative fees or rent adjustments (as opposed to demand driven)

• Atlanta Fed President Bostic reiterated his expectation for only one rate cut this year but signaled an openness to changing his view in either direction.

He said balanced risks and the resilience of the US economy mean he “can’t take off the possibility that the rate cuts may even have to move further out.” He also noted he is open to cutting sooner, if there are signals of coming pain in the labor market

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
