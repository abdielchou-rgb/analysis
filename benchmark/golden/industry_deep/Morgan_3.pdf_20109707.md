## Global Macro Commentary | North America

## August 6

Relief rally dominates price action; hawkish hold from the RBA; JGB 10y auction has largest tail in decades; strong Philippines CPI prompts hawkish BSP comments; BRL outperforms after hawkish BCB minutes; Fed's RRP below \$300bn; DXY at 102.90 (+0.2%); US 10y at 3.892% (+10.4bp).

• Relief rally dominates price action after a tumultuous Monday session, with gains in global equities (Nikkei: 10.2%; S&P 500: 1.0%), a rally in credit, and cheapening of global duration; USTs bearsteepen as demand for safe havens eases.

• A hawkish hold from the RBA supports AUD (+0.6%); statement remains hawkish, emphasizing the lack of progress on inflation, and Governor Bullock confirms the board “seriously” considered a rate hike.

• Easing of the global risk-off sentiment supports a bear-steepening in JGBs; the sell-off accelerates after a weak 10y JGB auction, with the largest tail since 2003.

• An upside surprise in Philippines July CPI prompts hawkish comments from BSP Governor Remolona, who says that an August rate cut is now “a little bit less likely”; PHP relatively outperforms in the region, with USD/PHP falling 0.1%.

• BRL outperforms and strengthens 1.2% against the USD, and DIs bear-steepen after hawkish minutes from the July BCB meeting which show the committee “unanimously reinforced that it will not hesitate to raise the interest rate” if appropriate.

• Fed’s Reverse Repo Program falls below \$300bn for the first time since 2021 as Fed rate cuts come closer into view.

Global duration primarily cheapened as demand for safe-haven securities waned.

Absent first-tier economic data or Fedspeak, USTs sold off, led by the long end, likely driven by a bounceback in risk appetite. STIR pricing reduced the amount of Fed cuts priced in for the rest of the year, and inter-meeting cuts remained an extreme tail-risk scenario, as fears of a recession eased. The auction was met with solid demand, with a cut-off yield of 3.81%, 0.2bp through the when-issued yield. The Fed’s Reverse Repo facility fell below \$300bn for the first time since 2021 as balances dropped \$62bn on Tuesday to \$292bn. Our strategists maintain the view that the RRP would resume its path toward depletion when the opportunity cost increased for MMFs remaining at the RRP. The catalyst for depleting would be rate cuts coming closer into view.

All 11 sectors in the S&P 500 gained, which supported the 1.0% lift in the index. This largely appeared to be the result of a correction of Monday’s price action and an easing of the risk-off sentiment. Oil also rebounded, with WTI crude oil futures up 0.4%.

## Japan:

JGBs sold off substantially after Monday’s strong rally. The risk-off sentiment reversed after the stronger-than-expected US ISM services data. In the Tokyo morning, JGB yields traded higher across the curve amid eased fears of an imminent economic slowdown. On the curve, long-end JGBs sold off ahead of the 10y auction, while the sell-off in the belly of the curve was more subdued. The 10y JGB auction was weak, with a cutoff price of 101.57 (C: 101.66) and tail of 50c, the largest tail since 2003. Long-end JGBs sold off further following the auction, but the sectors retraced some of their weakness supported by technical factors that drove demand. The JGB curve bear-steepened; 2y JGB sold off 2.5bp to 0.285%, 10y JGB sold off

13.0bp to 0.885%, and 30y JGB sold off 19.0bp to 2.10% on a simple yield basis.

TONA-OIS curve also bear-steepened but outperformed JGBs especially for shortand medium-term sectors. XCCY basis tightened particularly in the short end. Given that BoJ OIS pricing does not imply another policy rate hike within the year, and the 2y OIS rate is at 0.38%, BoJ Deputy Governor Uchida’s speech on Wednesday will be in focus in terms of whether he keeps his hawkish stance even after the deterioration in global sentiment.

USD/JPY climbed above 145.0 during the Tokyo trading hours. Eased fears of an imminent US economic slowdown on the back of strong ISM Services data supported the rise. The pair had ground higher \~145.9 around Tokyo close. Other CCY/JPY had traded in tandem with USD/JPY. RBA’s on-hold decision was in line with consensus expectations, and there was limited impact in AUD/JPY. A three-party meeting among the BoJ, MoF, and FSA was held at 3pm JST, which focused on the recent equity sell-off and rapid developments in the currency market. While the message was not so meaningful in our view – as the MoF maintained its stance and said it is “important for FX to move stably reflecting fundamentals” – the conduct of the unscheduled meeting itself might have had an impact on JPY. USD/JPY fell to 144.5 in the London morning.

## Europe:

Markets saw somewhat of a relief rally but remained cautious and reversed part of Monday's price action in European FX. Global equities rallied and fixed income primarily sold off on a data-light day. The biggest reversal was found in NOK, which rallied +1.2% against the USD and +1.4% against the EUR, only marginally buoyed by oil which rallied +0.5%. EUR also reversed course and fell -0.2%, accelerated by the weak June euro area retail sales data. GBP continued the momentum from Monday and fell -0.6% on the day. CHF performance was split; it partially reversed Monday's price action against the dollar and fell -0.1%, but continued to exhibit some safehaven demand against the euro and strengthened +0.1%. SEK continued to stay strong and gained +0.4% against the USD.

The European rates market found some stabilization following Monday’s trading, and the curve faced some bear-flattening pressure as whites underperformed during the day and closed 8bp cheaper. All in all, the 10y Bund closed the day fairly stable, just 1bp cheaper to 2.20%. The cash curve underperformed compared to swaps as the whole German ASW complex tightened, led by the 2y point which tightened 2.7bp. The reversal from Monday’s trading affected EGB spreads as well; both BTP/ Bund and OAT/Bund led the move richer and tightened by 4.6bp and 3.8bp, respectively.

## Dollar Bloc:

In the dollar bloc, CAD continued to strengthen and rose +0.3% against the USD. In the antipodeans, AUD was supported by a hawkish hold from the RBA and gained +0.6%, while NZD only gained +0.3% as market participants await 2Q24 employment data. Ultimately, given the reversal in global risk sentiment, the DXY USD Index reversed most of Monday’s price action and rose +0.2% on the day.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

Most AxJ currencies weakened as an upside surprise in US ISM Services on Monday put a pause to the carry trade unwinds and supported USD. After reaching as low as 7.0838 on Monday, USD/CNH rebounded by 0.2% to \~7.1510 as Chinese equity markets relatively underperformed. USD/INR, USD/SGD, USD/TWD, and USD/THB all gained \~0.1-0.2%. After outperforming for the past few sessions, MYR turned into the regional underperformer, and depreciated for the first time since July 22; USD/MYR rallied 1.1% to 4.47. KRW was also an underperformer and fell 0.6% to 1,378 despite \$138mn of foreign equity inflows. IDR and PHP were the outperformers in the region. USD/IDR weakened 0.2% to 16,165, while USD/PHP weakened 0.1% to 57.8 amid hawkish comments from the BSP after the higher-thanexpected July CPI data.

AxJ rates sold off broadly following the Monday overnight move in USTs and recovery in risk appetite. China rates outperformed on a relative basis and yields stayed largely stable across the CGB and NDIRS curves amid media reports of the PBoC asking rural banks to control risks associated with their long-term sovereign bond holdings. India and Indonesia rates were also largely stable, with 10y bond yields up just 1bp. Hong Kong, Singapore, and Korea rates underperformed in line with their higher beta to US rates. 2y swap yields rose 15bp, 11bp, and 10bp, respectively, and the 2s10s curve bear-flattened 3bp in Hong Kong and 4bp in Singapore, while it stayed unchanged in Korea.

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• The RBA maintained its policy rate at 4.35% at its August meeting, as was broadly expected after the consensus miss to 2Q24 core CPI. However, commentary in the statement was still relatively more hawkish and emphasized the extent of time inflation has been above target, and the lack of progress over the past year. The governor confirmed that the Board "seriously" considered a hike at the meeting, and emphasized that market pricing for rate cuts this year "does not align with the Board's thinking"

## Emerging Markets

• BSP Governor Remolona said a rate cut in August is “a little bit less likely” given the “slightly worse than expected” July inflation data. He stated the authorities can cut rates if growth is surprisingly weak, and that it appears inflation will be lower. He also noted the central bank is “always open to offcycle” adjustments

• Minutes from the July BCB meeting were released. Our economists note that the tone is more hawkish when compared to the statement from the meeting, due to three main reasons. First, policymakers opened the door for rate hikes, unanimously reinforcing that they will not hesitate to raise interest rate to ensure inflation convergence to the target if appropriate (paragraph 25). Second, all board members agreed that there are more upside risks for inflation, and some emphasized the asymmetry of risks (paragraph 20). Third, policymakers discussed that if the recent movements from inflation expectations and FX prove to be more persistent, the inflationary impacts arising from it will be incorporated by the committee (paragraph 16)

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. ## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
