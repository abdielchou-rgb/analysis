# Asia Pacific Credit Update

# India HY: YTD performance recap & recommendations update

Rating Change - Credit

## YTD performance recap

ICE BofA India HY index (EINH) has posted YTD total return of 11.0% (as of 28 Dec’23), outperforming EM HY total return of 9.4% and Asia HY total return of 1.3%. On a yieldto-worst (YTW) basis, India HY index has tightened by about 140bps YTD (as of 28 Dec’23) and is currently trading at YTW of c. 9.4%.

## Sector outlook: renewables positive, commodities mixed

The renewables sector is likely to see improving credit profile supported by improving receivables in 2024. This should be more positive for the restricted group (RG) issuers while Holdco capex remaining high will limit credit profile upside on a consolidated level. For steel companies, global macro uncertainty and high premium for domestic HRC prices in India vs landed cost of imports from China remains the key challenge though we believe Indian steelmakers have the headroom to withstand any such steel price volatility. Outside steel, we think Vedanta will likely continue to be event-driven in the near term given ongoing liability management exercise. Lastly, for non-banking financial (NBFC) sector, we see limited impact on Shriram Finance despite recent regulatory tightening.

## Supply technicals expected to remain supportive

Over the course of 2023, c. USD 3.9bn of India HY corporate bonds have been redeemed (USD 3.1bn on maturity & USD 0.8bn through calls, tender offer, and amortizations) compared to USD 835mn of supply YTD 2023. Looking ahead, we estimate USD 5.7bn and 3.9bn of maturities in 2024 and 2025 respectively. Vedanta accounts for USD 2bn/1.2bn in 2024/2025 of maturities (one-third of cumulative maturity over 2024-25). Outside Vedanta, the renewables sector has about USD 2bn of bonds with USD 1.25bn backed by operating Restricted Group (RG) and USD 750mn of Holdco bond (from Adani Group). We believe good onshore funding access (especially for operating renewable structure) would keep the supply risk low. Even for the holdco renewable 2024 maturity, Adani Group has announced alternate refinancing plan. Overall, we see limited offshore supply risk from India HY Corps and pencil in USD 3-4bn of USD bond supply in 2024.

## Recommendations summary

We are constructive on India HY renewables given (1) policy tailwinds targeting growth acceleration and operational issues, (2) good alternative funding access, and (3) attractive relative valuation compared to regional peers and US/EM BB indices. We view renewables as a core sector within India HY and are Overweight Greenko and Renew Energy Group bonds. The India steel sector is driven by strong and growing domestic steel demand despite near-term pricing risk. We are Overweight JSTLIN 26s/27s and Overweight TATAIN 28s (IG rated) on attractive valuation. For Vedanta, we see merits in the recently announced LME and prefer VEDLN 25s/6.125% 24s as key beneficiaries; Underweight 26s on structural subordination and continued refinancing risk. We downgrade SHFLIN 24s/25s to Marketweight from Overweight given fair valuation.

## Sector fundamentals & recommendations

## India Renewables: policy tailwinds & improving credit profiles

Constructive sector outlook on policy tailwinds targeting growth acceleration and operational issues: India government targets of 3x increase in annual auctions of renewable capacity to 50GM during ’23 to ’28 as part of its newest measure to reach 500GW of renewable capacity and 50% of energy requirements from renewable energy by 2030. Such ambitious aim implies c. 3x increase of renewable energy capacity from 179GW as of Sep’23. Apart from the accelerating auctions, the India government also announced measures to address operational challenges including: 1) Late payment surcharge scheme (LPS) in mid-’22, which has effectively shortened the payment delays from State DISCOMs; 2) Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS), which provides financial assistance for installation of pre-paid smart meter and optimization of distribution infrastructures. Policy tailwinds have successfully improved the receivables collection over the course of FY23 and we expect the improvement to continue in FY24 considering the gradual clearance of overdue payments under LPS and improving mix of counterparty profile (increasing portion of centre agencies / industrial & commercial users with faster receivable payments).

## India Steel: near term risks balanced by strong domestic demand

Constructive sector outlook: We are overall constructive on India steel sector driven by strong and growing steel demand in India from both government initiatives and private sector investments. On the public side, India government will continue its infrastructure investments with capex push (+18% for FY24 budget); in the private sectors, steel demand will be bolstered by 5G roll-out in telecom industry, growing renewable capacity/electricity consumption in utility and coming reforms in airport,

railway, defense, etc. To cater to the robust demand, India government plans to double steel capacity/production to 300 MTPA/255 MTPA by 2030 from current levels under the National Steel Policy, which implies significant improvement of per capita steel consumption of 160kg by 2030 vs current level of less than 80kg as of Mar’23.

Near term pricing risk balanced by strong domestic demand. While current India domestic HRC price premium vs. landed cost of imports from China remains near term margin headwind, such risk is partially offset by strong domestic demand in India (Apr-Oct’23 Indian steel industry finished steel consumption is up c. 15% YoY). We believe both JSW Steel and Tata Steel have enough headroom to withstand steel price volatility and are well positioned to benefit from the upward steel consumption trend in the medium term.

Company fundamentals and recommendations: Overweight JSTLIN 26s/27s; Overweight TATAIN 28s. Both JSTLIN and TATAIN reported Sep’23 results with stronger performance in India despite weak overseas operations. For domestic profitability indicator, the EBITDA/ton metric has improved from the low-level in 2QFY23 on account of moderated raw materials prices.

JSW Steel: 2QFY24 results came with strong sales volumes in India and improving margin vs 2QFY23 on healthy steel prices and moderated raw materials prices. Credit fundamentals improved on improving EBITDA though large capex for ongoing capacity expansion led to cash burn. We are Overweight JSTLIN 26s/27s given the pick-up over India airport names while we are Marketweight JSTLIN 24s/25s given shorter dated bonds trading tight. For longer dated JSTLIN 2032s there is no good comparable with India HY though valuation looks fair compared to EM steel names with similar rating and maturity.

Tata Steel: 2QFY24 results were weaker YoY due to lower sales volume, realized prices and weak operations in UK and Netherlands. India operation continues to see EBITDA/ton improvement though Europe operation recorded losses. Credit fundamentals thus deteriorated marginally on weak EBITDA. That being said, the company’s deleveraging efforts in the past few years have led to lower leverage and better position for capacity expansion plans. Short-term uncertainty would be on the UK transition plan as the company is still in negotiation with workers union on restructuring proposals. We are Overweight on IG-rated TATAIN 28s (IG-rated) as valuation looks attractive compared to India/Global BBB-rated peers. We are Marketweight on shorter dated TATAIN 24s on fair valuation.

## Vedanta: See merits in considering recently announced LME; Overweight 25s/6.125% 24s; Underweight 26s

We see merits in considering the recently proposed LME: Vedanta Resources (VRL) has announced a liability management exercise (LME) backed by a new fund raise of USD 1.25bn. Key terms of the proposed LME include a maturity extension with structural enhancements for three bonds (13.875% 24s, 6.125% 24s and 8.95% 25s) as well as modifications in certain covenants which are applicable for all the outstanding bonds (including 9.25% 26s). Please refer to Exhibit 8 for full details on extension terms, structural enhancements and proposed key covenant modification terms.

We have mentioned in an earlier note (see Vedanta Resources PLC: Vedanta - LME recap and bond recommendations update 22 December 2023) that we see merits in considering the proposed LME to address the maturity profile beyond Jan’24s in current market conditions. Our view is based on substantial Holdco debt reduction over the last 12-18 months and reasonable free cash flow generation at the opcos despite commodity prices headwinds, though not large enough to meet upcoming Holdco maturities. We do acknowledge that the increase in the Holdco interest bill under the proposed LME plan (estimated to be more than USD 800mn from current USD 600- 650mn) is a key concern given the backdrop of reduced financial flexibility at the opcos. However, we expect the Group to gradually bring down the interest bill through continued Holdco debt reduction supported by potential asset sales. In the near term, remaining debt maturities will come down to USD 496mn in 1HFY25 post the proposed LME, which along with interest cost should be manageable considering brand fees expected in 1QFY25 and recent dividend announcement by Vedanta Limited (c. USD 320mn inflow to Holdco).

## Recommendations: Overweight 25s/6.125% 24s; Underweight 26s;

Marketweight 13.875% 24s: We have looked at various yield and NPV (net present value) scenarios assuming the LME is completed for the outstanding VEDLN curve (Exhibit 7). We see limited upside for 13.875% 24s from current levels even with yields compressing to 20-25% levels (from current 30%), and thus we are Marketweight 13.875% 24s. Proposed terms for the 6.125% 24s and 8.95% 25s are largely similar (coupon & maturity profile), although the 25s will benefit from a higher upfront payment as well as better structure post the LME (100% guarantee, springing lien on brand fees and seniority over potential asset sale proceeds). We do note the current c.10pts cash price differential between the 6.125% 24s and 8.95% 25s, although this is largely reflective of favorable upfront terms for 25s and continued structural seniority. With the proposed increased in coupon, the 8.95% 25s (Overweight) remains our preferred pick on the curve. We are also Overweight 6.125% 24s as key beneficiary of the LME. On the other hand, we remain concerned about the deep structural subordination of the 9.25% 26s, especially as Holdco cash flows remain stretched. Thus, we maintain our Underweight stance on the 9.25% 26s despite being the widest trading bond on the curve.

## Shriram Finance (SHFLIN): Limited impact from RBI’s tightening on unsecured consumer loans

RBI’s recent moves targeting unsecured consumer lending & NBFCs: India’s central bank, Reserve Bank of India (RBI), started tightening unsecured lending and NBFCs in mid-Nov. RBI has raised risk weight by 25% on (1) unsecured retail lending (to 125% from 100% for NBFCs), and (2) Scheduled Commercial Banks’ lending to NBFCs. NBFCs, in general, will be negatively impacted from (1) Capital-to-Risk-Asset ratio (CRAR) on higher risk weight of unsecured retail loans and (2) higher cost of incremental bank borrowings. NBFCs with higher exposure to unsecured consumer lending and more reliance on bank’s fundings are likely to be impacted the most. (See our equity team’s report on RBI’s regulatory moves: Financials - India: RBI’s first warning shot this cycle – NBFCs double blow; mixed implications for banks 17 November 2023).

Limited impact on SHFLIN on lower unsecured lending: we see limited impact on SHFLIN from RBI’s recent regulatory moves on tightening unsecured consumer loans on (1) SHFLIN has limited exposure to unsecured consumer credits (<6%) in the total portfolio and thus its CRAR is expected to be less impacted by the higher risk weight assigned; (2) SHFLIN has strong capital buffers and access to onshore capital markets as alternative funding channels to banks’ funding, which may help SHFLIN to withstand the potential higher costs of funding from banks.

Downgrade SHFLIN 24s/25s to Marketweight on regulatory uncertainties: since RBI’s regulatory changes in mid-Nov, SHFLIN’s 24s/25s USD bond are +0.5/+1.5pts on sanguine outlook and relatively less direct impact from RBI’s latest move. We remain comfortable on SHFLIN’s fundamentals on cross-sell tailwinds (post merger), diversified funding sources and strong access to onshore capital markets. That said, we see limited upside for SHFLIN 2024s (\$99.25/99.50, ask YTW 6.9%) and 2025s (\$96.0/96.5, ask YTW 6.6%) on YTD outperformance and relative value compared to other BB-rated India HY names. Thus, we downgrade SHFLIN 24s/25s to Marketweight from Overweight.

## ABJA Investment (TATAIN)
