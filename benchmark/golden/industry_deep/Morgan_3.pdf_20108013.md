## Greater China

• China: Politburo meeting focused on policy continuity

• April PMIs suggest solid manufacturing activity while service sectors moderate

Industrial profits fell 3.5%oya in March on moderated IP growth and intensified PPI deflation

Hong Kong SAR: 1Q24 GDP beat expectations; revising up full-year GDP growth forecast to 3.2%y/y; retail sales disappointed in March

• Taiwan: 1Q24 GDP in line with expectations; further solid gain in April manufacturing PMI

The CPC (Communist Party of China) Politburo held a conference on 30 April to assess economic conditions and lay out policy guidance. The Politburo also announced that the 3<sup>rd</sup> Plenum of the 20<sup>th</sup> CPC Party Congress will be held in July, with the theme of comprehensively deepening the reforms and promoting China<sup>’</sup>s modernization.

In assessing the current economic conditions, the Politburo meeting noted the good start in 1Q economic performance, stating that the positive momentum has been accumulating, social expectations have improved and high-quality development has progressed steadily. The Politburo also pointed out key challenges faced by the economy, e.g., insufficient domestic demand, operating pressure in the corporate sector, potential risks in key areas, and rising complexity and uncertainty in the external environment. Overall, it maintains a balanced view, but stronger-than-expected real GDP growth in 1Q is encouraging. In terms of policy guidance, the Politburo emphasized policy continuity and the need to avoid a loss of momentum in the coming quarters. New development and high-quality growth remain the main theme of policy guidance.

On fiscal policy, there is no change in the magnitude of fiscal policy support (vs. what has been laid out in the NPC meeting), yet the Politburo called for early issuance of ultra-long central government bonds (1 trillion yuan this year, most likely will start to issue in the current quarter), accelerated issuance of special government bonds (3.9 trillion yuan special local government bonds this year) and the maintenance of sufficient intensity in fiscal expenditure. This is mainly to avoid a repeat of the fiscal drag last year that contributed to a rapid loss of growth momentum from 1Q to 2Q.

On monetary policy, the Politburo called for flexible use of interest rate and RRR policy instruments to lower the funding cost to the real economy. This supports our view of further rate cuts and RRR cuts. However, the room for further rate cuts and RRR cuts is modest, and the timing and pace of

interest rate cuts may depend on the NIM pressure by banks and the repricing of the Fed rate outlook (and the impact on the CNY).

On industry policy, productivity increase (“new quality productive forces<sup>”</sup>) is the key to high-quality growth, and the priority remains emerging and advanced manufacturing. The government is excited about the success in several new economy sectors (new energy, battery and NEV), calls for steady progress in green/low-carbon development, hoping to replicate the success in other new economy sectors. However, our concern is the unequal treatment between manufacturing and service upgrade, which does not seem to be addressed at this meeting.

On boosting domestic demand, the Politburo meeting called for implementation of the action plan for equipment replacement and upgrade, as well as the trade-in program for targeted consumer goods. However, recent policy announcements do not clarify the magnitude and funding sources for these programs, and overall the policy bias to support investment (vs. consumption) seems to have remained in place. To boost domestic demand, the Politburo also called for income growth for low-middle-income families and improvement in the social safety net.

Housing is raised as a major source of risk. The Politburo meeting reiterated the importance of ensuring home completion and the continuation of city-specific housing policy adjustments, meanwhile emphasizing a new development model for the housing market and policy measures to absorb inventory stocks and improve new home supply. In our interpretation, further housing policy relaxation will take place, but not sufficient to lead to a bottoming out of housing activity in the near term. The new development model of the housing market lies in public housing and rental housing. Recent experience with trade-in programs (e.g., acquisition of unfinished projects or existing secondary homes and conversion into public housing or rental housing) is interesting, and a key question is whether any institutionalized funding support can be introduced to support public housing.

On regional developments, the Politburo meeting emphasized the integration of the Yangtze River Delta (including Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang and Anhui) as a pioneer in promoting industry upgrades, higher-level openness and coordinated growth and development.

## April PMIs suggest solid mfg activity

China<sup>’</sup>s April manufacturing PMIs held up better than expected, suggesting solid manufacturing activity. Meanwhile, the NBS service PMI fell significantly, suggesting that the 1Q recovery in service activity and domestic demand may lose

momentum going into 2Q. The NBS manufacturing PMI fell by a modest 0.4-pt, to 50.4 in April (Figure 1). Production activity seems solid, as the output component rose notably by 0.7-pt, to 52.9. Caixin manufacturing PMI beat expectations and rose 0.3-pt, to 51.4, with the output component up 0.3-pt, to 53.1. The export sector seems to support solid industrial activity. The fifth consecutive monthly increase in the export orders component of the export-oriented Caixin manufacturing PMI hints at constructive near-term outlook on export sector activity.

The NBS non-manufacturing PMI moderated in April, falling 1.8-pt, to 51.2. The service activity component fell notably by 2.1-pt, to 50.3, partly reflecting the drag from sluggish real estate services. The impact of macro policy support appears to continue to come through, as the NBS construction activity index edged up 0.1-pt, to 56.3 in April, adding to the jump of 2.7-pt in March.

Following the strong 1Q GDP report, our baseline forecasts look for some moderation in 2Q growth (4.0% q/q saar). Signals from the April PMIs reinforce our outlook for moderating 2Q growth, along with the ongoing trend of divergent sector growth patterns (solid industrial production vs. moderating service activity; solid export sector outlook vs. softer domestic sentiments and lingering housing market weakness).

## Industrial profits fell 3.5%oya in March

The NBS announced that total industrial profits (including all industrial companies with annual sales from principal business exceeding 20 million yuan) grew 4.3%oya in January-March (Figure 2), down from 10.2%oya growth in January-February. The mid-single-digit growth rate came off a low base, as industrial profits fell 21.4%oya in January-March 2023. For the month of March, the NBS estimated that industrial profits fell 3.5%oya. Meanwhile, sales revenue grew 2.3%oya in January-March 2024, and cost of sales increased by 2.5%oya. Profit margin rose to 4.86%, compared with 4.70% in January-February. By ownership type, profits of

HMT and foreign enterprises and private enterprises outperformed, growing 18.1%oya and 5.8%oya, respectively.

March industrial profits growth was dragged on by the belowexpectation IP growth (+4.5%oya, or -0.8%m/m sa by our estimate) and intensified PPI deflation (-2.8%oya). According to the NBS, the equipment manufacturing sector<sup>’</sup>s profits grew 18.0%oya in January-March, contributing 4.9%pt to overall industrial profit growth. Leading sectors included electronic equipment, transportation equipment and automobile sectors. Meanwhile, the high-tech manufacturing sector<sup>’</sup>s profits grew 29.1%oya in January-March, with telecommunication terminal equipment, integrated circuits, display equipment, and computer manufacturing the leading sectors.

## Hong Kong: 1Q24 GDP beat expectations

Hong Kong SAR<sup>’</sup>s 1Q24 GDP (advanced estimate) significantly beat expectations, rising 2.7%oya in real terms (JPM: 0.6%oya; consensus: 0.8%oya). Seasonally adjusted, 1Q GDP rose 9.5%q/q saar after an 0.8%q/q saar rise in 4Q23 (Figure 3). Real private consumption expenditure (PCE) increased 1.0%oya following the 3.5%oya growth in 4Q23. Government consumption expenditure fell 3.0%oya in 1Q24, narrowing from the 5.2%oya decline in 4Q23. Goods exports and imports continued to grow, rising 6.7%oya and 3.2%oya, respectively. Exports and imports of services grew further, increasing 8.1%oya and 17.6%oya, respectively. Gross domestic fixed capital formation (GFCF) rose by a modest 0.3%oya, compared with +17.5%oya in 4Q23. HK C&SD revised up full-year 2023 real GDP growth to 3.3%y/y (previously: 3.2%y/y). For 1Q24, we estimate that the 2.7%oya growth was contributed to mainly by net exports (+3.2%pt), while consumption contributed +0.2%pt and investment dragged by 0.7%pt.

Following the upside surprise in 1Q24 GDP data, we revise up the 2024 full-year growth forecast to 3.2%y/y (previously 2.9%y/y). We expect full-year growth to be supported by consumption and net exports, while investment remains a drag.

## Retail sales disappointed in March

Hong Kong<sup>’</sup>s retail sales volume came in below expectations in March, falling 8.6%oya (Figure 4). Seasonally adjusted, we estimate that retail sales volume fell 3.1%m/m sa, paying back the 3.6%m/m sa gain in February. In value terms, retail sales fell 7.0%oya in March on a 2.9%m/m sa loss. We pointed out last month that March retail sales might see some payback following the Lunar New Year, but the magnitude was larger than expected. For 1Q24 as a whole, retail sales volume fell 3.0%oya (or -6.5%q/q saar), while retail sales value fell 1.3%oya (or -7.2%q/q saar). The overall softening trend is consistent with the PCE data in the 1Q24 GDP report, as 1Q24 PCE growth narrowed to 1.0%oya, compared with a 3.5%oya rise in 4Q23.

Figure 4: Hong Kong retail sales and private consumption
![](images/20e7bd63b39d66fec6c122e53600e43ff998843e2fb028ed9d36f1d17b788bb3.jpg)

Retail sales volume of most retail categories contracted in both m/m sa terms and year-over-year terms in March, reflecting payback following the Lunar New Year and the imbalance between inbound tourism and outbound HK residents. Looking ahead, we believe inbound tourism will still be the driving factor of consumption growth in HK, yet the imbalance between inbound and outbound visitor recovery may continue to weigh on HK<sup>’</sup>s consumption. Meanwhile, the low-unemployment environment, household income growth, together with various promotional campaigns and activities launched by the government, should continue to benefit the retail sector. In the near term, we expect retails sales to recover modestly.

## Taiwan: 1Q24 GDP in line with expectations

Taiwan<sup>’</sup>s advanced 1Q24 GDP was in line with expectations, rising 6.51%oya (implying modest sequential growth of 1.1% q/q saar following a strong growth pace in the previous three quarters, Figure 5). Beneath the modest sequential growth in headline GDP, exports of goods and services picked up in 1Q24 (16.5% q/q saar), along with a steady domestic consumer spending trend (2.9% q/q saar). Significant expansion in the imports of goods and services (24.0% q/q saar) came as a drag on headline GDP. Following the contraction in recent quarters, domestic capex shows a moderate recovery in 1Q24. The tech sector<sup>’</sup>s inventory cycle seems to be normalizing lately following significant de-stocking since early 2023.

Figure 5: Taiwan real GDP growth
![](images/6bcc94a9329c6629b1fa02b2d573d05e469e777c4596d9bcd96def3577f597cc.jpg)

We look for a steady upward trend in Taiwan<sup>’</sup>s tech exports in the coming quarters, while the tech sector<sup>’</sup>s de-stocking pressure eases. The constructive outlook on global goods demand, supported by resilient corporate sector fundamentals and solid labor income trends, bodes well for Taiwan<sup>’</sup>s export sector activity. Tentative signs of recovery in the domestic capex trend are encouraging, reflecting Taiwan manufacturers<sup>’</sup> improving sentiment on the export sector outlook. On the domestic front, recent easing in employment growth, and hence household income, may somewhat restrain the pace of domestic demand growth going ahead.

We expect the Taiwan economy to show steady sequential growth, with 2.3% q/q saar growth in 2Q and average 2.6% q/ q saar in 2H. Our forecast for full-year 2024 growth remains unchanged, at 4.3%yoy.

## Further solid gain in April mfg PMI

The latest signals suggest further solid gains in headline manufacturing PMI in April, which rose 0.9-pt, to 50.2 (Figure 6), adding to the uptick of 0.7-pt in March and registering the highest reading since May 2022, reinforcing the industrial sector<sup>’</sup>s constructive cyclical momentum.

Figure 6: Global and Taiwan manufacturing PMIs
![](images/8eb3cff941a062e0eb0e85dbf162f490b9a932b384cea56a2da5d9b67d98213a.jpg)

The latest signals from Taiwan<sup>’</sup>s manufacturing PMI continue to be encouraging. Regarding the cyclical momentum in the industrial sector, as the headline manufacturing PMI increased a further 0.9-pt, to 50.2 in April, it has returned to the highest level since May 2022. In particular, the gain in the output component (up 2.7-pt, to 51.4 in April) hints at solid near-term momentum in industrial production activity. Regarding external demand conditions, signals from the manufacturing PMI export orders component have been rather benign, with a further gain of 0.5-pt, to 49.3 in April, the highest reading since March 2023, hinting at a constructive near-term outlook on external demand. Meanwhile, regarding the latest trend in Taiwan<sup>’</sup>s inventory cycle, the manufacturing PMI finished goods inventory indicator rose 2.2-pt, to 50.1 in April, suggesting that manufacturers may be resuming some inventory building following a significant inventory correction in recent quarters.
