# Tracking China’s Semi Localization | Asia Pacific

# Large-scale AI GPU cluster by local vendor

## Key Takeaways

China continues its own AI ecosystem development

Concerns rising on potential US further restriction of semi equipment.

Recent Big IC fund investment focus more on upstream vendors

Chinese GPU maker Moore Threads' MTLink fabric tech challenges Nvidia's NVLink, and can now scale to 10,000 GPUs for AI clusters. Moore Threads has upgraded the AI KUAE data center servers, with a single cluster connecting up to 10,000 GPUs. The KUAE data center server integrates eight MTT S4000 GPUs, designed for training and running LLMs and interconnected using MTLink network technology, similar to NVIDIA’s NVLink. (Toms Hardware, July 8, 2024)

US floats tougher trade rules to rein in China's chip industry. According to Bloomberg news, "The Biden administration, facing pushback to its chip crackdown on China, has told allies that it is considering using the most severe trade restrictions available if companies such as Tokyo Electron and ASML continue giving the country access to advanced semiconductor technology… the US is mulling whether to impose a measure called the foreign direct product rule, or FDPR, letting the country impose controls on foreign-made products that use even the tiniest amount of American technology." So far it hasn't been effective, but unless China can develop its own lithography tools to replace ASML's DUV, it is a concern for China's capability for further capacity expansion. (Bloomberg, July 17, 2024)

Big IC Fund invested in silicon material fab: Recently, China’s Big IC Fund Phase II has made frequent investments, successively acquiring stakes in the wafer manufacturing company, Chongqing Xinlian Microelectronics Co., Ltd. and the new silicon wafer enterprise, Taiyuan Jinke Silicon Material Technology Co., Ltd. The Big IC Fund Phase II also has initiated several investments over the past half year, involving companies such as IC design company, Joywell Semi, EDA tool development startup Amedac, semiconductor equipment company SMIF, ceramic material developer Genori, EDA tool company Nine Cube, IP supplier KNL, JCET Auto Electronics (Shanghai) and CMOS millimeter-wave radar SoC chip company Calterah. (Trendforce, 19 July, 2024)

Stock implications: We think further restrictions on Global equipment players should be positive to China SPE players, as they could be able to gain more market share for those key projects. We like Naura (002371.SZ), AMEC (688012.SS) and ACM Research (ACMR.O). We also like local EDA vendors like Empyrean Technology (301269.SZ), because besides equipment, EDA is also one of the bottlenecks to achieving China semiconductor localization.

## China Localization Progress

We expect China's semiconductor self-sufficiency ratio to reach 25% by 2026e, up 5ppt from 20% in 2023. China semiconductor self-sufficiency ratio only increased to 20% in 2023, which fell short of our 23% expectation. We attribute this to three reasons:

• Weak consumer and industrial demand, as Chinese semiconductor vendors' exposure to the consumer market is relatively higher, thus the weak consumer and industrial demand as well as the end-customer inventory digestion affected revenue growth in 2023.

• Limited breakthrough in logic chips using leading node foundry. China's selfsufficiency remains low in CPU and GPU. Even though Huawei's 5G smartphone is using locally produced chips, the capacity is limited.

• Chinese memory vendors face tough restrictions from the US, and they had no presence in high growth areas like high bandwidth memory (HBM) in 2023, thus despite capacity additions, these companies were affected more during the industry down cycle in 2023.

## By segment:

• We are still positive on upstream vendors like equipment suppliers and EDA. They are likely to achieve a higher self-sufficiency ratio, as these are the chokepoints in China's semi localization and local development of these industries has become a strategic imperative for China.

• We are positive on the self-sufficiency ratio of semiconductor components increasing using mature nodes, such as image sensors and power semis, given local foundry capacity expansion.

• While we are relatively cautious on logic semis using advanced nodes, like CPUs and GPUs, given US export restrictions.
