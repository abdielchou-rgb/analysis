## Hui-Heubel Liquidity ratio = (Pmax – Pmin) / Pmin / (V/OI)

where Pmax is the highest daily price over a 5-day rolling period, Pmin is the lowest daily price over the same period, V is the average daily volume for a particular futures contract over a 5-day period, and OI is the average open interest over the same period. So V/OI effectively captures turnover. The lower this liquidity ratio is, the higher the number of trades behind each percentage price change, and thus the higher the market breadth of liquidity.

Figure 8 shows this market breadth metric for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 e-mini futures contracts. It suggests that in terms of market breadth, or the price impact of volumes, liquidity conditions have largely normalized to prepandemic levels for both contracts. Figure 9 show the same metric for futures on the Eurostoxx 50 and Topix, and suggests that the broad pattern for the former is similar the US indices in Figure 8Hui andeblqty rofs S&P50 N1 excepting some deterioration since the start of the April sell-off. By contrast, for futures on the Topix, the market breadth metric had largely normalized to pre-pandemic levels by early 2023 and saw an earlier deterioration starting around the turn of the year, peaking in end-April and showing some signs of improvement since then. More broadly, the market breadth metric suggests that the price impact of volumes for US and Euro area equities appears to have largely normalised to pre-pandemic levels, while market depth above suggests that the ability to execute larger trades has not.

## Recap on crypto regulatory initiatives in the US

US regulatory initiatives intensified in recent months raising questions about the direction of US crypto regulations. There are four main initiatives related to crypto markets considered by US lawmakers at the moment.

1) Clarity for Payment Stablecoins Act is a stablecoin bill that seeks to regulate aspects such as the legal authority for issuing stablecoins, the regulatory oversight of issuers and the classification of stablecoins. If passed this bill would reinforce US compliant stablecoins but create a headwind for non-compliant stablecoins such as Tether threatening its dominance ( see our previous publication F&L, Feb 15th). As a reminder there have been similar developments around the regulation of stablecoins such as MiCA in the EU and stablecoin regulations in progress in the UK.

2) The FIT21 bill was passed by the House with bipartisan support and has moved to Senate for approval. This is an important bill for crypto markets as it proposes “decentralization test<sup>”</sup> to classify cryptocurrencies as digital securities vs. digital commodities, separating oversight between the SEC and CFTC respectively. For digital commodities, futures contract listing at CME would become more likely, thus implying a higher likelihood of spot ETF approval in the US.

According to the FIT21 bill, a digital asset would meet the criteria for “digital commodity<sup>”</sup> status if 1) it is issued through a distribution that is not used for fundraising (i.e., involves only an exchange of nominal value for the digital asset) and is open to all participants equally (i.e. a rewards program) or acquired through a digital commodity exchange and 2) it relates to a blockchain protocol that is functionally decentralized (meaning no single entity can control changes to the blockchain, nor can any individual or group hold a dominant share (over 20%) of the digital assets or voting power.

As mentioned above the bill, still requires approval by Congress and eventually presidential approval to become law, which we believe is unlikely to happen before the US presidential election.

3) The SAB 121 (Staff Accounting Bulletin No. 121) accounting rule which was set to take effect this year but faced opposition from the crypto community, banks and lawmakers. A resolution was passed by the Congress to overturn the accounting rule which would make it more difficult for banks to provide custody for crypto assets at scale. The resolution was vetoed by the president, however, on the argument that overturning the accounting rule would undermine the SEC's authority over accounting

practices.

4) The CBDC Anti-Surveillance State Act. This bill got mixed reaction from Congress, though passed the US House on a partisan vote. This bill represents an anti-central bank digital currency legislative effort. The bill prohibits Federal Reserve banks from offering certain products or services directly to an individual, from using central bank digital currency for monetary policy and for other purposes. In particular, Congressman Emmer stressed: “Whatever is ultimately developed must emulate the core tenets of cash. Simply put: any digital currency issued by the government, again, must be open, permissionless, and private. It cannot be used in the way the Chinese have deployed their digital yuan to build social credit scores on their citizens based on their purchases and their behavior. These types of tools cannot exist in a free society like ours, and we should only accept a digital currency that<sup>’</sup>s consistent with our values.<sup>”</sup>

In all, we believe the first bill has a higher chance of approval before the US presidential election relative to the other three initiatives which are likely to take longer. Overall US crypto regulations appear to be moving towards a direction that is against a Fed coin, against US banks engaging with crypto, against non-compliant stablecoins such as Tether and against a blanket classification of all tokens outside bitcoin and ethereum as securities.

## Chart A11b: Option skew monitor

Skew is the difference between the implied volatility of out-of-the-money (OTM) call options and put options. A positive skew implies more demand for calls than puts and a negative skew, higher demand for puts than calls. It can therefore be seen as an indicator of risk perception in that a highly negative skew inequities is indicative of a bearish view. The chart shows z-score of the skew, i.e. the skew minus a rolling 2- year avg skew divided by a rolling two-year standard deviation of the skew. A negative skew on iTraxx Main means investors favour buying protection, i.e. a short risk position. A positive skew for the Bund reflects a long duration view, also a short risk position.

## Chart A11c: Equity-Bond metric map

The position of the blue line on each axis shows how far the current observation is from the extremes at either end of the scale.
