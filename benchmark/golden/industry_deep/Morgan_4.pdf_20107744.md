## Companies likely to break through negative legacies under deflation and declining population, the biggest challenge

We selected companies with strong growth potential over the next five to 10 years, based on their past earnings and stock price performance as well as their relevance to six themes for the future: semiconductors and AI, automation and labor saving, digitalization (DX), decarbonization, financial normalization, and governance reforms. In addition, while most of the world is deglobalizing owing to the fragmentation of supply chains, in Japan, where the population is shrinking, companies are under pressure to be international, so as to have global reach, and implement strong management controls and governance as necessary conditions to continue growing over the long term.

Specifically, from sectors where Japan has comparative advantages, our sector analysts selected 30 promising stocks from among those that outperformed their respective sectors in terms of margins, RoE, sales growth, EPS growth, and total capital growth, based on a bottom-up approach (using stocks covered by our analysts).

The sectors selected are those where Japan has global comparative advantages in terms of market capitalization and sales (scale): manufacturing (autos and machinery), semiconductors and AI, digital (information and telecommunications), consumption/ leisure/living (retail, daily necessities, logistics, games, pharmaceuticals, and real estate), wholesale, and financial services.

Since fundamentals are influenced by sector characteristics, we selected companies with outstanding fundamentals within each sector and focused on those that are future oriented and closely related to the six themes of semiconductors and AI, automation and labor saving, digitalization (DX), decarbonization, and financial normalization. ## Themes for the next decade

## Breaking through negative legacies under deflation and declining population

In Japan, the greatest issue is the decline in the potential growth rate owing to the shrinking population. There are also the challenges of improving the broader negative legacies that built up during the years of deflation, such as companies<sup>’</sup> low capital efficiency, the rigidity of the labor market, and the poor productivity of SMEs. In the international community, there is an urgent need to address growing geopolitical risks related to US-China tensions and the rising importance of sustainability owing to climate change.

Solutions to these domestic and international social issues that have been proposed and promoted include the domestic development of supply chains for semiconductors and other strategic goods, digitalization (DX) and automation, decarbonization, internationalization and global business development, and corporate governance reforms.

## Themes for the next decade:

## Semiconductors and AI

First, semiconductors and AI, which support the digital society and have been called the oil of the 21st century, are likely to drive growth in Japan over the next 10 years. According to SEMI, an international semiconductor industry association, the global semiconductor market is expected to grow 70% from ¥88 trillion in 2022 to ¥150 trillion (\$1 trillion) by 2030 (Nikkei article). Japan<sup>’</sup>s SPE industry has a 30% global market share and has the ability to compete with the US industry for the top spot. However, Japan<sup>’</sup>s semiconductor industry had a bitter experience in the late 1980s, when it was the world leader but stopped investing when the market shrank substantially. It subsequently weakened as a result. Drawing on this experience, Japan substantially changed its industrial policy in 2021. The Japanese government budgeted ¥4 trillion for semiconductor and digital industry investments, more than that of the US as a percentage of GDP, in 2021-23 and provided support by convincing TSMC to build a plant in Kumamoto, for example (Figure 5). It also set a sales target for semiconductorrelated companies of ¥15 trillion in 2030, up threefold from ¥5 trillion in 2020 (and an increase in their global market share from 8% to 10%; Figure 6). Furthermore, the government notes the need to continue to provide substantial support to the semiconductor industry, with additional investment of more than ¥10 trillion needing to come from the public and private sectors over the next 10 years. In addition, the Japan-U.S. summit held in April has strengthened the synergy between Japan and the U.S. On the back of benefits of friend-shoring, the semiconductor and AI markets in Japan are likely to grow over the next several decades.

## Themes for the next decade:

## Digitalization (DX) and automation

With Japan<sup>’</sup>s population likely to continue to shrink, digitalization (DX) and automation are important themes that cannot be avoided.

According to 2022 data from the Japan Productivity Center, labor productivity (value added per hour) has been declining in Japan in recent years. By this measure, Japan ranks 30th out of 38 OECD member countries, the lowest since 1970. Among the factors contributing to productivity improvement (IT and digitalization, education and human resources, innovation, the environment, income distribution, and supply chains), Japan ranks lower than the US and Germany in terms of particularly IT and digitalization but also income distribution and innovation (Figure 9).

The percentage of companies using automation technologies is 21% for robots, 10% for AI, and 7% for big data as of 2023, up from 16% for robots, 3% for AI, and 3% for big data as of 2018, but the percentages are still only 10-20%. AI boosts workers<sup>’</sup> efficiency by 22% on average across all industries, according to a survey, and the impact on efficiency is particularly significant in the services sector. Furthermore, according to a survey analyzing productivity (TFP) and wage premiums at companies that use automation technologies, the use of AI and big data increases both productivity and wages by 7-9% (Figures 10-11; survey by RIETI/Hitotsubashi University).

Companies in the digitalization (DX) and automation fields can also be expected to grow over the longer term.

## Themes for the next decade:

## Decarbonization

In March 2021, Japan<sup>’</sup>s government announced a carbon neutrality strategy through 2050 and has been accelerating decarbonization efforts, investment, and innovation. In February 2024, the first GX bonds for achieving carbon neutrality were issued. A total of ¥20 trillion is expected to be issued over the next 10 years. The GX bonds are intended to attract private sector investment and lead to more than ¥150 trillion in decarbonization investment over the next 10 years by both the public and private sectors.

Areas that are to get government support from the GX bonds include R&D in such areas as hydrogen reduction steelmaking, next-generation semiconductors, industrial reactors, and next-generation nuclear power, as well as growth in production of storage batteries and power semiconductors. In the 2022-23 supplementary budget, the government allocated about ¥3 trillion for storage batteries, power semiconductors, EVs, and energysaving measures for homes and SMEs.

Over the next 10 years, decarbonization investment is to increase in the energy sector (¥43 trillion of public and private investment in hydrogen, next-generation renewable energy, nuclear power, and CCS) as well as the auto (¥34 trillion), semiconductor (¥12 trillion), and storage battery (¥7 trillion) areas (Figure 13). The heavy industry, auto, and semiconductor/electric appliance sectors are likely to benefit over the longer term.

## Themes for the next decade:

## Corporate governance reform

TSE-led reforms were the driving force behind the value-driven rally in Japan<sup>’</sup>s market in 2023. Over the past decade since the Abenomics era, the amount of share buybacks announced by companies has tripled; the ratio of outside board directors has doubled to 40%; and the number of strategic shareholdings has decreased by 20%. Since 2023, the percentage of companies with P/Bs above over 1x has also risen from 50% to 60% thanks to the TSE reforms (report).

Corporate governance reforms are still going on; they are only about a third to halfway toward the target. The percentage of publicly traded companies that disclose information on cost of capital and stock price-conscious management, as reported monthly by the TSE, is about halfway toward the target. The percentage of companies with P/Bs above 1x (the target percentage for leading companies is 80% by 2030) is about a third of the way to the target.

Figure 15 shows net cash and capital ratios for companies in Japan, the US, and Europe. In terms of both indicators, Japanese companies have more capital on their balance sheets than US and European companies do. This low level of financial leverage is suppressing capital efficiency, as noted earlier. We think Japanese companies could boost their RoEs if corporate governance reforms lead to a decline in the average shareholders’ equity ratio from about 50% to 35%, which would be on par with European companies’.

In addition, companies are likely to make significant progress on reducing strategic shareholdings within the next 10 years. Following the FSA<sup>’</sup>s issuance of business improvement orders to P&C insurers, the three major groups have announced they plan to eliminate their strategic shareholdings entirely by 2030-31. Such moves are likely to catch on among companies in other industries but could require considerable time because they involve balance sheet adjustments.

## Remaining issues: Delays in corporate and business restructuring

One reason for Japanese companies<sup>’</sup> relatively low RoEs is the slow pace of corporate restructuring and intra-company business reorganization. As a result, Japanese companies have higher weightings of unprofitable businesses than their US and European counterparts. This factor may be contributing to the former group<sup>’</sup>s low margins. Distributions of profit margins by business segment show that 90% of Japanese companies have operating margins of less than 10%, compared with only 28% of US companies and 66% of European companies. The percentage of Japanese companies with a profit margin of less than 5% is 62%, much higher than the 10-30% of US and European companies (Figure 21).

The number of divestitures per Japanese company is only 30% that for US companies and 40% that for European companies, an indication that Japanese companies are relatively slow to get rid of not-so-profitable and unprofitable businesses (Figure 22). According to the results of a survey of corporate managers and investors on what needs to be done to improve capital efficiency, corporate managers emphasize growth in scale and market share and cost reductions, while investors emphasize a selective focus on businesses and a focus on profitability and efficiency indicators. It is apparent that there is a gap between managers<sup>’</sup> and investors<sup>’</sup> perceptions of the need for business restructuring. (Figure 23).

However, recent corporate investment has become more active due to corporate governance reforms and corporate value improvements, which have been driven at the behest of the TSE, and the number of cases leading to corporate restructuring and business restructuring has increased.

## Strengths of J.P. Morgan Japan Core 30 Basket: Boldly pursuing business restructuring

A reason for the high capital efficiency is that the J.P. Morgan Japan Core 30 Basket constituents have been boldly restructuring their businesses.

In the 10 years from FY2012 to FY2022, Sony substantially changed its business portfolio, shifting its main focus from electronics (TVs, for example) to entertainment (games and music). In FY2012, electronics accounted for a majority of sales and entertainment only about 25%. By FY2022, the company had increased entertainment<sup>’</sup>s weighting in its business portfolio to more than 50% (Figure 29). This business restructuring led to a significant rise in the stock price.

Over the eight years from FY2013 to FY2021, Hitachi reorganized its business portfolio by acquiring assets necessary to globally expand the social innovation business (the acquisitions totaled about ¥3.6 trillion) and selling business assets that had little connection to this business (the sales totaled about ¥2 trillion). The total sales of acquired assets (about ¥3.1 trillion) were less than the total sales of sold assets (about ¥5 trillion). Nevertheless, market participants liked the moves, leading to the stock<sup>’</sup>s significant outperformance since FY2021.

In addition, Recruit, Shin-Etsu Chemical, Mitsubishi Corp., NTT, and other companies shown in Figures 31-34 have significantly changed their business portfolios over the past decade, leading to their stocks<sup>’</sup> outperformance over TOPIX.

## Reason for selecting the subject stock (from the perspective of an investment period of 5-10 years)

## Long-term industry outlook and positioning of the subject stock in the industry

We think the competitive advantage that major trading companies have established, and are likely to maintain over the long term, comes from business model changes in response to geopolitical risks, the pandemic, climate change, and other environmental changes. Mitsui, which has long been involved in supply of natural resources and energy, sees the trend toward decarbonization as a business opportunity, is adding value to its businesses while fulfilling its supply role, and is allocating cash generated by the resource businesses to other businesses. From a long-term perspective, we will watch the growth of the food nutrition and healthcare businesses.

## Assessment of management quality

Management aims to increase the dividend steadily, based on stable core operating cash flow. It has been strategically and flexibly allocating funds through management allocations, trying to increase core earnings using turnaround and PMI cases, and steadily gaining the confidence of market participants through information disclosures and proactive dialogue that are a step ahead of other trading companies' efforts, such as updates on the expected timing of the start of earnings contributions from new projects.
