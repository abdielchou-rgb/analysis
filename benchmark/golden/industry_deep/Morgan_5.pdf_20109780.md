## Heating up

July-August will go down as one of the more memorable macro & political vol episodes in recent history. In the course of six weeks, investors witnessed the replacement of a US presidential nominee, an assassination attempt, a +10% JPY TWI rally, a pivot to jumbo Fed cuts in September, and the single-largest intraday spike in VIX since 1990 - among others. The FX response has been pronounced though the dust has yet to fully settle; the broad contours point to low-yield short-covering, high-yielding / pro-cyclical underperformance, and a volatile but net-weaker USD (Figure 2, Figure 3 EM).

JPY has been at the epicenter of the moves. But it was a perfect storm that drove JPY to these levels, and further gains from here are likely to be more grinding. The yen rally rode a wave that featured US CPI undershoots, MoF intervention, a ‘surprise’ BoJ hike, and the weak NFP print and attendant adjustment of bond/equity correlations (Figure 4). Those gains were facilitated by the unwind of highly-concentrated shorts, and so there was a major technical component alongside the various macro drivers - in essence a perfect storm. Our positioning indicators suggest that some 65-75% of JPY-funded trades have since been unwound as a result (see here and here). So while further aftershocks (including in other markets) are certainly plausible, it’s likely that in the meantime any additional JPY gains will be more grinding,

particularly as US rates pricing has once again gotten quite aggressive (Figure 5). Consistent with this, our USD/JPY forecast change is large but one that generally maintains the contours of a fairly range-bound, modest shift lower from current levels (4Q’24 156 to 146; 2Q’25 to 154 to 144); USD/ JPY in the mid-140s is approximately in-line with what 125bps of 2H’24 Fed easing would imply (see JPY chapter).

The main FX casualty in the vol spike was FX carry, which will be hard-pressed to recover the dominant status it enjoyed throughout the last 12-18m. While this was an accelerated washout, it is broadly consistent with our long-held expectations that the prime window for carry trades had passed. Significant damage was done to the highly-profitable carry trade over the last two months - first via the MXN unwind post-elections, and more recently after the JPY rally (which had knock-on deleveraging effects that

drove other funders like CHF & CNY higher) (see our full synopsis here). YTD carry returns have since been erased (Figure 6), and our various proxies for the broader carry trade positioning point to 65-75% of those positions having now been unwound (Figure 7). Importantly, it seems unlikely that traders will revert to trading FX carry in size, for several reasons. First, as we have long expressed, carry dispersion will continue to narrow - a process that would be amplified by the Fed’s new accelerated easing cycle (JPM 50bp cuts in Sept & Nov, see here) and EM central banks also accelerating their easing process (see here). Second, that narrower carry reward will have to contend with a higher vol base, upcoming US election risk and increased evidence that the broader economy is indeed slowing (US labor market, EU PMIs etc) suggesting that the global macro backdrop is now rather less conducive to the strategy’s pro-cyclical nature. Indeed, the ‘new news’ of the last week was the more pronounced weakening of the US labor market, which (in addition to carry dispersion narrowing) adds an important macro element to what had been a positioning-driven adjustment at first. The inverse is that valuation and rates-momentum strategies have recently outperformed.

We were openminded about some windows of tactical weakness in USD coming into July, especially where positioning was wrongsided, and the only modest sell-off in the broad USD is consistent with our prior. The USD is weaker on net over the last month or so, despite some the intense volatility and equity weakness that are typically associated with USD LHS safe-haven bids (Figure 8). That belies, however, some heterogeneity in FX returns - with USD predictably more resilient vs the higher-yielding / pro-cyclical blocs (Figure 2). And so we’ve lowered our USD forecasts - particularly through USD/JPY and USD/CNY. But the USD forecast profile is not too different from prior month’s (down 1.1% vs last month’s USD forecast horizon), and we still see reasons to be optimistic on USD’s overall prospects. We had discussed in these pages last month that there was a potential pocket for USD weakness given US data moderation, some tactical richness and one-way positioning against low-yielders (JPY, CHF, EUR, CNH) - but that any pullbacks would likely be shallow. What has transpired of late seems to have met those expectations, and the broad contours of our call that USD sell-offs are more likely tactical and short-lived remain intact.

From a tactical standpoint, the USD tends to consolidate after such large rate swings. Figure 12 illustrates USD TWI behavior following \~100bp rallies in the US

2Y. The tendency is for any USD weakness through the rally to consolidate, and in some cases rebound higher. What’s priced in rate forwards is still quite advanced (Figure 5).

The US labor market is weakening but other data since have been ok. Taking into account what’s already priced in rates, the bar for more rates-led USD weakness is higher from here, especially given more balanced positioning. US payrolls were holistically soft and warranted the change in Fed forecast - but data released since has been less negative (ISM services, US claims). That has contributed to +35bp rebound in US rates that has lent some relief to the USD (eg USD/JPY +3.5% from this week’s lows).

RoW cyclical data isn’t sufficiently strong to drive USD lower. US labor market softening may be somewhat unique, but two consecutive misses in EU PMIs point to both 1) peak ‘24 momentum possibly in the rear-view, and relatedly, 2) a more synchronized slowdown. And while still low nominally, our global economists have revised up their 2024 US/global recession probability to 35% from 25% (see here). USD is anti-cyclical, and per Figure 13The upsotfr USDia jyld vm, gbw  n c -  , tends to pivot stronger when PMIs show signs of inflecting. This dilutes the dollar-negative risk that the USD may be weakening in isolation amid a strong global backdrop (i.e. bottom of USD smile). This in part informs our reluctance to raise our EUR/USD forecasts. More broadly, there are few pockets of true bullishness from our team’s bottoms-up analyses across the rest of G10.

USD-positive risks from the US election still linger. There has been a tremendous shift in momentum under now-candidate Kamala Harris, with betting markets now pricing a higher likelihood of a Harris victory and with swing state poll margins trending towards Democrats (Figure 14). But with three months to go, the risk of tariffs is still there, and while it may command less of a risk premium now vs a month ago given new polls, this risk will persist until a winner is decided in November.

August seasonality still up for grabs. August - historically a month laden with macro shocks - may have already delivered. But the month as a whole is only onethird complete, and warrants some residual USD tactical length (vs NZD).

In G10, JPY sees the sharpest upgrade across the horizon, though the downward trajectory in USD/JPY remains shallow to reflect structural pressure from negative real policy rates in Japan. ## Less noise, back to the fundamentals

Since the last KCV published on July 12, JPY has appreciated more than 7% against USD. Starting from US June CPI, a hawkish BoJ and weak US job data were the main catalysts for deleveraging across the market. Along with an equity crash, the largely accumulated JPY short positions also bore the brunt. We have noted USD/JPY divergence from the fair value was in line with accumulating JPY short positions in early July.　After the position washout, the divergence from the model largely shrunk and USD/JPY is back to its fair value (Figure 2). Given the stretched positions have washed out, main driver for USD/JPY is likely to get back to the US-Japan policy rate expectation.

Following the latest US job data, our US economics team has changed its policy rate forecast, where it now thinks the FOMC will cut by 50bp at both the September and November meetings, followed by 25bp in December. On the other hand, our Japan economics team has held its forecast, expecting another 25bp hike in December, and bringing the policy rate to 50bp by the end of 2024. Although we were mainly using FF future so far in forecasting USD/JPY because Japanese policy rate expectation was static, now the Japanese policy rate finally starts to get moving with more dynamic market pricing for future BoJ policy rates. Thus, we think it is reasonable to take Japanese policy rate expectation into account from here along with the US in forecasting USD/JPY.

Based on the correlation between the expected US-Japan policy rate differential at the end of the year, implied by OIS and USD/JPY, the USD/JPY level consistent with our economists forecast (Fed 125bp rate cut + BOJ 25bp rate hike) is 145yen (Figure 3). Considering our forecasts change and market pricing, we lower our USD/JPY forecast across the horizon to 2024/4Q at 146 and 2025/2Q at 144.

In the face of heightened uncertainty in the market, some might be wary of the risks stemming from potential unwinding of Japanese investors’ foreign securities investments. In short, we think potential risk from unwinding of Japanese investors’ foreign securities investment is not large.

As the world’s largest creditor country, Japan holds a huge amount of foreign assets as a stock base. Note that, as of end-2023, Japan held JPY289 trillion of foreign equity/investment funds and JPY329 trillion of foreign bonds. In recent years, however, accumulation of foreign assets by Japanese investors has not been so large and some sectors have rather net sold foreign securities. Figures 4 and 5 show investor type breakdown of cumulative flows into foreign securities since January 2022. Of investor type, Bank, which finances foreign bond investments by repos, and Pension Fund, whose main strategy is rebalancing and seen as typical contrarian, are immune from the story that JPY appreciation would force the unwinding of their foreign investment positions. Thus, we think that investor types that are relatively vulnerable to such kind of risks are Lifer and Retail Investors (most part of the investment trust flow should be from retail).

Japanese retail investors have been net buyers of both foreign stocks and bonds in recent years (foreign stocks have been mainly through investment trusts ignited by new NISA, report). However, as retail’s risk tolerance is relatively high, we do not expect large unwinding of their investment positions even if they meet expanded unrealised losses. The net outflow amount might decrease if the equity crash and risk-off sentiment persist, the net outflow should continue (report).

As for Japanese lifers, they have been net sellers of foreign bonds mainly due to unwinding of FX-hedged foreign bonds amid elevated hedge costs. Meanwhile, they still keep FX-unhedged foreign bonds in their portfolio (as a result, the FX-hedge ratio has sharply declined, see report). Therefore, the potential risk of another round of unwinding of their foreign bond investment positions remains. However, if our expectation that USD/JPY downside is rather limited is correct, we

think it is unlikely that such a risk will materialise.

However, beyond the unwinding of security investments, it is important to consider the potential risk posed by corporate flows, which could drive the JPY higher. If USD/ JPY continues to trade below 145—the average exchange rate for major Japanese corporations—we may witness an acceleration in USD/JPY selling as Japanese exporters increase their hedging activities.

## EUR: Bearish cyclicals outweigh lower yields

Additional evidence has been building around the stalling regional growth momentum that we discussed in our last KCV publication. EUR/USD historically requires the Eurozone to be displaying some amount of cyclical strength in order to sustainably rally. The July PMI data disappointed expectations both at the regional and global level. It now appears as though the Eurozone composite PMI formed a top in May, which speaks to the seasonality in growth seen over recent years where a strong Q1 has proceeded a softer patch for the rest of the year. The Eurozone being among the more open G10 economies leaves it more exposed to global growth via trade and earnings channels, and so recent disappointments from China PMI and credit data are concerning for EUR. For the currency to attract supportive capital flows the manufacturing and industrial side of the economy is important given the overweight that Eurozone equities have towards those sectors. The breakdown in the internals of the global manufacturing PMI (Figure 36) is therefore a concern for us, and raises left-hand-side dollar smile risks (along with our economists raising their US recession probability to 35% from 25%).

Cyclical deterioration must be balanced against our US economists’ call for more aggressive Fed easing which has driven rate spreads in favour of EUR/USD. Fair value, however, remains around 1.09 still using real rate spreads and periphery spreads (Figure 37). Wider periphery spreads have provided an offset to the move in real rate spreads, which speaks to the growth vs rates trade-off for EUR/USD. We keep our forecast profile unchanged as we see the cyclical risks outweighing the force from yields and pressuring EUR/USD towards 1.05. Our US economists’ Fed call is now modestly more aggressive than market pricing, with the forecast looking for 125bps of easing by year end. At the same time, our Eurozone economist hasn’t changed his forecast since the revision by our US team. This raises the question of

whether we should adjust our EUR/USD forecast higher to reflect that differential. Our Eurozone economist expects \~20bps more ECB easing than is priced in by forwards to December 2025, while our US economist expects \~35bps more Fed easing than is priced in over the same period. We see the risks around this as quite balanced. Though its not our base case, the Fed could push back on current market pricing. This week the Atlanta Fed GDP nowcast printed 2.9%, which still represents solid growth. Fed speakers since last week's payrolls report have been much more measured than pricing would imply, even if they haven’t represented the core of the FOMC. Shifting to growth, both our relative FRI and relative EASI models (Figure 38) fFigure 38or EUR/USD suggest fair value is lower, in addition to regression-implied fair value using the global PMI orders inventories spread, being 1.08. There are clearly offsetting forces between growth and rates here for EUR/USD. Previous work in our April KCV showed that historical EUR/USD performance is mixed and less extreme when relative growth is bearish and rate spreads are bullish for the pair, with it selling off 54% of the time but rallying 0.4% on average since 2002.

## Solid internals but vulnerable to deleveraging

GBP has suffered versus USD and EUR since our last KCV publication as a broad carry trade deleveraging has taken place. We held a neutral bias on GBP in our last KCV publication as attractive entry levels hadn’t yet complemented the positive fundamentals playing out for the currency. Sterling continued to benefit from high carry (vs other G10), improving UK growth and a less dovish than hoped Bank of England. Since mid-July those fundamentals have taken a back seat to a washout in G10 carry trade positioning and deterioration in risk sentiment across asset classes, in terms of GBP drivers. From here, despite still improving UK growth and a BoE that showed a cautious easing stance at the August meeting, it will be hard for GBP to distance itself from how the broader cross asset and carry deleveraging unfolds (or reverses). Should the carry trade continue to unwind, then GBP is potentially one of the most vulnerable, given its extended positioning and limited drawdown seen thus far. We continue to hold a neutral stance towards Sterling and we raise the EUR/GBP profile slightly to reflect broader carry trade uncertainty and risk asset volatility.

In August the Bank of England cut rates as expected by surveyed consensus, but with only 14bps priced coming in to the meeting there was some initial kneejerk GBP weakness on the decision, though it was a much smaller reaction as the minutes showed hawkish pushback in the form of Bailey cautioning against cutting too quickly and that those voting for a cut saw it as finely balanced. Our rates strategists’ view was that, even on a cut outcome, 2024/25 pricing would be relatively sticky, and the information from the minutes backed that up. Language around the continued need for restrictive policy was tweaked but still present in the statement. The statement softened reference to the three measures of inflation persistence though, which suggests more open ended guidance. Growth forecasts were lifted mechanically in the front end, and inflation at the forecast horizon kept

below target to signal the easing bias, as expected. The outcome overall wasn’t too far from what our UK economist expected, with the 5-4 vote coming in as well. Its an outcome which the market was likely quite comfortable with. Its hard to get carried away about a deep BoE easing cycle at this stage. During the press conference sterling rallied back. The general tone of cautious easing meant the market said this isn’t the trigger for the derailing of GBP carry flows. Bailey remained generic and void of specific detail in terms of the pace and size of easing, as expected. It felt harder to maintain the patience of waiting for a GBP dip to buy with the MPC sounding in no rush to ease aggressively. Bailey said services inflation has been “a bit higher” than forecast, and that the MPC can tentatively say some of the volatile components look more stable. Not very dovish overall.

## Bullish risks materialised

In last month’s Key Currency Views, we highlighted upside risks to the franc given Eurozone growth data that appeared to be running out of steam amid stretched CHF shorts that were starting to screen less attractive. After European PMIs missed expectations for the second time in a row, we entered short EUR/CHF, noting that there was still likely a base of CHF shorts that can be eroded (screened the most short in G10 on CFTC positioning), while European macro developments and election risks warranted renewed CHF longs beyond the risk reduction as well. Even if most CHF shorts have likely been unwound, growth weakness and tariff risks are reasons for investors to add CHF longs beyond the carry unwind, in our view, though we are cognizant of less attractive levels that may stoke SNB concern. We upgrade our CHF forecasts to reflect weaker regional and global growth, as well as narrowing rate spreads.

Carry trade gains have been dented severely over the past month, with the franc a key beneficiary of this. We noted last month that, if lower US yields materialise, this would weigh on both the USD-CHF and EUR-CHF rate spreads, which the FX pairs have been closely correlated to since 4Q’23 (Figure 4), and thus support CHF both via rate spreads and decreased attractiveness of G10 carry. If the falling US yields continue to reflect risk-off dynamics, this provides an additional tailwind to the franc. Euro area yields have been stickier than their US counterparts, and the EUR-CHF spread has hence declined less than the USD-CHF one, but there is potentially room for this to catch down if growth deteriorates or Euro area inflation declines.

Price action and our positioning metrics would suggest that the short positioning unwind tailwind may be close to complete. Even so, weaker European (and potentially US/global) growth amid the US election drawing closer are reasons for investors to add CHF longs beyond the carry unwind, in our view, though we are cognizant of the unattractive levels that may stoke SNB concern. Renewed carry flows can continue to be a drag on CHF if markets stabilise given that the current yield dispersion is still high, though this is becoming less compelling amid the US rates rethink. Valuations per se are not supportive, but also not prohibitive of further CHF upside – EUR/CHF is undershooting short-term fair value on both our rates (rate spreads and peripheral spreads;

1.3%) and growth (euro area PMIs/ FRIs; 2.5%) models, but USD/CHF is no longer undershooting after the retracement.

We are increasingly mindful that the SNB is likely to feel discomfort with the rapid currency rally. Two questions emerge: (1) should they be concerned and (2) what would they do? On the first, the CB’s comfort zone for EUR/CHF so far has appeared to be 0.95-0.99, given Jordan’s contrasting rhetoric about franc-driven risks to inflation at each end of the range in May and June. The SNB continues to decline commenting on franc valuations (in contrast to the pre-2021 period), but the current REER deviation would place the franc into the ‘(significantly) overvalued’ bucket (Figure 49). In y/y appreciation terms, the situation is more benign, however, and inflation is also higher. Given that EUR/CHF spent most of 2H23 below 0.96, the pair around 0.91-0.92 is likely not concerningly disinflationary via y/y base effects in 2H24 (this would mean 2-6% appreciation vs 6.6% in early 2024) especially while Euro area inflation remains stickier, though it can be more so in 2025 onwards. Reaction-wise, unlike previous rate cuts this year, a September ease is already more than fully priced into rate markets, so shouldn’t move the needle on FX if realised. This leaves SNB verbal jawboning, an inter-meeting cut or FX intervention as potential tools.

Verbal jawboning: in May, comments by SNB governor that any upside risks to inflation would emerge from currency depreciation provoked a rally in the franc and any comments this time around are likely to be material at least over the short run. So far, the SNB has been called on by business lobbyists to act against franc appreciation, reflecting exporters’ discontent with the strong currency, though the SNB’s actions would have be motivated by downside risks to inflation from the currency.

Inter-meeting cut: we have discussed the high bar for such a cut previously. Previous such cuts happened in the context of global risk-off (2002 and 2008), excessive safe haven appreciation (Eurozone debt crisis & US sovereign downgrade in 2001) and alongside the removal of the floor in 2011. An inter-meeting cut would be more likely if the US/global growth picture were to rapidly deteriorate alongside the kind of riskoff behaviour we witnessed over the past fortnight, as that would mirror the 2002 and 2008 surprise cuts

(Figure 50Previous nt-mg cw dlh xfap CHF  b k ).

FX intervention: Given rates market pricing, FX intervention is the tool with the lowest bar of use, though we have been noting the SNB’s lack of enthusiasm for balance sheet expansion given concerns around the inherent riskiness of a balance sheet made up almost entirely of foreign assets. The lack of FX purchases in 1Q, despite CHF strength is perhaps symptomatic of this. To be sure, we would categorise this as a soft constraint only as speakers have repeatedly denied that they are targeting shrinking the balance sheet for the sake of it.

## Carry unwind less bearish for NOK

The Norwegian Krone has depreciated versus the USD, EUR and SEK since our last KCV. Markets have been shaken by a carry trade unwind since the first half of July. While the use of NOK as a funder limits the damage to the Krone during risk-off episodes, the shift in growth dynamics and drawdown in risk assets has still pushed NOK weaker overall during the last month. We viewed the valuation dislocation on EUR/NOK as stretched enough to add to shorts tactically last week, but we exited after the significant shift in risk sentiment following the US payrolls report, while maintaining some downside exposure via options. NOK is running around 3% weaker than Norges Bank forecasts, and the central bank is likely to be hawkish at next week’s policy meeting; as we have seen before, the board places high emphasis on the currency. While a hawkish Norges Bank would offer some tactical support for NOK, risks are more balanced now as support from carry underperformance comes up against the moderation in global/regional growth, souring risk sentiment and US labour market risks. We drag our EUR/ NOK forecast profile slightly higher as a mark-to-market, but still maintain a downward slope given valuations, the hawkish Norges Bank, domestic growth (outperforming the Eurozone) and support from carry trade underperformance.

Norges Bank meets next Thursday for what will be an interesting meeting given the weaker NOK. Coming in to the meeting the central bank faces a similar predicament to prior periods this year, with inflation surprising to the downside but the Krone weaker than forecast. Back when Norges Bank delivered a surprise hike in December 2023, NOK was running around 5% weaker than forecast. Today its not as weak as that, but should still prevent a dovish pivot. The central bank will most likely stick to guidance for a firm hold in policy set out at the June meeting where the statement read “if the economy evolves as currently envisaged, the policy rate will continue to lie at 4.5 percent to the end of the year, before gradually being reduced”. Downside inflation surprises probably mean a lower theoretical rate path, but its unlikely that the central bank will want to send a dovish message. The bar for a hike is high, if NOK is, for example, only 3% weaker than forecast around the time of the meeting, then its probably off the table. There is room for more hawkish guidance or language around the currency, and given that EUR/NOK printed new post pandemic highs in August, this is not a possibility we want to play down. There are therefore hawkish risks for NOK into the meeting. We enter short NZD/NOK cash today to try to trade those risks. The pair trades rich to 6.43 fair value, offers high beta RV protection from equity volatility and exposure to yield convergence and carry trade underperformance.

## Bearish after BoC, but rates & positioning still headwinds

We lower our USD/CAD forecasts by 1c across the horizon on the back of recent US developments, but our assessment of local conditions keeps us bearish CAD. The disappointing July US payrolls report marked a pivot point for markets, prompting a major rethink in Fed easing expectations. Our US economists have added an additional 75bp of Fed cuts in ’24 (125bp total) and now expect a terminal rate of 3%, while our US rates strategists have lowered their year-end 2yr Treasury yield forecast by 110bp to 3.20%. In sympathy with these forecast revisions and the broader post-payrolls price action (Figure 65), we now see USD/CAD slightly lower at 1.35 in 4Q and 1.33 in 2Q’25. We still see only a shallow downward trajectory in USD/CAD, however, given that the US economy arguably remains on a better overall footing than CA and the USD would likely see a bid in a more adverse downturn scenario, not unlike recently. Our forecasts have for some time embedded a 1-2c risk premium to reflect such USD-positive risk scenarios (not to mention US election risks), and that risk premium generally remains intact in our projections.

The CA OIS strip was well-priced to begin with, but the BoC’s decisively-dovish delivery this month should suppress any attempts by CAD to rally on its own domestic macro merits. The BoC delivered its second consecutive 25bp cut in July, which was not too surprising given that markets were fully priced as such. But the extent of BoC dovishness did prove surprising: 1) the Bank expressed “increasing” confidence that inflation will return to target even despite recent upside surprises (the core 3m ar has doubled to 2.9%); 2) the BoC, like the Fed, changed its assessment of risks, noting “downside risks are becoming more important to policy decisions"; and 3) Gov. Macklem maintained that the Fed-BoC policy rate gap was “still not close” to its potential limit. Consequently, our CA economists updated their BoC call (before the change in Fed call) to 25bp cuts at every remaining meeting through 1Q’25, to a terminal rate of

3%. We had argued previously that risk-reward to CAD shorts was diluted in part because of the extent of CA rates pricing and some questions about inflation persistence. But the degree of BoC dovishness, including its willingness to look through the recent jump in core inflation, given growth risks like the u-rate move (Figure 66) limits the potential for a bullish CAD u-turn. Said differently, any G10-CAD rate spread narrowing would likely be a function of the G10 leg. Indeed, the parallel shifts in reaction function/risk assessments between the Fed and BoC have us wondering whether 50bp eases from the Fed might require some risk premium of the same from the BoC in markets.

## AUD: Maintaining an upward bias

The unwind in the short JPY/global carry trade over the course of the past few weeks has dragged the Antipodean exchange rates lower. AUD/USD weakened in line with the first principal component (PC1) of 3m returns in G10 FX vs the dollar (Figure 72). Historically, PC1 is dominated by the loading on USDJPY, suggesting the Antipodeans’ slide has, at least in part, been an externality of the positioning washout in the Yen. The move lower in AUD has rapidly unwound richness vs JPY, and now screens cheap when fitted to US GDP FRI, rates and commodity prices (Figure 73). We are modestly lowering our near term AUD/USD forecasts to reflect recent market volatility and a modest rise in US/ global recession risk. But we maintain our bullish bias given the long-held view that the RBA will be a late cutter (discussed below). We expect AUD/USD to reach 0.67 by 4Q24 (prior forecast: 0.68), with slight appreciation still forecast in 1H25 (forecast: 0.68).

The domestic data has also played some part in recent softening with a slightly lower than forecast 2Q inflation print pricing out expectations for a final RBA hike. Headline inflation was commensurate with markets expectations at 1%q/q, while trimmed mean increased less than anticipated (actual: 0.8%q/q, consensus: 1%q/q). Importantly these outcomes were broadly aligned with the RBA’s standing forecasts and therefore lowered event risk heading into the the August policy meeting. While the miss on trimmed mean was broadly consistent with the standard consensus forecast error, it was enough, given global developments, to shift the bias in OIS pricing to the downside, a stark reversal from just a few weeks ago.

## NZD: Range ratcheting up on AUDNZD

Our relatively flat forecast profile similarly reflects the fact that the range for the pair should be disciplined by prospects for Fed easing on one side, but also an RBNZ which is likely to be very responsive to falling US rates. ## MXN has weakened through the unwind of the Latam carry trade but position looks much cleaner now

A collapse in risk appetite led to a crisis-level spike in FX volatility that hit MXN hard, but calls for patience as we wait for the dust to settle. The latest bout of volatility caught MXN at the tail end of several weeks of losses that started with the surprising results of the Presidential election and continued with the precipitation of the unwind of Latam carry, the latter driven by the appreciation of carry-funding currencies like JPY.

July weakness in MXN was mostly attributed to the continuation of carry unwinds, but positioning looks cleaner now. MXN has depreciated 14.0% vs. JPY since July 10, stoked by BoJ intervention and the BoJ’s hawkish meeting on 31 July. Recent analysis of CFTC data by our global FX Strategy team, however, suggests around 60% of JPY shorts/MXN longs opened over the past two years have been unwound since late May (see here). Moreover, some dovish signaling has come from BoJ members, with Deputy Governor Uchida saying the Bank should keep from hiking further, while financial markets are unstable, bringing some relief to MXN.

Valuations are now more appealing. MXN is screening cheap, at 2.9 standard deviations from fair value in our 3Y model. Meanwhile, the real effective exchange rate, which had risen since mid-2020 to nearly 20% above its 20Y average, has tumbled to around 3% above its 20Y average. With valuations correcting so materially, current levels could offer appealing entry points for fresh longs. However, patience is warranted as markets assess the true state of the US economy and the potential path forward.

Banxico cut the policy rate by 25bp to 10.75%, focusing on core disinflation. The vote was split, with three Board members voting for the cut and two to stay on hold. Banxico indicated that the inflation trajectory provides room to ease, and emphasized the deceleration of economic activity and the ongoing core disinflation trend (core printed at 4.05% oya in July, consistently falling from a high of 8.45% in Jan-2023). The Bank also pointed out that the recent rise in headline (which is at 5.57% oya in July, up from 4.40% in Feb-24) could be attributed to volatile items, and that this volatility is expected to fade. However, Banxico also noted that the balance of risks to inflation remains to the upside, and highlighted the depreciation of MXN as a risk factor.

Looking ahead, a more dovish Fed will likely open the door to a lower terminal rate for Banxico. Our economists have recently increased the scale and speed with which they expect Banxico to cut rates, on the back of our house call for an accelerated Fed easing cycle. They now expect Mexico’s rate at 10.25% by end-24 and at 7.5% by end-25 (from 10.5% for end-24 and 9.0% for end-25 previously); this entails 325bp of cuts until end-25 for Banxico, compared to 250bp of cuts for the Fed over the same period. Risks to the view are sustained MXN weakness and its potential effect on inflation.

Stay OW MXN in the GBI-EM model portfolio. We also keep our MXN hedge via -3M/+6M digital calendar spread (sell 22-Aug USD/MXN 18.50 digital call to finance long 20-Nov 18.50 digital call (spot ref. 16.63)).

## BRL: The technical setup looks favourable for BRL but conviction is low; stay MW in the Model Portfolio

BRL has suffered along with Latam high-yielders as domestic noise compounds souring global sentiment and a broad carry unwind. BRL is amongst the worst performing EM FX this year, having depreciated 12.1% vs. the USD YTD. The latest leg of BRL weakness followed a collapse in risk appetite driven by heightened fears of a recession in the US, which led to a spike in volatility amidst an ongoing process of carry unwind.

Domestic sentiment remains depressed despite some government efforts to reinstate confidence in the fiscal targets. Investors are waiting for the 2025 budget proposal (released by August 31). In July, the government announced plans to cut BRL 25.9 bn in public spending from the 2025 budget, and then announced BRL 15bn of spending freezes for this year while confirming commitment with the fiscal targets. The next goalposts for the administration will be presenting a 2025 budget consistent with the fiscal framework and primary target by 31 August, and delivering more detail on how spending freezes and cuts will be implemented.

The BCB kept the policy rate unchanged at 10.50% in its July meeting, maintaining a cautious stance but without validating the hikes embedded in the curve. The Copom projections still point to inflation almost converging to the target range by the end of the policy horizon, in a scenario with a stable Selic. Risks to the base scenario, however, are the de-anchoring inflation expectations (median Focus survey forecasts for the end-2024 IPCA are now at 4.12%, having risen steadily from 3.7% in early May) and the material weakening of the FX. Even so, our economist thinks the BCB will keep rates on hold through the rest of the year, and that the next move will still be a cut in mid-2025, which is in sharp contrast with markets pricing \~ 100bps of hikes this year.

The BCB has tools to intervene in the FX market, but we think it is unlikely to do so if the underlying source of concern (i.e. fiscal) is not addressed amid challenging global sentiment (see note). With BRL performance becoming less of an outlier across the EM space, we think that chances for BCB intervention have diminished over the last leg of weakness. For now, it seems more likely the BCB will keep a hawkish message as more information becomes available on the path of the public finances locally and the health of global growth.

BRL positioning is cleaner, but conviction for a sustainable recovery in the near term is low. From a positioning perspective, BRL already looks less vulnerable than it did earlier in the summer. Our JPM BRL positioning index has been around neutral since June, and is screening slightly short at the moment. In our EM Client Survey, investors have cut BRL longs since May, bringing positioning down to the lowest level in over a year. This leaves BRL in a much less vulnerable place, although purely from a technical point of view.

We are MW BRL in the model portfolio, and hold 1x2 USDBRL put spreads. While sentiment around Brazilian assets is still depressed, we think there is reasonable asymmetry for BRL put spreads at these levels.

## Lower USD/CNY target on faster Fed rate cut

Core rates have done a great favour to the PBoC as the collapse of US yields amid rising recession fears triggered further unwind of bearish CNY trades. The positioning shakeout has been so drastic that PBoC’s prolonged headache around outsized fixing-spot gap has suddenly fully dissipated. While the pace of yuan appreciation is getting closer to recent year extremes, our valuation metric has yet to flag alarming signs of stretch. Of note, the downdraft in USD/CNH in recent sessions has been a pure reflection of relative rate momentums: USD/CNH’s fair value based off US-CN yield gap now stands at 7.16 vs 7.30 a month ago. The broadly fair valuation marks an interesting contrast vs the meltdown in USD/CNH in late 2022 and 2023 as well when USD/ CNH reached \~3% and \~1% below rate fundamentals, respectively. The absence of a meaningful undershoot in USD/CNH vs rate differentials this time suggests the latest decline in spot looks more of a passive appreciation fueled by unwind of dollar longs instead of active positioning for fundamentals-driven CNY strength.

Short squeeze on speculative CNH short looks likely done but mindful of further unwind of dollar longs from corporates. We argued previously that the yuan is different from the yen as bearish positioning especially from speculative accounts is much lighter to start with. Judging by the fact that the spot-fix gap has broadly closed while USD/CNH has largely re-converged with USD/CNY, the short squeeze on speculative CNH shorts is probably done for now but further positioning adjustment from real money investors and corporates is likely still far from complete. Chinese corporates in particular, head into the latest CNY appreciation rather underhedged. They have been signing less short USD forwards and their overall hedge ratio has fallen to recent year lows . Additionally, corporates have been net long dollars. Based on different assumptions on the conversion ratio, our estimate indicates that Chinese corporates could have saved up to \$300bn-600bn dollars in the past two years that are now due for conversion. Against this backdrop, panic USD selling cannot be ruled out and even if a small share ends up being converted, it could be market moving over the short run.

Move MW CNY FX in GBI-EM and lower 1y ahead USD/CNY target to 7.05 (from 7.20 previously). With the latest weakness in the US labor market, our economists are now expecting an accelerated easing path for the Fed and our US rate forecasts have also been revised aggressively lower accordingly: we now expect 10y UST to move closer to 3% by mid-2025. Sharper falls in US yields would mechanically lower USD/CNH’s fair value in a rate differential model. While the path (lower) is directionally unchanged vs before, the delta is really a much lower ending point which moves the needle on the risk calculations: the fair value range for USD/CNH is now lowered to 6.95-7.15 from 7.18-7.25 earlier for the next 12 months, and risks around USD/CNH are thus less one-sided (weaker) compared to before. Against this backdrop, after closing out our outright CNH shorts earlier, we have also moved MW CNY FX in GBI-EM and lower our year-end USD/CNY target to 7.15 (vs 7.3 previously) and 1y ahead target to 7.05 (vs 7.2 previously).

Geopolitical risks remain an important swing factor for CNY FX. This is why we have set our USD/CNY forecasts broadly above the rate-based fair value, a small and likely incomplete risk premium for potential tariff risks. With geopolitical headwinds lingering, the path for USD/CNY could also be less linearly sloping in reality than what is indicated by the rate momentum. As a result, we have lower conviction on the far end of the forecast and reckon that risks remain two-sided for USD/CNH despite the trend lower in the US rate forecast.
