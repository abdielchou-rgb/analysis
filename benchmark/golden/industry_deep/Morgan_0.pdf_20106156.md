# Tactical Thoughts into Earnings

We highlight our thoughts on the various clean energy subsectors within our coverage as we head into earnings.

## Key Takeaways

Delays in renewable energy development (both resi and ute-scale) could drive downside to consensus estimates in 2024.

Elevated interest rates, macro uncertainty, and a lagging NEM 3.0 transition may serve as a growth overhang for residential solar.

Red Sea disruptions remains a key "wild card" for margins in 2024.

■ We recommend owning companies with a high degree of earnings visibility and/or strong balance sheets with limited equity capital needs (FSLR, AMPS).

Below we highlight noteworthy earnings dynamics and catalysts to watch throughout our coverage.

Residential Solar Installers: We expect mixed messaging across the residential solar installer group through 4Q23 earnings. On the positive side, we expect installers to highlight falling solar panel, inverter, and battery prices as a growth accelerator/margin expanding opportunity for 2024. However, ongoing NEM 3.0 challenges may serve as a growth impediment for installers with significant exposure to California (RUN and SPWR).

• NOVA (=): We expect NOVA to reiterate the 2024 customer growth, earnings, and funding guidance that it provided during 3Q23 earnings, which should lead to a relatively in-line quarter for the company. Ongoing pricing increases should lead to an expanding IRR-WACC spread.

• RUN (=/-): We expect RUN to hit the midpoint of its MW growth guidance in 2023 and stabilize NSV in 4Q23. Moving to 2024, we expect RUN's MW growth to slow to -12% (vs. +3.75% in 2023), driven by challenges in CA. We could see modest upside to our estimates if RUN continues to take share as the industry switches to a TPO model but we are taking a more conservative view into earnings given the longer-than-expected demand recovery under NEM 3.0. We also expect the company to initiate battery storage growth for 2024 following its strategic pivot towards a "storage-first" company during 3Q23 earnings. Lastly, we expect a modestly improving NSV through 2024 as the company pivots to a storage-heavy model and as it partially benefits from falling component prices.

• SPWR (-): We see risk to the downside to estimates in 2023 given the going concern warnings, which has likely impacted its ability to operate under "normal" conditions with business partners. Its recent restructuring plan could allow for platform investment savings, but we remain more cautious on the company's ability to meaningfully expand its margins in 1H24. Upcoming credit waiver deadline and likely equity raise remain an overhang.

Commercial and Community Solar: We expect AMPS to report 4Q results in line with its 2023 adj. EBITDA guidance range of \$97-103m (cons: \$100m / MS: \$100m). A positive update on its in-house project development plans (75 MW in 2023, 150 MW in 2024) is an important catalyst for the stock. We would expect a strong outlook for 2024, reflective of a healthy acquisition market for buyers with secure funding, falling equipment prices, and rising utility rates, coupled with various strategic financing agreements and acquisitions in 2023, which should drive adj. EBITDA growth in 2024.

Solar Inverters: Conviction on earnings estimates remains low given lack of demand/inventory visibility, especially in Europe. Nonetheless, we expect 1Q24 revenue guidance for both SEDG and ENPH to imply a modest (\~4%) Q/Q decline given normal seasonality and ongoing de-stocking. For SEDG, margins should trough in 4Q23, with improvement seen sequentially throughout 2024 as it (i) reduces its warranty accrual (should be largely complete by 3Q24), (ii) lowers underutilization expenses through CM's (also should be largely complete by 3Q24) and (iii) lowers service margin drag as volumes improve in 2H24. Additionally, its recently announced global workforce reduction could drive additional downside to opex estimates, but we await further details from the company. For ENPH, we expect slightly less earnings volatility, driven by a more stable margin outlook, but we remain somewhat concerned about their customers' positioning in the US market (i.e. long-tail). We also expect an update on its contract negotiation with SPWR, which is set to expire at the end of 1Q24, but we estimate the current contribution to earnings from this contract is fairly immaterial.

Utility-Scale Solar: Within the utility-scale solar sector we expect headwinds related to permitting, financing, and supply availability (panels and transformers) to remain a key theme, despite strong underlying economics that support strong growth prospects. Furthermore, we expect challenges will be cited across the developer landscape (mostly small developers) that didn't properly hedge financing or capital costs, which could result in further project delays. Company-specific thoughts across utility-scale solar:

• FSLR (+): We expect FSLR to report strong 4Q23 results, meetings its 2023 earnings targets. Its sold-out capacity position at contracted pricing, coupled with hedged raw material and freight costs, should lead to a fairly de-risked earnings profile for 2024. While the company is certainly exposed to the broader industry dynamics (i.e. permitting/interconnection delays, financing challenges, etc.) it also over-allocates capacity (by \~20%), providing a cushion against some project delays giving us increased comfort that we will not see a material surprise to the downside on volume/revenue in 2024. Lastly, while bookings may slow, we expect relatively stable pricing on incremental contract signing's in 4Q23.

• ARRY (-): We believe the company will likely deliver against its 2023 revenue and earnings guidance but see 12% downside to consensus revenue estimates in 2024 unless book-to-bill comes in at 1.9, which is well above recent trends (0.2-1.2 in the first three quarters of 2023). Customer progress on PPA renegotiations and industry pricing will remain key topical questions that we expect the company to address through earnings. On the positive

side, the company may include 45X credits in guidance, providing clarity around the expected margin/cash flow benefit it expects to accrue.

• SHLS (=): We do not expect the company to upwardly revise its warranty liability estimate range this quarter, which will likely be viewed positively, but we also do not expect a significant update on its litigation with Prysmian (covered by Max Yates). As such, we expect the warranty issue to remain an overhang for the stock, although we frankly feel fairly comfortable with the company's ability to cover the liability with its current liquidity and ongoing FCF, as well as the arguments made within its lawsuit against Prysmian for future recovery. As it relates to earnings, we expect results to come in line with guidance in 4Q23, but enter 2024 with a more conservative estimate for revenue growth than consensus given the recent industry challenges. As it stands, we currently estimate SHLS will generate \$534m of revenue in 2024, which is in line with its 2024 backlog as of 9/30/23. We'd admit, however, that SHLS's book-to-bill has consistently remained above 1, so we could see upside to our estimates if that trend continues and if all incremental bookings from 4Q23 are recognized in revenue in 2024 (this does remain somewhat uncertain given the elongated project development timelines).

• MAXN (=/-): We remain somewhat cautious on MAXN given the global oversupply of panels in the large-scale market (mostly outside of the US), which could challenge the profitability profile of its business in light of continued weakness in the DG market. While we view the announcement on the start of its commercial solar partnership as a net-positive, we expect that focus will likely remain on MAXN's ability to re-allocate SPWR's DG volumes through its various sales channels. As a result, we see a negative risk-reward for MAXN ahead of its 2024 guidance.

Energy Storage: We expect resilient growth within the energy storage market in 2024 given the strong underlying economics and vast oversupply (thus rapidly falling costs) of energy storage hardware. That said, falling battery prices could pressure ASPs and revenue estimates for storage integrators in 2024. Companyspecific thoughts across energy storage:

• FLNC (+/=): We are expecting a relatively in line quarter for FLNC: Revenue - cons: \$406m / MS: \$408m, Gross margin - cons: 8.4% / MS: 7.8%, and adj. EBITDA - cons: -24m / MS: -35m). For the call we expect four areas of focus to dominate the conversation including: (i) the implications of the counterclaim suit filed by Diablo Energy Storage on FLNC's past project with LS Power, (ii) pricing dynamics on incremental bookings as battery prices continue to fall, (iii) the path forward for AES's stake in FLNC, and (iv) working capital needs and potential sources of funding (if needed).

• STEM (=/-): We expect the company will fall short of its annual software and services growth rate given an elongated conversion cycle from contracted to operating software assets under management. That said, we do expect the company to hit its positive adj. EBITDA target in 2023, which is an important milestone for the company. Growth in software is a key investment thesis for STEM, and as a result, we'd expect this to be a core

focus when it provides 2024 revenue and margin guidance.

Hydrogen and Fuel Cells: Key topics will likely include (i) implications of the draft clean hydrogen guidelines under the IRA, (ii) impact of rising renewable electricity costs and capex inflation on green hydrogen economics, (iii) role of fuel cells in serving growing power needs from AI and data center growth. Company-specific thoughts:

• BE (+/=): We are modestly lower than consensus on revenue (cons: \$473m / MS: \$448m) and adj. EBITDA (cons: \$59m / MS: \$39m). We see potential revenue growth deceleration in 2024 and downside to consensus estimates (cons: \$1.8bn / MS: \$1.6bn) due to significant revenue benefit from its final consolidated repowering in 2023. That said, we see strong fundamental tailwinds to support strong growth in 2024 (i.e. Series 10 and the opportunity for it to take share with data center customers), but await clarity on order backlog before increasing our 2024 revenue growth estimates. We also expect the company will continue to improve margins (+380 bps Y/Y in 2024) as it continues to reduce product costs and remains disciplined on pricing. Furthermore, its recent contract extension with SK includes quarterly take or pay commitments, which should improve earnings visibility and de-risk the guidance it provides for 2024.

• PLUG (-): At its business update call on 1/23 (see our takeaways here) PLUG highlighted that it expects 4Q23 revenue to be "just over" \$200m (cons: \$387m / MS: \$237m), which was lower than the company's prior expectations (\~\$530m), partially driven by ongoing hydrogen fuel shortages. Furthermore, the lower volumes resulted in higher fixed cost absorption, which when combined with new product investments, resulted in "lower than anticipated" gross margins for 4Q23. We see downside to estimates in 2024 following recent commentary from the company highlighting slowing volume growth. Lastly, we expect the company to provide additional details on how it expects to achieve a 70% Y/Y reduction in its cash-burn in 2024.

Wind: For TPIC (=), we expect focus will likely remain on TPIC's path to profitability and its strategy to continue preserving liquidity following the recent refinancing agreement with Oaktree, which we view as a net positive since TPIC can elect to delay cash interest payments on its converted senior secure term loan by another two years. We also anticipate an update on its warranty campaign and expect close scrutiny on any additional warranty charges taken by the company. Lastly, based on recent commentary from industry participants (including TPIC), we do not expect to see revenue growth in 2024 as the wind market continues through its "reinvestment cycle" and gears up for a growth inflection in 2025.
