## Developed Markets

## United States:

Thursday price action was choppy across the globe in a partial reversal of Wednesday’s risk-off sentiment and global steepenening. Overnight UST yields rallied, and the long end gave up some of Wednesday’s cheapening. During the London session, European duration also richened amid weaker-than-expected business survey data in Germany and France, which offered a bleak outlook for 3Q24 European growth.

Sentiment began to turn alongside the release of US 2Q24 GDP data, which exhibited surprising strength. Real GDP accelerated to 2.8% q/q (C: 2.0%; P: 1.4%), with private domestic demand showing no signs of slowing from the prior quarter. The strength was driven by stronger consumption and business investment. Additionally, the core PCE quarterly data rose 2.9% q/q (C: 2.7%), with the upside surprise adding risk to the July PCE data, which will be released on Friday. USTs sold off following the GDP data, led by 2y yields.

The curve began to flatten NY midday, seemingly driven by a reversal of the steepening from Wednesday. The front end continued to sell off given the robust data, while the long end traded choppily but managed to maintain its overnight gains. Even with the upside surprise in GDP, STIR pricing was less volatile, and market-implied probability for a September rate cut remained over 100% as the tail scenario of a 50bp cut continued to be modestly priced in.

USTs sold off into the \$44bn 7y auction which, in contrast to this week’s 5y auction, was digested smoothly with a cut-off yield of 4.162%, 0.4bp through the whenissued yield. Underlying details showed that the allotment to primary dealers of 8.9% (P: 11.9%) was the lowest in over a year. This was driven by strength in indirect participants whose allotment grew to 74.4% (P: 69.7%), which was more than 1 standard deviation above the one-year average, offsetting the fall in allotment to direct participants to 16.8% (P: 18.5%).

The auction results supported the flattening of the curve, with demand for the belly and long end continuing to persist as the front end was weighed down by economic data. Ultimately, 2y yields reversed the overnight gains and ended the day flat, while 5y yields fell 4bp and 30y yields fell 6bp

Equities initially benefited from the reversal of the risk-off sentiment and alongside data that showed the US consumer remained resilient. The S&P 500 saw moderate gains in the NY morning. However, losses in the technology industry caused a reversal of the trend and resulted in another daily loss for the S&P 500, which fell - 0.5%.

## Japan:

JGB yields initially traded higher in the Tokyo morning. An article published overnight suggested the BoJ is likely to discuss whether to raise its policy rate at the July meeting, which weighed on short-end JGBs. Concession effort ahead of Friday’s 2y auction may have also played a role in the sell-off. On the other hand, heading

into the Tokyo afternoon, long-end JGBs began to rally back. Given their attractive yields, coupled with the heightened risk-off sentiment under the equity market selloff and the light supply schedule in the near term, market participants may be interested in buying the super-long sectors. The momentum continued in the Tokyo afternoon, and the 40y sector outperformed the rest of the curve. The JGB curve twist-flattened; 2y JGB sold off by 2.5bp to 0.39%, while 10y JGB rallied by 0.5bp to 1.065% and 40y JGB rallied by 4.0bp to 2.42% on a simple yield basis. The TONA-OIS curve also twist flattened but underperformed JGBs. XCCY basis continued to tighten. For the 2y JGB auction, we expect the result to be sluggish, while demand to square positions should partly support demand. Risk of further news around the July BoJ meeting could weigh on the sector.

USD/JPY continued to decline in the Tokyo morning to \~152.2 from \~153.8. A combination of the broad risk-off move and concern around a possible BoJ hike at the upcoming July meeting seemingly led to further unwinds of JPY carry trades. In the Tokyo afternoon, the downward trend moderated, and USD/JPY slightly rose to \~152.8 toward Tokyo close, but it started to decline again in the London morning. Meanwhile, the risk-sensitive currencies such as AUD, SEK, and NZD underperformed on a broad basis, and AUD/JPY dropped below 100.00 toward Tokyo afternoon.

## Europe:

The London morning began with price action similar to Wednesday, with risk trading heavy and consequently risk currencies lower and safe havens higher. However, the stronger-than-expected US GDP data managed to somewhat turn the tide, though not equally across the G10 complex. EUR climbed +0.2%, as the turn in sentiment reversed some of the USD strength seen previously that had weighed on EUR. Some of the biggest intraday turns were found in Scandinavia, with NOK up 0.4% and SEK down 0.1% despite their intraday sell-off that reached close to 1%. The holdouts that did not turn with sentiment were GBP, which fell -0.2%, and more significantly, CHF, which gained +0.6% despite being a safe-haven currency that typically sells off when risk picks up.

## Dollar Bloc:

There was choppy price action on Wednesday in the dollar bloc. Stronger-thanexpected US economic growth data resulted in a change from the early morning risk-off sentiment. CAD ended the day flat, despite an early morning sell-off. The antipodeans largely did not change much with the turn in sentiment; AUD and NZD both fell -0.4%. Ultimately, the DXY USD Index declined -0.1%.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies were a mixed bag amid an extended rally in JPY and a softer USD (DXY USD Index: -0.1%). CNY and offshore CNH both gained given the renewed correlation with the yen and unwinds of carry trades. USD/CNY slipped 0.6% to 7.218, with a slightly stronger fixing by the PBoC. USD/CNH fell 0.6% to 7.223. KRW underperformed in the Tokyo morning as Korea growth data came in below consensus. USD/KRW was up by 0.1% at 1381.8, and equities declined in tandem with the sell-off in the US technology sector (KOSPI: -1.8%). IDR underperformed as some market participants no longer favored the rupiah as an EM carry trade pick. USD/IDR rose 0.2% to 1,6250. USD/MYR weakened for a second consecutive session, down by 0.2%. SGD remained stable while INR traded in a narrow range around \~83.68. TWD and PHP markets remained closed due to Typhoon Gaemi

AxJ rates mainly rallied on Thursday. In China, 10y and 30y CGB yields rallied \~2bp as the PBoC cut the 1-year MLF rate by 20bp. Short-end 2y yields were also down 1bp. On the NDIRS curve, the 2s10s bull-flattened 2bp. The short end of India G-secs gained while the long end rallied in a tight range. 2y and 5y yields were down 4bp and 3bp, respectively. 10y IGB yields rallied 1bp, and the 10y premium over UST rose 2bp. In contrast to FX, Korea bonds performed well, with yields down across the KTB curve; 10y yields were down 1bp. In Indonesia, 10y yields remained steady; the short end of the INDOGB curve rallied \~2bp. Singapore bonds outperformed with 10y yields down 6bp to 2.97%. In Hong Kong, the 2s5s curve bull-steepened 5bp. In Thailand, long-end rates rallied, with 10y yields down 2bp while 2y yields sold off 2.4bp. The Philippines bond market remained closed.

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
