# European Economic Comment Eurozone: Inflation down in February, but less than expected

## Headline and core down -0.2pp to 2.6% and 3.1%, less than expected

February flash Eurozone inflation fell by 0.2pp to 2.6% y/y (2.58), slightly above expectations (consensus and UBSe at 2.5% y/y). A bigger surprise came from core inflation which also fell in February, although the 0.2pp decline to 3.1% y/y was significantly less than expected (consensus: 2.9% y/y; UBSe: 2.8%). Within core inflation, both goods and services inflation were stronger than we had expected with goods inflation down 0.4pp to 1.6% y/y (UBSe: 1.5% y/y) and services inflation easing only 0.1pp to 3.9% y/y (UBSe: 3.6% y/y). The main driver behind the decline in headline was the 1.6pp fall in food inflation to 4% y/y (UBSe: 4.2% y/y). In contrast, energy inflation rose by 2.4pp to -3.7% y/y amid higher fuel prices across the region. Sequentially, the run-rate of services inflation increased for the second consecutive month to 0.5% m/m swda (from +0.4%), while the rate of goods inflation edged down to 0.1% m/m swda (from 0.2%). The annualised rates of seasonally adjusted goods and services inflation showed a tick up with services inflation staying north of 2% with goods inflation remaining below 2% (Figures 8 and 9). Looking ahead, we are currently track unchanged headline inflation in March (2.6% y/y) with further increase in energy inflation expected to be offset by lower core (-0.1pp to 3% y/y) and food inflation. Given the upside surprise in February and an upward revision in our March energy inflation forecast on the back of the electricity VAT rate increase in Spain, we now expect headline inflation to average 2.4% this year (+0.1pp vs previously) and 2.1% in 2025.

## Country detail: headline inflation down across the board

In Germany (out on 29 February), headline inflation declined 0.4pp to 2.7% y/y, in line with consensus (UBSe: 2.8% y/y). The decline was largely driven by lower food inflation with energy inflation edging up. Based on the limited details released with the flash estimate, we estimate that German core inflation was broadly stable (at 3.4% y/y). In France (out on 29 February), inflation was also in line with consensus, declining 0.3pp to 3.1% y/y (UBSe 3% y/y). Compared to our expectations, the upside surprise appears to have come from core inflation (services in particular), while food inflation was weaker and energy was broadly in line with expectations. In Spain, February inflation came in slightly above expectations, falling by 0.6pp to 2.9% y/y (consensus: 2.8%; UBSe: 2.7%) with the statistics office noting a fall in electricity prices as the main driver of the decline in headline. Inflation in Italy (out today) was unchanged at 0.9% y/y (consensus: 1% y/y, UBSe: 0.9% y/y). Inflation in the Netherlands (out today) fell by 0.4pp to 2.7% y/y (consensus: 2.8% y/y; UBSe: 2.9% y/y).

## ECB: Will 7 March bring clarity on the "April vs. June" issue?

In the upcoming ECB meeting on 7 March, the focus is likely to be on signals as to whether an April cut is still possible, and in fact likely, or whether the first cut should be expected only in the following meeting on 6 June. Two items are likely to stand out. First, the ECB's updated macro projections. Second, Ms Lagarde's press conference which might provide a better idea of the dominant view in the GC. While today's inflation print marks further progress in terms of disinflation, it probably plays to the favour of those GC members who are advocating a more cautious approach in terms of the timing of the first rate cut. We expect the ECB to deliver the first rate cut on 11 April (-25bps to 3.75%), but we see a clear risk that the first cut will come in June - and even more so after today's inflation data. At the time of writing, the markets are pricing c.5.6bps of cuts by April, a cumulative 24bps by June and 92bps by end-2024. See more details in ECB: What to expect on 7 March.
