# February 22

AI optimism lifts SPX to record high; USTs twist flatten, curves end at session lows; Fed's Jefferson cautions on consumer spending; European rates twist flatten, ECB Accounts show cuts still "premature"; KRW gains, BoK on hold; MXN weakens post-CPI; DXY at 103.94 (-0.1%); US 10y at 4.321% (+0.2bp).

February PMIs and durable US labor market data provided little obstacle for markets to continue to push out implied central bank policy easing. Currency moves across the G10 were muted but aligned with the prevailing macro sentiment. Gains were led by the risk-sensitive SEK and juxtaposed against safe haven JPY underperformance. NOK weakness ran counter to a rise in Brent crude oil (+0.8%), but the percentage of households who expected inflation to be higher over the next 12 months in Norges Bank’s 1Q24 Expectations Survey fell. Both investment grade and high yield corporate credit CDX spreads tightened to new lows since 1Q22, prior to when the Fed commenced its tightening cycle.

Given close proximity to the US equity open, details from the preliminary February S&P Global US PMIs likely slipped under the radar of market participants. Although the Composite PMI dipped to 51.4 (C: 51.8; P: 52.0), driven by softer Services to 51.3 (C: 52.3; P: 52.5), Manufacturing activity expanded at its fastest pace since September 2022 at 51.5 (C: 50.7; P: 50.7).

Factory output increased at the fastest pace since April 2023. Goods producers attributed the expansion to stronger client demand and a sharper uptick in new orders. The rise in new orders was the steepest since May 2022; some businesses attributed this to clients having worked through their excess inventories. Amidst stronger demand conditions, goods producers recorded the fastest uptick in employment since September 2023.

In prepared remarks, Fed Vice Chair Jefferson outlined that the weakness in household balance sheets toward the end of 2023 led him to expect slower growth in spending and output in 2024. However, he cautioned continued strength in spending is an “important upside risk” to his forecast. Consumer spending that is more resilient than he currently expects could cause progress on inflation to fall.

He remains “cautiously optimistic” about their progress on inflation. He characterized January inflation data as “disappointing” but believes core services price increases will continue to moderate as the labor market cools. Yet, he remains “attentive to other possibilities.”

He reiterated the view shared by the Committee: “...if the economy evolves broadly as expected, it will likely be appropriate to begin dialing back policy restraint at some point this year.” He highlighted the “careful easing” in the July 1995 easing cycle allowed the FOMC to assess incoming data and other information to ensure inflation was under control. He pointed out in that cycle, the FOMC left rates unchanged for three meetings as it awaited more information, and then continued to ease.

## Japan:

Weak price action in USTs overnight on Wednesday initially led to broad weakness in JGBs on Thursday, but JGBs recovered ahead of the liquidity tap auction for the 5- 15.5y sector at noon in Tokyo hours. The strong auction led to further demand to square short positions and the belly of the curve outperformed. Long-end JGBs lagged, possibly due to squaring of flattener positions before the long weekend in Japan. But long-end JGBs gradually caught up into the 3:00pm Tokyo close, supported by strong demand to buy the dip. 20y JGBs particularly outperformed in the duration rally, while short- to medium-term JGBs lost their initial strength. 20y JGBs rallied by 2.0bp d/d to 1.45%, while 2y JGBs sold off by 0.5bp d/d to 0.155%. TONA OIS rallied and broadly outperformed JGBs, except for the 20y tenor. XCCY basis tightened, led by the long end of the curve.

As strong semiconductor earnings buoyed global risk sentiment, JPY broadly sold off on Thursday after USD weakness overnight on Wednesday. A Japanese Ministry of Finance official reiterated they will closely monitor FX movements with a high sense of urgency, but impact on the FX market was limited. While CCY/JPY pairs traded rangebound during Tokyo trading hours, they broadly rallied from the London morning alongside the strong risk appetite. Yet, European currencies quickly lost upside momentum after mixed European PMIs; EUR/JPY broke above 163.00 and approached 163.50 but quickly fell back near 163.00.

## Europe:

Most G10 currencies, including the US dollar ended the London session little changed. EUR closed flat after below-consensus German PMIs reversed prior grains. Performance of the Scandinavian currencies diverged, with NOK (-0.2%) weakness after households reported lower inflation expectations. Supported by a rally in equities, SEK (+0.3%) gained against the US dollar. Slightly stronger-than-expected UK PMIs provided for modest GBP (+0.2%) strength. Both JPY and CHF were 0.2% weaker by London close.

European curves flattened aggressively on Thursday. An initial bear-flattening, spurred by strong French PMIs, turned into a twist flattening due to strength in the long end. The 2y sector remained under pressure, alongside USTs, despite a 1bp rally in 10y Bunds to 2.44%. Reds led the sell-off (\~10bp in OIS) as the amount of ECB rate cuts priced in 2024 fell to 94bp, with the implied terminal rate \~2.40%. EUR 2s10s (against 6m) flattened 6bp to -48bp, a new low since late September, while 10s20s flattened by 3bp to -28bp, new local low. Strong performance in risk assets (Euro Stoxx 50: +1.7%) supported peripheral spreads and the 10y BTP-Bund spread tightened 3bp, to 147bp. The entire core ASW complex richened (Schatz ASW: +1bp).

## Dollar Bloc:

The DXY USD Index ended the London session flat after initial weakness of 0.6% in early trading. AUD pared its initial equity-driven gains alongside weakness in commodities by London close, but NZD (+0.2%) gained ahead of retail sales data. CAD (+0.1%) gained after mixed December retail sales data.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies strengthened broadly alongside gains in most local equity markets. KRW advanced 0.5%, to 1329, after BoK kept its benchmark rate unchanged at 3.50% (C: 3.50%). MYR gained 0.5%, to 4.77, and partially retraced losses from the prior week. PHP remained strong and USD/PHP fell to 55.72, although the local equity market closed flat. IDR rallied 0.3%, to 15,590, after BI reiterated it will continue to support the currency. THB appreciated 0.2%, to 35.79, alongside a moderate rise in gold prices for two consecutive weeks. TWD remained at 31.51,

although the local equity market took in US\$522mn of foreign inflows. CNY remained subdued, around the 200-day moving average (7.1883).

AxJ rates had mixed moves. Korea and China outperformed. In Korea, the 2s10s NDIRS curve bull-steepened by 2bp, while the KTB yield curve shifted 5bp lower. In China, the NDIRS curve shifted 2bp lower, while the 2s10s CGB yield curve bullsteepened by 4bp. In India, the 2s10s NDOIS curve shifted 2bp higher while 10y Gsec yields also rose by 2bp. In Hong Kong, the swap curve shifted 4bp higher. In Singapore, 10y bond yields rose 2bp, to 3.10%, while 2y yields remained unchanged.

## Latin America:

LatAm currencies ended the session mostly weaker. CLP (-0.9%) led losses, while BRL (-0.5%) followed despite few local developments. MXN (-0.4%) weakened after lower-than-expected Mexico Headline CPI at -0.10% for 1H February/ 2H January (C: 0.13%; P: 0.32%). This data, along with minutes from the February Banxico meeting, which generally reinforced prospects of the start of the easing cycle in March, helped TIIEs outperform core rates. Initially, 2y TIIEs rallied by \~8bp but gave back most of the gains. The CAM curve sold off with losses concentrated in the belly (5y: +6bp), while the IBR curve twist-steepened (1s10s: -9bp). DIs underperformed peers with few local drivers (January 2027s: +10bp).

## Central Bank Monitor

## Developed Markets

• Accounts from the January ECB meeting noted downside risks to growth and inflation, but a discussion on rate cuts was deemed premature. Data dependence rather than a calendar-based approach was reinforced, in-line with the press conference messaging

• Fed Vice Chair Jefferson said it is likely appropriate to start cutting rates later this year. He noted he is cautiously optimistic about the inflation progress, but cautioned on easing too much on improving inflation

## Emerging Markets

• BoK kept its key rate unchanged at 3.50% (C: 3.50%). BoK Governor Rhee said a rate cut is unlikely in 1H24 as the committee is not confident inflation will trend as per projections due to not only supply-side risks, including oil prices, but also high inflation for living necessities. The decision for rate policy over 2H24 will depend on the economic data released in May

• BI Senior Deputy Governor Destry said the central bank purchased IDR25.1 trillion worth of government bonds in the secondary market this year. Bank Indonesia has pledged to continue its triple intervention in the spot, domestic NDF, and bond markets to steady the rupiah. In the primary market, Bank Indonesia purchased IDR9.1 trillion of government Sukuk

• CBT maintained its one-week repo rate at 45.00% (C: 45.00%) and the Committee assessed the “current level of the policy rate will be maintained until there is a significant and sustained decline in the underlying trend of monthly inflation and until inflation expectations converge to the projected forecast range” 1

• BanRep Board Governor Villar acknowledged growth was slowing in Colombia, but also stressed the importance of getting inflation under control, especially when inflation is still high on an outright level and expectations remain above target. Markets have priced in -29bp for the March meeting

• Minutes from the February Banxico meeting continued to signal an openness from the board about the start of the cutting cycle in upcoming meetings. While some members noted risks to inflation are to the upside, many members noted real rates remain restrictive and likely some nominal easing is needed. After release of the minutes, markets priced in -20bp for the March meeting

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
