# Global Macro Commentary | Global

# April 25

1Q24 US Core PCE upside surprise pushes out Fed rate cut pricing, bear-flattens the Tsy curve; European rates also bearflatten; USD/JPY trades tentatively before BoJ, Tokyo CPI; USD weakness surprises; ECB's Nagel stresses rate path uncertainty; DXY at 105.57 (-0.3%); US 10y at 4.704% (+6.2bp).

• An upside surprise in the US Core Price Index at 3.7% q/q in 1Q24 (C: 3.4%) resets expectations, assuming no January or February revisions, March Core PCE inflation may have risen 0.48% m/m.

• Treasuries bear-flatten as STIR markets push pricing of the first full 25bp rate cut out until December 2024 and price less than 100bp of total cuts by December 2025; 2y UST yields close at 5.00%.

• European duration bear-flattens in sympathy with the move in the US; Bonos underperform amidst political uncertainty.

• USD/JPY (+0.2%) trades tentatively above 155 ahead of the April BoJ meeting and release of Tokyo CPI; a media report suggests the BoJ is set to consider a reduction in its JGB purchase sizes.

• Despite strong inflation data, higher US real yields, and equity weakness, the US dollar pares initial gains and the DXY USD Index (- 0.3%) settles lower.

• ECB Governing Council Member Nagel stresses a June cut "would not necessarily be followed by a series of rate cuts...given the current uncertainty, we cannot pre-commit to a particular rate path."

Treasuries immediately sold off across the curve and bear-flattened. Front-end losses were underpinned by a notable pricing out of Fed rate cuts. 2y Treasury yields exceeded 5.00% intraday as the first full 25bp rate cut was pushed out to the December 2024 meeting. ## Japan:

JGBs sold off across the curve as USD/JPY continued to rise, without any response by the Japanese Ministry of Finance. 30y JGBs rallied in the Tokyo morning session but sold off in the Tokyo afternoon. Consensus expects the BoJ will remain on hold and maintain its current pace of JGB purchases (JPY 6tn per month), but the uncertainty for the policy stance regarding currency depreciation weighed on the rates market. 5y JGBs sold off by 0.5bp d/d to 0.495% on a simple yield basis while 30y JGBs remained unchanged at 1.95%. TONA OIS underperformed relative to JGBs and XCCY basis continued to tightened across the curve.

During the Tokyo session, USD/JPY rose to 155.60 without a clear fundamental catalyst to support the rally. Japanese Finance Minister Suzuki refrained from commenting on the currency market. There seemingly was increased speculation about MoF intervention before or after the BoJ meeting on Friday. Our economists expect the BoJ will maintain the status quo, but note a reduction in the BoJ’s JGB purchases is a risk scenario. CCY/JPY pairs increased across the board, especially GBP/JPY, CHF/JPY, and EUR/JPY.

## Europe:

G10 FX price action was dominated by US data. Higher-than-expected quarterly Core PCE led to significant US dollar strength as inflation worries intensified and US rates rose. However, despite rate cuts having been priced out in the front-end, the initial US dollar strength reversed. Risk currencies made up for most of their lost ground. GBP (+0.4%) was particularly resilient and reversed its entire move weaker against the initial USD strength. The Scandinavian currencies mostly recovered; NOK (+0.2%) and SEK (-0.1%). CHF (+0.3%) and EUR (+0.4%) traded through pre-data highs. USD/JPY pushed further past 155 up to 155.60 in the London afternoon but ultimately steadied ahead of Japan Tokyo CPI and the April BoJ meeting on Friday.

The focus of the London session was on the advance 1Q24 US GDP release; growth came in below consensus expectations while core PCE was stronger than expected. A bear-flattening of the Treasury curve spilled over to European duration. 10y Bunds sold off 4bp to 2.63%, a level last reached in late November. The move was entirely driven by real yields with inflation expectations relatively unchanged (2y2y inflation swap: +1bp). Risk assets sold off as the upside core PCE surprise led the rates market to push pricing of the first full Fed rate cut out to December; ECB meetings repriced accordingly, led by 2025 meetings (+7bp). Market pricing implied 67bp of cuts in 2024 and 55bp of cuts in 2025 by the London close. Initially, the 10y BTP-Bund spread widened but the move was quickly faded. Within the G4, Bonos underperformed amid media reports from overnight which said Prime Minister Sanchez had announced that he is considering whether to resign. 10y OATs outperformed 10y Bunds by \~0.5bp. Core ASW continued to cheapen but led by the long-end as the rise in volatility capped Schatz cheapening.

## Dollar Bloc:

Dollar bloc currencies largely reversed initial weakness; CAD (+0.2%), AUD (+0.3%), and NZD (+0.2%). Counter-intuitively, the DXY USD Index (-0.3%) fell despite strong inflation data, higher US yields, and a sell-off in equities.

## Emerging Markets

## Asia:

AxJ currencies weakened broadly, especially TWD and KRW, as most equity markets traded lower after weakness in US technology stocks. Despite a surprise reacceleration in Korean growth in 1Q24, KRW depreciated 0.4% to 1,375 alongside underperformance of local equities (KOSPI: -1.76%) and foreign equity outflows of US\$403mn. USD/TWD rallied 0.2% to 32.59, amid a decline in the local equity market of 1.4% and net foreign outflows of US\$756mn. USD/PHP rose 0.4% to 57.80 as the Philippines finance secretary, also a BSP monetary board member, signaled there is not a need for the BSP to hike rates currently. IDR depreciated 0.2% to 16,188 and partially retraced gains from the prior session. CNH and SGD were the exceptions. USD/CNH fell 0.1% to 7.2660, aided by relative outperformance of local equities (CSI 300: +0.3%) and a steady USD/CNY fixing. SGD gained 0.2% to 1.3595 while S\$NEER rallied to \~1.6% above the midpoint of the MAS policy band. THB and INR remained stable.

Most AxJ rates sold off, weighed down by the higher-for-longer rate outlook. Korea was the major underperformer; the 2s10s KRW NDIRS curve bear-steepened by 4bp.The 3s10s KTB yield curve also bear-steepened by 2bp and 10y KTB yields rose 5bp to 3.71%. In Thailand, the 5s10s NDOIS curve bear-flattened by 1bp while

ThaiGB yields remained steady. In India, both 2y and 10y G-sec rose 2bp while the NDOIS shifted 2bp higher. In Indonesia, the INDOGB yield curve twist-steepened; 1y rates fell 2bp but 10y yields rose 3bp. China rates outperformed peers; 2y and 5y CGB yields fell 3bp and 2bp, respectively, after the retreat on Wednesday. The CNY NDIRS curve held steady.

## Latin America:

Most LatAm currencies were weaker in the session following the upside surprise in US Core PCE. MXN (-0.8%) underperformed peers in the sell-off, although COP (- 0.4%) and BRL (-0.3%) also weakened. CLP (+0.2%) was stronger, likely helped by higher copper prices (+1.5%). Most rates were higher as USTs sold off and drove price action. TIIEs had losses in the belly (5y: +11bp), while the long-end of IBR underperformed (10y: +14bp). The CAM and DI curves also suffered losses, but the very front-ends (1y and January 2025 contracts) ended lower.

# G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy

MORGAN STANLEY & CO. LLC

Matthew Hornbach, CMT

Matthew.Hornbach@morganstanley.com

+1 212 761-1837

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies.

## Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and the underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves.

## Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
