# Venture Vision: Pre-IPO > Post-IPO

One chart in particular caught our eye last week from Michael Cyprys' work on how much value now accrues to investors prerather than post-IPO.

## See Michael's original report here

Private companies are growing increasingly important to public markets. Emerging technology and market color from private markets allow investors to glean meaningful insight about the competitive landscape and look around the next corner. Years ago many of the household-name larger private companies would likely have been public by now. Yet, today these private players reference a large number of dominant public players as direct competitors for market share.

We see companies staying private for longer, with a median age of \~11 years on average before coming public over the past two decades, an increase vs. \~8 years in prior years. As such, key competitors and potential disrupters are operating out of the spotlight, making it all the more important for investors to be aware of what's going on in the private markets and assess the potential impact to public peers. We also see more value creation occurring pre-IPO as these companies stay private for longer. Unicorns are no longer as rare and the number of publicly listed companies in the US has dropped by -43% since 2000.

Private capital is overtaking public capital, with private equity deployment in North America surpassing public capital raised, a trend that started in 2018 and continues to gain momentum. In 2023, there was \~\$800b of capital deployed in the private markets, far larger than the \~\$500b raised via IPOs. Private market funds have grown at a 14% CAGR over the past decade to \~\$14Tr of assets under management (AUM) with \~62% in buyout, growth, and VC strategies. Looking forward, we see a number of tailwinds that should continue to support double-digit growth, including rising allocations from under-allocated investors (e.g., retail) and a strong performance track record.

## Real-time Valuation Monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
