# SPE Market Shares in 2023 & Outlook

Sharp growth in sales on accelerated demand for China gave ASML the top global share in SPE in 2023. With attention on generative AI, GAA and flash multi-layering from 2024, in the short term we see relatively large benefits for names such as DISCO and Advantest.

## Key Takeaways

2023 saw noticeable growth in the lithography market and ASML claim the top global share. Effects of yen weakness hurt shares of TEL and other Japanese firms.

DISCO and Lasertec stand to benefit from market growth for grinders (generative AI) and mask inspection tools (shift to higher-priced Actinic tools).

TEL is gaining more share in cleaning and heat-treatment systems, and has potential to take more in etching tools as well.

Many of the equipment makers who stand to benefit from GAA and multilayer flash memory are US and European firms – eg, AMAT, Lam, ASMI.

Generative AI is the current hottest topic for equipment markets. Grinders and SoC testers should benefit as activity heats up still further.

ASML in pole position in 2023: In this report, we round up the competitive landscape in 2023 and the outlook ahead for SPE markets in which equipment makers in our coverage are involved. The SPE market was down 1% YoY to \$106B in 2023, and the WFE market was up 2% YoY to \$96B. The WFE market, which was expected to shrink significantly at the beginning of the year, continued to peak. By device, Dew Stepper grew 49% year-on-year, and ASML, the world’s top-selling device maker in this category, became the top manufacturer in the semiconductor manufacturing device market. SEMI expects 4% YoY growth in 2024 in the SPE market and 3% YoY growth in WFE. We expect growth of more than 20% YoY for both SPE and WFE in 2025.

In 2023, the overall WFE market was flat YoY, but the lithographic equipment market grew by 49% YoY and SAM shrank at most firms excluding ASML. As a result, though Applied Materials saw strong sales growth for core PVD equipment and offset the slump for other devices, pushing sales just above previous-year levels, it dropped to second place. Lam Research　saw sales decline as major memory maker clients suppressed investments. Although in the previous year it was just behind ASML's second place, in 2023 it saw the gap widen between it and the top two firms.

TEL in 4th as share shrinks: Tokyo Electron (TEL) ranked 4th in 2022, but after excluding yen depreciation impact and adding back-end device sales, it was 2nd after AMAT. It ranked 4th again in 2023, but like Lam Research, the gap widened between it and the top two firms. Ongoing yen depreciation is negative for TEL's market share, given transactions in yen. TEL expanded its share for core products such as coater developers, dielectric etchers, and tube CVDs, but its share for conductor etchers remains stagnant and for washers fell as SCREEN Holdings boosted production capacity and gained share.

Expect TEL counteroffensive in 2024: We forecast a slight recovery in market share for TEL in 2024. This is because 1) large-scale capex is planned in Japan & China, where the firm has high market share; 2) we think the lithographic equipment market will remain flat in 2024 and WFE market will grow, resulting in expansion of TEL's SAM; and 3) TEL's competitive edge vs. Japanese peers is steadily increasing thanks to tech improvements for washers & vertical furnaces/CVD.

Chinese equipment market share expanding, localization progressing (AMEC & ACMR covered by Charlie Chan, Naura covered by Ray Wu). Chinese manufacturers saw significant market share gains in dry etch, deposition, and wet cleaning, as Naura & AMEC gained market share in dry etch and deposition while ACMR gained in wet cleaning. We see two drivers here: 1) Customer willingness: We got feedback from most Chinese customers that they are more willing to adopt China WFE now vs. a few years ago. 2) Expanding product portfolios: Most Chinese WFE players have plans to introduce more comprehensive product portfolios.

China's Naura soaring: Thanks to the active Chinese SPE market and domestic production policies, Naura of China has soared above Japanese mid-sized makers such as Kokusai Electric, Hitachi High-Tech and SEMES (South Korea's biggest SPE maker) to rank 8th.

Europe sees strong share gains (ASML covered by Lee Simpson, ASM International covered by Nigel van Putten). ASML moved into the top spot for the first time with 23% overall share as the company was able to execute strongly against a considerable backlog. As seen elsewhere, China saw particular strong growth y/y. At a respectable distance from the "big 5", Dutch equipment maker ASM, with dollar equipment revenue of \$2.4bn, and a WFE share of 2.3%, is now the #6 player in the market. The company gained 350bps in SAM share which is largely due to mix (ASM has relatively low exposure to Memory) and a timely acquisition in Silicon Carbide (LPE) but also successful product introductions in recent years.

Japan's SPE market share drops to 3rd for first time: By country, Japan's SPE market share dropped to #3 for the first time due to ASML's strong showing and the yen's depreciation. The continued decline in the share of Japanese SPE makers is presumed to be due to the fact that there are no semiconductor makers such as Intel and IBM in North America, and there are no joint technology development associations such as imec in Europe that develop advanced semiconductor manufacturing technologies, and as a result, the tech sensitivity and marketing power of SPE makers are falling, which is causing the growth area to be overrun. Since the late 2010s, Japanese WFE makers have gained significant share in innovative differentiated products and improved their profit margins only in the ACTINIC mask inspection equipment for EUVL by Laser Tech and supercritical dry cleaning equipment by Tokyo Electron.We believe it is highly likely that the share for the Japanese makers will taper down in future if there is no growth in the share and the birth of advanced semiconductor manufacturers in Japan, and joint technology development unions such as LSTC are not successful. We'll be watching how the establishment of Rapidus and JASM has an impact on the SPE market in Japan.

On the other hand, the profitability of Japanese SPE manufacturers has improved greatly: Over the past 25 years, profit margins for SPE makers have improved steadily, with the following at play: 1) rapid growth in sales; 2) SPE commercialization fueled by demand that has often far outpaced supply capacity since 2017; 3) high added value due to differentiated products by Tokyo Electron, Disco, Laser Tech, etc.; and 4) streamlining by various companies to reduce fixed costs and shortening production leadtimes. Profitability improvements have been a big achievement for most companies, and we believe that this has led to higher valuations in the SPE industry.

Accelerated Chinese demand and the rise of generative AI fueled strong growth in some equipment categories: Lithography tools and PVD (on strong orders from China), grinders (driven by generative AI), and mask inspection tools (progressive shift to more expensive actinic units) all saw sizeable market growth. Markets for almost all other types of equipment contracted YoY.

Three cycles of the SPE market: The semiconductor production equipment market grew rapidly until the 1990s, then lost steam from 2001, and since then earnings and stock prices in the industry have stagnated. The SPE market in 2017 hit a record high (first peak in the 17 years dating back to 2000), with both the semis and equipment markets surging in a ''supercycle" that began in late 2016 (sparked by a surge in demand for flash memory). Earnings during that stretch for the leading industry companies repeatedly set records too. Tokyo Electron back to 2001 had seen valuations contract (being regarded as in a mature field), but has rapidly expanded on the back of the supercycle that emerged from 2016 and the recent generative AI boom. We believe the SPE market will continue to see strong growth ahead too, with a variety of themes (discussed below) driving the way.

Generative AI, the most topical investment theme, and HBM: Exhibit 11 matches the topics that we expect to be the main investment themes for the industry with the related equipment categories. Currently, production equipment for high-bandwidth memory is seeing considerable growth in demand. Capex is mainly focused on back-end processes, where DRAM are layered on logic wafers known as base dies . Longer DRAM test times are significantly increasing demand for memory testers and probers.

Threat from replacement of multiple exposure equipment with EUVL vs. opportunities for deposition tools with GAA and 3D-NAND multi-layering: Adoption of EUV lithography tools and replacement of multiple exposure are expected to exert further downward pressure on equipment such as etching tools, deposition tools and cleaning systems. Conversely, GAA (gate-all-around) architecture stands to increase the number of processes in which deposition tools are used. Adoption of BS-PDN architecture should lead to increased demand for grinders for use in hybrid bonding. For advanced packages, we foresee rising demand for i-line lithography tools and for testers and probers for KGD (known good die) screening. Etching tools stand to reap considerable benefits of 3D-NAND multi-layering, and multi-tiering should also boost demand for other deposition tools including batch ALD.

## ASML dominant

We estimate a market size of \$24.5bn in 2023, representing growth of about 50% YoY despite the YoY slump in most of the SPE market. The increase likely owed to large imports of ArF immersion scanners by Chinese semiconductor manufacturers to prepare for upcoming US export restrictions. ASML has virtually monopolized the market by value share. Canon (covered by Mie Yamazaki) dropped out of the competition for cutting-edge development in the latter half of the 2000s. Today, it does not make any steppers more advanced beyond the ArF dry equipment. Nikon has lost market share in ArF immersion scanners due to ASML’s overwhelming productivity advantage, and was at one point on the verge of withdrawal. Nikon’s sales in the space were previously highly dependent on Intel, but it is now working to diversify its customer base.

## Etching equipment

## Applied Materials increasing its market share

We peg the market at \$15.3bn in 2023. Applied Materials, TEL, and Lam Research had battled for share until 2004, but Lam has pulled far ahead since 2005, thanks to a major performance improvement in its etching equipment through successful development of ICP plasma sources. In 2023, Lam Research lost market share due to curbed flash memory investment, while the weak yen led to a lower share for TEL. Hitachi High-Tech gained share in 2022 thanks to aggressive investment by Intel, but lost share in 2023 on slower capex by Intel, and has resigned itself to a low market share for memory applications.

## Potential for TEL to expand market share for cryo etching equipment

We see prospects for market share gains by TEL. Etching equipment is key in the multilayering of 3D-NAND. The etching process for insulation film must be completed before the hard mask is depleted. Focusing on the huge increase in SiO2 film etching speed with CF4/H2 gas at an ultra-low -60 , TEL has more than doubled etching speed. Its research shows the reaction between HF gas and SiO2 film is strengthened at lower temps and the etching speed can be raised by adding gases containing phosphorus (and others) and water to the HF plasma. Two passes of etching had been needed for 200-layer 3D-NAND, with 3-4 passes required for 500 layers, but TEL’s new etching equipment has reduced this to one pass for 200 layers and two for 500 layers. We think memory makers with lower multi-tier tech capabilities could be keen to adopt TEL's new etching equipment.

## Lam Research sees TEL cryo etching equipment threat, preps response

Lam Research is exploring countermoves to TEL cryo etching equipment. The firm is aware of the high performance of TEL’s cryo etching and the acquisition of DTOR (Development tool of record) from customers, and acknowledges it may lose share to some extent. The firm now owns 100% share in NAND channel hole etchers, and admits that clients may consider to dual source purchases. Further, for some clients, when the 500 layers of 3D-NAND are etched in two separate stages, they may use TEL's equipment for the first stage, and Lam Research's for the second. Lam, meanwhile, said the firm has already delivered 6,500 etching devices in total, and maintained strong client relationships. The firm also think in manufacturing sites there is a tendency to prefer Lam's more familiar equipment.

## Cleaning equipment

## Single-wafer cleaning equipment now the mainstream choice

For 2023, we estimate market size of \$1.13bn in batch cleaning equipment and \$4.15bn in single-wafer cleaning equipment (including scrubbers). Batch cleaning equipment was the mainstream choice to 2007, but single-wafer cleaners have recently become the standard.

## Tokyo Electron growing stronger, but SCREEN slightly improves share of single-wafer cleaning systems in 2023

TEL raised its share for batch cleaning equipment in 2023, as it had done in 2022. The gains are attributable to the major improvement in the company’s tech prowess since 2020. While SCREEN's share of single-wafer systems was nearly 60% vs. TEL's just over 10% in 2009, the two companies’ shares have since rapidly converged. TEL dominates the market for cleaning equipment with supercritical drying capability for memory applications. We estimate unit sales prices for this equipment are 4-5x higher than conventional machines. The firm is steadily improving product competitiveness and added value. In 2022, TEL led industry peers in expanding production capacity. By contrast, SCREEN was not able to ship sufficient equipment due to capacity limitations, losing clients to TEL in the Chinese market because of the delayed shipments. However, many companies have now resumed procurement from Screen, partly based on a preference for market leaders’ products within the Chinese market. We believe SCREEN management made a reasonable decision. Having expanded its production structure, the company was able to ship sufficient equipment to the clients that it had failed to supply in 2022, leading to a slight YoY improvement in market share despite the weak yen. Meanwhile, TEL lost share owing to a comeback by SCREEN and yen depreciation.

## Lam Research strengthens packaging cleaning with acquisition

Lam Research has been expanding its share with single-wafer cleaning equipment in recent years, and the increase in share in 2023 was mainly due to the contribution of SEMSYSCO, which was acquired in November 2022. Lam Research intends to strengthen its packaging business through SEMYSCO’s cleaning and electrification plating.

## Market cannibalization through etching equipment in form of dry cleaning systems

As semiconductor miniaturization progresses and liquid cleaning approaches its limits, we expect to see the introduction of dry cleaning equipment, which breaks down and vaporizes contaminants via plasma or gases without the use of chemical solutions that can cause pattern collapse and are unable to reach deep into high aspect ratio holes. Dry cleaners are a type of dry etching equipment. The shift to dry cleaners is thus akin to the cannibalization of the cleaning equipment market via etching equipment, which is likely to limit market growth.

## Mask inspection systems

## Lasertec finally takes pole position

We estimate the market at \$1.96bn in 2023. KLA increased its market share in 2022 but Lasertec took the top spot in 2023, its rise fueled by its dominance in high-priced actinic patterned mask defect inspection for EUV lithography.

## KLA’s EUV lithography mask inspection strategy

KLA sees EUV lithography mask inspection systems as a key business. There are three types of inspection systems: optical; electron beam; and actinic (EUV light source). KLA says it is currently focusing on optical inspection systems, as the throughput of electron beam systems is too low and it is still developing an actinic system for launch in or after 2026, when high-NA EUV lithography is set to start mass production. Our research indicates that KLA’s development efforts remain ongoing but it is likely that it has missed out on business opportunities.

## Leading chipmakers regard actinic as best EUV lithography mask inspection solution

Intel (covered by Joseph Moore), Samsung (Shawn Kim) and TSMC (Charlie Chan) gave presentations at the session on actinic inspection systems at Photomask Japan 2024 (April 16-18). They all concluded from their evaluations of Lasertec’s actinic mask inspection system ACTIS that it is the optimal solution for high-NA EUV lithography mask inspection.

## Actinic advantages and evolution

All highlighted the following main advantages of actinic systems’ DUV and EB technologies. They offer high resolution, offer more accurate transfer defect inspection, enable the inspection of pellicle transmission and make it possible to visualize phase defects. All three companies said it is possible to apply machine learning to the D-DB model to achieve automatic classification in defect inspection for curvilinear masks involving complex curves, which had previously been an issue in actinic inspection.

## ACTIS business opportunities: 1) Phase-shift masks

Another issue for high-NA EUV lithography, due to be utilized in mass production from 2027, is 3D mask effects that distort patterning resulting from the shadowing effect caused by the oblique angle of illumination. The use of thinner mask absorbers, phaseshift masks, high-n masks and high-K masks are being explored as potential means to reduce the surface area of shadow. We believe phase-shift masks are closest to commercialization. We forecast that when this happens it will drive further growth in demand for Lasertec’s inspection systems, as the number of instances that can only be handled by Lasertec’s mask inspection systems and blank inspection systems will increase.

## ACTIS business opportunities: 2) Large masks

Another question for EUV lithography high-NA masks is reduction in exposure area, with introduction of large masks a possible solution here. Blanks makers have completed trial production of 6x12x0.4 inch blanks already and indicated additional dedicated production lines are needed for mass production. Commercial application of large masks would likely drive expansion in Lasertec’s EUV lithography-related inspection system sales.
