# Venture Vision: IPOs #2 – What Next?

We present 3 theoretical scenarios for IPO activity: (1) if our strategists' view on the S&P500 materialises; (2) if January S&P500 returns foretell what happens next; and (3) if this cycle restarts like the last five did. From most to least bullish, the scenarios rank 3 then 2 then 1. US election years are positive in any scenario.

Three scenarios… all pointing to a return to more "normal" levels: Below, we summarise three hypothetical scenarios and the possible \$bn IPO proceeds in 2024 that could result from them:

1. Our equity strategists' view on the S&P500 plays out, implying a possible bull/bear IPO proceeds range of \$12-40bn – even at the low end, this would be a 20% uplift YoY in 2024.

2. January S&P500 returns YoY presage what happens next, pointing to a \$42bn year in 2024 – a strong start to the year for the S&P500 has typically led to a strong year for global capital markets. That said, narrow breadth to date in 2024 may lend itself more prudently to an equal-weight S&P500 benchmark.

3. This cycle restarts like the last five did, suggesting a \$43bn proceeds year. While this >\$40bn upper-end projection is 62% below the 2021 high, it would be c22% above the inflation-adjusted average since 2000.

(1) Applying the top-down MS market view points to IPO activity below the through-cycle average: Our equity strategists have bull, base and bear targets for the S&P500 of 5050, 4500 and 3850 respectively (see their note here). These equate to YoY performance of +17%, +4% and -19%, respectively. Applying these outcomes to the 24-year relationship between monthly IPO activity on a YoY basis suggests that the next 12 months could be relatively muted compared with the long-term average for IPOs – if this range of outcomes proves correct.

(2) Strong January = strong year: If we look at only January YoY S&P500 returns over the past 25 years, the correlation points to a strong 2024 for IPO proceeds. January performance has been up c20% YoY for the headline index. Intersecting this with the typical cycle correlation, the IPO proceeds would land around the \$42bn mark – the seventh best year since 2000. We caveat that this analysis does not make any attempt to adjust the likely outcome for the historical narrowness of the index rally in 2024 YTD. If we use the equal-weight S&P500 January YoY performance instead, the outcome would be a more modest c\$30bn.

(3) Historical cycles point to a strong 2024/25: Unsurprisingly, prolonged periods of weakness usually coincide with either bear markets (Dot Com Bubble, GFC) or periods with a mix of subpar equity returns, increasing rates or rising inflation (1988- 90, 2015-17). However, if we break down the market into percentile performance through time, that suggests 2024 is highly unlikely to be a bottom-quintile year: we have never seen more than two years in a row with below 20th percentile proceed levels. To help read Exhibit 8 below, each year is rebased versus 100% in 2021. Years of bottom-quintile proceeds have almost always been followed by years of >2x higher proceeds.

Finally… election years generally bode well: We should start by caveating that some US election years have also coincided with bubbles through history – 2000 and 2020, notably, are distortive to the figures below. However, even averaging out these effects, US election years have tended to see greater volume of IPO activity – and particularly so in the second half of the year. There is no notable slowdown (other than the typical summer slump) evident in the deal-making before elections are run.

## Real-time Valuation Monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
