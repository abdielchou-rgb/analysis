# Venture Vision: M&A > IPO

This week we published our "10 Themes for 2024" progress report. The M&A story is playing out as expected. IPOs on the other hand...the wait goes on. We struggle to find prior precedent for the current correlation divergence.

Morgan Stanley - per our original report - expects 2024 to be a year of >\$5 trillion in M&A volume across the world. While this would be a 47% uplift from 2023, it would simply mean a return to the pre-Covid average level. The YTD level is at c. \$1.8 trillion, which therefore leaves work to be done in the second half of the year, but which we believe - given the bottoming in activity and recent re-acceleration in deal announcements - should still be achievable. M&A activity is now starting to detach from its prior correlation with IPO activity - more below.

Digging further into the M&A and IPO trends, we have been right and wrong. There have been signs of cyclical recovery in deal volume and value. As explained above, we expect the M&A recovery to continue through the course of 2024. We were also correct in our assertion that if there were to be a recovery from cyclical lows, it would more likely be M&A that would recover before IPOs thereafter. This has typically been the case in prior cyclical recoveries and appears to be no different this time.

However, what is different this time is how much longer the IPO cycle is taking to restart. Typically, IPO cycles tend to trough 0-6 months after M&A cycles trough. So far, this has been the difference versus our original thesis. Depending on whether we look at deal count or value, one could argue that the lag to the M&A recovery has been closer to a year at this point. IPOs have not only not recovered in unison with M&A, listings have continued to fall since M&A activity troughed; something we're hard pressed to find comparisons for from history. One could conclude, therefore, that either M&A activity has recovered too soon - which we do not agree with given dry-powder, valuation opportunities and buoyant leading indicators, or that IPOs should show increasing signs of recovery as the year progresses and into 2025.

## The Fintech Radar (30 JUN 2024)

• #1 Klarna plans to divest Klarna Checkout (KCO): This week (24th June), Klarna announced its plans to divest their acquiring business, Klarna Checkout, to a consortium of investors, including BLQ Invest and Systematic Growth. According to Bloomberg, the deal valued the business unit at \$520m, and is expected to conclude in October 2024 (the company has not commented). According to the company, this move will allow KCO to continue its evolution within the acquiring space as an independent entity. Launched in 2012, KCO is a full-stack payments solution. It is a market leader in Sweden, with over 40% market share, and over 20% in the Nordics more broadly.

• #2 Payabli secures \$20m in Series A funding: This week (25th June), payments infrastructure provider for Software companies Payabli announced that it has closed a \$20m Series A funding round. The round was led by QED Investors with participation from existing investors TTV Capital, Fika Ventures, and Bling Capital. According to the company, this is on the back of a >340% y/y increase in group revenue over the past 12 months. Payabli's payments infrastructure enables software companies to makes payments a part of their business model (through payment acceptance and issuance) through a single unified API.

• #3 Rainforest announces \$20m in Series A: This week (26th June) Rainforest, a payment processor for software platforms, announced that it has raised an oversubscribed \$20m in Series A funding. The round was led by Matrix Partners, with additional investors including Accel, Infinity Ventures, and BoxGroup, The Fintech Fund, Tech Square Ventures, and Ardent Venture Partners - which also participated in Rainforest's seed round in 2023. The latest round brings the company's total funding to c. \$32m which will be used to invest in its core product offering, and accelerate its growth via new go-to-market initiatives. Rainforest's platform enables software companies to grow revenue by embedding payments into their products. Since its seed round in 2023, Rainforest has grown payment volume by 17x, according to the company.

Unless otherwise indicated, the news reports referenced above have not been confirmed.

## Real-time Valuation Monitor:

A simple top-down metric to look at is the Refinitiv Venture Capital Index, which provides an aggregated and rebased index of US venture capital-funded firms. The index:

• Leverages Refinitiv private company data of over 22,000 US firms;

• Estimates the value of each company over time by compiling known firm values, estimating missing values and interpolating values between events; and

• Aggregates month-to-month returns to compute a market cap-weighted index tracking the broad US venture capital universe since 1995.

While the log-scale sees a more modest pull-back in valuations of US VC-backed companies, the index as a whole is nonetheless nearly 18% down YoY and 40% off its highs.
